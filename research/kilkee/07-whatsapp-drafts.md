## To Yuksel Kangal

Lead with the urgent item. He owns property near the site, so his position is materially different from a visitor's and there may be a live deadline.

---

Yuksel, I have been going through the Kilkee treatment plant situation in some detail and there is one thing I wanted to put to you directly because it may affect you and it may be time sensitive. Most of the coverage says the fight is over because the residents lost in the High Court in July. That is only true of the planning permission. Uisce Éireann still does not own the site. It is acquiring the land compulsorily, and a compulsory purchase order is a completely separate legal process with its own objection rights, its own hearing and its own deadlines. Those deadlines are short and missing one is usually fatal. So the first question is simply whether you have received any notice from Uisce Éireann relating to a compulsory purchase order, a wayleave or a right of way, including anything that came through during 2025. If you have, or if you are not sure, I would talk to a solicitor this week rather than next month. The second thing is that the deposited map and schedule for the order are the definitive record of whose land is affected, not the newspaper coverage, and it is worth checking whether your property or any access you rely on appears in it. I have put together a short briefing on where the whole thing actually stands and what is still open, including the argument I think is the strongest one and which has barely been made in public, which is that the plant is being built to a treatment standard that European law will withdraw before the planning permission itself expires. I will send it on to you now. Nothing in it is legal advice and one or two points still need verifying, which I have flagged in it, but the property notice question is worth checking today. Happy to talk it through any time.

## To Ger Blake

Different position. No property, so no CPO exposure and no deadline. His useful contribution is the EPA submission, the European Commission complaint, and carrying the right argument.

---

Ger, you know Kilkee better than most people I could ask, so I wanted to send you something. The coverage since July has left everyone thinking the treatment plant is now a done deal because the residents lost the High Court case. It is not. The planning permission survived, but Uisce Éireann still needs two other things it does not have, the land, which it is acquiring compulsorily through a separate process with its own objection rights, and an authorisation from the EPA to discharge, which is a separate public process run by a body independent of the planning system. Neither has been contested yet and both are open to submissions from anybody. The argument that I think matters and which has hardly been made is this. The plant is designed for preliminary and primary treatment only. The revised EU wastewater directive requires secondary treatment for places Kilkee's size by the end of 2035. The planning permission was granted in June 2025 for ten years. So the plant reaches the end of its permission at roughly the moment European law requires a higher standard than it can deliver, and nobody has published an assessment of whether the site can even take the upgrade. On top of that, Ireland was supposed to provide treatment for Kilkee by the end of 2005 and provided none for twenty years, and that delay is now being used as the reason this particular scheme has to go ahead without further argument. The position that wins is not no plant here. It is build it once and build it right. Kilkee needs treatment and everyone accepts that. The objection is to spending public money twice. I have written up a short briefing on all of it and I will send it on. Anyone can make a submission to the EPA and anyone can complain to the European Commission about a member state not applying EU law, with no standing requirement and no cost. If you are down there and talking to people, that argument is worth carrying. Give me a shout if you want to go through it.
