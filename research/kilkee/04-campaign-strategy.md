# Kilkee: Strategy to Stop the Permitted Scheme

Prepared 8 August 2026. Cuan. For John Webb O'Rourke.

Objective as set by John: support the residents and get the permission and the project stopped.

This document is strategy, not legal advice. The residents' solicitors and counsel own the litigation. Cuan's role is to find the fronts nobody is fighting on and to arm them.

---

## 1. The reframe

Two objectives are being treated as one, and they have very different odds.

**Objective A: quash the ACP 321258 permission.** One route remains, the appeal. On the public record the probability is low. The High Court has dismissed. Certification under section 50A(7) is a demanding filter applied by the same court, and the leapfrog to the Supreme Court requires exceptional circumstances. This is not hopeless, but it is not where the leverage is.

**Objective B: stop this plant, as designed, on this site.** Multiple routes remain, most of them untouched, several of them free, and at least two of them stronger than the appeal. The permission is a consent to build. It is not the only consent required, and it is not land.

The campaign has spent just under €100,000 and two years fighting on Objective A. Objective B is where the remaining chances live, and progress on B also improves the odds on A.

**Nothing gets built without three things: the planning permission, the land, and an EPA discharge authorisation.** Uisce Éireann has one of the three.

## 2. The six fronts, ranked by leverage

### Front 1. The CPO, ACP 322828. Highest leverage. Time critical.

Uisce Éireann does not own the site. It says it is progressing land acquisition, wayleaves and rights of way and will use compulsory acquisition where agreement cannot be reached. The Uisce Éireann Compulsory Purchase (Kilkee Wastewater Treatment Plant) Order 2025 is before An Coimisiún Pleanála as case 322828 (Tier C, single source, confirm first).

Why this is the strongest front:

- It is a **separate consent with a separate decision**, which means a separate objection process, a possible oral hearing, and a fresh judicial review window on the confirmation decision. The res judicata problem that kills a rerun of the planning arguments does not apply to a challenge to the CPO confirmation, because it is a different decision by a different process on different statutory criteria.
- The statutory test is different and more favourable. Uisce Éireann acquires under sections 32 and 93 of the Water Services Act 2007. On confirmation, the Commission must be satisfied the acquisition is necessary. A compulsory taking of private property engages Article 43 of the Constitution and Article 1 of Protocol 1 ECHR, and must be proportionate. Proportionality of a taking is a genuinely justiciable question in a way that visual amenity is not.
- **The 2035 argument lands here with full force.** It is one thing to say the design is short sighted at a planning appeal. It is another to say to the body confirming a compulsory taking of a family's land: you are being asked to find it necessary to take this land for a plant that EU law will render non compliant before the permission expires, on a site nobody has assessed for the upgrade that is already legislated for. Necessity is the CPO test. That argument is aimed squarely at it.
- **No land, no plant.** A CPO refused or delayed stops the project as effectively as a quashed permission, and costs a fraction as much.
- Objectors to a CPO are the affected landowners and parties with an interest. That is a smaller, better funded, more focused group than a town meeting, and their objections are legally weightier because their property is being taken.

Actions, in order:
1. Confirm ACP 322828 is the Kilkee CPO and establish its stage. Has the objection period closed. Has an oral hearing been sought or scheduled.
2. Identify the affected landowners from the deposited schedule.
3. If the objection window is open, ensure every affected owner objects and requests an oral hearing. An oral hearing puts Uisce Éireann's engineers under examination in public, which has not happened yet at any point in this process.
4. If the window has closed, establish whether an oral hearing will be held and whether observers can be heard.

**This is urgent. CPO objection periods are short and unforgiving. Find out where it stands this week.**

### Front 2. The EPA discharge authorisation, D0078-01. High leverage. Certain to arise.

The existing licence authorises the existing discharge. A new treatment plant with a new discharge arrangement will require the licence to be reviewed or a new authorisation obtained under the Waste Water Discharge (Authorisation) Regulations 2007 to 2020. That process is public. The EPA notifies and invites submissions, and notifies everyone who made a written submission of its decision.

Why this bites:

- It is the one forum where **treatment standard is the actual subject matter**, not a side issue. In planning, treatment standard was context. At the EPA, it is the whole question.
- If the 2024 Annual Environmental Report does record a secondary treatment plant as a specified improvement with a 2027 completion date, then Uisce Éireann is asking the EPA to authorise a discharge that falls short of the improvement its own licence file records. That is a direct, documented inconsistency, put to the body that wrote the requirement. **Verify this first. It is the highest value single fact in the case.**
- The EPA is independent of the planning system. It is not bound by the Commission's view and it has no institutional stake in defending the permission.
- Submissions cost nothing and carry no costs risk. Anyone can make one.

Actions:
1. Pull the licence, the inspector's report and the 2024 AER for D0078-01. Confirm or kill the secondary treatment commitment.
2. Establish whether a review or new application has been lodged, and monitor for it.
3. Prepare a technical submission now, so it is ready the day the process opens.

### Front 3. European Commission complaint. Free. No standing. No costs risk. John can file this personally.

Any citizen may complain to the Commission that a Member State is not applying EU law correctly. No standing requirement, no fee, no exposure. It is submitted on the standard complaint form and handled through CHAP.

Two candidate grounds, both requiring verification before filing:

1. **Urban Wastewater Treatment.** Kilkee has been discharging untreated sewage. Article 7 of Directive 91/271/EEC required appropriate treatment for coastal discharges below 10,000 p.e. from 31 December 2005. Kilkee has had none. That is a long standing compliance failure by the State, and it is a matter of fact, not opinion. Separately, the State is now permitting a primary plant with no evident plan for the secondary standard the recast Directive imposes by 2035 or 2037. Note the interaction flagged in the evidence pack: the 2037 derogation appears conditional on appropriate treatment being applied on 1 January 2025, which Kilkee was not.
2. **EIA screening.** If the sub threshold screening determination did not properly assess location sensitivity, that is a Directive 2011/92/EU issue.

Why this matters even though it is slow: the Commission does not resolve individual cases, but a credible complaint creates a documented State level compliance question, and infringement risk is the one thing that reliably changes Irish departmental behaviour. It also creates the political cover for the domestic route at Front 4.

This is the single most useful thing John can personally do, and it requires no connection to Kilkee.

### Front 4. Political and governance pressure on the contradiction. Cheap. Fast. Underused.

The story is not "locals object to sewage plant". The story is: **two arms of the State have specified two different plants for the same town, and the citizen is paying for the one that has to be built twice.**

That framing is factual, it is checkable, and it is uncomfortable in a way that landscape objection is not. It is also the frame that makes the campaign look responsible rather than obstructive, which is currently its biggest public problem.

Actions:
1. Parliamentary Questions to the Minister for Housing, Local Government and Heritage: what treatment standard Kilkee will have on 31 December 2035, and what the estimated cost of the subsequent upgrade is.
2. PQ on how many Uisce Éireann schemes currently in planning or construction are designed to a standard that will not meet the recast Directive.
3. Oireachtas committee route. This is a national question with a Kilkee example, which is far more likely to get a hearing than a Kilkee question.
4. Brief the Clare Oireachtas members with the documented contradiction, not with the campaign's grievance.

### Front 5. The appeal. Low probability. Not free. Decision imminent.

Everything turns on the unread judgment, as set out in the evidence pack. If Humphreys J found legal error and refused relief on discretionary and proportionality grounds, there is a genuine point of general public importance about the limits of judicial discretion to withhold remedies where an EU environmental breach is established. That is an appeal worth running. If he found no error, it is not.

One important and probably under appreciated point for the campaign: **costs exposure may be far lower than they think.** Section 50B of the Planning and Development Act 2000, as amended by the Environment (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2011, provides special costs rules in environmental judicial review, and the Supreme Court in the Heather Hill line of authority held that the protection applies to all grounds in the proceedings where at least one ground invokes an EU environmental directive. If the residents pleaded any EIA or Habitats Directive ground, and on the reported facts they almost certainly did, they may be substantially protected. Get the costs ruling. If they are protected, the fear driving people away from continuing is misplaced, and that changes the fundraising position.

Verify the current state of that authority with counsel before relying on it. Costs rules in planning judicial review have been under active reform.

### Front 6. Uisce Éireann's own internal gate. The persuasion route.

Uisce Éireann's stated sequence is land, wayleaves and rights of way, then internal approval, then implementation. There is a decision point ahead that is not a court and not a regulator.

A commercially rational utility facing a legislated obligation to reach secondary treatment by 2035 or 2037, on a site it has to compulsorily acquire, against organised opposition, with a permission expiring in June 2035, has a real question about whether building the permitted scheme is the cheapest path to compliance. Nobody has put that question to it as a business case rather than as an objection.

This is where John's actual skill applies. A costed, unemotional paper showing that build once to secondary is cheaper over twenty years than build primary now and upgrade later, delivered to the right person, is a legitimate and potentially decisive intervention. It is also the only route that ends with Kilkee getting a better plant rather than no plant.

## 3. The reframe the campaign needs

Current position: "not here". Reads as obstruction, loses in planning, has lost in court, and hands Uisce Éireann the moral argument every time it points at the raw sewage.

Recommended position: **"build it once, build it right."**

- It concedes the need, which the campaign already concedes but is not credited for.
- It moves the fight from taste to compliance, where the facts favour the campaign.
- It is impossible to caricature as nimbyism.
- It gives politicians something to support.
- It is the only frame under which the campaign can win and Kilkee still gets treatment.

Three things the campaign should stop saying immediately, because each one costs it credibility on the points where it is right:

1. Comparing 20,000 summer visitors to 7,926 p.e. as though they were the same unit. They are not, and every engineer who hears it discounts everything that follows.
2. Describing primary settlement tanks as constantly agitated open tanks of raw sewage. Primary settlement is not aeration. If the description is wrong, it will be used to discredit the whole case.
3. The claim about sludge incineration in Limerick, unless it can be evidenced. Uisce Éireann has denied operating such a facility, and the denial appears to be correct.

Dropping those three costs the campaign nothing and removes Uisce Éireann's best line, which is that the opposition is built on misinformation.

What John is uniquely placed to do:

1. **File the European Commission complaint.** No standing required, no costs, and it is a governance act rather than a protest act. Genuinely his.

## 6. Honest probabilities

Stated so John is not operating on optimism. These are Cuan's estimates, Tier D, and they will move once the primary documents are read.

| Outcome | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Permission quashed on appeal | Low. Improves materially only if the judgment shows error plus discretionary refusal of relief. |
| CPO refused or materially delayed | Moderate, and the highest of any single front. Depends on stage and on whether affected owners are objecting properly. |
| EPA process forces a higher treatment standard or a redesign | Moderate. Strongest if the AER secondary commitment is real. |
| European Commission infringement action naming Kilkee | Low in the short term, but the complaint is free and the pressure it creates is real. |
| Uisce Éireann redesigns voluntarily | Low now, rising if Fronts 1 to 4 land together and the business case is put properly. |
| Project simply does not proceed as permitted before June 2035 | Higher than any individual number above. Delay is a real outcome and the permission has an expiry date. |

The honest summary: the odds of the specific outcome John asked for, the permission quashed, are low. The odds of the plant as designed not being built are considerably better than the campaign currently believes, and almost all of that lies on fronts nobody is fighting yet.
