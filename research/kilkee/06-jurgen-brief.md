## Context in three lines

An Coimisiún Pleanála granted permission (ACP 321258, 11 June 2025, ten years) for a preliminary and primary wastewater treatment plant at Foohagh and Kilkee Lower, overturning Clare County Council's refusal and departing from its own inspector's recommendation to refuse. Judicial review 2025 JR 1205 dismissed by Humphreys J in July 2026, reportedly on the basis that quashing would be disproportionate. Leave to appeal pending, decision expected September 2026.

The argument to be tested is that the permitted plant will fail the treatment standard European law imposes before its own permission expires, and that this bears on the necessity test for the compulsory acquisition of the site.

## Propositions to test

### Group A. The treatment standard argument. Priority 1.

**A1. TO VERIFY.** Under Article 7 of Council Directive 91/271/EEC, discharges to coastal waters from agglomerations below 10,000 p.e. require only "appropriate treatment", and the compliance date was 31 December 2005.

Retrieve: Article 7, and Article 2(9) for the definition of appropriate treatment. Articles 4 and 6 for context on where secondary treatment and the less sensitive area regime apply.

Questions: does "appropriate treatment" as defined permit preliminary and primary treatment only, for a coastal discharge at circa 7,926 p.e. serving a designated bathing water? Is the absence of any treatment at Kilkee since 31 December 2005 a breach by the State, and is that breach a matter of record?

**A2. TO VERIFY, and this is the decisive one.** Directive (EU) 2024/3019 extends secondary treatment to all agglomerations of 1,000 p.e. and above, with a general date of 31 December 2035, subject to a derogation to 31 December 2037 for agglomerations of 2,000 p.e. and above but below 10,000 p.e. discharging to coastal waters "which apply appropriate treatment in accordance with Article 7 of Directive 91/271/EEC on 1 January 2025".

Retrieve: the operative Articles of Directive (EU) 2024/3019, not the EUR-Lex summary. Identify the Article and paragraph numbers precisely.

Questions:
1. What is the exact wording of the derogation and where does it sit?
2. Is the derogation conditional on appropriate treatment being applied in fact on 1 January 2025, or does it attach to the class of agglomeration by size and receiving water regardless?
3. Kilkee applied no treatment at all on 1 January 2025. On the operative wording, does Kilkee fall inside or outside the derogation? If outside, the applicable date is 31 December 2035.
4. What is the transposition deadline, and what is the current state of Irish transposition?
5. Does the Directive impose any intermediate obligation before 2035, for example a national implementation programme with earlier reporting dates that Kilkee would appear in?

**A3. TO VERIFY.** The permission was granted on 11 June 2025 for ten years and therefore expires in or about June 2035, at or about the point the secondary treatment obligation bites.

Question: does anything in the Planning and Development Act 2000 permit extension of duration in these circumstances, and would an extension carry the plant past the compliance date without the upgrade?

### Group B. The compulsory purchase front. Priority 1, time critical.

**B1. TO VERIFY.** Uisce Éireann acquires compulsorily under sections 32 and 93 of the Water Services Act 2007, and An Coimisiún Pleanála confirms, amends or rejects the order.

Retrieve: sections 32 and 93 of the Water Services Act 2007 and any implementing regulations.

Questions:
1. What is the statutory test the Commission applies on confirmation? Is necessity the test, and if so, necessity of what, precisely?
2. What are the objection procedure and the statutory time limits, and who has standing to object?
3. Is an oral hearing available as of right on objection, or at the Commission's discretion?
4. What is the route and time limit for challenging a confirmation, and does section 50 of the Planning and Development Act 2000 apply to it or does ordinary Order 84 apply?

**B2. TO VERIFY.** A compulsory acquisition engages Article 43 of the Constitution and Article 1 of Protocol 1 ECHR and must be proportionate.

Question: on Irish authority, how intensively is proportionality reviewed on a CPO confirmation, and is the obsolescence of the scheme the land is being taken for capable of bearing on necessity? This is the point of the whole exercise. If proposition A2 holds and necessity is the test, the argument is that it cannot be necessary to compulsorily acquire land for a plant that will not meet the legal standard within the life of its permission, absent any assessment that the site can take the upgrade.

**B3. TO VERIFY.** Whether the dismissal of 2025 JR 1205 creates any res judicata or abuse of process bar to raising treatment standard arguments in objection to the CPO confirmation, or in a challenge to that confirmation.

This is important. The working assumption is that it does not, because the CPO confirmation is a separate decision by a separate process on separate statutory criteria. Test that assumption properly rather than accepting it.

### Group C. Grounds in the planning decision not yet fully examined. Priority 2.

**C1. TO VERIFY.** Material contravention. If the pumping station site carries a tourism zoning objective under the Clare County Development Plan that does not permit utility use, the grant was a material contravention.

Retrieve: section 37(2)(b) of the Planning and Development Act 2000, and the current Clare County Development Plan zoning objective for the site.

Questions: was the Commission obliged to invoke section 37(2)(b) expressly and identify the limb relied on with reasons? What is the consequence where a material contravention exists and the section was not invoked? Is that jurisdictional?

**C2. TO VERIFY.** Adequacy of reasons for departing from the inspector. Confirm the standard in *Connelly v An Bord Pleanála* [2018] IESC 31 and retrieve the operative passage. Confirm whether any later authority qualifies it.

**C3. TO VERIFY.** Section 146A of the Planning and Development Act 2000. Retrieve the section and confirm the scope of the power to amend a Board order. Can an amendment alter substance, or is it confined to clerical error and correction? Relevant because the ACP 321258 file lists both an Order and an Amended Order, and both a Direction and an Amended Direction.

**C4. TO VERIFY.** Sub threshold EIA screening. Identify the correct class and threshold in Schedule 5 of the Planning and Development Regulations 2001 for wastewater treatment plants, and confirm that circa 7,926 p.e. falls below it.

Questions: what is the screening obligation under Article 4(2) and Annex III of Directive 2011/92/EU as amended, and the corresponding domestic provision? Must the screening address the sensitivity of the location, including proximity to designated sites and to landscapes of recognised value, and cumulative effects? What is the standard of review of a screening determination?

**C5. TO VERIFY.** Appropriate Assessment. Retrieve Article 6(3) of Directive 92/43/EEC and the standard in Case C-127/02 Waddenzee and Case C-258/11 Sweetman. Confirm the "no reasonable scientific doubt" formulation.

Question: where a scheme includes an emergency overflow, must the Natura Impact Statement assess the effects of overflow events on the European sites, or may they be excluded as exceptional? Retrieve any authority on the treatment of low frequency or emergency discharges in Appropriate Assessment.

### Group D. The appeal and costs. Priority 2, but time bound to September.

**D1. TO VERIFY, and this determines everything about the appeal.** Whether Humphreys J found legal error and declined relief on discretionary and proportionality grounds, or found no error at all.

The judgment is not yet on the Courts Service site. Retrieve when published, or obtain via the Central Office on record number 2025 JR 1205.

**D2. TO VERIFY.** Where a breach of an EU environmental directive is established, to what extent may an Irish court exercise discretion to refuse certiorari?

Retrieve: authority on the principle of effectiveness in this context, Article 9(4) of the Aarhus Convention as given effect in EU law, and any Irish or CJEU authority on discretionary refusal of relief in environmental judicial review.

Question: is this a point of law of exceptional public importance within section 50A(7) of the Planning and Development Act 2000, or a matter of general public importance within Article 34.5.4 of the Constitution? This is the appeal, if there is one.

**D3. TO VERIFY.** Section 50A(7) certification, the Article 34.5.4 leapfrog, and the interaction between the statutory filter and the constitutional jurisdiction. Retrieve the authority. Do not assert a settled position without it.

**D4. TO VERIFY.** Costs protection. Retrieve section 50B of the Planning and Development Act 2000 as amended, Part 2 of the Environment (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2011, and *Heather Hill Management Company CLG v An Bord Pleanála* [2022] IESC 43.

Questions: does the protection extend to all grounds in proceedings where one ground invokes an EU environmental directive? Has any subsequent statutory reform of planning judicial review costs altered the position as at August 2026? This matters practically: if the residents are substantially protected, the fear driving people away from continuing is misplaced.

### Group E. The EPA route. Priority 2.

**E1. TO VERIFY.** Retrieve the Waste Water Discharge (Authorisation) Regulations 2007, SI 684 of 2007, as amended.

Questions: in what circumstances must a licence be reviewed rather than amended, and does a new treatment plant with a changed discharge arrangement require review or a new application? What are third party submission rights, what notice is given, and what are the rights of a person who made a submission to be notified of and to challenge the decision? Can a third party request a review?

**E2. TO VERIFY, factual and highest value.** Whether the 2024 Annual Environmental Report for licence D0078-01 records construction and operation of a secondary treatment plant as a specified improvement with a completion date of 2027.

If it does, the legal question is what status that record has. Is it a licence condition, a schedule of improvements with binding effect, or a programme statement? What follows if the EPA is now asked to authorise a discharge from a plant that does not deliver it?
