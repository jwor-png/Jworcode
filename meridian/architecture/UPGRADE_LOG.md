**1. Portfolio conflict check** — Legal and Governance.

> *tests a proposed action against John's own board seats, shareholdings and live mandates, names any conflict of interest or of duty before it arises, and routes anything touching a directorship to Directors' duties.*

**Why it was earned.** John chairs AHL PLC and is Independent Non-Executive Director and Chair of the Audit and Risk Committee at United Hardware, while holding equity in Meridian, Velocity and Ambrion, and advising Barber Republic, Stargas and others. The governance file already carries a standing UHL board conflict discipline, applied by hand. This session repeatedly surfaced portfolio-level conflicts that no single manager owns: three brands claiming the same governance ground, Ambrion revenue routing through a company with a different split, United Hardware appearing as both a board seat and a prospective retained client. Directors' duties covers the client's exposure. Nothing covered John's own. It sits in Legal and Governance because it is a duty question, and it routes to Directors' duties where a directorship is engaged.

**2. Support and grant scan** — Business Transformation and Growth.

> *identifies the State supports, grants and funding offsets a client or a project can actually draw on, from Enterprise Ireland, the Local Enterprise Offices, Skillnet and InterTradeIreland, with eligibility checked to source and anything unconfirmed marked UNVERIFIED.*

**Why it was earned.** Grant intelligence has already appeared unprompted in delivered work: the Stargas one-pager carried grant aid, the rollout roadmap carried grant intelligence, and the Skillnet offset was live on the United Hardware programme. It was being done by hand every time without a skill behind it. This week's registration work also proved why the grounding rule matters here specifically: the Innovation Voucher route was assumed available, checked, and found closed to a private consultancy, and the voucher value was wrong by half. Eligibility is exactly the kind of fact that must be pulled from source rather than recalled. It sits in Business Transformation and Growth because it is value-finding work, and it inherits the floor.

### Added, new

**Prior-decision recall** — orchestrator, cross-cutting.

> *before answering, check what Meridian has already concluded on a related matter, and say so. Where the new work agrees with the earlier position, note the convergence. Where it departs, say plainly what changed and why, and treat a silent reversal as a failure.*

**Why it was earned.** The commercial documents claim a compounding corpus of decision precedent as one of Meridian's four defensibility layers, and until now that claim had nothing behind it operationally. Barber Republic already demonstrated the value: Meridian's funding analysis and Shane's independently written founder documents converged on the same instrument, and it was the *visibility* of that convergence that gave the recommendation its authority in the room. This session also produced two silent-reversal risks worth guarding against, where an earlier position was superseded and only caught because someone remembered: the Innovation Voucher route, and the Ambrion primary-revenue-vehicle line. Treating a silent reversal as a failure is the point of the skill.
