# Meridian — The Wiring
### How the engine thinks, and the floor every answer stands on

The Map is the parts list. This is the machine in motion. After nine weeks you have felt all of this working; here it is laid out, because understanding it is what lets you trust it in front of a client, and later, sell it.

---

## 1. One question does not go into one manager. It fans out.
When you brief Meridian, it does not just pick a manager. It works out **every** manager the question genuinely touches, runs them at the same time, and then pulls their work together into a single answer for you. A business you are asked to improve will usually pull Business Transformation, Finance and Commercial at once, and Legal the moment a structure or obligation appears. You never have to know which managers worked it. You get one answer.

Meridian's four jobs, in order: **Classify** the question, **Route** it to the right managers, **Consolidate** their work, **Return** it to you with a status and a short record of how it was checked.

## 2. Under every skill is one governance floor. This is the part worth money.
None of the fifty skills is trusted on its own. Each one stands on the same floor:

- **The grounding rule.** No statute, case, figure, title fact or valuation reaches you unless it was pulled from its primary source and the passage actually supports it. Anything from memory is never treated as fact. Every hard fact is tagged VERIFIED, REPORTED or UNVERIFIED, and a citation that will not resolve is held, not shipped.
- **The independent critic.** Whatever a skill produces is re-read by a separate agent, on a stronger model, that goes back to the original source. The one who makes the work and the one who checks it are never the same, so they do not share the same blind spot.
- **A status on everything.** DRAFT, IN REVIEW, or DECISION-READY. Only DECISION-READY means you can rely on it, and if a piece is not there yet, Meridian tells you exactly what is missing.
- **Fail closed.** Near a legal, financial or regulatory line, or below 80% confidence, Meridian holds and pulls you in rather than guess.
- **The human gate.** On the four high-stakes managers (Legal, Finance, Property, AI Equity), anything that will actually be acted on stops for you. Meridian cannot quietly grade its own work down to skip that.
- **The audit record.** A real log of who produced what, from which sources, what the critic found, and the status. Every answer can show its own lineage.

## 3. The boundary.
Meridian informs you. It never represents you or a client. A signature, a filing, a conveyance, anything a client leans on, goes to a qualified professional before you act. Meridian is the experienced team behind you. The judgement at the front is always yours.

## A real job, traced end to end
So you can see all of it working at once.

> **You brief Meridian:** *"A family builders' merchant in Galway, turnover about four million, second generation coming in, cash is tight. Where do I start?"*

1. **Classify.** Meridian reads this as a business-health question with a finance strain and a succession angle.
2. **Route, in parallel.** It sends the work to **Finance & Restructuring** (cash flow, the real state of the business), **Business Transformation & Growth** (where value is leaking), and **Legal & Governance** (directors' duties, because cash is tight and that lens is not optional near strain).
3. **Produce.** Each manager's skills do their work. Finance builds the cash-flow read. Business Transformation runs the process and revenue-gap look. Legal checks the directors' duties position.
4. **Check.** The independent critic re-reads the numbers against source, flags anything stale, and returns a per-claim verdict before anything moves.
5. **Consolidate.** Meridian merges it into one answer: the true cash position, the two fastest value wins, the directors' duties flag while the company is under strain, and the succession conversation that is coming whether or not anyone has raised it.
6. **Return, with a status.** DECISION-READY on the grounded pieces, with the one item that needs a qualified accountant's sign-off named plainly, and the human gate raised on anything to be acted on.

One brief. Three managers. One checked, labelled answer. That is the engine.
