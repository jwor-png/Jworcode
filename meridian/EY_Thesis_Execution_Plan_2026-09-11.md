## THE OPPORTUNITY, STATED PRECISELY

EY Ireland's TMT Leader has published, twice and in two places, that Ireland's highest-value AI opportunity is **not hosting servers** but becoming the European location with expertise in **capital allocation, risk management, regulation, energy planning, corporate finance and technology governance**, and that long-term winners will be the countries with **the skills, talent, governance, financing expertise and trusted institutions** to make AI work.

**Five of those six capabilities already sit inside Meridian's domain set.** That is the opportunity, and it is a positioning opportunity before it is a product one.

**And the hard truth first.** EY is not describing a gap in the market. **EY is describing a market it intends to serve itself**, and it has several hundred people in Ireland to serve it with. Meridian will not win on scale, brand or balance sheet. If Meridian competes where EY competes, Meridian loses. Everything below is built on where EY structurally cannot go.

---

## WHERE EY CANNOT GO, AND MERIDIAN CAN

**1. The mid-market, because the economics do not work for a Big Four firm.** A business turning over between five and fifty million euro has exactly the exposures EY describes and cannot buy a Big Four engagement to address them. That is not a slight on EY. It is arithmetic. **That segment is Meridian's entire addressable market and it is being described, free of charge, by EY.**

**2. Speed.** Meridian returns a position in days. A comparable scoped engagement elsewhere takes weeks to start.

**3. Independence from the implementation.** Meridian does not sell the build, the platform or the managed service. The advice is not a route into a larger engagement, and that can be said plainly.

**4. A named individual carrying it.** A serving PLC chair and audit and risk committee chair with forty years behind him, whose name is on the answer. A firm gives you a team and a methodology. That is a different product and for some buyers it is the better one.

## THE PRODUCT THAT DELIVERS THE THESIS

The EY column contains a ready-made brief, and it is the closest thing to a market-validated product specification Meridian has been handed.

### The AI Dependency and Resilience Review

Built directly on the five questions the column says executives are asking. Every one of them is answerable inside Meridian's existing domains today. No new capability required.

| The question, as EY frames it | The Meridian work |
|---|---|
| What if access to critical AI capability becomes constrained | Map every AI dependency in the business, who owns it, what the contract says on continuity, and what happens if it is withdrawn |
| What if regulatory frameworks diverge | AI Act position, sectoral regulation, cross-border exposure, and what a divergence costs in this specific business |
| What if geopolitical tension intensifies | Supplier jurisdiction, data location, the EU cloud and AI residency proposals, and the five named infrastructure sectors |
| What if tokens become too expensive | Unit-cost exposure, what a price movement does to margin, whether there is a switching path and what it costs |
| What if the technology sits outside our control | Concentration on single providers, lock-in, exit cost, and whether the business could operate if it stopped tomorrow |

**What the client gets.** A dependency map, a ranked exposure list, the contractual and regulatory position on each, the cost of the realistic adverse cases, and the specific actions in the order they should be taken. Plus the questions the board must be able to answer, which is the part that changes behaviour.

**Why this is the right product to lead with.** It is defensive rather than aspirational, which is a far easier sale to an owner-manager than a transformation programme. It requires no AI adoption by the client, so it is sellable to businesses that have deliberately stayed out and are now exposed through their suppliers anyway. And it is **the only product on the list that a client can be told is a direct response to what EY Ireland has publicly said executives are asking.**

**Positioning against Ambrion.** Ambrion does AI Act compliance. This is not that. This is continuity, concentration and cost exposure, and the regulatory element is one of five strands rather than the product. **The boundary must be agreed with Shane before this goes to market**, because the two will otherwise be confused in front of the same buyer.

## THE GAP, NAMED

**Energy planning is on EY's list and Meridian does not hold it.** Six capabilities are named and Meridian covers five. Three honest options.

1. **Exclude it and say so.** Cleanest. Meridian does not advise on energy planning and says so plainly.
2. **Hold it as a risk factor rather than a capability.** Energy cost and grid access as inputs to a brief, not as advice. This is defensible now and is already how Property and Development handles adjacent matters.
3. **Partner.** A named energy specialist to bring in where a brief turns on it.

**Recommendation: option 2 now, option 3 when a live matter requires it.** Do not claim it.

## ARGUE THE OTHER SIDE

**This is validation of a market, not of Meridian.** A Big Four partner saying a capability matters is an argument that many firms will now build it. It raises the value of the category and it also raises the competition in it. Meridian's advantage is early positioning and nothing more durable than that.

**The thesis is written by an interested party.** EY sells advisory services into exactly this market, and the column serves EY's commercial interest as well as being true. Both can hold at once, and Meridian should not adopt the framing more enthusiastically than the evidence supports.

**Meridian has no delivered engagement of this specific type.** The Dependency and Resilience Review is a specification, not a track record. The first one should be priced and framed accordingly, and it should be done properly rather than quickly.

**And the capacity question.** If this lands, the constraint is John. The engine scales and the final gate does not. That is a known limit of the model and it argues for a small number of well-chosen engagements rather than a campaign.
