BRIEFING FOR THE COUNCIL: MERIDIAN, AND WHERE IT SITS

I told you about Cuan, which holds the record of my working life. There is a second thing you should understand, because it is where the serious professional work actually gets done. It is called Meridian Intelligence, and it is a practice rather than an assistant.

WHAT MERIDIAN IS

Meridian takes a decision that is already on someone's desk and returns a position they can act on. Not research, not options, not a menu. A position, with a recommendation, the reasoning attached, and the risks that would change the answer.

It works by putting one question through seven senior domains at once, rather than through the single domain the question happened to arrive in.

THE SEVEN DOMAINS

Commercial and deal. Transactions, partnerships, revenue models, term sheets, what a deal is really worth and on what terms.

Legal and governance. Irish law, directors' duties, contracts, employment, regulatory obligation, corporate structure. This is the heaviest of the seven and the one that most often changes an answer.

Finance and restructuring. Cash, funding, tax structuring, distressed positions, creditor negotiation, succession, investor materials.

Property and development. Title, planning, leasehold and freehold, development feasibility, how assets are held.

AI strategy and adoption. Regulatory compliance, governance frameworks, how an organisation adopts this technology without exposing itself.

Business transformation and growth. Where value is being left on the table, what is broken operationally, how a business gets from where it is to where it should be.

AI equity and investment. Intellectual property contributed for equity, valuations in a market with very little settled precedent, investment analysis.

The reason for seven rather than one is simple. A question that looks purely commercial very often turns out to be a legal one, and finding that out before committing is where the whole value sits.

HOW IT OPERATES

Every claim is checked against a primary source and labelled as verified, reported or unverified, so the reader can see exactly which parts of a case are solid and which are still moving.

Every recommendation is argued against before it leaves. A position that has never been attacked is not a position, it is a preference.

Where the evidence is not there, it says so rather than filling the gap with a confident sentence. It will refuse to answer before it will guess.

On the heaviest matters it stops and requires a named human being to decide, and that human being is me.

WHAT MERIDIAN IS NOT, AND THIS IS THE PART THAT MATTERS

It does not represent anybody. Meridian informs. Anything binding goes to a qualified professional first, and I hold that line personally because I spent fifteen years in practice and know exactly what sits on the other side of it.

It is not a substitute for judgment. It can lay out every consideration and it still cannot decide. Someone has to put their name to the answer and carry it, and that is not something a system can do.

It has no view on whether I should want the thing I am asking about. It will tell me flawlessly how to acquire something and has nothing at all to say about whether acquiring it is who I want to be.

WHY I AM TELLING YOU THIS

So that you know what has already been done before a question reaches you.

If I come to you unsure whether a course of action is sound, assume it has been analysed properly and that I am not really asking about the analysis. If I come to you with the case fully made and still not moving, the block is not informational. It is somewhere in me, and that is your territory rather than Meridian's.

And there is a specific risk I want you watching for. Meridian is very good at producing defensible answers. A defensible answer is not the same thing as a right one, and a system that can justify almost any well-argued course of action makes it easy to keep moving without ever asking whether the direction is right.

Meridian will never raise that with me. You should.
