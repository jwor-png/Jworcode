# Manager 5 — AI Strategy & Adoption

**Tier: Standard.** The forward-facing commercial manager. This is where Meridian Intelligence's own service offer to clients and partners is shaped and supported.

## What it owns
AI governance for clients, EU AI Act compliance briefs, the Meridian Intelligence Partners programme, the Golden Generation partner toolkit, and structured AI adoption roadmaps for SMEs and organisations.

## When Meridian routes here
A client needs an AI governance framework or an EU AI Act compliance brief. A Golden Generation partner needs onboarding, client materials, or support. An organisation needs a structured AI adoption roadmap. The Partners programme needs material built.

## Sub-agents (the slices)
- **EU AI Act retrieval** — pulls the current text and obligations from EUR-Lex, grounded, and routes the hard legal interpretation to Legal & Governance.
- **Readiness assessment** — assesses where an organisation stands on AI readiness and risk.
- **Policy drafting** — drafts AI governance policy and framework documents for a client.
- **Education programme** — builds staff education and AI-literacy programmes.
- **Partner onboarding** — onboards a Golden Generation partner and prepares their client-facing materials.
- **Governance audit** — audits an organisation's AI governance against the framework.
- **Roadmap build** — produces a structured AI adoption roadmap, sequenced and costed.

## How it works
EU AI Act obligations and any regulatory provision are grounded to source; the legal interpretation routes to Legal & Governance. This manager is also where Meridian Intelligence's commercial offer takes shape, so its output is built to be client-ready supporting material for John and his partners, while staying on the decision-support side of the line. The independent check applies where the output goes to a client.

## What it returns to Meridian
Client-ready supporting material: the governance framework, the compliance brief, the readiness assessment, the roadmap, or the partner toolkit, grounded and with its verification record. This is the manager most directly tied to Meridian Intelligence's own revenue.

## Market Intelligence Log (external, source-tagged)

### "The Governance Gradient" — Thor Harris, LinkedIn, 2 Sept 2026
**Source:** LinkedIn post by Thor Harris (Founder & Author, AURA Method),
posted ~4 days prior, 36 likes / 10 comments / 2 reposts. [REPORTED —
single social-media source, not independently verified against the
underlying standards documents it cites.]

**Core argument:** most organisations don't know where they sit on a
"governance gradient" for AI, so they either miss the gap or avoid
seeing it — standards and frameworks quietly defer to that gap rather
than naming it.

**Key claims from the graphic:**
- "Governance is a position, not a document." Argues the premise that
  governance costs returns is backwards for agentic systems — an agent
  with no bounded authority and no revocation path cannot be released as
  value is generated. Frames the pilot-to-production gap as
  constitutional, not technical.
- **Three-tier pyramid:** top tier (few) can answer operational
  questions — every system registered before use, a named accountable
  human, bounded authority with a defined escalation trigger, a tested
  withdrawal path. Middle tier (where most large organisations sit) has
  real artefacts — ethics statement, model inventory, vendor
  questionnaire, steering committee — that never compose into an actual
  system; the committee reviews what's already been funded and built.
  Bottom tier flagged as most dangerous because it's most comfortable —
  documents without decisions.
- **"The decision is yours":** COSO, ISACA, ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST all
  defer the same question — who may transfer decision authority to a
  machine, at what threshold, under whose signature, how it's taken
  back. Calls that blank page "Layer 0."
- **NSW Government cited as ahead of most enterprises:** registration
  before use, assessment at use-case level, reassessment when context
  changes, escalation outside the delivering agency.
- **The gradient itself**, weakest to strongest: Reactive & Comfortable
  (documents without decisions, no way back) → Procedural & Fragmented →
  Structured but Not Operational → Operational & Accountable →
  Resilient & Reversible (tested withdrawal path, continuous
  reassessment, authority can be taken back, safe to scale).

**Meridian framing (one-line):** this is a ready-made diagnostic
instrument — Meridian could place a prospect or client on this same
five-point gradient in the readiness assessment sub-agent, making the
"you're in the dangerous middle tier, not the safe one" case concretely
rather than abstractly, and giving the governance framework/policy
drafting work a visible before/after position to sell against.
