## PART TWO — THE EU AI ACT CLAIM, AND WHY THE OBVIOUS WORDING IS THE WRONG ONE

You want the site to stress that Meridian is EU AI Act compliant. **The instinct is right and the phrase is wrong**, for three reasons.

**It is unfalsifiable, so it carries no weight.** "EU AI Act compliant" with nothing after it is a claim no reader can test. On a site whose whole argument is that untestable claims get tagged, it is the weakest sentence you could write, and it is the one a competitor would screenshot.

**There is no certification to point at.** The Act has no general compliance mark that an advisory firm obtains. Saying compliant invites the question "certified by whom", and the honest answer is nobody.

**It understates what is actually true.** Meridian's real position is better than the generic claim, because it can be stated specifically and checked.

### What is actually true, and is worth saying

Meridian uses AI systems in its own work. In AI Act terms that makes it a **deployer**, not a provider of high-risk systems. The obligation that bites on a deployer is **Article 4, AI literacy**, which requires that those operating AI systems have sufficient understanding to do so responsibly. It entered into force on 2 February 2025 and became enforceable on 3 August 2026.

**Meridian can say, truthfully and specifically, that it meets that obligation and how.** That is a far stronger statement than "compliant".

### Ready to paste

> ### **Our own AI Act position**
>
> We use AI in our work, so the EU AI Act applies to us as it applies to our clients. We think a practice that advises on governance should be able to state its own position plainly.
>
> **We are a deployer, not a provider.** Meridian uses AI systems to produce analysis. It does not build, train or place AI systems on the market, and it does not deploy AI in any of the high-risk uses the Act lists.
>
> **We meet the Article 4 AI literacy obligation.** Article 4 has applied since 2 February 2025 and has been enforceable since 3 August 2026. It requires that people operating AI systems understand them well enough to use them responsibly. Every engagement here is directed by a named principal who operates the system daily, who documents how each output was produced, and who is accountable for it.
>
> **We say where AI was used.** Every brief records how it was produced, what was checked, and against what source.
>
> **What we do not claim.** There is no general certification of AI Act compliance for a practice like this, and anyone offering you one is selling something. What we offer instead is a stated position you can read, question and hold us to.

**Why this is better.** It names the article and the dates, which are checkable. It states a limit, which is the pattern used everywhere else on the site. And the last paragraph turns the absence of a certificate from a weakness into the most credible thing on the page.

**Verification note.** The Article 4 dates are held in Meridian's knowledge base as VERIFIED. **Have Ambrion or a solicitor confirm them once before this goes live**, because it is a statement about the law on a public website and it should not rest on our record alone.
