## Check
- **Donella Meadows is real and the reference is apt.** She wrote *Thinking in Systems* (published 2008, after her death) and the essay "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System" (December 1999 per the Systems Practice page; one wiki says 1997), which ranks twelve leverage points from parameters (least effective) to paradigms (most effective). Sources: [Donella Meadows Project](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/), [Systems Practice](https://www.systemspractice.org/resources/leverage-points-places-intervene-system). Her death in 2001 is from memory and was not confirmed in search.
- **The "5 principles" are not Meadows's framework.** Her book covers stocks, flows, feedback loops, delays and leverage points. The five-principle list is the poster's own repackaging. Putting her name and portrait on slide 1 implies she wrote it. No source is given for the five.
- **The portrait is not a genuine photograph.** The post is platform-labelled AI content, so a real person's likeness has been generated and presented as hers.
- **Slide 6 is loosely consistent with Meadows:** rules, incentives and information flows rank high among her leverage points. The "root cause" slide uses an iceberg graphic, which is a different systems-thinking tool, not hers (from memory, UNVERIFIED).
- **Slide 7 is the purpose of the carousel:** a lead-generation call to action for a paid product. "Science-based" and "proven strategies" are claims with no source.
- **Slide 8 is not captured.**
