## A. THE INSTALLATION DECISION, AS REPORTED

**Sizing starts with the Eircode and the annual usage.** Roof orientation is examined with planning software and combined with usage to produce a plan. Dormer roofs, Velux windows and chimneys reduce the number of panels that fit. Ground mounted panels are more expensive but can face directly south, which maximises generation.

**Panel type.** Treacy Solar installs PV only, converting sunlight to direct current, with an inverter converting to alternating current for lighting, appliances, heating, hot water and vehicle charging. Solar thermal heats water only. Hybrid combines both. PV is described as the most versatile.

**Eight panels is the number usually cited for a three bedroom house and a family of four, and the reported view is that the reality is more nuanced.** The investment horizon is 25 to 30 years, so the plan should anticipate a heat pump and an electric car even where neither exists yet, since new houses are increasingly built without chimneys and are heavily electricity dependent.

**The single most quotable operational point:** *"If you've got space to put up 24 panels on the house and you think that 15 will cover your usage now, put up the 24. The cheapest time to put them up is the day that the installers are there because you've got a full roof and crew on site."* Retrofitting later is expensive.

**Batteries** allow long summer generation to be banked as credits or as self generated electricity rather than leaving the household grid dependent. An Eddi diverter can send excess to an immersion heater, though the reported view is that a pre set immersion is more economic for domestic use.

**Changeover switches.** ESB Networks can provide standby generation with solar and battery systems once designed, installed and tested by a registered electrical contractor. The switch must disconnect the home from the ESB Networks supply to isolate it safely during a power cut.

**Farm use, and this is the structural point.** Where a farm is a limited company, it may be eligible for a **non domestic grant for solar on farm infrastructure**, described as avoiding the hoops of the TAMS grant. Batteries are said to be key on farms because milking times do not align with generation times.

**Costs, as reported.** An **SEAI grant of €1,800** is available. Energia is cited for a ten panel installation on an average three bedroom home at **€6,000 to €9,000 after the grant**, excluding add ons, with more advanced systems **upward of €18,000**. Several quotes recommended.

**And the point most often missed.** Billgenie's founder says the biggest mistake is installing panels and expecting them to automatically reduce spend. Getting the benefit requires reviewing energy use and tariff, selecting a tariff that suits the usage pattern, and then adjusting usage so appliances run when electricity is cheapest or generation is highest. With batteries, daytime solar is stored and used alongside a night time tariff. **This is called load shifting.**

## B. THE TAX TREATMENT, AS REPORTED BY IFAC

**The income is technically taxable in Ireland**, with a specific exemption.

- **The first €400 of profit from selling electricity to the grid is automatically exempt** from Income Tax, USC and PRSI.
- **The capital cost of the panels cannot be deducted**, so the profit is effectively the total **Clean Export Guarantee (CEG)** credits appearing on utility bills over the calendar year.
- **The exemption is per person, tied to the individuals named on the electricity bill.** Where both spouses are named, each may claim €400, giving **€800 per household**. Some suppliers limit the number of names, and there are issues with settling income on minor children.
- **It applies only to a qualifying residence**, meaning the sole or main family home. **Solar arrays on commercial farm sheds or out farms on a business meter cannot use this relief.**
- **There is no cliff edge.** At €500 of credits the first €400 remains exempt and only the €100 excess is taxed at the marginal rate with USC and PRSI.
- **Form 11.** A standard PAYE worker has no requirement to declare microgeneration income at or below €400. Someone operating a farm business is already registered for self assessment and files a Form 11 by 31 October; if under the limit the credits need not be recorded in the farm books or the return, because the house sits on its own separate domestic meter and the income is outside the business. If the limit is exceeded, the excess must be declared under **Case IV, miscellaneous income**.
- **The €400 exemption has been extended in the tax code to run until at least 31 December 2028.**
- **Typical annual export credits for a standard domestic setup are €150 to €350**, so most households stay inside the exemption.
- Keep residential electricity statements so the exact export total can be verified each year.

### 1. This is the routing argument, illustrated

A homeowner asks an energy question. The answer is decided by tax and by metering.

**Whether the array sits on the house or on the shed changes the tax treatment entirely.** Whether the farm trades as a limited company changes which grant is available. Whether both spouses are named on the electricity bill doubles the exempt amount. None of those are energy questions, and none of them would be raised by an installer, who is answering the question that arrived.
