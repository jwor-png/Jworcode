## What it says (REPORTED)
Ambitious, talented people stay financially behind because talent and making money are different skill sets. Patterns named: starting new things and never staying long enough to make one work; confusing ideas with progress; being "addicted to potential, not execution"; reinventing yourself instead of building on what exists; being good at the work and poor at selling it (overdelivering, undercharging, guilt about asking for more); wanting to be discovered instead of learning distribution; being busy without building assets ("building a job for yourself, even when you think you're building a business"; no systems, leverage, recurring revenue or compounding); and being scattered across "10 business ideas, 15 interests, 20 things". The author says she is working on selling, negotiating and pricing, distribution, building fewer things and committing longer, turning knowledge into products and IP, systems that do not need her constant involvement, cash flow, profit and reinvestment, and a reputation that compounds. Closing: "potential doesn't pay bills."

## Check

- The pattern list is familiar, plausible and unevidenced. Several points are sound business advice (price properly, build for repeatability), but the thread offers no evidence that these patterns explain who is "broke".
