## 2. THE SIX RISKS THE STUDY NAMES, AND HOW MERIDIAN ANSWERS THEM

The article is REPORTED as identifying increasing board risk arising from: time pressures, disconnects with executive management teams, a focus on past events, cultures that stifle debate and challenge, increasingly complex organisational structures, and underutilisation of AI.

**Meridian answers five of the six directly, and this is the sharpest mapping the practice has been handed.**

| Risk named | What Meridian already does about it |
|---|---|
| **Time pressures** | A consolidated position with the reasoning attached, in days rather than weeks, so board time is spent deciding rather than reading. |
| **Disconnect with executive management** | An independent read that is neither management's nor the board's, produced for the decision rather than for the relationship. |
| **Focus on past events** | Every brief is forward-facing by construction: the risks that would change the answer, and what the board must be able to answer before it commits. |
| **Cultures that stifle debate and challenge** | **Argue-the-other-side is run on every recommendation before it leaves.** The challenge arrives in the papers rather than depending on somebody being brave in the room. |
| **Complex organisational structures** | Partly. Seven domains run in parallel rather than one function answering within its own silo. |
| **Underutilisation of AI** | Meridian is a governed AI system operated by a named principal, with the grounding rule, confidence tagging and a human gate published. |

**The fourth row is the one to lead with.** Boards do not fail for want of information. They fail because nobody in the room is willing to be the person who says the thing. **A written, sourced counter-argument arriving with the papers removes the need for courage**, and that is a governance improvement, not a report.

## 3. THE PRODUCT THIS POINTS AT

Meridian should **not** build a board effectiveness review. That market exists, it is served by Deloitte, the Chartered Governance Institute, Lawdebenture and others, it is process-focused, and it is a poor fit for a practice whose value is judgement on substance.

**The product is narrower and nobody is selling it.**

### The Board Paper Review

**What it is.** The papers going to the next board meeting are read before the meeting, and what comes back is what a hostile, well-briefed reader would ask.

**What the client receives.** The questions the papers do not answer. The claims that are asserted rather than evidenced, labelled. The risks the paper does not mention. The one or two places where a different domain changes the conclusion. And a short list of the questions a director should ask on the day.

**Why it is the right product.**

- **It is bought by the chair or the senior independent director, not by management**, which is exactly the buyer Meridian can reach and exactly the person who feels the risk.
- **It recurs by construction.** Boards meet on a cycle. This is the first genuinely recurring Meridian product that is not a retainer.
- **It is small enough to say yes to once** and large enough to matter.
- **It directly attacks four of the six risks EY names**, and that argument can be made with an EY citation rather than an assertion.
- **John's own standing sells it without a case study.** A serving PLC chair and audit and risk committee chair reading board papers for a hostile question is a proposition that needs no explaining.

**The boundary, stated in the product.** Meridian informs. It does not attend the board, does not advise the board as a body, and nothing it produces is legal or financial advice. That keeps it clear of shadow directorship territory and clear of the audit and assurance perimeter.
