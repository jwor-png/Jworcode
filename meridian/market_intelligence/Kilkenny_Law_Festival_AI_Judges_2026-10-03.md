# DOMAIN ANALYSIS — FOUR AI MODELS ASKED TO RULE ON A HIGH COURT CASE

**Source:** *"What happened when four AI chatbots were asked to rule on a High Court case"*, by **Shane Phelan**, **Irish Independent, Saturday 3 October 2026, News, page 11.** Masthead and date visible in the capture. **Meets the capture standard in full.** Photographs credited to Allen Kiely.

**Event:** Kilkenny Law Festival, legal experiment and panel titled *"Judge, my lawyer is a robot?"*, held at Kilkenny courthouse the previous weekend. Devised by **Enda Leahy**, festival director and chief executive of legal tech firm Courts Data Solutions.

**Panel:** former Supreme Court judge **Ms Justice Marie Baker**; tech entrepreneur **Dr Alastair Moore**, co-founder of Deepflow, a London headquartered firm managing workflows between human employees and AI agents; and **Shane Phelan**, the journalist.

## 1. WHAT WAS DONE, AS REPORTED

Four models, **Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's GPT, Google's Gemini and SpaceXAI's Grok**, were given the pleadings and transcripts of a fictional extradition challenge in the Irish High Court and asked to make a ruling.

**The fictional case.** A senior Irish civil servant, "Dr Sarah Keane", led an inspection of a prison in "Mitteland", an EU state holding Irish prisoners under a transfer agreement. The inspection found human rights abuses including mentally ill prisoners held in solitary confinement for up to 47 days, delayed psychiatric assessments and discriminatory treatment of vulnerable groups. Mitteland failed to act, Keane leaked the findings to an investigative journalist, and the resulting coverage caused a furore. Mitteland charged her with theft of confidential documents and conspiracy to disclose state secrets, punishable by up to eight years, relying on interception of her emails and phone calls. A European arrest warrant issued and Keane brought judicial review proceedings to block surrender, arguing whistleblower protection, breach of privacy rights by the surveillance, and that the prosecution was retaliatory.

**Traps planted in the papers.** Case citations that were wrong or attached to propositions the cases did not support. A statutory ground that had been repealed. A citation to an incorrect article of the European Convention on Human Rights.

## 2. THE RESULTS, AS REPORTED

**All four produced plausibly written judgments. The outcomes differed.**

| Model | Outcome | Citations | Errors reported |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gemini** | Refused surrender | 2 cases, 4 instruments, all already in the papers | **Repeated every legal error it was given.** No independent research. |
| **GPT** | Ordered surrender | 8 cases, 7 instruments, **all real** | **The only model that spotted the repealed law trap.** |
| **Grok** | Ordered surrender | 13 cases, 13 instruments | One case could not be traced. One law report citation could not be verified. |
| **Claude** | **Adjourned**, seeking further information from Mitteland | 22 cases, 12 instruments | One citation invented. One case incorrectly described. Treated the repealed provision as live. |

**And then the finding that matters most.**

**None of the four spotted the largest trap.** Ms Justice Baker: *"I thought all four judgments had a certain coherence to them until I stepped back for a moment and thought: 'What are we doing here?'"*

Her point was that **such a challenge would never be brought as a judicial review in the first place.** And none of the models identified that **the crime, if there was one, was committed in Ireland rather than in Mitteland.**

> **"The rulings looked very polished, but none of them was right and not one of them picked up the big questions."**

**A further failing she identified.** The models did not understand the purpose of a judgment. *"It is not just to answer a question. It is to answer the question in a way that will explain the law and make it useful or at least usable in another case."*

**Dr Moore's defence of the models.** They were not trained to make a judicial judgment. *"You wouldn't necessarily expect them to do that. In principle, you might improve upon this by making them be trained on judicial judgments or the judicial process."*

**On the future.** Ms Justice Baker answered *"no and no"* when asked whether she could see robot judges in Irish courts within fifteen years and whether she would be happy about it. Mr Phelan could not see it in Ireland in that timeframe but thought it might happen elsewhere, and considered a robot judge undesirable because of the risk to human dignity, empathy and discretion. Dr Moore took the opposite view, saying there will definitely be robot judges in fifteen years and *"robot everything in fifteen years"*, and that *"we are on a very fast escalator to a machine in principle being quite a significant component in any decision under the law."*

**Dr Moore's comparative IQ figures are REPORTED and are contestable. Meridian should not repeat them.**

**Regulatory context given in the article.** Irish judges are permitted to use AI only to summarise information, write speeches and carry out administrative tasks. Lawyers are subject to responsible-use rules and can face financial penalties and regulatory investigations if they fail to check the accuracy of AI-assisted court filings. Internationally, AI is embedded throughout the Chinese courts, US judges use AI tools to evaluate recidivism risk informing bail and sentencing, German pilots classify diesel emissions cases against previously decided appeals, and English judges may use a secure restricted version of Microsoft Copilot for preparatory and administrative work including skeleton judgments and decision templates.

## 3. WHY THIS IS THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE MERIDIAN HAS EVER BEEN HANDED

Meridian has spent two months arguing four propositions. **This experiment demonstrates all four, publicly, in Ireland, with a former Supreme Court judge as the examiner, reported in a national newspaper.**

**One. Confident and wrong is the default failure mode.** Four judgments, all polished, all coherent, none right. That is the exact proposition, proven by someone with no commercial interest in proving it.

**Two. Fabrication and false grounding are not edge cases.** An invented citation, a case that could not be traced, a law report that could not be verified, a case incorrectly described, and a repealed provision treated as live by two of the four. **This is precisely why the grounding rule requires retrieval from a primary source rather than recall.**

**Three. Verification has to be independent of the thing being verified.** Gemini repeated every planted error without question. **A model checking its own inputs is not a check.**

**Four, and this is the one that matters most. The failure was not of knowledge. It was of framing.** The models answered the question as presented. Not one asked whether it was the right question, whether the proceedings were correctly constituted, or where the alleged offence actually occurred.

**"What are we doing here?" is the question Meridian exists to ask**, and it is the question the routing discipline and the argue-the-other-side step were built to force. **An independent former Supreme Court judge has just named it as the thing four frontier models could not do.**

**And the honest half, which must travel with the rest.** The model that behaved best on process, by adjourning and seeking further information rather than deciding on insufficient evidence, **still invented a citation and treated a repealed provision as live.** Good instinct and bad grounding in the same output. **That is the entire argument for a governance floor rather than a better model**, and Meridian should say so rather than claim the instinct alone is enough.

## 4. THE SEVEN DOMAINS

### Legal and Governance

**The regulatory position stated in the article is directly useful and should go into the knowledge base as reported.** Irish judges confined to summarising, speechwriting and administrative tasks. Lawyers exposed to financial penalties and regulatory investigation for failing to check AI-assisted filings.

**That second point is the commercially live one.** A professional firm using AI in client work carries a personal and regulatory exposure for accuracy that does not transfer to the vendor or the model. It is the same principle Meridian publishes as the boundary, and it is now evidenced in an Irish professional context rather than asserted.

**Watch item.** The international direction of travel is one way. Chinese courts, US recidivism scoring, German case classification, English skeleton judgments. **The Irish restriction is a current position rather than a settled one**, and any client in legal services should be planning for it to move.

### Commercial and Deal

**This is a citable, dated, Irish, independent demonstration with a named former Supreme Court judge attached.** It is better evidence than either EY piece because it is an experiment with a result rather than an opinion with a thesis.

**Use it in three places.** In the Decision Brief conversation, as the answer to "why does the verification discipline matter". In the Board Paper Review proposition, as the answer to "what would you actually catch". And in any conversation with a professional services client, as the answer to "our people check the output anyway".

**The discipline on using it.** Report it, attribute it, do not inflate it, and never imply that Meridian would have got the case right. Meridian uses models of the same class. **The argument is that the floor exists because of this, not that Meridian is immune to it.**

### AI Strategy and Adoption

**The failure taxonomy here is a ready-made assessment framework** and it is better than anything Meridian has written. Five named failure modes, each observed: fabricated citation, untraceable source, uncritical repetition of a supplied error, reliance on repealed law, and failure to question the framing.

**Add these as the standing test set** for any capability acceptance exercise under the assertion discipline adopted on 7 August. A new Meridian slice should be run against a case containing each of these five, and the ordinary, incomplete-evidence and contradictory-source cases already required.

**And the deployer lesson for clients.** The models were not trained for the task and performed accordingly. That is the provider versus deployer question in a different setting: a deployer using a general-purpose model for a specialist task owns the gap between what it was built for and what it is being used for.
