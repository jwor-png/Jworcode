## THE URGENT ITEM

**ACORNS 12 applications close at midnight on Friday 18 September 2026.** Registration of interest is via `acorns.ie`, after which a short application form is sent by email.

- **Programme runs October 2026 to April 2027.**
- **Up to 50 places, and there is no charge** to those selected.
- **Eligibility:** early-stage female entrepreneurs living in rural Ireland who have generated sales no earlier than the end of June 2023.
- **Funded** through the Rural Innovation and Development Fund by the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine, launched this summer by Minister for Agriculture, Food and the Marine Martin Haydon. Delivered with the voluntary contribution of time by Lead Entrepreneurs.

## THE CASE STUDY, BRIEFLY

Adrienne Diamond runs **The Glass Farm** at Dromahair, Co Leitrim, an eleven acre organic farm with sheep and donkeys, a glass making studio and workshop, and a Social Farm initiative. She took part in ACORNS to grow the glass making business.

Points worth keeping:

- **The craft facilities were built using eco fibre board and sheep wool insulation**, with support from the ACORNS LEADER programme.
- She bought equipment when the Manorhamilton Sculpture Centre closed, having held the idea for around ten years.
- The studio runs on **electricity rather than gas**, which is unusual in glass making and is the sustainability angle. She is now searching Ireland and the UK for a **smaller scale electrical reheating chamber**, and it appears it may have to be designed and developed from scratch because available units are produced only for commercially scaled factories.
- **Ecoglass classes use glass recycled from discarded double glazing panes.**
- She rents the facility, designed so equipment can be moved to accommodate other classes, workshops and exhibitions.
- On the peer group: *"There are seven others in the group and we're sharing information all the time. I could go to them with anything. It's a support group and I think that's going to grow and grow."*
- On social farming: *"We sit around telling stories, and we learn from them as well, and it breaks the farmer's isolation too."*

## THE COMMERCIAL READ FOR MERIDIAN

### 1. Farm diversification is a named category, and it is Meridian work

The headline treats diversification as a business discipline rather than a hobby, and the substance of this case is a sequence of ordinary commercial decisions: what to buy when a competitor closes, whether to rent capacity or hold it, how to price classes, and whether to commission equipment that does not yet exist.

**Every one of those is a Decision Brief.** The domains it touches are Business Transformation and Growth, Commercial and Deal, Finance and Restructuring, and Property and Development where the farm and the buildings are concerned.

**This strengthens the agri and succession positioning already established**, and it does it in a sector where John has direct practice experience.

### 2. The peer group is the real product, and it is a lesson

What the participant praises is not training. It is **seven other people she can bring anything to**. That is the same insight behind the retained tier of Meridian's own ladder, and it is worth noticing that a State programme has arrived at it independently.

### 3. Support and grant scan fires

ACORNS is free at the point of use, State funded, and runs for seven months. **It belongs on the standing list of Irish supports that Meridian checks before recommending any paid engagement to an early-stage rural business.** Recommending a fee where a free State programme fits better is the fastest way to lose credibility with this buyer, and the fastest way to earn it is to say so.

**Note for the list:** ACORNS is capacity building for early-stage founders. It is not a substitute for a decision brief on a specific transaction, and the two are complementary rather than competing.

### 4. And the honest limit

**John is not eligible as a Lead Entrepreneur.** The programme's Lead Entrepreneurs are established female entrepreneurs volunteering their time to an all female cohort. That route is closed, and it should not be pursued.

**What is open is the ecosystem around it.** LEADER companies, the Rural Innovation and Development Fund, the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine, and the local development companies that deliver rural enterprise supports are all relevant to Meridian's grant and support scanning, and to any farm diversification or succession engagement.
