## 1. Article (capture standard met)

**Business Post, Property Plus, 11 October 2026, page 11.** Conor Lennon (director, Lisney Commercial Real Estate), "The €80 question: What are office tenants really paying for?" The image is rotated and partly cropped; figures below were read from it and are REPORTED.

Claims in the article (REPORTED, author's):
- Prime Dublin office rents are in the mid to high €60s per sq ft for CAT A delivered space, with evidence of €70.
- Fit-out cost was about €100 per sq ft in 2019, now often more than €150 and can reach above €200.
- Example: 40 to 60 employees in 6,000 sq ft can mean over €1.2m of capital before staff move in.
- Landlords increasingly deliver fully fitted space; some schemes reportedly reach €80+ per sq ft. Part of that rent is an embedded repayment of fit-out cost.
- Consequence: headline fitted rents should not be read as like-for-like with CAT A rents. Rent reviews are assessed by reference to the premises specification at the start of the lease. Tenants who signed fitted leases in the post-Covid years may face higher review uplifts if landlords cite recent fitted-space evidence.

**Source weight:** the author is a landlord-side agency director. The article is opinion from market practice, not data. Treat the figures as UNVERIFIED market opinion until checked against a published index or a second agent.

**Meridian read:** a single "€80 per sq ft" number hides three questions: what is in the rent (space, fit-out repayment, service), what the rent review will compare it to, and what the tenant is left with at lease end. That crosses Property, Legal (review clause, specification, reinstatement), and Finance (capital outlay avoided versus total cost over the term). It is a clean example of a question that arrives in one domain and is decided in another. A usable LinkedIn angle for a later post, written without reference to any client or John's own board roles.
