## What it says (REPORTED)
"How people got rich in 2017: Bitcoin, dropshipping, Amazon FBA. How people will get rich in 2027:" an AI agency. The model: run a local business's ads, qualify every lead, book the good ones onto the owner's calendar, get paid for every job the owner closes, and use AI for fulfilment. Claimed to be "one of the best business models to make 6-7 figures/year with minimal time investment."
Steps shown: 1 ask Claude for 10 niches where one job is worth at least $10k, the sales cycle is short and few agencies advertise; 2 pick a market that can afford you (a $20-a-client massage parlour needs 125 new customers to justify a $2,500 monthly fee; a home-improvement firm closing one $50k to $100k kitchen covers the fee for 12 to 24 months); 3 take a small setup fee covering the first three jobs, refunded on any not closed in 30 days; 4 with $1,000 or more saved run paid ads, otherwise cold SMS and cold email; 5 call every lead within five minutes, "statistics show that between minute 5 and 10, contact rate falls 900%", and double-dial.

## Check
- "Falls 900%" is **mathematically impossible**: a rate cannot fall by more than 100%. It is probably a garbled version of a widely quoted lead-response study (UNVERIFIED, from memory: the odds of contacting a lead drop sharply within the first 30 minutes). No source is given.
- Step 2 compares a kitchen's revenue to the agency fee; revenue is not profit, so the "covers your fee for 12 to 24 months" claim overstates what the owner gains.
- "6-7 figures/year with minimal time" is an uncorroborated income claim with survivorship bias, and the 2017 comparison is a sales device.
- Cold SMS and cold email to individuals are regulated in Ireland and the EU (ePrivacy and GDPR consent rules, UNVERIFIED for specifics). The thread is US-framed and ignores this.
- A refund guarantee and a pay-per-result fee raise consumer-protection and advertising-claims questions for anyone copying the model.
