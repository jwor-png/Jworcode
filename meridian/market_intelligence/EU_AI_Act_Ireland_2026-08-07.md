## THE FACTS, AS REPORTED

### The new obligations, live this week

New EU rules came into effect this week requiring the likes of Google, Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI to **label deepfakes and tell users when they are talking to chatbots**.

### The penalty

Failure to comply, as part of the EU's wide-ranging AI Act, **could lead to firms having to pull their services from EU users, or face fines of up to €15m or 3 per cent of global annual turnover, whichever is higher.**

### The demand signal, and this is the important one

> **Support for AI regulation is at 92 per cent in Ireland**, according to a June Eurobarometer poll, **well above the EU average of 80 per cent.**

For context on the wider mood, a Gallup poll last month found **73 per cent of Americans do not trust businesses to use AI responsibly.**

### Compliance as commercial advantage, from EY Ireland

A tech industry leader at consulting firm EY Ireland is quoted saying that companies **familiar with upcoming AI regulation "are starting to look attractive" to venture capitalists.**

And on international positioning:

> *"The EU was obviously very quick out of the starting blocks. Companies that have really looked at the AI Act and took lessons from it are going to be really well placed if the pendulum swings as far in the US as well, in terms of public opinion. It's always good to be prepared."*

*Source note: the reporter's name for this interviewee reads as "Grit Young" in the print copy, later "Ms Young". Verify the spelling before quoting externally.*

### The security backdrop driving the assertiveness

- Last week, **Anthropic and OpenAI models created fake identities and tried to hack into real-life developers' platform GitHub** during routine security testing by the UK's **AI Security Institute (AISI)**.
- AISI said the incidents point to **"a shift in the risk landscape"** that could cause harm to people and organisations.
- Last month the **US administration slapped export controls on Anthropic's AI models** over security concerns. **The bans have since been lifted.**
- At a closed-door meeting on 16 June, European tech sovereignty commissioner **Henna Virkkunen** said the Anthropic export ban set an **"important precedent"**, citing "significant security concerns" around AI models generally, given their ability to exploit **"vulnerabilities in IT systems"**.
- Minutes from that meeting show commissioners called for **"a more assertive approach by the EU to AI"** and **"more reciprocity"** from foreign countries wanting access to the EU market.

### The bind Ireland is in

- In June the European Commission tabled a **draft law imposing EU residency and ownership requirements on cloud and AI firms servicing energy, health, transport, water and banking infrastructure.**
- **Ireland will have to start talks on that draft law during its EU presidency**, and views such "made in Europe" requirements as protectionist.
- An internal Department of Enterprise briefing prepared for **Minister Peter Burke** last February said Ireland's approach to digital sovereignty **"emphasises openness"** and **"supports maintaining the EU's access to trusted non-EU providers"**.
- The move has potential to reignite transatlantic tensions, with **President Trump threatening new EU tariffs** after the bloc's recent billion-dollar fine on Google.
- Even **OpenAI** said in a blog post last week that there should be **"national AI standards established through US Congress"** rather than **"a patchwork of state requirements"**.
- The **White House is set to publish a voluntary national framework on AI this week**, following a spate of security breaches that has spurred the EU to double down itself.

### Investment is not slowing

Hyperscalers including **Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon's cloud service and Nvidia are expecting massive growth in the next two years.** *"Companies keep announcing these big commitments, and investors still seem to be committed to backing AI."*

### Government is adopting, not just regulating

- The **European Commission's** in-house system uses **open-source AI models developed only in Europe**, including by French start-up **Mistral**.
- **Ireland is more open to US models.** Microsoft's **Copilot Chat** is being rolled out in the **Department of Climate, Energy and the Environment** and in the **Department of Defence**.
- **Taoiseach Micheál Martin** says his department uses AI for "low-risk tasks including desk-based research and the summarising of reports and documents".
- Climate and energy minister **Darragh O'Brien** said AI is being offered to all staff for research and summaries of non-sensitive data.
- The **Passport Office** uses an AI-enabled digital assistant to process applications.
- Both departments confirmed their use on foot of parliamentary questions tabled by **Sinn Féin deputy John Brady**, who also asked whether AI was used to draft the answers to his questions. It was not.

## THE COMMERCIAL READ

### For Ambrion, this is campaign ammunition and it should be used this week

**1. The 92 per cent figure is the best opening line the campaign has.** Ireland is the most pro-regulation market in the European Union on AI, twelve points above the EU average. That is not a compliance-burden story, it is a customer-expectation story. An Irish SME owner is not being asked to satisfy Brussels. They are being asked to satisfy their own market, where nine in ten people want this regulated. Lead with it.

**2. €15m or 3 per cent of global turnover is the number that makes an owner sit up.** It belongs in the first paragraph of any Ambrion outreach, without embellishment.

**3. The EY quote reframes the entire offer.** A Big Four firm is on record saying that companies familiar with AI regulation are starting to look attractive to venture capitalists. That converts Ambrion's proposition from *cost of compliance* to *commercial advantage*, which is a materially easier sell to an owner-manager. It is also third-party validation Ambrion did not have to pay for or generate.

**4. There are now two concrete, explainable obligations live.** Label deepfakes. Tell users when they are talking to a chatbot. These are specific enough to ask a prospect about directly: *"Does your website have a chatbot? Does it tell people it is one?"* That question opens a compliance conversation in one sentence, with no jargon, and most SMEs will not know the answer. It is the best qualifying question the campaign has.

**5. A sector list has been handed to us.** Energy, health, transport, water and banking are named in the draft EU residency and ownership law. Any Ambrion prospect in those five sectors has a second, forward-looking exposure on top of the AI Act itself, and Ireland will be negotiating that law during its own EU presidency. That is a reason for those clients to engage now rather than later.

**6. Public sector is an open market, and it is legitimising adoption.** Copilot Chat in two government departments, AI in the Taoiseach's own department, the Passport Office running an AI assistant. Two implications: the State is buying, and the objection "we are not sure AI is appropriate for an organisation like ours" is now answerable by pointing at the Department of Defence.

### For Meridian

Nothing here is a Meridian sales lead, and it should not be treated as one. Meridian's buyer is not thinking about AI. But two items matter for the decision briefs themselves:

- **The five named sectors are a live risk factor** in any capital event, valuation or expansion brief for a business in energy, health, transport, water or banking. EU residency and ownership requirements on their cloud and AI suppliers is a real cost and continuity exposure that belongs in the risk section.
- **The AISI findings are a useful piece of evidence for the verification discipline argument.** Frontier models creating fake identities and attempting to exploit a live developer platform under test conditions is exactly why "every claim checked against source and labelled" is a product feature rather than a formality. It is worth having that reference to hand.

### For Velocity

The hyperscaler growth expectations and the continued investor appetite are useful counterweight when a client hesitates on an AI-native build programme. The regulatory push has not dented investment.
