## THE FACTS, AS REPORTED

### The headline finding

Economic shocks hit spending on infrastructure and consumer goods **more** than they hit spending by firms on research or software, including for AI.

> *"Investment in intangibles, including AI-related technologies, has shown greater resilience than investment in tangible assets during recent episodes of high uncertainty."*

The ECB says this goes some way to explaining Ireland's ongoing economic strength and AI boom, despite the inflationary effects of the war in the Middle East.

### Why intangibles hold up

Investment in intangible assets such as software and patents holds up better than investment in factories or equipment, because those investments are **usually more long-term and rely less on bank lending**, which insulates them from a downturn.

By contrast, firms' investment in **tangible assets such as machinery and new factories takes the biggest hit and is slowest to recover** after a shock.

### What happens to consumers

- Consumer spending on **restaurants, holidays and culture takes longer to recover** after an economic shock than expenditure on big-ticket items such as houses or cars.
- **Consumers are quicker to start spending again than businesses.**
- **Services take a bigger hit than goods.**
- *"Under uncertainty, households prioritise essential spending such as food, utilities and healthcare, while sharply curtailing discretionary items such as hospitality, recreation and cultural services."*
- *"Individuals can delay decisions on buying durables such as housing, cars and furniture."*

### The shocks studied

The pandemic in 2020, Brexit in 2021, Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, last year's US tariff regime, and this year's war in the Middle East. Recent snap French elections also caused spikes in economic uncertainty. **The next French presidential election is due next spring**, which the ECB flags as a forthcoming uncertainty event.

### Ireland-specific numbers

- The **ICT sector was worth €128bn to the Irish economy last year** and **supports 180,000 jobs**, per Department of Finance figures.
- Irish jobs and tax revenues are heavily dependent on large foreign direct investment from US firms in the **pharma and tech sectors**, which rely heavily on intangible assets such as patents, licences and other intellectual property.
- **Investment in AI-related data-centre construction by Google, Amazon and Microsoft has not halted**, despite a series of crises and warnings of an AI bubble.
