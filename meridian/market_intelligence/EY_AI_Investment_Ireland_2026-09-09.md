## THE ARGUMENT, AS REPORTED

**The bubble question is the wrong question.** Sceptics point to previous technology booms and ask whether the spending can pay off. That is fair, but the more important question is **whether companies and countries can afford not to invest**.

**The scale.** AI infrastructure spending is **projected to reach almost $2.9tn (€2.5tn) between 2025 and 2028**.

**Why the ROI lens is failing.** Traditional return on investment made sense when technology investment was about efficiency, growth or productivity. It makes less sense for technology that is **becoming part of the infrastructure that underpins economies and societies**.

**The railway analogy.** Enormous capital is committed to infrastructure before anyone knows where demand will settle.

**But AI differs in one respect.** Governments and businesses are not simply buying computer capacity. They are buying **resilience, strategic autonomy, and a degree of insurance against an increasingly uncertain world**. That changes how investment decisions are made.

**Not a claim that everything succeeds.** There will be winners and losers. Some infrastructure will be underused. Some companies will fail to capture the returns they expected. That does not automatically make the investment irrational.

**What executives are actually asking.** What happens if access to critical AI capabilities becomes constrained, if regulatory frameworks diverge, if geopolitical tensions intensify, if tokens become too expensive, or if the technologies they depend on sit largely outside their control. In that environment **AI infrastructure looks less like a discretionary investment and more like a strategic asset**.

**Europe.** The goal is not merely to produce the next AI champion. It is to retain **meaningful influence over technologies that will shape the decades ahead**. Regulation, infrastructure, investment and talent cannot be viewed in isolation; they are one competitiveness conversation. The challenge is not whether Europe regulates, but ensuring regulation is **accompanied by investment, capability and ambition**.

**And then Ireland, which is the part that matters to us.**

> The Irish debate has focused on data centres, electricity demand and infrastructure. **"We need to expand the conversation."**
>
> **"The highest-value opportunity for Ireland may not be hosting every server. It may be in becoming the European location with expertise in capital allocation, risk management, regulation, energy planning, corporate finance and technology governance."**
>
> The future AI economy will be shaped by those who decide **where it is built, how it is financed and who gets access to it**. Ireland already has significant strengths in those areas, and that may prove to be one of the most important investment stories of decades to come.

## CAPTURE STANDARD FOR NEWSPAPER SOURCES — adopted 11 September 2026

Applies to every clipping entering the knowledge base from now on.

**A capture is only fully VERIFIED when three things are held:** the **masthead**, the **date**, and the **page or section**. A photograph of the column alone verifies authorship and wording, nothing more.

**Practical rule when photographing:** one extra shot of the masthead and date line, or the front page of that day's edition, converts an attributed capture into a verified one and takes two seconds.

**Where only the column is captured:** grade authorship VERIFIED, the contents REPORTED, and the publication either UNVERIFIED or ATTRIBUTED to the person who took the photograph. Never assert a masthead nobody has seen.
