## 1. WHAT IS REPORTED

**The case.** A final-year music student at a Russell Group university, given the name "Alice", submitted an essay in May and received an email accusing her of using AI to write it. She had not. She shared her notes and early drafts with administrators and was still referred to a faculty misconduct panel. Her university's student services told her they were too busy to support her. **She hired a solicitor at a cost of more than £3,000 and the allegation was dismissed.**

**The volume.** **Daniel Sokol**, barrister at 12 King's Bench Walk, describes a *"tsunami"* of inquiries. **His firm received 300 requests related to unauthorised AI use at universities in the first half of this year, roughly 40 per cent of total business inquiries.** **Louise O'Driscoll**, the education law solicitor who acted for Alice, reports a *"notable increase"* this year, while accepting that AI misconduct *"goes on a lot more than it probably comes to light"*.

**The cost to the accused.** Defending a typical internal misconduct panel runs **£2,000 to £3,000**, higher where forensic linguists or other experts are engaged.

**The defence that works.** Lawyers are focusing on **ambiguity in the institutions' own policies**. Policies are written at institution level while individual departments and lecturers draw the line differently. Sokol: *"You get innocent students being found guilty. Those are the biggest tragedies."*

**The detection problem.** Sokol describes AI detection tools as *"flawed"*. As the software has been integrated into digital submission systems, complaints of false positives have increased. **A 2024 Stanford study is cited as finding that AI detection software disproportionately flagged writing by non-native speakers.** **Ciaran Donaghy**, lead policy officer at the Quality Assurance Agency for Higher Education, says a *"lack of clarity"* over policing is *"fuelling anxiety"*, and that **international students speaking English as a second language are at higher risk of being falsely accused.**

**Escalation.** Cases are moving from internal discipline to litigation. **Sokol recently settled an AI academic misconduct case out of court with a university.** In the US, a history student **successfully sued Adelphi University** in New York after being accused of using AI on an assignment.

**The institutional response.** **The University of Bath and Queen Mary University of London** have published guidance using the softer term **"poor academic practice"** to distinguish certain forms of AI use. Universities UK says both staff and students are alive to the risks, particularly around exams and assessment, and that *"students must learn to use it honestly and well."*

**Scale, as reported.** A 2024 survey of more than 95,000 students at 20 US public research universities found about 9 per cent admitted using AI to cheat.

**And the line that will outlast the article.** Alice, explaining how she came to be accused: *"I use AI day-to-day with emails and admin, kind of as a personal assistant. If you are at university right now, you are going to pick up the language and the way that chatbots talk."*

## 2. THE FINDING: THIS IS NOT A HIGHER EDUCATION STORY

**Substitute four words and this is an employment file.**

| Higher education | The organisation |
|---|---|
| Student | Employee |
| Misconduct panel | Disciplinary hearing |
| Degree and tuition fees | Job and livelihood |
| Institutional AI policy applied differently by department | Company AI policy applied differently by manager |

**Every element that produced this situation exists already inside employers that have written an AI policy.**

A policy written centrally and interpreted locally. Detection tools that are flawed and produce false positives. A disproportionate effect on people whose first language is not English. No workable definition of what unauthorised use actually means. An accused person with no support and a strong incentive to settle. And accusations escalating from internal process to litigation and settlement.

**This is a preview, and the lead time is short.** The sector that moved first is simply the sector that assesses written work for a living. **Every knowledge-work employer is on the same path and most have not noticed.**

## 3. THE SEVEN DOMAINS

### Legal and Governance, and this is the heaviest return

**The exposure for an Irish employer is not the AI policy. It is the disciplinary process built on it.**

**Fair procedures.** Accusing an employee of misconduct on the output of a tool the employer cannot explain, from a vendor the employee cannot cross-examine, is a weak footing for a disciplinary hearing. An employee dismissed on that basis has a straightforward argument about the fairness of the process rather than the merits.

**Discrimination risk, and it is the sharpest point in the whole article.** If detection software disproportionately flags writing by non-native speakers, then an employer using it in a disciplinary context is applying a tool with a known differential effect on people by nationality. **In Ireland that engages the race ground under the Employment Equality Acts.** An employer would need to be able to explain and justify it, and most could not, because they have never asked the vendor the question.

**Inconsistent application.** The defence that is working for students is that the policy said one thing centrally and something else in practice. **That is the same defence an employee will run**, and it is easier to prove inside a company than inside a university, because managers leave a written trail.

**Burden and evidence.** Detection output is probabilistic. The article reports counsel describing these tools as flawed. **An employer treating a percentage score as a finding of fact is making an evidential error, not a technological one.**

**Routed to the WRC trigger within this domain. This is live now and does not wait for the AI Act.**

### AI Strategy and Adoption

**The policy failure is specific and fixable.** One policy, written centrally, with no definition of permitted and unauthorised use at the level where the work actually happens.

**The institutional reframing is the useful idea.** Bath and Queen Mary moving to **"poor academic practice"** rather than misconduct creates a middle tier between acceptable and disciplinary. **An employer needs the same middle tier**, because the alternative is a binary in which ordinary daily use of a tool the organisation encourages becomes a dismissible offence.

**The AI Act connection, and it cuts both ways.** Article 4 as amended requires providers and deployers to take measures supporting the development of AI literacy among staff, taking account of their knowledge, experience, education, training and the context of use. **An employer disciplining staff for AI use while providing no training is exposed on both sides of the same obligation.**

**And the structural problem that will get worse.** Alice's own explanation is the important one. People who use these tools daily absorb the register. **Detection accuracy will degrade as AI-influenced writing becomes the normal way educated people write.** Any control built on detecting the style has a short life.

### Commercial and Deal

**A legal services market has formed in under a year.** Forty per cent of one set's business inquiries. That is a demand signal rather than an anecdote.

**The equivalent market on the employer side has not formed yet**, because employers have not yet reached the point universities reached. **The gap between the two is the window.**

**The Meridian product this points at is narrow and sellable now:** a review of an organisation's AI policy against the disciplinary process it would actually have to support, the detection tooling it relies on, and the equality exposure that tooling creates. **It is not an AI adoption engagement and it should not be sold as one.** It is a governance and employment exposure review that happens to concern AI.

**Boundary note.** This sits close to Ambrion. The distinction is that Ambrion sells AI Act compliance, while this is employment law exposure and fair procedures. **It should be agreed rather than assumed, like the rest.**

### Business Transformation and Growth

**The operational point.** Organisations are doing to staff what universities did to students: encouraging the technology on one hand and policing it on the other, with no definition of the line between them.

**The fix is cheap and nobody has done it.** Write down, at the level of the actual job, what is encouraged, what requires disclosure, and what is prohibited. Three categories rather than two. The article shows what it costs to get this wrong and the number is in the thousands per case before anyone reaches a tribunal.

### AI Equity and Investment

*Frontier caveat applies.*

**AI detection vendors are carrying an unpriced liability.** A tool with a documented differential effect by nationality, integrated into institutional submission systems and relied on in disciplinary processes, is a product liability and discrimination claim waiting for a claimant with resources. **The Adelphi outcome and the settled UK case are the first data points.**

**For any client acquiring, investing in or deploying such a tool, that exposure belongs in diligence.**
