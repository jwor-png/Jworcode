One commitment on the Meridian site matters more to me than any other, and it is what happens when the evidence is simply not good enough to support an answer.

Much analysis fills that gap rather than naming it. A figure gets rounded into certainty, an assumption quietly acquires the grammar of a fact, and a sentence that should read "we do not know" ends up reading as though somebody checked. It is rarely dishonest, it is just the natural pull of a document that is expected to conclude.

Meridian is built to stop instead, and three things make it stop. The grounding rule means no figure, ruling or valuation is presented as fact unless it has been retrieved from a primary source, not remembered and not inferred from something adjacent, and where it cannot be retrieved it is tagged as unverified or held back, with the gap stated rather than smoothed over. Confidence tagging means every claim carries its status as verified, reported or unverified, so a reader can see which parts of a case are solid, which are somebody else's word and which are still moving. And failing closed means that near a legal, financial or regulatory line the work names the boundary and stops rather than guessing past it, because anything binding belongs with a qualified professional.

The practical consequence is that a brief will sometimes come back saying the question cannot yet be answered, along with precisely what would have to be found before it could be. That can surprise people, but the alternative is an answer they might act on without knowing what it rests on.

All of this is published rather than promised, which means it can be held against the work rather than taken on trust.

The engine reads widely and quickly, and it is also the part most capable of producing a confident sentence with nothing underneath it, which is exactly why the discipline exists. The judgment at the end is mine, and so is the name on it.
