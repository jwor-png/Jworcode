A Decision Brief answers a question you already know you have, which makes it the easier of the two problems. The harder one is the question that has not been asked yet, and it is usually the one that ends up costing the money.

Most businesses I have worked with are not short of information at all, they are short of a position. There is a view in the finance function, another in operations and a third in the boardroom, and those three have never once been laid over each other and tested in the same room. So the business keeps moving, and the thing that will eventually hurt it is sitting quietly in the gap between two people who have never had a reason to compare notes.

The Executive Intelligence Review exists for exactly that situation. It takes the whole picture rather than a single question, putting one business or one division through all seven senior perspectives at once and asking in each of them the questions a serious buyer, lender or regulator would ask. Where is this business exposed, where is value being left on the table, what is coming that nobody has priced yet, and what would a hostile reader of these accounts say about them.

What comes back is a consolidated position with the reasoning left intact, the key risks ranked rather than merely listed, because a risk register that does not rank anything is a comfort blanket rather than a control. It sets out a programme of recommended actions in the order they should actually be taken, since sequence is where good advice most often fails, and it closes with the questions the board has to be able to answer before it commits to anything, which is the part most owners tell me they found both the most uncomfortable and the most useful.

It is the work I wanted available to me when I was sitting on the other side of the table with a week to form a view and a fiduciary duty to get it right.

There is one thing worth saying because it is the honest part. A review of this kind quite often corrects an earlier and more optimistic read, including one of my own, and that is not a failure of the process but the entire purpose of it. Anything that only ever confirms what you already believed is not analysis at all, it is agreement with extra pages.

The engine reads everything, misses nothing and holds every perspective at once without tiring or growing attached to a view, and then a human being who has sat in the chair decides what it means and puts a name to it. AI driven and human led, in that order, every time.

If you are heading into a capital event, a succession, a sale, or simply a year whose shape you cannot yet see, that is the conversation worth having.
