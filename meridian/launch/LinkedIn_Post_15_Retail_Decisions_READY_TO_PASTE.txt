Ask the owner of a well-run family retail business what keeps them awake and it is rarely the margin. The margin is watched every week, it is reported on, and there is usually a good adviser close to it. What keeps them awake is the decision the margin cannot make for them.

Whether to take on a second store, and on what lease. Whether a refit is worth a long commitment to one site. Whether to sell the property and keep the trade, or the other way round. Who takes over the business and what everyone else in the family receives. How to fund any of it. Each of those begins as a number and ends as a question about property, law, tax, family and risk, and each tends to sit unresolved because the person closest to the numbers is not the person placed to weigh all of the rest.

That is the gap Meridian was built for. It is not a retail product and was never meant to be one, because the buyer is not a sector. The buyer is an owner facing a decision. A single question is put through every domain that could change the answer, the findings are checked against primary sources and labelled as verified, reported or unverified, the recommendation is argued against before it reaches you, and anything binding goes to your own solicitor or accountant.

For anyone who advises owner-managed businesses, I think the opportunity is not in competing with the analysis you already do well. Your numbers show a client what is happening. What to decide, and who is accountable for it, is where clients most often go looking for someone else, and it is better for everyone that they find a second opinion inside a relationship they already trust than outside it.
