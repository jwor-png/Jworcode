A Decision Brief answers a question you already know you have.

The harder problem is the question nobody has asked yet.

Most businesses I have worked with are not short of information. They are short of a position. There is a view in the finance function, another in operations, a third in the boardroom, and they have never been laid over each other and tested in one place. So the business keeps moving, and the thing that will eventually cost it money is sitting in the gap between two people who have never had a reason to compare notes.

The Executive Intelligence Review exists for that.

It takes the whole situation rather than a single question. One business or one division, interrogated from all seven senior perspectives at once, with the questions asked in each of them that a serious buyer, lender or regulator would ask. Where is this business exposed. Where is value being left on the table. What is coming that nobody has priced yet. What would a hostile reader of these accounts say.

What comes back is a consolidated position with the reasoning intact. The key risks ranked rather than listed, because a risk register that does not rank is a comfort blanket. A programme of recommended actions in the order they should be taken, since sequence is usually where good advice fails. And the questions the board has to be able to answer before it commits to anything, which is the part most owners tell me they found the most uncomfortable and the most useful.

It is the work I wanted available to me when I was sitting on the other side of the table and had a week to form a view.

One thing worth saying, because it is the honest part. A review of this kind quite often corrects an earlier optimistic read, including one of my own. That is not a failure of the process. It is the process. Anything that only ever confirms what you already believed is not analysis, it is agreement with extra pages.

The engine reads everything and misses nothing. It holds every perspective at once without tiring and without becoming attached to a view. Then a human being who has sat in the chair decides what it means and puts a name to it.

AI driven. Human led. In that order, every time.

If you are heading into a capital event, a succession, a sale or simply a year you cannot see the shape of yet, that is the conversation.
