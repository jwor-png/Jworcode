A five-stage growth model has been doing the rounds on social media this month: sales, then operations, then talent, then systems, then acquisitions. It is tidy and it is sensible as far as it goes, and anyone who has built a business will recognise the stages. What it cannot tell you is what to do on the day a decision arrives that does not belong to any of them.

In owner-managed businesses those decisions rarely come in order. A lease comes up for renewal halfway through an expansion. A family member wants in, or wants out. A bank asks for security just as a buyer shows interest. The question the model asks at the systems stage, whether the business runs without you, is the right one, but it is treated as an operations test when for an owner it is also a question about ownership, tax, family and who is accountable if you are not there.

The model also ends with the owner as a buyer. For many owners the largest decision of a working life runs the other way: whether to sell, to whom and on what terms, or whether to hand the business to the next generation and what everyone else receives. None of the five stages covers it, and it is the hardest decision to take back once it has been made.
