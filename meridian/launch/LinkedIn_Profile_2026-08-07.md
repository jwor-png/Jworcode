## THE CREDENTIAL, CONFIRMED AND CHECKED

**John holds F.I.I.L.Ex., Fellow of the Irish Institute of Legal Executives, and practised for fifteen years in general legal practice.**

Two things follow, one of which is more valuable than it first appears.

### One: the boundary must be exact, and it is easy to get right

**A legal executive is not a solicitor.** The profile must not permit that inference, on any reading, by anyone skimming it. The correct form is to state the fifteen years and let the post-nominal carry the professional standing:

> *"Fifteen years in general legal practice, and a Fellow of the Irish Institute of Legal Executives."*

Avoid the bare phrase *"practised law for fifteen years"* standing alone. It is true, but a reader who does not know the distinction will supply *solicitor* themselves, and a credential that invites a wrong inference is worth less than one that does not.

### Two: Fellowship is a distinction, not the ordinary grade, and that is the part to surface

Per the Institute, **Fellowship is limited to founder members, to those who have served on the Council for three years or more since 2007, or to honorary and life memberships awarded by the Council for special services.** It is not the standard practising qualification.

**Which means the Fellowship implies either founding involvement or service on the governing body of a professional institute.** That is a *governance* credential, not merely a legal one, and it speaks directly to the non-executive positioning: he has sat on the council of a professional body, subject to its codes of conduct and confidentiality.

> **Question back to John, and it changes one line: by which route was the Fellowship conferred?** Founder member, Council service, or honorary for special services. **If it was Council service, "served on the governing Council of the Irish Institute of Legal Executives" belongs in the profile in its own right.** It is a board seat, and it predates the others.

### And it strengthens the Meridian boundary rather than complicating it

Meridian's rule is that it informs and never represents, and that anything binding goes to a qualified professional before it is relied on.

**Fifteen years inside a general practice is where a person learns exactly where that line sits.** It is not a policy John adopted for a governance document. It is how he worked for fifteen years, and it should be said that way, because it is the most credible version of the claim available to anyone.

### One further question, because it is directly load-bearing for succession

**Did the general practice work include probate and conveyancing?** Probate is succession law. If John has actually administered estates and handled the transfer of property and business assets on death and on retirement, that is not an adjacent credential for the succession positioning, **it is the credential**, and it should be stated explicitly rather than left inside the phrase "general practice."

Most people offering succession advice have the commercial half or the legal half. Very few have both, and almost none also chair a PLC.

- **Any claim about being among the few, the first, or uniquely placed.** Unverifiable. State the facts and let the reader reach the conclusion themselves; it is more convincing arrived at than asserted.
