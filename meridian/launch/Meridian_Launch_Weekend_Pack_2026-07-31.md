## 3. LAUNCH ARTICLE (LinkedIn article, ~900 words)

### Why Executives Need a Different Kind of Intelligence

Every leader I talk to has more data than they've ever had. Dashboards. AI tools. Analysts. Reports. And most of them still tell me the same thing, in different words: when it actually comes to the decision — the acquisition, the pivot, the hire, the deal — they're working off instinct and a pile of unread PDFs.

That's not a data problem. It's a synthesis problem.

**Why AI alone is insufficient**

AI is extraordinary at generating analysis. It is not, by itself, good at knowing what's true, what's missing, or what actually matters to the person making the call. Ask most AI tools a strategic question and you'll get a confident, well-formatted answer — and no way to tell whether it's grounded in fact or generated to sound plausible. For a leader whose decision carries real financial and reputational weight, "confident and plausible" isn't good enough. It has to be checked, sourced, and honest about what isn't known.

**Why executives need a different approach**

The decisions that actually matter — an acquisition, a market entry, a governance call, a transformation programme — rarely sit inside one discipline. They're commercial and legal and financial and operational all at once, and most advice comes from one lens at a time: the lawyer's view, the accountant's view, the consultant's view. Nobody sits above all of them and says, clearly, "here is the one recommendation, and here is exactly what it rests on."

**How Meridian reaches a decision-ready recommendation**

Meridian was built to be that single point above the silos. Every engagement runs a strategic question through multiple structured lenses — commercial, legal, financial, operational, governance — using AI to do the heavy lifting of research and first-pass analysis, and human judgement to test it, challenge it, and decide what's actually load-bearing. Every material claim is tagged: verified, estimated, or flagged as unverified and not to be relied on. Nothing gets presented as fact that hasn't earned it.

The output isn't a report you have to interpret. It's a recommendation you can act on, with the reasoning and the sources laid out plainly enough that you could defend it to your own board.

**Why this, why now**

AI has made analysis cheap and abundant. It has not made judgement cheap. If anything, the flood of AI-generated content has made real judgement more valuable, not less — because now the hard part isn't finding an answer, it's knowing which answer to trust.

That's the gap Meridian exists to close. Not another AI tool. An Executive Intelligence practice that uses AI properly, checks it rigorously, and stands behind the recommendation with human judgement at the front.

If that's the kind of clarity you need on a decision in front of you now, I'd welcome the conversation.
