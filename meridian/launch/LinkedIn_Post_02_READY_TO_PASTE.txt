Every board I have sat on has faced the same moment, where a decision is on the table, everybody around it has an opinion, and nobody can point to the piece of work that actually settles it. That is the gap a Decision Brief was built to close.

It begins with one specific question that has a yes or a no at the end of it, whether that is buying a business, entering a market, or taking someone's money and deciding what instrument to take it on. That question is then put through the domains that could genuinely change the answer, rather than the single domain it happened to arrive in.

There are seven of those domains, covering commercial and deal, legal and governance, finance and restructuring, property and development, AI strategy and adoption, business transformation and growth, and AI equity and investment. A question that looks purely commercial very often turns out to be a legal one, and the whole value lies in discovering that before you commit rather than eighteen months afterwards.

What comes back is a written position you can carry into a board meeting, setting out a clear recommendation with the reasoning still attached to it, the risks that would change the answer stated plainly rather than buried, and every claim labelled as verified, reported or unverified so you can see precisely which parts of the case are solid and which are still moving. It closes with the questions you should be able to answer yourself before you commit to anything.

There are three things it will not do, and I want to be as clear about those as about everything else.

It will not tell you what you want to hear, because every recommendation is argued against before it leaves, and a position that has never been attacked is not really a position at all, it is a preference wearing better clothes.

It will not dress an assumption up as a fact, so where the evidence is not there it says so and tells you what would need to be found before the question could properly be answered.

And it will not represent you, because Meridian informs and anything binding goes to a qualified professional first. I say that as someone who spent fifteen years in practice and knows exactly where that line sits and why it matters.

The engine does the work that a machine is genuinely better at, reading everything, missing nothing, and holding seven perspectives at once without tiring or becoming attached to a view, while the judgment at the end of it is mine and so is the accountability that goes with it. That is what AI driven and human led means, and the order of those two words is the whole point.
