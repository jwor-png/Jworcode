=== ABOUT (2597/2600) ===
I began in law. Fifteen years in general legal practice, and I am a Fellow of the Irish Institute of Legal Executives. Forty years on, most of my career has been spent in and around boardrooms instead: deal-making, governance, restructuring, property, and the decisions directors end up carrying personally. I chair AHL PLC, and I am an independent non-executive director and chair of the Audit and Risk Committee at United Hardware. The law never left. It is still how I read a risk, a set of terms, and the gap between what a document says and what people think it says.

Succession is where the two halves of my career meet. I practised conveyancing and probate, and handled succession in the farming community, which is the hardest version of it. The land, the home and the business are the same asset, one child usually takes it and the others do not, and the conversation at the kitchen table is harder than any of the legal work around it. More recently I have been working on succession planning for the member businesses of United Hardware: rural, family-owned, second and third generation. The same market from the other side of the counter.

Most owners put it off for years, not from avoidance but because no single adviser covers the whole of it. The accountant sees the tax. The solicitor sees the instrument. Very few see the business, the family and the paperwork together.

The other is the boardroom and artificial intelligence. The EU AI Act has made AI governance a board obligation with directors' duties attached, and most boards have nobody at the table who understands what that means in practice. I am not a technical specialist and have no ambition to become one. What I bring is knowing which questions to ask, and when an answer is sound rather than merely confident.

Most leaders do not lack information. They lack a defensible answer at the moment a decision has to be made. That is why I built Meridian Intelligence: an executive intelligence practice, not an AI company. One high-consequence decision, one written position, evidence checked to source, and the things that would change the answer named. It informs; it does not represent. Anything binding goes to a qualified professional first, and I know where that line sits from fifteen years beside it.

Forty years has taught me the value is rarely in the answer. It is in knowing what stands up and what does not, and saying which is which.

If you are weighing a succession, or a decision too expensive to get wrong, or you want a director who can hold the AI conversation at board level, I am glad to talk.
