Over the past few weeks I have written here about how the work is done. The Decision Brief, the Executive Intelligence Review, the seven domains a question gets put through, and why succession keeps turning out to be a governance problem dressed as a family one. The site now sets all of that out properly in one place.

There is one thing on it I want to draw attention to, because it is the part most firms leave off.

It says what Meridian will not do.

It will not represent you, because Meridian informs and anything binding goes to your own solicitor or accountant. It will not present an assumption as a fact, and where the evidence is not there it says so rather than filling the gap with a confident sentence. It will not tell you what you want to hear, because every recommendation is argued against before it leaves. And it is not a substitute for judgment, since somebody still has to put their name to the answer and carry it.

Publishing your limits is unusual and I think it should not be. A client is entitled to know the shape of what they are buying before they buy it, including the edges.

Forty years of it sits behind this, the first fifteen in legal practice and the rest around boardroom tables, and that is where the judgment at the end of every piece of work comes from. The engine reads everything and misses nothing. The decision is human and it is mine.

Have a look, and tell me what is missing. I would rather hear it now than in six months.
