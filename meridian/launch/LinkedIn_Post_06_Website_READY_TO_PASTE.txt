Over the past few weeks I have written here about how the work is done, covering the Decision Brief, the Executive Intelligence Review, the seven domains a question gets put through, and why succession so often turns out to be a governance problem dressed as a family one. The site now sets all of that out properly in one place.

There is one part of it I would draw attention to, because it is the section most firms leave off entirely, and that is the page saying what Meridian will not do.

It will not represent you, because Meridian informs and anything binding goes to your own solicitor or accountant. It will not present an assumption as a fact, and where the evidence is not there it says so and names what would have to be found instead of filling the gap with a confident sentence. It will not tell you what you want to hear, because every recommendation is argued against before it leaves. And it is not a substitute for judgment, since somebody still has to put their name to the answer and carry it.

Publishing your own limits is unusual and I think it should not be. A client is entitled to know the shape of what they are buying before they buy it, edges included, and a limit that is written down can be held against the work rather than taken on trust.

Forty years sits behind this, the first fifteen of them in legal practice and the rest around boardroom tables, and that is where the judgment at the end of every piece of work comes from.
