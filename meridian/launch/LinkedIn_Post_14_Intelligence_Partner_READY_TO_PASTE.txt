The Decision Brief, the Executive Intelligence Review and Transaction Intelligence each answer a question, and then the engagement ends. For a single decision that is exactly right. For an owner, a founder or a family business, though, the hard questions rarely arrive one at a time. They keep coming, they connect to each other, and the answer to the third depends on what was decided about the first.

That is what the Intelligence Partner relationship is for. It is a standing arrangement rather than a project, so that when something lands on your desk you can bring it as it arises, to someone who already knows the business, the people and the position, instead of explaining it all again to a new adviser who then has to be brought up to speed at your expense.

Every piece of work is recorded, including what was recommended, what was set aside and why, and the next question starts from there rather than from nothing. Over time that record becomes the most useful thing in the arrangement, because most bad decisions in owner-managed businesses are not made on bad information. They are made without the memory of what was decided last time and the reasons it was decided that way.

It works the same way as everything else here. A question goes through every domain that could change the answer, findings are checked against primary sources and labelled as verified, reported or unverified, and the recommendation is argued against before it reaches you. Anything binding goes to your own solicitor or accountant. The engine does the reading and the cross-checking, and the judgment at the end is mine, with my name on it.
