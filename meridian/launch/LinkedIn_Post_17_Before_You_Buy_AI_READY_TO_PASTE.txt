Owners of established businesses are being approached more often by people selling AI, whether it is lead generation, automation or an agent that is promised to run part of the company. Some of it is genuinely good. A great deal of it is sold on the same pattern, a confident promise, a number that sounds precise and a short window to decide, and the owner is left to work out which is which with very little to go on.

The first question is what exactly is being claimed and what it has done for a business like yours, shown rather than described, because a claim with no worked example behind it is a hope. The second is where the number came from, who measured it and over what period, since a figure such as a 60 per cent saving means very little without its source. The third is who is accountable when it is wrong, a named person you can reach or a platform with terms you have not read, and what the contract actually says about that.

The fourth is who owns what it produces and what happens to your data, including whether it is used to train anything, because the terms of the underlying AI platform sit beneath whatever the seller promises you. It is also worth asking what happens when you stop, meaning whether you can take your data, your configuration and your results with you, or whether you are renting something you cannot leave.
