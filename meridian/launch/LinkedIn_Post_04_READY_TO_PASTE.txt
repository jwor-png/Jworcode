The term sheet is where a transaction is actually settled. Everything after it is drafting, and by then the leverage has gone, because the party asking to reopen a point is the party who looks like they are moving the goalposts.

That is the thinking behind Transaction Intelligence, which is due diligence grade analysis produced before you sign rather than after, and structured the way the other side's adviser will attack it.

The work covers the valuation and what happens to your position across the rounds or the stages that follow, the instrument itself with the alternatives set out and the reasons they were rejected, and a term by term negotiating position that says plainly what to concede and what not to. It includes a read on the counterparty, meaning what they will push for, why, and how to answer it, and it stays available through the negotiation window rather than arriving as a report and disappearing.

Three things come up again and again, and none of them is exotic.

The first is that costs are almost always understated, and not by a little. Advisers on both sides, diligence in three or four disciplines, tax structuring, insurance, and the tax treatment of the money itself, all of which lands on the party who did not think to ask about it early enough. Very few people model the full cost of completing a transaction before they agree the headline number, and the headline number is the one everybody remembers.

The second is that the same money can be worth materially different amounts depending on how it is structured, and that difference is decided by a single clause agreed weeks before anyone thinks about tax.

The third is that the protections written into a deal to bind one party are drafted broadly, and broad drafting reaches into things nobody was thinking about at the time. Reading those clauses against everything else you own is not paranoia, it is basic hygiene, and it is the check most often skipped.

None of this replaces your solicitor or your accountant, and it is not meant to. It makes sure you walk into the room already knowing your position, so that the professional advice you are paying for is spent on the things only a professional can do.

The engine does the reading, the modelling and the pattern matching across every domain at once. The judgment about what to accept and what to walk away from is human, and it belongs to the person whose name is on the signature page.

AI driven and human led, in that order, as always.
