Read that table again and one thing is true of every single row.

Not one of those clients came to Meridian with an AI question. Eight of the nine were not thinking about AI at all. Anthony Hennessy was thinking about gas cylinders. John Lynch was thinking about what his business is worth. Fleming Medical's board was thinking about a capital event. Johnny Shanahan was thinking about a fifty thousand pound investor.

They came with **a decision they could not make alone and could not afford to get wrong.**

And in every case Meridian did the same thing: it put seven domains of senior analysis at that one decision simultaneously, and handed back a written, decision-grade position in days rather than weeks, with John's judgment as the final gate.

That is the business. It has been the business since Arcade Trader. It has just never been said out loud.

## THE FOUR ANSWERS

### 1. What is it?

**Meridian Intelligence is a decision intelligence business. It takes a single high-consequence business decision and returns a written, decision-grade position in days, using seven domains of senior analysis run simultaneously, with forty years of board-level judgment as the final gate.**

Note what that sentence does not contain. It does not contain the words AI advisory, AI adoption, AI strategy, or AI governance.

AI is how Meridian is built. It is not what Meridian sells. The client buys the decision. The engine room is our business, not theirs.

This is the single most important line in this document, and it is the line that resolves everything else.

### 2. Who is it for?

**Owners, chairs and boards of Irish and UK mid-market businesses, turnover roughly five million to one hundred million, facing a decision worth more than the fee by at least two orders of magnitude.**

Specifically, four recurring situations, all of them evidenced above:

1. **A capital event.** Raising, selling, taking an investor, structuring an instrument. Barber Republic, Fleming Medical, Arcade Trader.
2. **A category or expansion move.** Should we enter this, buy this, form this. Stargas, United Hardware paint group, United Hardware garden centre.
3. **A governance or board question with teeth.** Audit soundness, directors' exposure, vendor challenge. United Hardware ARC.
4. **A proposition on the table that needs verifying before it is backed.** UroPharma, Mike Molloy.

Not "all things to all people." Four situations. Each one recognisable in a single sentence by the person living it.

### 3. How does it make money?

Three revenue lines, in order of how proven they already are.

**Line one: the fixed-scope decision brief.** This is what has actually been sold and delivered nine times. One decision, defined scope, written deliverable, days not weeks. It prices per brief because it is a unit of work with a boundary, not open-ended advisory time. The cost and time comparison work already filed (`meridian/briefs/Meridian_Cost_Time_Comparison.pdf`) makes the value argument: consultancy-grade depth across seven domains, at a fraction of consultancy elapsed time. Indicative: three to fifteen thousand euro per brief depending on domains engaged and stakes.

**Line two: the retained decision seat.** United Hardware is already this shape without being named as such. Multiple decisions across a year, one board, standing access. This is where the real revenue is because it removes the sell every time. Indicative: monthly retainer, four to eight thousand euro, covering an agreed number of briefs plus standing availability.

**Line three: AI equity and investment (Manager 7).** Structuring arrangements where AI capability is contributed for equity. This is genuinely differentiated, it is already written into the manager set, and it is the line that converts Meridian from a fee business into an asset business over time. Barber Republic is arguably the first live instance. It should be treated as line three, not line one, because it is the slowest to convert and the hardest to sell cold.

### 4. What is defensible?

Shane is right to press here. Frameworks are copyable. So the honest answer has to be about what cannot be copied quickly. There are three things, and only the third is really durable.

**First, the method delta, which is real but temporary.** Seven domains run in parallel rather than sequentially, producing a full investigative analysis in days. Fleming Medical is the proof: seven domains, eight to ten interrogated questions each, consolidated into one executive brief with five risks, a four-programme recommendation and a human gate. A traditional firm would have taken weeks and charged multiples. That advantage is real today and will erode as others build similar engines. Eighteen to thirty-six months of lead, not permanent.

**Second, access, which is genuinely hard to copy.** John chairs AHL PLC. He is Independent Non-Executive Director and Chair of the Audit and Risk Committee at United Hardware. Forty years of board-level relationships across commercial, property, healthcare, governance and capital. This is not marketing. It is the reason Meridian is in the room where the decision is made rather than the room where a report is received. A competitor can copy the method in a year. They cannot copy forty years of being trusted by the people who own the decision.

**Third, the corpus, which compounds and is the real moat.** Nine engagements are now filed with their reasoning intact. Every engagement adds a decision pattern to a body of Irish and UK mid-market decision precedent that only Meridian holds. Barber Republic proved the value of this directly: Meridian's analysis and Shane's independently written founder documents converged on the same instrument, which is what gave the recommendation its authority. That convergence is only possible because the reasoning was written down and retrievable. In three years this corpus is the asset. It is also, incidentally, exactly what makes Meridian saleable rather than just billable.

## THE FIFTH QUESTION SHANE DID NOT ASK OUT LOUD

His actual words: *"you need to be careful that your naturals come and replicate everything that's in Velocity. And I mean that avoid, because you can't have two people talking about the same thing in the same way in different businesses."*

He is right, and our own file has flagged it since 29 June (`cuan/business_architecture.md:63`) as unresolved.

**Velocity AI, verified from its own site:** "We are not consultants. We are operators who build and deploy AI at scale." "We diagnose where AI will hit your business first, then we build the response." Its products are a Strategic Clarity Diagnostic, a Live Strategic Simulator, a 90-Second Strategic Brief, and four tiers of Migration Education.

**Where the collision actually is.** It is not in the whole business. It is in exactly two places:

1. Meridian's Manager 5, "AI Strategy and Adoption, AI governance frameworks, EU AI Act compliance, readiness assessments, structured adoption roadmaps." That is Velocity's core product, described in Velocity's own words. Export Anatolia, our one AI-adoption engagement, is the one row in the evidence table that should have been a Velocity job.
2. The word "clarity." Velocity leads on it. Meridian's overview language reaches for it too.

**The resolution, and I am recommending it rather than offering options.**

> **Velocity sells AI. Meridian sells the decision.**
>
> Velocity's buyer has already decided AI is their question and wants someone to build the answer. Meridian's buyer is not thinking about AI at all. They are thinking about a buying group, a valuation, an investor, an audit. Different question, different buyer, different room, different conversation.

Three concrete changes make that true rather than merely stated:

1. **Meridian gives up AI adoption as a front-door offer.** Manager 5 stays in the engine, because a decision sometimes touches AI and we need the domain. But it comes off the shopfront. Meridian no longer sells AI readiness, AI roadmaps or EU AI Act compliance to the market. Any inbound of that shape goes to Velocity. Export Anatolia-type work is Velocity work.
2. **Meridian stops using "clarity" and "strategic intelligence" as its headline.** Velocity owns that ground and owns it first. Meridian's headline becomes the decision. Something in the shape of: *the decision you cannot afford to get wrong, answered properly, in days.*
3. **The referral runs one way and it is worth money.** Meridian is in the room where the owner is making a commercial decision. That room routinely reveals AI need. Meridian hands those to Velocity. Velocity's buyer, mid-AI-programme, routinely hits a commercial or governance decision. Velocity hands those to Meridian. Formalise it, including the fee split. That converts the overlap from a competition problem into a distribution channel for both.

This is not Meridian shrinking. It is Meridian getting a front door of its own instead of standing in Velocity's doorway.

On EU AI Act, both Ambrion and Velocity lead on it and Meridian's Manager 5 claims it too.

A single client could meet three of John's businesses at the same door and be pitched the same thing three ways. That is worse than the Meridian/Velocity issue because two of the three are John's own.

**This needs to be on the agenda with Shane at the same sitting.** Meridian's definition cannot be finalised in isolation from Ambrion's.

## WHAT I AM CORRECTING FROM MY EARLIER ANSWER

Earlier in this session I said Meridian's capacity ceiling was low because everything runs through John's head, and that it scales only by hiring people who think like him.

That was too pessimistic and I had the constraint in the wrong place.

The product is a fixed-scope written brief, not open-ended advisory days. The analysis is produced by the engine. What requires John is **review and judgment**, not production. That is a materially higher ceiling than I implied, and it is raised by adding senior reviewers who can pressure-test a brief, which is a far easier hire than finding another cross-disciplinary synthesiser.

Realistic capacity today: eight to twelve briefs a month with John reviewing, alongside his existing board commitments. That is a real business at line-one pricing before any retainer.
