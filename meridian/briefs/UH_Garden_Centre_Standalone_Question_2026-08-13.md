## Should United Hardware Consider a Standalone Garden Centre Group?
*Prompted by the Rathwood/Evergreen rescue coverage. Prepared for John Webb O'Rourke, 13 August 2026. Commercial & Deal lens, business-only. Sources tagged VERIFIED/REPORTED/UNVERIFIED.*

---

## THE ARTICLE, IN BRIEF

Rathwood (Tullow, Co. Carlow — a large single-site home-and-garden retailer) collapsed into examinership with liabilities well above assets and 62 jobs at stake. [VERIFIED] It's being rescued by **Evergreen Home & Garden Holdings Ltd**, a consortium led by corporate advisor Kevin Nolan, via a court-approved €750k examinership scheme. [VERIFIED — court approval; Nolan's specific role REPORTED, not independently corroborated beyond the source article] Evergreen's own statement is explicit: the Irish garden centre sector is "still dominated by single-site independents," and their strategy is to stabilise Rathwood first, then **expand by acquiring more garden centres** — a genuine consolidation/roll-up play, not just a rescue.

## IS THE FRAGMENTATION CLAIM REAL?

Directionally, yes. No independent market-structure data (Bord Bia, CSO) was found to quantify it precisely, but the evidence supports it: named significant Irish garden centres (Johnstown, The Arboretum) are large, well-known, and single-site/family-owned. No existing Irish garden-centre buying group, franchise or ownership consolidator was found operating before Evergreen — if Evergreen executes as described, it would be a genuine first for the sector.

## THE UK COMPARATOR — WHO ACTUALLY DOES THIS ROLL-UP

This is the most useful data point for your question. In the UK, Dobbies Garden Centres has been divesting sites through 2025, and they're being bought up by **British Garden Centres** (now the UK's largest operator by site count, 72+ sites) and **Blue Diamond** (group turnover now past £400m on the back of these acquisitions). [VERIFIED] **Both are dedicated garden-centre specialists consolidating a distressed rival — not hardware or DIY chains diversifying into garden centres.** No evidence was found anywhere (UK or Ireland) of a hardware/DIY/builders-merchant group — Kingfisher/B&Q, Wickes, or United Hardware itself — acquiring standalone garden centres as a separate business line. Kingfisher's own consolidation moves (Focus DIY, Homebase stores) are DIY-on-DIY, not DIY-into-garden-centres.

## WHAT UNITED HARDWARE IS ALREADY DOING, AND WHY THAT'S THE RIGHT SCALE

United Hardware is confirmed to be developing garden as an in-store category — the new Homevalue concept-store format explicitly targets "an improved balance between trade, DIY, home and garden categories," and garden items sit within the Commercial Team's existing purchasing categories. [VERIFIED] This is a department-level move inside stores UH already owns/supplies, using buying, logistics and stockist infrastructure UH already has. It is a fundamentally different, and much lower-risk, move than acquiring standalone garden centre businesses.

## THE HONEST ANSWER TO YOUR QUESTION

**Standalone garden centre acquisition is a different business, not an extension of the current one.** Destination garden centres carry live-plant/nursery supply chains, seasonal stock and shrinkage risk, horticultural expertise, and increasingly a hospitality/destination retail model (cafés, events) — none of which UH's existing hardware buying-group model, infrastructure or team expertise is built around. The market precedent (Evergreen in Ireland, British Garden Centres and Blue Diamond in the UK) consistently shows dedicated garden-centre specialists doing this consolidation, not hardware groups moving sideways into it — that pattern is worth taking seriously as a signal, not a coincidence.

**Recommendation:** continue and lean into the in-store garden category expansion already underway — it's the right-sized, right-capability move and rides the same fragmented-independent dynamic Evergreen is exploiting, just inside UH's own stores rather than as a separate acquisition programme. Don't build a standalone garden centre acquisition strategy as core UH strategy. The one scenario worth keeping half an eye open for: an opportunistic, one-off acquisition of a *specific* distressed independent garden centre that has clear commercial synergy with an existing UH member's site or region (e.g. a struggling local garden centre near a UH store, bought for its site/customer base rather than as the start of a roll-up programme) — that's a real option, but it should be evaluated case by case, not planned as a Rathwood-style consolidation play.
