Meanwhile the demand context has moved from theoretical to live: Uisce Eireann has named Kilmallock itself among Limerick supplies under severe drought pressure (VERIFIED, water.ie/Limerick Leader), a six-week hosepipe ban began 16 July 2026 across Dublin and four other counties (REPORTED, RTE/Irish Times), contingency tankering is running now in West and North Cork (VERIFIED, water.ie), and a Meath precedent (Ballivor) shows the utility paying €180k-250k a year to tanker water into one village (REPORTED, Meath Chronicle). ## The case in five lines

1. **Demand is real, local and paid for.** Uisce Eireann and councils are contracting tankering in Munster today; group water schemes are eligible for emergency tankering; dairy farms in dry spells are a further seasonal segment (pricing unverified).
2. **Economics clear at tiny scale.** Contract haulage means no tanker capex; indicative break-even at 1-2 loads a week; a single baseload customer (construction, quarry, dairy washdown) carries the business between dry spells and drought work is upside, not the plan.
3. **Regulation is layered but navigable, with a fork that matters.** Non-potable and raw bulk supply is the simple path. Potable tanker supply pulls in the full drinking-water and food-hygiene regime. Start non-potable/raw.
4. **The 2025 groundwork still pays.** Shane's research pointed at Deebert as the likely source area; the Diviner and the Ishka comparable remain available; Ger Blake's objections are now answered and his read should be re-taken.
5. **Timing.** The dry-weather cycle that creates demand is the same cycle that will eventually bring competitors and tighter abstraction control. Early registration and site control are the moat.

## The honest risks

- **Spiky demand:** drought work alone is not a business; the baseload contract question decides viability.
- **Pricing gap:** no Irish per-load tanker pricing is public (UNVERIFIED); direct quotes required before any model is relied on.
- **The abstraction paradox:** the same scarcity driving demand may make the EPA harder on new abstractions in stressed catchments. Registration threshold is 25 m3/day (about one small load); a serious operation may face a "significant abstraction" assessment and possibly a licence (REPORTED, EPA/MHC/ALG commentary; primary statute text 403-blocked this session, FAILED CLOSED).
- **Procurement route:** how Uisce Eireann buys tankering (framework vs spot) is unknown and determines access to the anchor buyer.
- **Planning:** the domestic borehole exemption does not cover commercial sale; Limerick CCC permission for works, loading bay and HGV movements should be assumed.
