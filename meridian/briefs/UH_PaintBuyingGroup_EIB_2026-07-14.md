## 1. The opportunity in one paragraph

Independent paint shops in Ireland buy alone, against multiples and manufacturer-owned chains that buy at scale. In the United States this exact problem was solved in 1960: ALLPRO Corporation, a buying cooperative of independent paint and decorating dealers, today serves roughly 300 member companies across 2,000+ storefronts with negotiated vendor programmes, aggregated rebates and a private-label range (VERIFIED). We found no dedicated equivalent for independent paint shops anywhere in the UK or Ireland (REPORTED; an unprovable negative, but nothing surfaced across the UK merchant societies, the Irish groups, or trade press). United Hardware already runs the closest structural precedent in the country in hardware, and after the Expert Hardware acquisition (June 2026, CCPC-cleared) stands at roughly 200 stores and over €500m combined retail turnover with a 150,000 sq ft central distribution centre at Citywest (VERIFIED). The gap is real, the model is proven abroad, and United Hardware is the natural owner of it in Ireland.

## 2. The market

- Statista puts Ireland's consumer/DIY "Paint, Wallpaper & Supplies" segment at US$90.3m revenue (2024) (VERIFIED as to the estimate; consumer segment only, excludes most trade volume, so total decorative coatings spend is materially higher).
- There is no authoritative public count of independent paint and colour shops in Ireland. The best proxy found: Colourtrend (General Paints Group, Celbridge) alone distributes through 180+ independently owned stockists (VERIFIED). Fleetwood (Virginia, Co. Cavan) supplies 400+ retailers and describes itself as Ireland's largest indigenous paint manufacturer, a title General Paints also claims for itself (both self-descriptions, attributed not adjudicated).
- Two of the brands independents lean on most are Irish family-owned manufacturers whose route to market depends on the independent channel. That gives a buying group genuine negotiating gravity with domestic suppliers, before any conversation with AkzoNobel/Dulux, Crown (Hempel) or PPG (Johnstone's).
- Cautionary comparable: PaintWell in the UK, a private-equity-backed consolidator (Cairngorm Capital) of decorators' merchants, entered administration in February 2025; Brewers acquired 38 of its 44 stores (VERIFIED). The lesson: buying scale bolted together with debt fails; member-owned aggregation, the ALLPRO and United Hardware way, endures.

## 3. The commercial architecture

The proven buying group value stack, assembled from ALLPRO, NMBS (UK merchant society: 1,000+ members, ~3,100 branches, ~£8bn member sales, average rebate 3.84% of processed invoices, affiliate entry at £1,500/yr with a £600k turnover minimum and a three-year affiliate cap, all VERIFIED) and United Hardware's own model:

1. Negotiated supplier terms and aggregated rebates (the core)
2. Central billing, one invoice, del credere comfort for suppliers
3. Central distribution through the existing Citywest infrastructure
4. Group marketing and a shared retail identity for those who want it
5. Own-label range in Phase 3 (the ALLPRO play, and the deepest margin)
6. Training, business advisory and member services UH already runs for hardware

## 4. The numbers (indicative; every input an assumption, arithmetic independently checked)

Working assumptions: average paint purchasing €300k per shop per year; membership fee €2,000; group rebate share 1.5% of volume; 25% of volume routed through central distribution at a 10% handling margin.

- Break-even at roughly 13 to 15 member shops.
- The member's side: a 3 to 7 point net buying improvement on €300k of purchases is worth €9,000 to €21,000 a year against a fee of €2,000 to €3,000. Payback of 3x to 10x the fee. That is the recruitment pitch in one line.
- The three assumptions that most move the answer: share of volume through the warehouse (paint is heavy and often direct-shipped), the rebate spread UH can negotiate and retain, and true average purchasing per shop.

## 5. Legal and governance (the one named exception)

The framework is clear and the structure is plausibly lawful, but this section carries a mandatory gate.

- Joint purchasing sits under Article 101 TFEU and section 4 of the Competition Act 2002. The EU's 2023 Horizontal Cooperation Guidelines provide a soft safe harbour for joint purchasing where the members' combined share is below 15% on BOTH the purchasing market and the downstream selling market; buyer cartels (disguised price-fixing) are by-object infringements with no safe harbour at any share. This is corroborated across five independent law firm analyses (REPORTED, multi-source) but this session could not retrieve the primary texts (EUR-Lex and the Irish Statute Book were blocked at the network layer), so it is not VERIFIED to Meridian's standard and is flagged, not relied on.
- Design constraints regardless of thresholds: member paint shops are downstream competitors, so individualised price, volume and margin data must be firewalled (aggregated and historical only); membership non-exclusive; no resale price maintenance; and United Hardware organising a group of retailers it also supplies adds a vertical dimension needing its own review.
- **Mandatory human gate:** formal competition law advice before any supplier negotiation or member agreement is signed. At realistic scale (even 120 shops) combined shares are likely far below 15% of the Irish paint purchasing and retail markets, which is encouraging, but that is a judgement for counsel, not for this brief.

## 6. The AI layer (what makes this a 2026 buying group, not a 1970s one)

All design proposal, not existing product. The data a buying group naturally aggregates (purchasing, rebates, POS sell-through) is exactly the data AI runs best on:

1. **Demand forecasting** by store, SKU, season and colour trend: shops order to actual demand, dead stock falls
2. **Dynamic rebate and terms optimisation**: tiers renegotiated continuously on live volume, not once a year
3. **Local price intelligence**: each shop sees its pricing against the multiples in its own catchment
4. **Assortment and colour-trend intelligence**: range-planning insight only the multiples have today
5. **Automated replenishment** through Citywest: reorders driven by forecast, working capital released
6. **Member intelligence dashboard**: one page per shop showing margin, velocity and rebate earned, a retention tool as much as analytics
7. **EU AI Act-clean by design**: purpose limitation, member consent and audit trail from day one, a trust point no legacy group offers

The strategic point for United Hardware: the group gets smarter with every transaction, and the aggregated dataset becomes an asset no individual shop, and no rival group, can replicate.

## 7. Rollout

- **Phase 1, months 0-6: prove the deal, not the tech.** 10 to 15 founding shops, 2 to 3 anchor supplier agreements (the Irish manufacturers first), manual rebate tracking. Note the finance table: break-even sits at 13 to 15 shops, so Phase 1 is designed to reach it.
- **Phase 2, months 6-18: scale.** Central billing on existing UH infrastructure, AI dashboard v1, formal rebate engine.
- **Phase 3, months 18-36: the margin.** Own-label paint under the UH umbrella; opt-in cross-sell both ways, hardware range into paint shops and the paint category across the ~200-store network.

## 8. Risks, answered

- **Member data trust:** value flows to the member first (their own dashboard) before any group-level use of their data
- **Supplier resistance:** start with two or three anchors who gain from consolidation; let the rest follow proof
- **Channel conflict with existing UH members who sell paint:** paint specialists join as a new adjacent member category with its own terms; Phase 3 cross-sell is opt-in
- **Execution capacity:** a dedicated paint category lead before Phase 2; the pilot stays deliberately small
