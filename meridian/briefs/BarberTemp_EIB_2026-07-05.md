## 1. Commercial & Transaction Intelligence
- **The wedge is cover bookings on commission. Everything else is roadmap.** The teaser should sell one loop: shop posts chair, barber fills it, both pay, both rate.
- **Unit economics do not exist anywhere in the pack.** Day rates are stated (£120-£180+); the commission rate is not. Build bottom-up now: at an illustrative 12-15% take, a booking yields ~£18-£38. Break-even at ~£29k/month costs implies roughly 900-1,500 bookings/month or meaningful membership mix. That arithmetic, honest and shown, is worth more to an investor than the £1bn TAM line.
- **Serviceable market definition:** the opportunity is the UK's cover-days pool (holidays, sickness, day-off gaps), not industry revenue. Sizing it bottom-up (shops x uncovered days x fill rate) is a one-page job that answers the TAM criticism permanently.

## 2. Legal & Governance Intelligence
- **NEW, and material: employment-agency status.** A platform supplying temp labour to UK businesses may fall within the Employment Agencies Act 1973 and the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations 2003. Whether BarberTemp is an "employment business," an "employment agency," or a pure marketplace depends on how the T&Cs and payment flows are structured. This is a deliberate design decision to make BEFORE the platform T&Cs are finalised, not a compliance afterthought. Get a UK employment-law opinion.
- **Worker status:** self-employed barbers on day rates suit the model, but the platform must avoid drifting into control indicators (setting rates, requiring exclusivity, disciplinary mechanics) that recharacterise the relationship.
- **NEW: the EU Platform Work Directive** (in force Dec 2024, member-state transposition due Dec 2026) regulates algorithmic management of platform workers: transparency about automated matching, human review rights. The UK is outside it, but **Phase 3 is Dublin**, and the directive will be live law by then. Design the matching engine's transparency now; retrofits are expensive.
- **Insurance and liability:** a cover barber injuring a client in a host shop is the first bad headline. Public liability arrangements (platform-arranged or verified per barber) belong in the retention mechanic, not the fine print.
- Standing flags re-affirmed: Ambrion/Meridian branding off third-party investor documents pending Shane sign-off; trademark filing before any wider circulation.

## 4. AI Strategy & Governance Intelligence

- **NEW: the matching engine itself may be in-scope AI.** An AI system that allocates work opportunities to workers sits close to the EU AI Act's high-risk employment category (Annex III) and to the Platform Work Directive's algorithmic-management rules. UK-first launch keeps it out of EU scope initially, but the Dublin phase brings it in. The house expertise (the UHL EU AI Act paper) transfers directly: BarberTemp should be designed deployer-clean from day one. ## 5. Business Growth & Transformation Intelligence
- **The pilot IS the product discovery.** Twenty manual bookings measure the four numbers that decide everything: repeat-booking rate, leakage rate (who goes direct after booking one), fill time, and both-sides willingness to pay. Track on one sheet.
- **Build the retention mechanic before the app:** in-app payment, guaranteed cover (a backup barber if one cancels), dispute handling, verified insurance. That bundle, not features, is the answer to disintermediation, and the pilot will show which piece matters most.
