COMMERCIAL INTELLIGENCE

The Irish landscape in 2026

Ireland hosts one of Europe's densest concentrations of hyperscale and colocation data centre capacity. Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon AWS, and Apple have all established significant operational footprints, predominantly in Greater Dublin -- Grange Castle, Clonshaugh, Mulhuddart, Ballycoolin, and Leopardstown. Combined, hyperscalers account for well over 1GW of contracted or operational capacity across the island. Colocation operators are equally significant: Equinix operates multiple IBX campuses in Dublin; Digital Realty (via Interxion) runs Park West and Ballycoolin facilities; Echelon has developed large-scale campuses at Clondalkin and Arklow. Irish-owned operators including Dataplex and Viatel serve enterprise and public sector clients at a smaller scale.

The Dublin connection position -- corrected

The CRU replaced the blanket Dublin moratorium in December 2025 with conditional connections: new applicants must pass localised constraint tests and commit to an 80 percent renewables obligation. Dublin is not fully closed, but the bar is materially higher and the timeline for compliant connection is longer. The structural bias toward regional sites -- where grid headroom is available, renewables are accessible, and land is cheaper -- remains intact. The constraint has changed in form, not in commercial effect.

The AI compute supercycle

Since 2023, AI training and inference workloads have transformed what data centre operators need. GPU-dense racks demand 50 to 100 kW per rack versus the 8 to 15 kW standard for traditional cloud compute. This forces demand for liquid cooling, high-power distribution infrastructure, and purpose-built AI facilities. Hyperscalers are signing 10 to 20 year leases, and lead times for new AI-optimised facilities are 3 to 5 years, creating acute near-term supply pressure across Western Europe with Ireland a primary target. Wholesale powered-shell lease rates in Dublin trade at EUR 10 to 14 per watt of IT load annually. Greenfield sites for data centre use are transacting at EUR 500,000 to EUR 1.5 million per acre depending on grid proximity and planning status.

Who is buying and building

Active buyers in Ireland in 2025 to 2026 include Microsoft, Google, and emerging AI-native operators seeking wholesale colocation capacity. Regional sites trade at a 15 to 25 percent discount to Dublin rates, but that gap is closing rapidly as Dublin alternatives become the only realistic option for operators unwilling to meet the new Dublin renewables conditions.

AI STRATEGY INTELLIGENCE

How data centres are actually managed in 2026

The leading hyperscalers have moved decisively from human-supervised to AI-driven operations. This is production-grade practice, not aspiration.

Google DeepMind's cooling AI uses reinforcement learning to control HVAC systems in real time, delivering 30 to 40 percent reductions in cooling energy and a 15 percent reduction in overall power usage effectiveness. Microsoft operates AI systems across Azure facilities for power load forecasting, cooling optimisation, and generator scheduling. Meta's network AI handles traffic engineering and failure rerouting autonomously, reducing human intervention in network incidents by an estimated 70 percent. AWS uses machine learning for capacity planning and predictive physical provisioning.

Staffing ratios have inverted. Where 2015-era data centres required approximately one engineer per 1,000 servers for routine management, AI-driven hyperscale facilities now operate at closer to one engineer per 5,000 to 10,000 servers for monitoring and exception handling. Physical presence is required primarily for hardware swap, structured cabling, and regulatory inspection.

The Irish mid-tier gap

Irish mid-tier and colocation operators remain materially behind. They typically manage 40 to 60 percent of operational decisions manually. AI-powered cooling optimisation platforms from Schneider Electric and Vertiv are commercially available, but capital cost and integration complexity remain barriers. Predictive maintenance tools are available but automated incident response is largely absent. Security AI is the most mature area.

The Velocity AI relevance

Shane's Velocity AI analysis is itself a demonstration of what AI-enabled spatial intelligence delivers: 289 transmission stations mapped against 580 industrial parcels, scored on five dimensions, independently verified, and produced faster and at a fraction of the cost of a traditional property consultant's site survey. The same principle -- AI doing the heavy analytical work, human expertise providing ground truth and relationships -- is exactly what the managed operations service layer would offer to data centre operators. The analysis proves the model works.

The opportunity

A managed AI operations service -- combining cooling optimisation, predictive maintenance alerting, and capacity planning intelligence -- positioned for Irish colocation and enterprise operators who cannot build these capabilities in-house is a serviceable proposition. The ROI case is straightforward: energy savings of 15 to 30 percent on the largest operational cost line create a board-level business case without needing to sell on technology alone.

PROPERTY INTELLIGENCE

The Velocity AI dataset: what Shane has built

The Grid-Proximity Land Analysis produced by Shane on 2 July 2026 is a significant asset. It maps every industrial-zoned parcel in the Republic of Ireland within 1,000 metres of the national transmission grid. The dataset:

- 289 transmission stations mapped (5 x 400kV, 50 x 220kV, 234 x 110kV)
- 580 zoned parcels within 1,000 metres of the grid
- 308 of those within 500 metres
- 39 prime non-Dublin sites identified and scored

Every parcel is scored out of 100 across proximity, voltage level, zoning strength, and scale, with the CRU Dublin connection policy applied as a discount. The dataset was independently verified by a second model pass -- every top-ranked station cross-checked against EirGrid project records, CRU records, and ESB Networks capacity data.

The model validated itself independently. It ranked the Bracetown-Clonee corridor in Co. Meath as the number one site nationally -- before it was known that Meta chose that exact corridor for its Irish data centre campus, 330 to 460 metres from Bracetown 220kV station. That is the strongest possible validation a scoring model can receive.

The site origination play

The value in this market is not conventional property agency. The Velocity AI dataset provides the scoring layer. Malachy provides the ground truth and access. This layer can be positioned as a managed service, a consulting engagement, or an equity stake in an operator that adopts it.

The combined position
