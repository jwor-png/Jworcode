# GRANT AGENCY REGISTRATION PLAN
## Meridian Intelligence · Ambrion AI · Velocity AI

*Prepared 5 August 2026 on John's instruction that all three brands be registered with every suitable grant-aid agency. Scheme rules verified against source this week. Where a route is closed or uncertain, it is stated plainly rather than assumed.*

## CORRECTION TO THE PRICING DOCUMENT, FIRST

The pricing paper issued earlier today recommended pricing the Decision Brief at €4,950 so it would sit inside a €5,000 Enterprise Ireland Innovation Voucher. **Two things in that were wrong and the recommendation does not stand.**

**One. The voucher is now up to €10,000, not €5,000.** A fully funded €10,000 standard voucher is available, alongside co-funded vouchers covering project costs up to €20,000 where the company contributes 50 per cent.

**Two, and this is the one that matters. Meridian cannot become a registered knowledge provider.** Innovation Voucher knowledge providers are publicly funded research organisations: universities, institutes of technology including the seventeen Technology Gateways, and other state research bodies. The scheme exists specifically to connect private SMEs *to* public research. A private consultancy sits on the wrong side of that relationship by design.

**What this does not change.** The Decision Brief price stands at €4,950 on its own merits, because it sits inside a managing director's signing authority. That was always the stronger reason. The voucher was a bonus and the bonus is not available directly.

**What it opens instead.** Meridian can work *with* a registered knowledge provider rather than as one. See route four below.

## THE HARD BLOCKER, BEFORE ANY OF THIS

**Every registration on this list requires a legal entity with a CRO number, a tax clearance certificate, and in most cases professional indemnity insurance.**

Ambrion's incorporation is in progress. Until it completes, Ambrion cannot be registered with Skillnet, with a LEO panel, or with Enterprise Ireland. That is not a governance concern, it is a scheduling one, and it puts incorporation on the critical path to funding rather than merely to tidiness. Skillnet is Ambrion's genuine revenue channel and the compliance campaign is live now, so the sooner the CRO number exists the sooner that channel opens.

**Two further items that need settling for Meridian at the same time:**

- **Professional indemnity insurance.** Meridian gives written advice that boards act on. It needs PI cover regardless of grant registration, and most panels require evidence of it before they will consider an application. This is a gap today.
- **Meridian's own legal entity and the 70/30 shareholders agreement.** Still undrawn.

## THE ROUTES, IN ORDER OF FIT

### Route 1. Local Enterprise Office mentor panels — the best fit, and open now

**What it is.** Each of the 31 Local Enterprise Offices maintains a panel of experienced business practitioners who deliver mentoring to small business owners in their county. Panels are procured openly and applications are generally accepted on a rolling basis through the life of a panel.

**Why it fits John personally, better than anything else on this list.** The panels are looking for exactly what he is: decades of practical board and commercial experience, not academic credentials. A serving PLC chair and an audit and risk committee chair is an unusually strong application.

**Be clear-eyed about what it is worth.** LEO mentoring is heavily subsidised and pays modestly. **This is an acquisition channel, not a revenue line.** Its value is that it puts John in a room with owner-managers who are making exactly the decisions Meridian sells against, with the State making the introduction and vouching for him. A proportion of those become Decision Brief clients at full rate afterwards. That is the return, not the mentoring fee.

**Which brand.** Meridian, for business and commercial mentoring. Ambrion once incorporated, for AI and EU AI Act mentoring, which will be in heavy demand from 2 August.

**Action.** Apply to Meath and Louth first as home counties, then Dublin City, Fingal and Kildare. Applications are per LEO. Watch for panel calls; several run tranches with cut-off dates.

### Route 2. Enterprise Ireland Mentor Network and Mentor Grant

**What it is.** The same shape as the LEO panels but at Enterprise Ireland level, working with larger client companies rather than micro-enterprises. The Mentor Grant supports EI client companies to bring in external expertise.

**Why it fits.** The client size is closer to Meridian's actual target, which is €5m to €100m turnover. An EI client company facing a strategic decision is a Meridian buyer in every respect.

**Which brand.** Meridian.

**Action.** Apply to join the Mentor Network. Verify current intake process directly with Enterprise Ireland.

### Route 3. Skillnet Ireland — the strongest fit for Ambrion, not for Meridian

**What it is.** Skillnet Ireland is the national workforce development agency, operating through **68 Skillnet Business Networks** nationwide. It is not a single central register. Training providers engage network by network, and each network manager sources programmes for their member companies. Funding works as joint investment: a government grant plus the company's own contribution.

**Why it matters commercially.** Skillnet subsidy materially reduces what the client pays for training-shaped delivery. **This is a genuine revenue channel, not just acquisition**, and it is already in play: the Skillnet offset was live on the United Hardware programme.

**Which brand, and this is the important allocation.** Skillnet funds *training*. Meridian does not sell training, it sells decision briefs, so Skillnet is largely irrelevant to Meridian and should not be chased there. **Skillnet belongs to Ambrion**, and it is the natural funding mechanism for the three AI products moving across: AI in Plain English, AI Governance Readiness Assessment, AI Readiness Accelerator. Velocity's Migration Education tiers are also training-shaped and would qualify.

**Action.** Target networks by relevance rather than applying blanket. Priority networks to approach: the technology and digital networks, the management and leadership networks, and any network whose members are SMEs now inside EU AI Act scope. Contact network managers directly. **Ambrion cannot do this until it is incorporated.**

### Route 4. Innovation Voucher, as a partner rather than a provider

**What it is.** Meridian cannot hold knowledge provider status. But a client company can take a €10,000 voucher, engage a university or Technology Gateway as the registered provider, and Meridian can be involved in framing the question and applying the commercial judgment around the technical work.

**Why it is worth exploring.** The Technology Gateways in particular are practical and industry-facing rather than purely academic. A relationship with one or two of them is a route to co-delivering work Meridian could not access alone, and a source of referrals in both directions.

**UNVERIFIED and must be checked before relying on it.** Whether a knowledge provider may subcontract or partner on voucher work, and on what terms. Scheme rules on this are strict and this could turn out to be closed. Do not put it in front of a client until confirmed.

**Action.** Approach one or two Technology Gateways in relevant domains and ask the question directly.

### Route 5. InterTradeIreland — worth checking, given the UK dimension

**What it is.** The cross-border trade and business development body, operating consultant registers for its business support programmes.

**Why it is relevant.** Barber Republic's rollout is London and Manchester. Meridian's stated market is Irish *and UK* mid-market. Cross-border and Ireland-to-UK expansion decisions are precisely Meridian's product.

**Which brand.** Meridian.

**Action.** Verify current programmes and whether consultant registration is open. Treat as second wave, after the LEO applications are in.

### Route 6. Client-side grants, which are worth more than provider registration

The routes above register *you*. These reduce what the *client* pays, which removes the price objection at the point of sale.

- **LEO grants and vouchers**, which vary by office and by scheme, some of which can be applied to external business advice.
- **Enterprise Ireland Digital Transition Fund and digitalisation supports**, relevant to Ambrion and Velocity rather than Meridian.
- **Regional Skills Fora**, for training-shaped delivery alongside Skillnet.

**The practical point.** Knowing which grant a given client can draw on, and telling them so unprompted, is worth more in a sales conversation than any registration Meridian holds. It reframes the fee from a cost into a co-funded investment, and it makes Meridian look like it is on the client's side of the table. **Build a one-page internal crib sheet of what each client type can claim, and keep it current.**

**Read the pattern.** Meridian's routes are acquisition routes. They put John in front of the right people with the State's endorsement, and the money comes afterwards at full rate. Ambrion's and Velocity's routes are revenue routes, because they sell training and training is what these agencies actually fund.

That is not a weakness in Meridian. It is a consequence of selling judgment rather than programmes, and judgment is not a fundable category. It does mean Meridian should not be built on an assumption of grant-subsidised fees.
