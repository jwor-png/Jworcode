## TOP FIVE OPPORTUNITIES — CORE GAS SUPPLY

### 1. The online-ordering gap in bottled gas — the sharpest, most immediate opportunity

Both major competitors have built genuine digital self-service — but only for bulk-tank customers. Flogas UK's "My Account" portal lets bulk customers order online, pay by card, view invoices and see estimated days of gas remaining. [VERIFIED — flogas.co.uk] Calor UK's "Calor Account Online" does the same for bulk ("On Demand") customers — but cylinder/bottled-gas customers are explicitly routed to a retailer-finder tool to buy in person at a local stockist instead. [VERIFIED — calor.co.uk] **Neither major offers real online ordering for the cylinder customer specifically.** Stargas's own site already has a basic "buy online, collect at Stargas agents" feature — a genuine head start to build on, not a gap to close from zero. [REPORTED — stargas.ie] The clearest near-term win: extend this into a proper cylinder-focused ordering and reminder service (even simple "time to reorder" prompts based on typical cylinder life, short of full telemetry) — a real, evidenced differentiator against both multinationals in exactly the segment they've left underserved.

### 2. Autogas (LPG as vehicle fuel) — live, under-promoted, infrastructure already exists

Autogas is available at 1,400+ refuelling sites across Ireland already, and Calor actively markets LPG vehicle conversions under its own "Autogas Ireland" offer — confirming at least one major incumbent sees a live commercial case. [REPORTED — myLPG.eu, calorgas.ie] No confirmed Irish government incentive (grant, VRT, BIK) specific to LPG vehicles was found in this pass — worth confirming directly with Revenue or SEAI before this goes into anything client-facing as an incentive claim, since Ireland's current published grant focus is EVs, not autogas. Carbon Tax now applies across all road fuels including auto-LPG and is rising toward €100/tonne by 2030, which affects the whole category, not just LPG. [VERIFIED — Revenue.ie, RTÉ Budget 2026 coverage] **The opportunity is real but under-promoted nationally** — a distributor willing to actively push conversions and autogas availability at partner stockists/forecourts, rather than waiting for demand, has room to move in a category the majors are marketing but not majoring on.

### 3. Off-grid rural and agricultural heating — the largest addressable base, already Stargas's natural territory

Only around 700,000 of Ireland's roughly 2 million homes are connected to the national gas grid — a large, structural off-grid base for which LPG is a genuine heating solution, not a niche one. [VERIFIED — SEAI National Heat Study] Named agricultural use cases both majors actively market: poultry-shed heating (litter dryness, disease control), calf-shed heating in rearing season, grain drying, and dairy-farm hot water. [REPORTED — Calor, Flogas NI marketing pages] No independent (non-supplier) data confirms measured *growth* in agricultural LPG demand — these are currently competitor marketing claims, not proven trend data, and should be treated as directional encouragement rather than a cited growth statistic. **The opportunity for Stargas:** this is core territory already, not a new market — the value is in naming these specific agricultural use cases explicitly in Stargas's own marketing and stockist conversations (grain drying season, calving season) rather than generic "bottled gas" messaging, matching what Calor and Flogas already do deliberately.

### 4. Leisure and campervan/motorhome LPG — a genuinely growing segment with reasonable evidence behind it

Roughly 20,000 campervans and 30,000 caravans are in use in Ireland as of 2024, caravan numbers up around 12% since 2019, with growth accelerating post-pandemic (2–3% annual growth pre-pandemic, rising to 4.5% in 2021–22) — over a third of campervans currently in use were registered since 2020. [REPORTED — CamperChamp Ireland statistics, corroborated qualitatively by Irish Times coverage of the post-pandemic campervan boom] These figures come from a market-aggregator, not the CSO — worth a follow-up check against CSO vehicle registrations or the Irish Caravan and Camping Council if a hard number is needed for anything formal, but the direction is well-corroborated across sources. This is a genuine, evidenced growth segment for cylinder-exchange demand, and one neither major appears to specifically target in marketing — a clean opening for Stargas to name explicitly (campsite/caravan-park stockist partnerships, campervan-specific cylinder sizes/exchange points).

### 5. Industrial and welding gases — the natural adjacent line using infrastructure Stargas already has

The Irish industrial gases/welding gas market (oxygen, acetylene, argon shielding gas for metal fabrication and welding) is characterised as mature and stable, with steady demand tied to Ireland's manufacturing base. [REPORTED — IndexBox market analysis, a paid aggregator — top-line figures not independently verified in this pass] No hard Irish market sizing is available without a paid report, so any figure quoted to Anthony should be caveated as directional. **The opportunity:** Stargas already fills and distributes industrial gases per its own stated product range — this is about deliberately growing that line using the same filling depot, vehicle fleet and stockist relationships built for LPG, rather than a genuinely new capability. Lowest-effort, most infrastructure-efficient item on this list.

## SUMMARY FOR ANTHONY

Ranked by combination of evidence strength and ease of near-term action: **(1) the online-ordering gap** is the sharpest opportunity — real, evidenced, and buildable on what Stargas already has; **(3) off-grid/agricultural heating** is the largest base and already core territory, needing sharper marketing rather than new capability; **(5) industrial/welding gases** is the lowest-effort adjacent-line growth using existing infrastructure; **(4) campervan/leisure LPG** is a genuinely growing, under-targeted segment worth a deliberate push; **(2) autogas** is real but has the most open questions (no confirmed incentive) and would need that confirmed before building a pitch around it.
