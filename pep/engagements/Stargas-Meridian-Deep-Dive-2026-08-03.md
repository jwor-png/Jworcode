## 2. THE CYLINDER TESTING OPPORTUNITY — THE CORE OF ANTHONY'S AMBITION

**The regulatory anchor.** EN 1440 is the applicable European standard for periodic inspection of refillable welded/brazed steel LPG cylinders (0.5–150 litre water capacity) — Stargas's core stock. [VERIFIED — BSI/EN standard listings] A successor standard, EN 16728, is in development. [VERIFIED] The directive that actually governs LPG cylinders as transportable pressure equipment is the **Transportable Pressure Equipment Directive (2010/35/EU, TPED)** — not the Pressure Equipment Directive most people default to, which covers *stationary* equipment only. This is worth getting right in any client-facing material, since it is the kind of precision detail that signals real expertise. [VERIFIED — TÜV SÜD, EUR-Lex]

**The competitive field is thin — this is the finding that matters most.** Independent research identified at most two named independent Irish cylinder test houses:
- **Cylinder Testing Services**, which explicitly markets itself as **"Ireland's only independent cylinder testing facility,"** offering testing, refurbishment, transport, storage and filling, with inspectors trained to UK/EU legislation. [VERIFIED as to the claim being made — cylindertestingservices.ie; the underlying accuracy of "only" is the company's own marketing claim, not independently adjudicated]
- **Industrial Pressure Testing Ltd**, testing transportable seamless steel, aluminium and composite cylinders since 1995, an IDEST test house since 2006 with UKAS-accredited inspectors — but this firm reads as oriented toward diving/breathing-air and industrial gas cylinders, and it is **not confirmed whether they test LPG cylinders specifically**. [VERIFIED presence; UNVERIFIED LPG scope]

**The open question worth settling before anything else.** Whether Irish LPG cylinders currently go abroad (e.g. to the UK or Netherlands, given SHV's and DCC's group structures) for testing/requalification, or whether the majors test only their own fleets in-house, was not confirmed in this research. This is the single fact that would tell Anthony whether he is looking at a genuine open gap or a market the majors have already closed off internally. **Recommend asking Anthony directly** — he may already know the answer from industry contacts — rather than presenting this as settled either way.

**What "best equipment in Ireland" actually requires.** Standard practice for steel LPG cylinder requalification under EN 1440 is visual/internal examination plus a pressure test (commonly hydrostatic, with acoustic emission or ultrasonic methods used as approved alternatives in some jurisdictions) — this is standard industry practice but the exact method mix should be confirmed against the primary EN 1440 text or HSA guidance before it goes into anything client-facing as a technical claim. [UNVERIFIED against primary text in this pass] Separately, and materially: **no Irish national accreditation body (INAB) was confirmed to accredit LPG cylinder test houses** — UK practice relies on UKAS/IDEST accreditation, and whether Irish operators (including the two named above) rely on UK-based accreditation or something else was not established. This matters directly to Anthony's ambition, because "the best equipment" is only half the claim — the other half is who certifies that the equipment and the process meet the standard, and that answer needs to be nailed down before any investment case is built or any grant application references it.

**Third-party/contract testing as the actual business opportunity.** The evidence supports a genuine gap: a thin field of named independents, one of them explicitly claiming to be the only one. If Stargas builds testing capability to the standard Anthony wants, the natural extension is not just testing Stargas's own cylinders but **offering contract testing/requalification services to other Irish distributors, stockists and industrial gas users** — a second, recurring revenue line built on top of infrastructure Stargas would need anyway for its own fleet. This is the clearest "new opportunity in the supply of gas" available to Anthony that isn't really about gas supply at all — it's about becoming critical infrastructure for the whole sector.

**The trade mechanics — realistic, not simple.** Under the Windsor Framework, goods moving from the Republic into Northern Ireland for final sale within NI can generally use the "green lane" via the UK Internal Market Scheme (UKIMS), avoiding full customs declarations — provided the mover is UKIMS-authorised. ## 4. EUROPEAN CONTEXT

**The regulatory framework, precisely.** LPG cylinders are governed by TPED (2010/35/EU), not PED — this distinction is worth Anthony's team getting right, since it is the kind of detail a genuine expert gets right and others don't. EN 1440 (periodic inspection) and ADR (carriage of dangerous goods by road) sit underneath TPED. Under ADR, LPG is a Category 2 dangerous good with a 333kg small-load threshold before hazard-warning plating and driver ADR-certification requirements apply — relevant to how Stargas plans any expanded distribution logistics. [VERIFIED — HSE.gov.uk, FETA guidance, TÜV SÜD]

**Green gas at EU level.** Beyond BioLPG, **rDME (renewable dimethyl ether)** is chemically similar to propane/butane and storable/transportable like LPG — a second renewable "drop-in" option worth Stargas being aware of even if not immediately actionable. Liquid Gas UK (the UK trade body, an equivalent forum to LGI) has published modelling on renewable liquid gas supply and heating-demand pathways for both BioLPG and rDME. Liquid Gas Europe is the EU-level trade body for the sector — a plausible network entry point if Stargas wants visibility on where the renewable-gas conversation in Europe is heading. [VERIFIED — Liquid Gas UK, Liquid Gas Europe] No EU funding line (Horizon Europe, Interreg) specifically targeted at bio-LPG/rDME cylinder distribution was found in this pass — this should not be presented to Anthony as a live funding opportunity without further, more targeted searching of the EU Funding & Tenders Portal. [UNVERIFIED]

**Market size — use with real caution.** Commercial forecasters give wildly divergent figures for the UK and European LPG market (growth estimates ranging from roughly 1.2% to nearly 13% CAGR depending on the vendor), and none of the figures found are specific to cylinder distribution or cylinder testing services — they cover the whole LPG market including bulk, autogas and chemical feedstock use. **Recommend not quoting any single market-size figure to Anthony** without naming the source and its limits; the honest position is that no clean, cylinder-specific market figure exists in open sources.
