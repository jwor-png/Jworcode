AI Governance Readiness Checklist

A practical self-assessment for Irish business leaders. Understand where your organisation stands today, where the gaps are, and what to prioritise before the next phase of EU AI Act obligations takes effect.

EU AI Act — Next phase: 2 August 2026

How to use this

Work through each section honestly. Mark each item with one of the four responses below. A gap is not a failure — it is a priority. The value of this checklist is in what it surfaces, not what it confirms.

Awareness and Inventory
Do you know where AI is already operating in your business?
Question Yes Partial No Don't know
1.1We have identified all AI tools currently in use across the business, including tools embedded in software we already use
1.2We know which decisions AI is influencing or making in our business right now
1.3We have a named person responsible for understanding our AI footprint
1.4Leadership and the board have a basic understanding of what AI is doing in the business

Governance Structure
Is there a structure in place to oversee how AI is used?
Question Yes Partial No Don't know
2.1We have a clear policy or set of principles governing how AI is used in our organisation
2.2There is a named person or team accountable for AI governance
2.3Our board or senior leadership team receives regular updates on AI use and risk
2.4We have a process for approving new AI tools before they are deployed
2.5We have a process for reviewing AI tools already in use
3
Risk and Compliance
Are you managing the risks AI introduces?
Question Yes Partial No Don't know
3.1We have assessed which of our AI uses fall under the EU AI Act (high risk, limited risk, or minimal risk)
3.2We understand our obligations under the EU AI Act as they apply to our business
3.3We have reviewed our data protection obligations in the context of our AI use (GDPR)
3.4We have a process for identifying and logging AI-related incidents or errors
3.5We have considered the reputational, legal, and operational risks of our current AI use
4
Data
Is the data powering your AI trustworthy and handled correctly?
Question Yes Partial No Don't know
4.1We know what data our AI tools are using and where it comes from
4.2We have lawful basis for the data being processed by our AI systems
4.3We have assessed whether our data is accurate, current, and representative
4.4We know whether our data or outputs are being used to train third-party AI models
5
Human Oversight
Are humans appropriately in the loop?
Question Yes Partial No Don't know
5.1Decisions with significant consequences for people are reviewed by a human before action is taken
5.2Staff know when they are working with AI-generated outputs and understand their limits
5.3There is a clear escalation path when AI produces an unexpected or concerning output
5.4We do not treat AI output as fact without verification
6
Team Capability
Can your people work responsibly with AI?
Question Yes Partial No Don't know
6.1Our team has received basic training on AI tools and their limitations
6.2Staff understand what they can and cannot use AI for within our policies
6.3Leadership understands enough about AI to make informed decisions about it
6.4We have a plan to keep team capability current as AI evolves
7
Transparency and Trust
Are you building appropriate confidence with clients and stakeholders?
Question Yes Partial No Don't know
7.1Where AI is used in client-facing work, we are transparent about it
7.2We have considered how to communicate our AI governance approach to clients and stakeholders
7.3We can demonstrate, if asked, that our AI use is responsible and compliant
