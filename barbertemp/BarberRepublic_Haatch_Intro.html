The Problem

The UK barbering industry is worth over £1 billion a year, and it runs on a structural inefficiency nobody has solved. Every barbershop is two barbers away from serious trouble: a holiday, a sick day, or a barber simply taking a day off means empty chairs and lost revenue that cannot be recovered. On the other side, most barbers today are self-employed and have days they want to work with no guaranteed place to go. The shop has an empty chair. A skilled barber wants to fill it. No platform connects them in real time.

Working from home and flexible work patterns have broken the predictable busy/quiet cycles that barbershops relied on for decades. Walk-in traffic is less reliable. Permanent staff scheduling has become harder. Emergency cover is now a permanent operational need—and the industry has no solution.

The Solution

BarberRepublic is the labour exchange for the barbering industry. Barbers post their available days, tier and day rate. Shops post open chairs. The platform matches them in minutes, both sides confirm, the barber shows up, the work gets done, payment goes through the app, and both sides rate the experience. Emergency cover carries a surge premium; the shop keeps revenue it would have lost; and the barber earns more when needed most.

Barbers are matched by a four-tier skill system (Barber, Barber Pro, Super Pro, Super Pro Hair) so every shop gets the right skill and every barber earns at the correct rate. The platform is AI-native; intelligent matching, demand prediction and platform intelligence are designed in from the ground up, so the system gets smarter with every booking and builds a proprietary data layer that a copied app would not have.

Our retention mechanic is built on guarantee: guaranteed work for the barber, guaranteed cover for the shop, verified barbers, payment rails, and platform rating. That's what stops disintermediation—not a high switching cost, but real utility on both sides.

Market and Validation

Unfirst. Unproof of concept. The UK market exceeds £1 billion annually. In summer 2026 OpenChair launched publicly in the United States, an app allowing barbers and stylists to find and rent chairs nationwide, confirming demand for real-time chair-and-barber matching. No equivalent labour exchange platform exists in the UK. The first-mover window is open now.

We are past aspirational validation. Johnny Shanahan has been in direct contact with his colleagues across London barbershops. The response has been consistently positive. These are experienced operators—and they're signalling real demand for exactly what we're building. Soft expressions of interest from founding barbers and barbershops in London demonstrate willingness-to-pay and confirm that our unit economics work for both sides.

Unit Economics

Barber perspective:
2 shifts per week through BarberRepublic at average £180 per shift yields approximately £1,440 monthly gross. Super-professionals can condense their client base into 3 days, freeing 2 days for travel and platform bookings. Guarantee of work through the platform matters more than marginal leakage risk.
Shop perspective:
Emergency cover at £250 per shift is easily acceptable on a once-off basis. A fully booked premium shop revenues approximately £550 per day; the £250 emergency fill is much better than the alternative of losing the customer. Emergency cover is a reactive problem-solver, not a profit center.
Platform perspective:
Commission-based model on cover bookings. Break-even occurs at approximately 900-1,500 bookings per month (illustrative 12-15% take rate at current day rates). Clear path to profitability as booking volume grows.
