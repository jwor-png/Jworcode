2. WHAT THE DEEP ANALYSIS FOUND - THE STRENGTHS

I ran BarberRepublic through a full multi-domain review: commercial, legal, financial, technical and growth. The strengths first:

- The market gap is real. OpenChair's US launch proves appetite for real-time chair and barber matching, and no equivalent labour exchange exists in the UK. The first mover window is open.
- Your unit economics hold up on both sides. Barber: two shifts a week at an average 180 per shift is roughly 1,440 a month through the app. Shop: 250 for emergency cover against a 550 fully booked day is an easy yes compared to losing the customer. Platform: at an illustrative 12 to 15 percent commission, break-even sits around 900 to 1,500 bookings a month, which the London launch is scoped to prove.
- Your direct contact with London operators is the strongest card we hold. Experienced shop owners responding consistently positively is a real proof point, and every signed LOI hardens it.
- The retention answer is guarantee, not lock-in: guaranteed work for the barber, guaranteed cover for the shop, verified pool, in-app payment, ratings and dispute handling. Against that bundle, the saving from going direct is marginal and the risk moves back onto the shop. This answers the first question every investor asks about marketplaces: why will people not go around you?

3. WHAT THE DEEP ANALYSIS FOUND - THE ITEMS WE MANAGE

The same review flagged the risks, and each one has a management plan rather than a hope:

- Employment status and liability. A platform supplying temp labour to UK businesses may fall within employment agency regulation, and how we structure the T&Cs and payment flows decides whether we are a marketplace or an employment business. This is a design decision to make BEFORE the build, not a compliance afterthought. You flagged you will seek advice; this is the single most important pre-build workstream and it must not lag the build.
- Worker status. Self-employed barbers on day rates suit the model, but the platform must avoid controls (setting rates, requiring exclusivity, disciplinary mechanics) that would recharacterise the relationship. The design already reflects this: barbers set their own availability, tier and rate.
- Insurance. A cover barber injuring a client in a host shop is the first bad headline. Verified public liability per barber belongs in the platform offer itself, part of the guarantee bundle, not the small print.
- The pilot is the product discovery, not a delay. Twenty real bookings measure the four numbers that decide everything: repeat-booking rate, leakage rate (who goes direct after booking one), fill time, and willingness to pay on both sides. Those four numbers, tracked on one sheet, are exactly what Haatch asked to see, plus weeks live, usage and adoption.
- Valuation framing. The Year 5 ambition of 100m to 150m is presented as a planning ambition grounded in marketplace comparables, with the near-term case resting on London proof. That framing survives investor scrutiny; harder claims do not.
- Future regulation is a selling point, not a threat. The EU platform work rules and AI rules coming through mean the matching engine should be built transparent and audit-ready from day one. UK launch keeps us outside them initially, but Dublin in Phase 3 brings them in, and arriving already clean is a differentiator no competitor will match.

4. THE FUNDING PLAN - THREE LEGS

Leg 1: Your private funds get us moving now.
You mentioned you may have private funding available. The structure I propose we design gives your funders a clean choice, and there is an important tax dimension to put in front of them:
- Funders who want equity upside can come in under an Advance Subscription Agreement (ASA): their money converts to shares at an agreed basis, it cannot be repaid, and because of that it can qualify for SEIS or EIS tax relief, which for UK taxpayers is worth up to 50 percent (SEIS) or 30 percent (EIS) of their investment back in income tax relief. That materially de-risks their cheque.
- Funders who want a fixed return instead can come in under a convertible loan note with a coupon and a repayment option, knowing that route carries no SEIS or EIS relief because it is debt.
We can run both side by side and let each funder choose their lane. The company's own SEIS/EIS eligibility needs confirming, and both documents get papered by a UK solicitor and tax adviser before anyone signs. I will drive that piece.
