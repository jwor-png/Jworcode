US-oriented advice; the claims about specific tools are Johnson's and are not independently verified here.

## The checklist as posted

1. Set up a password manager (he names 1Password); give your spouse or partner access to a shared vault and teach them to use it.
2. Update passwords for critical accounts (email, bank and financial); store them in the manager; use the strongest password you can.
3. Use two-factor authentication for everything you can: SMS is not ideal though better than nothing; an authenticator app or a passkey is better; a hardware key is safest.
4. Add a SIM PIN to your phone (makes SIM-swapping harder).
5. Update your phone and computer to the newest releases and turn auto-updates on.
6. Freeze your credit (US: takes about five minutes).
7. Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN (US only).
8. If you use Claude Code or AI agents that browse the web, they probably use Node.js, which he says is also vulnerable: update Claude Code and Node on your machine.
9. If you build things on your computer, ask an AI assistant to audit your repos for vulnerabilities, and make it a daily scheduled task, "things are moving very quickly in the space right now".
10. "Light resiliency": printed copies of important documents (especially financial accounts); some cash on hand; shelf-stable food and clean water or a filter; a map; a generator that can at least power your fridge or some devices; cheap insurance. His framing: have a Secure plan, a Comfortable plan and a Wealthy plan, and the secure plan now needs to factor cybersecurity risk as a "legit threat": "Take a weekend and shore yourself up."
