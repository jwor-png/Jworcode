# "Investment in data centres and AI is driving 20pc of growth in Irish economy", John Mulligan, Business p.28, newspaper (John's photo, 19 Nov 2026; paper name not shown). Report by Goodbody (Irish Economy Health Check). Figures as reported, UNVERIFIED.

- AI and data centre investment accounts for about a fifth (20pc) of the increase in Ireland's modified domestic demand (MDD) in the year to Q2 2026. Imports of AI and data centre technology equipment used for capital investment reached 11.4bn euro in the 12 months to end of Q2 2026: 59pc of modified machinery and equipment investment.
- Ireland's AI and data centre hardware trade (semiconductors, processors, servers) has more than doubled since 2019, reaching 40bn euro by end of August; Asia supplies nearly a quarter of Irish goods imports (about an eighth in 2020). IMF: Ireland is among the top 15 global locations for exports of AI hardware. Caveat: the Irish Fiscal Advisory Council says semiconductor machinery imports may have distorted MDD; Goodbody says a "significant proportion" of the hardware imports flow into domestic investment.
- Operational data-centre capacity has doubled since 2019; planning permission exists for significant further growth to 2030, "but this does not guarantee delivery". Power availability, grid congestion and connection timelines increasingly constrain investment, particularly for larger AI campuses.
- Goodbody forecasts MDD growth 3.2pc this year, 3.5pc in 2027 and 3pc in 2028. Challenges: equipping the workforce for AI, infrastructure gaps (housing, energy, transport), staying attractive to investment. Favours saving windfall corporation tax. Housing: homes built at the fastest rate in Europe per head but a significant deficit remains.
