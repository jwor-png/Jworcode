## 1. ifac Food and Agribusiness Report 2026 — "Growth and pressure"
9th edition of ifac's annual report, launched at the report's own
launch event. **John Donoghue, chief executive, ifac:** this year's
theme is "Ready or Not," reflecting businesses moving forward "with a
mixture of confidence and caution" amid record cost pressures. Key
findings:
- **65% of food and agribusinesses** are optimistic about growth over
  the next 12 months.
- **91% intend to grow** over the next 12 months, but only **38%**
  actually increased net profit over the past year — reflecting rising
  labour, energy and other compliance costs squeezing margins even
  where revenue grows.
- Profitability described as under sustained pressure: "After a period
  of caution, Ireland's food and agribusinesses are sharpening again,"
  with 80% of businesses reporting faster change than the structures
  they have in place to manage it.
- **Artificial intelligence** flagged as one of the clearest examples
  of this gap: while AI use has risen sharply (up from 21% three years
  ago to 48% now), **75% of businesses have nothing in place to govern
  employees' use of AI** — a significant AI-governance gap.
- **David Leydon (group head of agrifood consulting at ifac)** and
  **Professor Karina Pierce** (Head of UCD School of Agriculture and
  Food Science) gave the report's keynote interview, per the coverage,
  addressing succession/leadership transition and workforce
  challenges: 38% of businesses employ more people over the next year,
  but succession/leadership transition planning remains a "significant
  challenge" for the sector, with no clear succession plan identified
  as an important part of the sector's future in the report.
- Pierce's broader argument (as summarised): Ireland's agrifood
  competitiveness will depend not just on technical expertise but on
  preparing students/graduates on how to use AI-driven technology
  effectively alongside industry work — echoing themes already logged
  around AI-literacy and generational skills gaps.
- Full report available at ifac.ie/report (as printed).

## 2. AgrifoodTEF (Testing and Experimentation Facilities) network
**Kieran O'Donoghue** (outreach manager, AgriTech Cluster at MTU,
co-funding lead for the Irish node of the EU Digital Europe Programme)
describes AgrifoodTEF as a new AI/robotics testing-and-validation
service for agrifood SMEs — helping companies test, de-risk and
validate new technology (AI, robotics, digital tools) in real-world
farm/field conditions before market launch, rather than relying purely
on lab-based testing. Framing: "If an SME has an idea or a product that
is maybe not at the market yet, or a feature of an existing technology
that has some new technology that has yet to be tested... the services
are designed to test that idea and provide an independent evaluation."
- Backed by the **Irish AgriTech Cluster** (part of the European
  Network of Test and Experimentation Facilities, TEFs), MTU-based,
  with €160,000 in EU Digital Europe Programme funding secured; MTU
  and Teagasc/Lero co-lead the Irish node.
- **Two live projects cited:** a Terra NutriTech verification system
  establishing a dosing system for a liquid methane inhibitor for use
  on farms; a second project (details less clear) also in the
  10-project pipeline the node has approved so far.
- Positioned as bridging AI/robotics/ICT specialist services (via the
  IMaR Research Centre at MTU) with agrifood-specific compliance,
  standards and business validation testing — described as one of the
  network's clearest example use cases, since "many companies have
  limited R&D resources... we can access testing that they might not
  otherwise be able to do themselves."
- Positioned as the 10th node to join the wider European AgrifoodTEF
  network, aiming to generate technical/business evidence to move
  promising agrifood technologies closer to commercial deployment.
  More info: agrifoodtef.eu.
