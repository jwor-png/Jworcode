# Two reusable reference frameworks, 21 Oct 2026

## 1. "How to Value Any Business in 10 Minutes" — EduLaw/FinEdge

A one-page business valuation framework, five methods:

1. **Earnings Power** — EBITDA (operating profit before interest/tax/
   depreciation/amortisation), EBIT (after depreciation), PAT (profit
   after tax). Use sustainable earnings, not one-off spikes.
2. **EBITDA Multiple** — Enterprise Value = EBITDA × industry multiple.
   Common for established businesses; higher quality/stronger moat/
   better growth = higher multiple.
3. **Revenue Multiple** — EV = Revenue × sector multiple. Useful when
   profits are weak/inconsistent; common in high-growth/early-stage
   businesses.
4. **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)** — value = present value of future
   free cash flows; focus on cash generation, growth, discount rate;
   best for long-term intrinsic valuation.
5. **Asset-Based Valuation** — Net Asset Value = Total Assets − Total
   Liabilities; useful for asset-heavy businesses, common in
   manufacturing/real estate/liquidation cases.

**Key red flags listed:** profit growing but cash flow staying weak;
revenue rising with falling margins; heavy debt or repeated dilution;
customer concentration or unstable promoters.

**Five key questions to ask:** is earnings quality strong; what
multiple do peers get; is cash flow supporting profits; how much debt
is on the business; what can go wrong in the next three years.

**Relevance:** a genuinely useful quick-reference checklist across
several live threads — AHL's premises/Magna valuation questions,
Julianstown Mill's sale structuring, Barber Republic's investor-pack
work, and ODIN's own valuation gap (the $20m/20% vs $200m/20% Saudi
offer discrepancy already tracked). The red-flags list and five
questions are worth running against any of these directly.

## 2. "Top 20 Reasons Startups Fail to Reach Their First 50 Customers" — The Revenue Coaches

Estimated share of early-stage teams affected by each issue, grouped
into four categories:

**Buyer & Problem:** target customer too broad/vague, no usable ICP
(64%); no urgent problem buyers will prioritise (55%); product built
before testing demand with buyers (48%); trying to sell to too many
segments at once (42%); wrong person targeted, buyer cannot approve
(38%).

**Value & Offer:** no proof buyers will pay for the solution (52%);
positioning sounds interchangeable with competitors (51%); messaging
explains features not buyer outcomes (46%); no clear first offer easy
to say yes to (39%); price doesn't fit value/buyer economics (33%).

**Sales Conversation:** founder avoids selling to customers (44%);
technical founder cannot lead sales calls (40%); demo before
understanding the buyer's problem (36%); no agreed next step,
follow-up inconsistent (34%); pain not tied to cost/risk/business
priority (31%).

**Repeatable Acquisition:** chosen channels don't reach buyers with
trust (37%); activity scattered across too many channels (32%); wins
and losses don't improve the next sale (30%); likes/sign-ups mistaken
for customer demand (29%); hiring sales before founder can repeat the
sale (27%).

**Source note:** based on The Revenue Coaches' own stated experience
with "hundreds of startups and 25+ years," described as estimates,
not survey results — treat as a useful diagnostic lens, not verified
statistics.

**Relevance:** directly reinforces the Startup Channel Map and ReAble
Labs material already logged — the "founder avoids selling to
customers" and "technical founder cannot lead sales calls" lines are
particularly sharp against Meridian/Velocity AI/Ambrion AI's own
go-to-market, and the whole Buyer & Problem category is a useful
checklist for the ~200-company AI Adoption prospecting work.
