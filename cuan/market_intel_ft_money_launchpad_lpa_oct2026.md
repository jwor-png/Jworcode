# FT Money section batch, 3/4 October 2026

## 1. "The launchpad of mum and dad" (cover story) — Aliya Shibli

Source: FT Money cover story. VERIFIED — citing named founders and a specific Starling Bank research study.

**Core finding — new Starling Bank research on the role parents play in creating UK start-ups:**
- 38% of young founders were living at the family home when they launched their first business.
- A third said their parents provided services such as cooking meals and doing laundry so they could focus on the task at hand.
- A third used the parental home as a business address.
- Nearly half of SME founders received up to £10,000 in financial support from their parents.
- 74% of founders said they had bought a family member a gift, paid off a major debt, or contributed to their housing costs to thank them for support while starting their business.
- A quarter of entrepreneurs surveyed used their parents as a sounding board for ideas.

**Case studies:**
- **Chester Robinson** (27, philosophy/politics graduate) quit his full-time job, moved back home to build snack start-up **Raise**, inspired by his mother Oyinda's homemade granola. First pallet of product delivered to his mother's house ("I had no idea what a pallet looked like... we all unpacked the pallet box by box and put it into my mum's sitting room"). The whole family acted as couriers dropping off samples. Raise is now working with Steven Bartlett and Gary Neville after successfully pitching for investment on BBC's *Dragons' Den*; expects to turn over £1mn this year, still looking to break even.
- **Lee Denny** held his first festival ("LeeFest," later "Neverworld") in his parents' garden in 2006 at age 16, while they were on holiday — grew from 150 attendees to 300 crammed into his parents' 310 sq m garden the following year. After ~10 years, co-founded events company **Wildkind** in 2015, which turned over £3.5mn last year. His father still proudly helps out as "chief litter-picker" at Wildkind events today.
- **Emma Broom** set up **Opulence**, a wine-tasting/experiences business, from her parents' study in Kent four years ago; her parents now help her bottle wine, run ideas past her, and pack/label/spellcheck materials. "Even if I tried to [pay them], I don't think they would take it... they want to support me as much as they can."

**Pitching to the parents sidebar:** some entrepreneurial-rich families run a literal "Dragons' Den, but the dragons are your parents" process — children pitch ideas to family/family office staff for funding (per Fiona Graham, COO, Family Business UK). Maximilian Kunkel (UBS family office chief investment officer), citing UBS's 2025 Billionaire Ambitions report: 82% of billionaires surveyed want their children to follow their own path rather than relying solely on inherited wealth — the most valuable thing parents can offer is "the learning and failures that [they] had to go through."

**Context — why this matters now:** jobs website Adzuna reported this summer that advertised graduate roles fell 46% in a year, with total entry-level jobs down over 8%, partly blamed on higher employer National Insurance contributions and employers turning to AI to reduce costs — making the traditional career ladder harder to climb and increasing reliance on family-funded entrepreneurship as an alternative path. Small Business Britain (CEO Michelle Ovens) runs a six-week "mini MBA for micro businesses" programme; the Federation of Small Businesses has campaigned to triple the tax-free trading allowance threshold from £1,000 to £3,000 (government agreed to raise it by end of this parliament, no date yet set).

## 2. "Lasting Powers of Attorney complaints hit a record" — Aliya Shibli, FT News

Source: FT News, citing Ministry of Justice/Office of the Public Guardian data, Court of Protection figures, and named solicitors (Caroline Abrahams, Age UK; Stuart Downey, TWM Solicitors). VERIFIED.

- **Complaints about alleged financial misconduct by Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) holders have risen to a record level.** Complaints of financial mis-conduct rose to 11,910 in the year ending March 2026, up from 10,240 the previous 12 months and 9,538 the year before that.
- **263 attorneys had their authority revoked or partially revoked by the Court of Protection during the year**, per data obtained under a Freedom of Information request to the Ministry of Justice.
- An LPA enables someone to appoint a person they trust to make decisions on their behalf if they become unable to do so themselves, due to illness or loss of mental capacity. Two types: health/welfare, and property/financial affairs. Problems involving LPAs can include financial fraud or mismanagement, as well as unauthorised decisions relating to residential property.
- **Stuart Downey (partner, head of will/trust/estate disputes at TWM Solicitors):** "Any misuses the authority given to them under an LPA... The record number of financial abuse disputes highlight the potential for an attorney to transform their standard of living in our country most people are incredibly high." Problems can include an LPA used as the mechanism by which people mismanage a donor's property or finances, rather than other people held by family members or professional advisers known to the donor.
- **Caroline Abrahams (charity director at Age UK):** "probably asking too much," protecting the vulnerable against financial abuse is not as easy as it should be. Where there are serious concerns about an attorney's conduct by those acting under an LPA, the Office of the Public Guardian and the Court of Protection can intervene.
- **An LPA enables someone to appoint a person they trust to do so on their behalf if they are unable to do so themselves, such as in the case of illness or loss of mental capacity.** There are two types for a person's health and welfare and one for a person's property and financial affairs.
- Downey: the process can be daunting for families, and changes to complex arrangements or an aging bank accounts and other decisions, as well as making decisions such as selling the person's home, are often held by family members or professional advisers given to them under an LPA. "It would be nice to think that we were all more highly minded than that, but at a time when living standards are so constrained that we are so considerably vulnerable or otherwise given to a change in our country, misconduct is concerning, particularly where the donor is elderly or otherwise vulnerable, particularly where living standards are incredibly high."

## 3. "Six financial tips for success as a freelancer" — Claer Barrett, Serious Money column

Source: FT, Claer Barrett (FT consumer editor). VERIFIED, citing named accountants/advisers (Nicole Zalys/Villiers & Co, Deborah Edwards/Harland Accountants, Philippa Davis, Maike Currie/PensionBee).

Context: accountants report more clients in their 40s/50s leaving senior roles for more autonomy, driven by corporate restructuring, redundancy, return-to-office mandates, or simply wanting more flexibility/earning potential.

1. **Know your numbers** — scrutinise household expenses to find the bare minimum (after tax) needed to replace a salary; essential with variable income.
2. **Separate personal and business finances** — use HMRC-compliant accounting software; self-employment/property-rental income over £50,000 (revenue, not profit) must register and file quarterly updates under Making Tax Digital; threshold falls to £30,000 from next April. Understand "payments on account" — once generating a certain self-employment income level, HMRC expects half of next year's estimated tax bill paid in advance in January, with a second payment in July.
3. **How to price your services** — the hardest thing to get right; research what others charge, resist underselling to win work; practise saying your day rate aloud; don't charge the same price to everyone, agree a contract setting out scope to avoid scope creep; your fee isn't just your "wage," it covers your own tax/National Insurance, paid holiday/sick leave you'd have had as an employee, and unbillable hours (quoting, emailing, invoicing, travelling).
4. **Get an accountant** — advise on allowable expenses offsettable against tax (usually includes their own fee); help decide sole trader vs limited company; limited liability status legally separates the business from its owner, protecting personal assets, particularly relevant with higher risk (employing people, leasing property); the £90,000 VAT threshold is measured over a rolling 12-month period; be careful with director's loans given strict tax rules.
5. **Think about your pension** — only 16% of self-employed people save for retirement, per PensionBee; locking money away in a pension is a barrier for those with variable income, but aim to set money aside in savings and make pension contributions quarterly/annually; limited company directors can offset pension contributions against taxable profits, reducing their corporation tax bill.
6. **Embrace the hustle** — biggest mindset shift from corporate life: you have to go out and find the work yourself. Invest time building a professional network; use LinkedIn's "Open to Work" banner and contact previous colleagues; be aware the freelance life can be lonely, build connections with like-minded people for emotional support.
