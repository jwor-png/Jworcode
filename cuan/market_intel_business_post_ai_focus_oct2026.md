## 1. "Building foundations for safer AI" — Damien Mallon, Senior Cybersecurity and AI Architect, Datapac

- **The shift Mallon identifies:** the question organisations ask has
  changed from "what can AI do for us?" to "are we actually ready to
  use it safely?" — AI readiness is now treated as **inseparable from
  data readiness.**
- **Core risk framing:** AI sits on top of an organisation's existing
  technology, identity and data environment. Weaknesses already
  present don't disappear when AI is introduced — "it can make their
  consequences much more significant."
- **The access-control question, stated plainly:** "being technically
  able to access something is not always the same as having a genuine
  business need to access it." Introducing AI should prompt a return
  to foundational questions: who has access to information, why, where
  sensitive data is stored, how it's shared.
- **Key structural point — policy should follow governance, not
  precede it:** "The principle we have arrived at... is that policy
  should be an output of the governance process, not its starting
  point." Good governance should tell an organisation not just that it
  needs an AI policy, but **why** specific controls exist, which risks
  they address, who is accountable, and how controls stay relevant as
  usage changes.
- **Leadership must own this, not just IT/cybersecurity:** "AI
  governance cannot sit solely with the IT department... the decision
  about whether a particular AI use case is appropriate is often a
  business decision as much as a technical one."
- **Risk is not uniform across use cases:** "using AI to help generate
  ideas from non-sensitive information is very different from
  uploading confidential customer, employee or commercially sensitive
  data into an unapproved service" — a proportionate, risk-graded
  approach, not a blanket policy.
- Closing framing: the real question for organisations "is no longer
  about whether or not to adopt AI. It's already inside due to eager
  employees. Instead, it's how to prepare for a full rollout and adapt
  to its potential."

**Cross-reference:** this is close to a textbook articulation of
Meridian's own AI Strategy & Governance domain thesis — "policy as an
output of governance, not its starting point" is a genuinely sharp,
directly reusable line for Meridian's own positioning material.

## 2. "Turning AI into action with a focus on security" — Günter Bayer, co-founder and Chief Information Officer, Stryve

- Stryve (founded 2019, 70 employees) built its own AI-agnostic
  automation platform, **"Supercharger,"** because "a lot of the
  platforms available are narrowly focused on specific use cases."
- **The adoption pattern Bayer describes:** AI rollout typically starts
  with text generation ("let's get data perspective... write emails"),
  then moves to automation once AI is paired with it — "that's where
  businesses can really reap the benefits."
- **The real bottleneck identified:** not the AI itself, but **data
  structure** — "the biggest stumbling block we've come across is that
  data isn't as structured as it needs to be... that's as good as
  people think it is." Low-level/"hanging fruit" automation (writing
  better scripts/tooling) comes before more complex automation.
- **Practical advice for businesses starting out:** "start the way you
  want to go" — invest in proper data structure and governance before
  scaling AI, rather than retrofitting it later; "it's unwieldy" to tie
  AI onto automation afterward without that foundation.
- Stryve's own customer base spans different customer types/sectors
  via its platform, with human oversight retained for complex/
  judgement-based tasks even as AI absorbs repetitive mundane work.

**Cross-reference:** reinforces item 1's data-readiness argument from
a different angle (automation-platform builder's perspective rather
than cybersecurity's) — a second independent voice making the same
"foundations before features" point.

## 3. "Taking the simple work out of customer support" — Aidan O'Shea, co-founder and CEO, Otonomee

- Otonomee (founded 2020, 700 staff) is an outsourcing company whose
  work is "increasingly focused on more complex tasks" as AI absorbs
  the easier ones — **50-60% of customer interactions currently
  handled by AI**, per O'Shea.
- **Core argument:** AI is best at "the ones left over are more complex
  and technical... you've got to be better and smarter." As AI handles
  simple/repetitive queries, the customer support agent role shifts to
  be "more empathetic, more positive" and higher-value.
- **A genuine risk flagged directly:** AI can make customer support
  agents who deal with meaty, complex challenges jump into "do your AI
  and technical issues jump out there's a risk that organisations
  amplify problems" — i.e. poorly scoped AI handling of complex issues
  can actively make problems worse, not just fail to help.
- **Practical staffing implication:** trained people don't want to do
  basic/simple stuff anymore — "the role is becoming more complex" for
  humans, requiring investment in human skill development alongside AI
  rollout, not instead of it.
- O'Shea's framing for leadership: "you need to have a really good
  understanding of your AI handle escalations, where does your AI
  hand off to a human and how is that done? If you've got that right,
  you're going to have problems that don't come out for the sake of
  it, going to amplify potentially have serious issues down the line."

**Cross-reference:** directly useful for Meridian/Velocity AI/Ambrion
AI training content on the human-AI handoff design question — a
named, large-scale (700-employee) outsourcing operator's concrete
figure (50-60% AI-handled interactions) and a sharp warning about
AI amplifying rather than resolving complex issues if escalation design
is wrong.

## 4. "AI changing the job — but not the human role" — Crystel Robbins Rynne, CEO, HRLocker

- HRLocker (founded 2013, 35 employees) — HR technology platform.
- **Core thesis:** the deepest AI impact isn't the technology itself,
  it's how organisations redefine jobs, measure performance, and
  decide where people add value. Most job specs, interview questions,
  policies, meeting notes and emails are now AI-drafted, human-edited —
  "more and more of the first version is being written by AI and then
  edited by a person."
- **"Judgement is the most valuable skill in an AI-driven workplace"**
  — directly quotable line: "AI can give you something that looks
  completely convincing and is completely wrong. You need somebody who
  knows enough about the subject to spot that."
- **Job design shift, explicitly stated:** "most roles today are built
  around tasks. As AI takes on more of the repetitive work,
  organisations will need to reconsider what individual roles are
  actually designed to achieve... jobs will centre on judgement,
  decision-making, problem-solving and outcomes, rather than long task
  lists."
- **Middle management flagged as a particular pressure point:**
  managers are being asked to lead increasingly complex workplaces
  while also helping employees adapt to AI, "often without sufficient
  training themselves" — HRLocker's own advice is that organisations
  need to invest here specifically, since managers are now expected to
  "lead remote teams, manage performance, keep people engaged and now
  help their teams adopt AI as well."
- **A sharp reframe on what AI-driven time savings should fund:**
  "if AI can remove five hours of admin from somebody's week, the
  question should not immediately be 'how do we remove five hours of
  cost?' The question should be 'what more valuable thing can this
  person now spend those five hours doing?'" — directly consistent
  with the PwC/Aisling Curtis "operating-model redesign, not just
  productivity" argument already logged in yesterday's batch
  (`market_intel_sunday_times_business_batch_oct2026.md`).
- **HR-specific principle:** "HR technologies like HRLocker can
  automate the transaction, but they should never automate the
  conversation" — leave requests, documents, training records,
  probation dates can be automated; grievances, poor-performance
  conversations and judgement calls cannot.

**Cross-reference:** directly extends and reinforces the PwC/Curtis
"redeploy time saved into real value, not just cost-cutting" argument
from yesterday's batch — now with two independent sources making
essentially the same point from different angles (strategy consulting
vs HR tech). Also directly usable for Golden Generation/Velocity AI
executive training material on managing the human side of AI adoption.
