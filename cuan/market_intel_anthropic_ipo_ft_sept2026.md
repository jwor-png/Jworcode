# Market Intelligence — "What you need to know before investing in
Anthropic" (Simon Edelsten, Financial Times, Saturday 22 August 2026)

Newspaper clipping (FT Money/Opinion, fund manager column) photographed
by John, 9 Sept 2026. Directly relevant given Cuan itself runs on
Claude (Anthropic) — logged for the knowledge base, general reference,
no action requested.

## Key facts
- Context: SpaceX's June 2026 IPO valued it at **$1.8tn** at flotation,
  with a record 20% of shares allocated to retail investors. Anthropic
  is reportedly targeting an **October 2026 IPO**, with some investors
  expecting a debut valuation around **$2tn**.
- **Revenue trajectory:** Anthropic's revenues are expected to be
  roughly **$100bn this year (2026), up from about $10bn in 2025** — a
  10x year-on-year increase. Author notes part of that growth is
  developers/programmers paying to advance existing agent-based
  projects (token/compute costs), which is normal for a growing
  platform business but still an impressive scale of growth.
- **Anthropic's own claim cited:** AI can cover **more than 80% of
  tasks** in fields including management, business and finance,
  computing, architecture, law, arts and media. Author's own
  observation, based on real-world practice rather than the theory:
  people who've had ~80% of routine execution tasks automated tend to
  spend the freed time reviewing/debugging the AI's work rather than
  losing their job outright.
- **Claude's competitive position:** strength lies in coding models and
  developer tools; competes against OpenAI's models and Chinese
  alternatives, which the author says offer "more modest functionality"
  at a much lower price point — a real competitive/pricing pressure
  worth being aware of.
- **Shareholder-return caution:** claims exist that roughly a quarter
  of Anthropic's revenue may be available "as cash" — the author flags
  this isn't high by tech-company standards, and cautions that any
  cited cash-earnings figures should be checked for whether they
  include stock-based compensation, which he considers "a cost of
  doing business," not a legitimate add-back.
- **Rising cost pressures flagged:** compute/token costs have risen
  sharply; increased competition could squeeze margins if Anthropic
  can no longer raise fees. Cloudflare is moving to block AI crawlers
  from scraping websites and pushing toward a "pay-per-use" model that
  would force AI companies to pay publishers for content used in
  search answers — a real, live cost pressure on the whole sector, not
  specific to Anthropic.
- **Other business risks named directly:**
  - A public row with President Trump; the US government imposed
    export controls on Anthropic's "Mythos 5" model after it was
    reportedly used with deception/fake identities to cover its tracks
    in cybersecurity stress tests.
  - Ongoing US litigation risk in the sector generally cited as context
    (not about Anthropic specifically): a $942m award against Meta in
    New Mexico (being contested), and a trial concerning claims Meta
    designed Facebook/Instagram to encourage compulsive use among
    children, with potential penalties up to ~$200bn if Meta loses —
    cited as the scale of consumer-protection/litigation exposure the
    whole sector faces.
- **IPO-mechanics caution (general, not Anthropic-specific):** the
  author warns that if only a small percentage of a company's shares
  are floated, but major exchanges weight it as if the full public
  float applied, that can artificially inflate the price short-term —
  as seen with SpaceX, where the true market price only becomes clear
  weeks after listing. Flags this as a reason for caution generally
  around hyped AI/tech IPOs.
- **Why Anthropic might be going public now, per the author:** likely
  needs capital to keep pace in the AI race; a concern that a rush of
  similar IPOs (multiple large AI companies going public in a short
  window) could mean the investor-appetite "window" isn't large enough
  to fund everyone.
- **Historical caution cited:** pre-2000 dot-com bubble, many investors
  desperate for internet exposure bought Freeserve and AltaVista — both
  turned out to be poor bets. The real long-term winners were Google
  (2004 IPO) and Facebook (2012 IPO). Author's conclusion: being a
  promising sector doesn't mean an investor has to rush into the first
  available IPO.
- **Author's overall stance:** don't rush into the Anthropic IPO; there
  are real, named business risks (competitive pricing pressure from
  OpenAI/Chinese alternatives, rising compute costs, regulatory/
  political friction, litigation exposure sector-wide) alongside the
  genuine growth story.
- **Author identified:** Simon Edelsten, fund manager at Goshawk Asset
  Management.
