## 1. "Meet the Collison brothers' right-hand woman at Stripe" — Eileen O'Mara (Linda Daly, interview)

**Eileen O'Mara**, 52, Clare native (raised Clarecastle), now Stripe's
new vice-chair, splitting her time between Dublin and Tribeca, New
York. Joined Stripe in 2016 to launch European expansion; became
senior VP for Europe/Asia-Pacific; now the company's growth-and-lead
figure under founders John and Patrick Collison.

- **Stripe's own stablecoin/crypto push, directly relevant:** bought
  **Bridge for $1.1bn** (stablecoin infrastructure), **Privy**
  (crypto wallet infra), and Stripe now accepts **stablecoin payments
  from African business customers**; stablecoin payments volume
  **quadrupled** last year; **60% of payments handled by Bridge are
  business-to-business.**
- Stripe processed **$1.4tn** total payments last year (reported
  figure); Dublin remains dual HQ with San Francisco; **about 57% of
  new companies signing up to Stripe's services last year were from
  outside America.**
- O'Mara's own framing on Europe: "There is zero shortage of ambition
  and ability in Europe that's not where the friction comes from" —
  argues Europe's constraint is regulatory/market friction, not talent
  or ambition.

**Cross-reference:** general Irish-tech-success and fintech/stablecoin
market awareness — relevant background for Meridian's own
AI-governance-meets-finance positioning and for ODIN/Tairseach's
funding-market context given Stripe's scale as an Irish-headquartered

## 2. "AI danger lurks in plain sight unlike last crisis" (David Smith, British economy columnist)

Written around the 20th anniversary of the first stirrings of the 2008
financial crisis (Northern Rock run, the "phoney war" before Lehman).
Central argument: **this time the risk is visible, not hidden** —
regulators are naming it directly rather than being blindsided.

- **Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee (FPC) has explicitly
  flagged AI-related vulnerabilities** as one of its named risk
  categories, alongside interest-rate/quantitative-easing unwind
  risk and geopolitical/sovereign-debt stress (Middle East,
  intensifying conflict).
- **AI-specific risk framing from the FPC, directly quotable:** "Even
  if AI does not kill us, it does not create cyber-havoc" — the
  committee treats AI as both an investment-bubble risk and a cyber-
  threat vector ("malicious actors" using AI to find and exploit
  vulnerabilities at scale).
- **Circular AI financing deals estimated at $450bn** by JPMorgan,
  with concern that these asset-backed/vendor-financing arrangements
  obscure real leverage, echoing pre-2008 off-balance-sheet structures
  (directly reinforces the Darren Gillen piece already logged in
  today's first batch — same $450bn-plus zone of concern, independent
  sourcing).
- **Global AI-related bond/debt issuance** estimated at **$4.1tn in
  2026**, forecast to grow toward **$4.4tn by 2030** (Morgan Stanley
  estimate cited).
- Historical framing: the FPC itself was created in 2011, in direct
  response to 2007-08, specifically to monitor exactly this kind of
  systemic risk before it crystallises — the column's point being that
  having the right institutional "oversight gap" filled this time is
  itself meaningful, not a guarantee of safety.

**Cross-reference:** a second, independently-sourced piece
reinforcing the same AI-infrastructure-financing risk thread as
today's Darren Gillen piece (`market_intel_sunday_times_business_batch_oct2026.md`)
— two separate named columnists, two different data points ($1.7tn
off-balance-sheet debt vs $450bn circular-deal estimate, $4.1-4.4tn
bond issuance), both converging on the same underlying concern.
Strengthens Meridian's AI Strategy & Governance macro-risk framing
with a genuine second source rather than a single columnist's view.

## 4. "AI is central to Ireland's next phase of economic growth" (Jenny Melia, CEO, Enterprise Ireland)

Published ahead of the **International AI Summit, Dublin, this
Wednesday** (matches the PwC piece already logged in today's first
batch — same summit).

- **Headline figures:** AI adoption this week alone represents
  spending equivalent to **€42.7bn weekly** of the domestic economy;
  **€840m** will be spent this week specifically on AI R&D,
  deployment and training in Ireland.
- **182 AI-related deals since the beginning of 2025**, with **nearly
  €1bn invested in Irish AI companies** over that period.
- **Nearly half of all 2026 Enterprise Ireland client-company R&D
  activity is in AI** — up from a ninth of EI-backed start-up capital
  in 2025.
- **Start-ups backed by Enterprise Ireland client companies exported
  €28.8bn in 2025.**
- **Named companies applying AI across non-tech sectors:** H&MV
  Engineering and CEL Critical Power (data centres/energy), Mercury
  Engineering (advanced manufacturing/construction), **Equal1**
  (quantum computing infrastructure), **Kerry Group** (AI in food and
  nutrition — highly relevant given Meridian's own agribusiness/food
  sector lens), **Fenero** (AI applied to highly regulated financial-
  services environments).
- Melia's own framing: the "immediate opportunity" is embedding AI
  into existing operations, governance and measurable value, not just
  building AI-native companies — "the global prize will go to
  companies that out-compete on how effectively we convert AI
  capability into commercial value."

**Cross-reference:** strong, directly citable Enterprise Ireland data

## 5. "Harry's big problem wasn't the tech, it was him walking off and blaming AI" (Gina London, The Communicator)

Fourth Gina London column now logged (after the speak-up-culture,
negotiation and presentation-delivery pieces already tracked).

- **The story:** Prince Harry, speaking at the Clinton Global
  Initiative in New York, had a teleprompter malfunction (the device
  reportedly restarted/failed), stumbled, then stopped and walked off
  stage, appearing to blame the AI-assisted teleprompter technology
  directly.
- **London's core argument, directly quotable:** "You should know it
  so thoroughly that you can express it six ways from Sunday. You
  shouldn't need a perfectly crafted message... understand the idea
  well enough to communicate it naturally." A speaker should never be
  dependent on any single tool (teleprompter, slides, notes) to the
  point that its failure derails the whole message.
- **Three concrete pieces of advice given:**
  1. **Prepare your own material** — know your message so well you
     could explain it without a script at all.
  2. **Support points, don't memorise sentences** — stories, examples
     and support points are more memorable and flexible than a
     word-perfect script.
  3. **Rehearse for disruption deliberately** — practise what you'd
     say if the tech fails, so a glitch becomes a minor stumble, not a
     walk-off.
- London's closing framing: technology (including AI tools like
  teleprompters) can help communication, but audiences will forgive a
  technical glitch far more readily than they'll forgive a speaker
  abandoning them mid-message — "don't let them see you sweat... don't
  let whatever you do, not walk off the stage."

**Cross-reference:** directly reusable for Velocity AI/Ambrion AI
training content and Golden Generation's executive-communication
material — a concrete, named, high-profile example of over-reliance on
an AI-assisted tool (teleprompter) without the underlying human
preparation to fall back on, which is exactly the "AI informs, it
doesn't replace judgement/preparation" theme already core to
Meridian's own positioning.
