# "The Startup Channel Map" — The Revenue Coaches

Source: Instagram infographic, account therevenuecoaches (Gustavo Bravetti, Babel). UNVERIFIED — social media source, a framework/opinion, not an independently tested or academically sourced model. Directly applicable as a thinking tool regardless.

## What it says

A four-stage customer-acquisition framework, mapped on two axes (High-Touch ↔ Scalable; Customer Learning ↔ Predictable Revenue):

1. **0→10 customers — Start with conversations.** Use: founder network, niche communities, warm introductions, direct outreach. Prove: one buyer type pays for one urgent problem. Avoid: paid reach before the message earns replies.
2. **11→25 customers — Borrow trust.** Use: customer referrals, partner introductions, narrow outreach, niche events. Prove: similar buyers respond to the same trigger and promise. Avoid: adding channels before one produces a pattern.
3. **26→50 customers — Build one motion.** Use: best direct channel, referral loop, founder content, focused partners. Prove: the channel produces customers beyond your warm network. Avoid: automating a message that still needs rewriting.
4. **51→100 customers — Scale the winner.** Use: invest in the proven channel, add sales support, test paid carefully. Prove: more spend brings good-fit customers who stay. Avoid: scaling leads while conversion or retention falls.

Core thesis: "the wrong channel can make your first 100 customers take longer and cost more" — different stages need different evidence and different channels, not one escalating approach from day one.

## Cross-reference

Directly relevant to the exact problem John flagged in the 1 Oct session capture: posting on LinkedIn for Meridian with "no feedback... no line of business." This framework's diagnosis fits that symptom precisely — a single LinkedIn post is "founder content," which only belongs in **stage 3 (26-50 customers, Build one motion)**, after a channel has already proven itself through direct conversations and warm referrals at stages 1-2. Posting content before the warm-outreach/referral stage has produced a repeatable pattern is, per this framework, the "avoid" at stage 1 ("paid/broad reach before the message earns replies") — worth weighing against the existing advice already on file (from the 1 Oct session capture): "posts alone rarely bring advisory work... comment daily on target prospects' posts, send each post personally to five relevant people." Both independently arrive at the same conclusion: direct, warm, person-to-person outreach should lead; broadcast content follows proof, not the other way around.

Worth considering explicitly for Meridian's, Velocity AI's, and Ambrion's current stage (all appear to be in the 0-25 customer range), before investing further in a LinkedIn-first posting cadence.
