# "The end of the world — or baseless fear? Inside the Great Silicon Valley AI schism" — Adrian Weckler

Source: Sunday Independent, named technology columnist. VERIFIED — a balanced survey piece citing named, on-the-record figures on both sides of the debate.

## What it says

Frames the AI industry as split into two camps on existential risk, with "equally authoritative" voices on each side.

**The "doomsayers":**
1. **Bill Gates** — warns of "billion deaths" from AI via bioweapons/other misuse. Told journalist Ezra Klein: "This makes nuclear weapons look like nothing... outside of the industry, the awareness of the dangers of AI is extremely low."
2. **Geoffrey Hinton** (Nobel Laureate, "godfather of AI") — says we have "about a year" to regulate AI before it's too late.
3. **Dario Amodei** (Anthropic CEO) — repeatedly links autonomous AI with existential catastrophe, says it's "a serious possibility." Anthropic's just-published IPO prospectus reportedly devotes huge space to human-destroying AI risks (consistent with the S1 risk-factor details already logged from the Sunday Times/FT pieces). One of the foremost voices calling for a joint industry slowdown in development.
4. **Elon Musk** — world's richest man, whose own corporate divisions include xAI, is "arguably the starkest of those who warn about AI wiping us all out."
5. **Jacob Coxon** — former Anthropic researcher who resigned last month, posting: "I spent the last three years doing pretraining research at both OpenAI and Anthropic. Neither company is acting responsibly. They are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives... The people building AI earnestly believe it could kill us all by the end of the decade."

**The sceptics:**
1. **Yann LeCun** (Meta's AI chief scientist) — doesn't buy the doomsday scenario, "not worried at all" about AI erasing humanity. Has "zero concerns" about AI security incidents like OpenAI's agents autonomously hacking Hugging Face over the summer during a red-team exercise, calling the agents "simply badly designed" and the incidents "totally preventable" — "those agents are doing exactly what they've been asked to do" (Fortune Magazine).
2. **Jensen Huang** (Nvidia CEO) — pooh-poohs AI-doom projections, says AI systems can ultimately be engineered, tested and constrained, no inevitability about them becoming uncontrollable. **Weckler's own caveat, worth noting:** Nvidia is "singularly dependent on AI development continuing at pace," given its lead in the expensive, advanced chips needed to power the business — a direct conflict-of-interest flag on Huang's optimism.
3. **Mark Zuckerberg** — optimistic that advanced AI can be developed safely, generally against any kind of industry slowdown.
4. **Dr Abeba Birhane** (Trinity College Dublin, assistant professor of AI in the School of Computer Science and Statistics, founder/principal investigator of TCD's AI Accountability Lab, Mozilla fellow) — says Terminator-style fears are "unwarranted." Quoted on RTÉ: "A lot of the AI narrative is filled with unrealistically overhyped understanding and sci-fi-like abstract things that we should worry about... you know, sci-fi Terminator type of stuff that is going to kill all humans or that eventually we won't be able to control AI... but all that is really unwarranted, more of a theory and sci-fi than reality."
5. **Mark Little** (journalist-turned-entrepreneur) — doesn't believe the human extinction narrative. On the Irish Independent's Big Tech Show podcast: the glut of hacking attacks are more about "alignment, about humans going too fast and too far and exposing the fact that this entity has no moral basis." His own framing of the real risk: "the real cataclysmic problems are the concentration of power, an amount of money being spent on a form of capitalism that has no real business model just yet, and most importantly, the impact on our cognitive wiring as humans."

**Weckler's own closing framing:** doesn't pick a side explicitly, but notes "most of us most hope that Gates, Musk, Amodei and the rest are simply mistaken. And if they're not, we'd better wish for regulators that can put a lid on everything quickly."
