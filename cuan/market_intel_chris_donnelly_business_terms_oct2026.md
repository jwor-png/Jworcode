# Chris Donnelly (Instagram carousel): business terms every founder should know (captured 28 Oct 2026; slides 4 to 13 of 14)

Source: Instagram carousel by chrisdonnellyofficial ("Want to run a business..."), screenshotted by John 28 Oct 2026, slides 4 to 13 now seen (4 to 8 added later the same day). Generic educational definitions; slides 1 to 3 and 14 not seen. No data or sources.

| Slide | Term | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Unit economics | Whether each sale makes or loses money: revenue per unit minus all variable costs tied to delivering it. Scaling a unit that is already losing money multiplies the losses; if unit economics are broken, growth makes the problem bigger. |
| 5 | Cash flow | Profit is an accounting concept; cash flow is money you actually have in the bank, moving in and out. A business can look profitable on paper and still be unable to pay its bills. "Manage cash first and let profit follow." |
| 6 | Runway | How long the business can operate before cash runs out: available cash divided by monthly burn. Knowing it determines when you must raise, cut costs or grow faster. "Most founders don't track this closely enough." |
| 7 | Burn rate | How much cash the company spends each month; the true cost of operating today. Controlling it gives time, leverage and optionality; not controlling it reduces your options very quickly. |
| 8 | Working capital | Current assets minus current liabilities: the cash actually available to keep things moving day to day, pay suppliers and salaries, or jump on growth opportunities. "No working capital, no real business." |
| 9 | Product-market fit | Your idea genuinely solves a problem customers want to pay for; crucial for a startup to become scalable; most businesses skip over it without really understanding it. Is your business actually addressing a customer need? |
| 10 | Churn | How many customers stop using your product; losing customers kills growth and drives up CAC (customer acquisition cost); many teams chase new users, but focusing on why existing ones leave can increase profitability. |
| 11 | Operating leverage | The ability to grow revenue without matching cost increases; software businesses often have high operating leverage, services businesses less so; high leverage drives outsized profits at scale and gives the people running the business more freedom. |
| 12 | EBITDA | Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation; strips away the noise so investors can see how much the business makes before the extras; one of the key numbers used to value a company. |
| 13 | Gross margin | How much money remains after direct costs are removed from revenue; shows whether the core business model is viable; a strong margin gives room to invest in growth and operations. |
