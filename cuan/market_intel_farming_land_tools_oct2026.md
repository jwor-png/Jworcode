# Land-mapping tool + general farm-practice colour, 6 Oct 2026

## Tailte Éireann / GeoHive — free public land/boundary mapping tool (Richard Moeran, "Our Farm" column)

**Directly useful reference for Tairseach's property-intelligence/site-sourcing work:**

- **Tailte Éireann** (formerly the Land Registry, merged with the Property Registration Authority, Valuation Office and Ordnance Survey Ireland) operates **landdirect.ie** — enter any property as a guest to view current boundary maps.
- **GeoHive** (geohive.ie) is the companion viewer, and goes further: provides **aerial photographs from 1995, 2006 and 2013**, plus **Ordnance Survey maps back to the original first-edition six-inch-to-the-mile survey, published 1846** (earlier in Ulster — the columnist's own Cavan farm map dates to 1836). Can show what was on a site before the Famine.
- **Practical value flagged by the columnist, first-hand:** boundary/mapping errors (incorrect lines supplied to the Land Registry historically) are common and often unknown to current owners until a sale or transfer surfaces them — one case cited had a boundary line running straight through a neighbour's living room. Corrected via "Rule 7 – Correction of Errors or Rectification" where both parties agree.
- **Separate but related, same column:** the Heritage Council's **Traditional Farm Buildings Grant** — funds "repair, not replace" restoration of old farm buildings (the fabric must be kept intact), administered with conditions; columnist's own son successfully used it to restore a mothballed, 50+-year-derelict two-room stone outbuilding on their Cavan farm.

**Why this is worth having on file:** a genuine, free, publicly-accessible historical land-record tool — directly usable for any Tairseach site-history, boundary-verification or land-provenance work, and a concrete example of the kind of public data source a property-intelligence platform would want to index against.

## General farm-practice content (logged for completeness, no direct venture tie)

- **"How to make every tonne of silage count this season"** (Gerry Giggins, animal nutritionist, Co Louth) — technical guidance on second-cut silage quality issues from this year's dry summer, maize silage as a cost-effective concentrate-feed substitute (currently trading €70-90/tonne), and protective-cover practice to prevent bird damage to maize pits.
- **"I feel that even in a bad year I am financially better off as an organic farmer"** (Grace Maher, Irish Organic Association) — two named organic farmers (Fergal Byrne, Calverstown Little, Co Kildare; Joe Nolan, Fenagh, Co Carlow) on building climate resilience into mixed/tillage farms via cover crops (red clover, phacelia, fodder rape), reduced chemical fertiliser dependence, and diversification (turkeys, sheep wool sales) to buffer against increasingly erratic weather (drought, flooding, the 2018 "Beast from the East").
