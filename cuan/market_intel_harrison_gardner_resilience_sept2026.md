# "Home truths" — Harrison Gardner profile + resilience checklist

Source: Irish Times Magazine profile of Harrison Gardner — Australian-born eco-builder, author, podcaster, presenter of RTÉ's *Build Your Own Home* (mentors people doing self-build/renovation projects on a budget), co-founder of Common Knowledge (social enterprise skills-sharing centre, Kilfenora, Co Clare). Lives in a self-renovated 200-year-old stone cottage near Ennistymon, Co Clare — off-grid-leaning, solar + battery storage, water harvesting, building a food-waste biodigester for cooking gas. New book, *Heat, Shelter, Water, Food: How to Be Ready for the Unexpected* (Gill Books), published the week of this piece.

## The practical content — "Preparing for the unexpected," a 5-part checklist

Prompted directly by Storm Éowyn (Jan 2025), which left Gardner's own household without power or water for days. His framing: build genuine household resilience yourself rather than "contracting it out to someone else." Each category has a "right now" (cheap/immediate) and "long game" (investment) step:

1. **Electricity** — Now: portable power bank with solar panel, battery radio. Long game: solar panels, battery storage, changeover switch for self-generation.
2. **Heat** — Now: keep doors/windows shut tight to retain existing heat if power goes. Long game: a wood-burning stove as backup, keeping at least one room warm regardless of the grid.
3. **Shelter** — Now: use storm-warning lead time to clear gutters, secure loose garden items. Long game: external insulation, draught-proofing doors/windows/attic hatches before heat pumps — "the cheapest and most effective thing you can do" for thermal performance.
4. **Water** — Now: a garden water butt doubles as emergency drinking supply with basic treatment knowledge; slimline rainwater harvest tanks fit even small urban gardens. Long game: a drinking-water filtration system.
5. **Food** — Now: swap some weekly shop items for quality Irish tinned goods, so a fridge outage isn't a crisis. Long game: buy from a local community-supported farm — a relationship worth having when supply chains falter.

**Gardner's own stated motivation, explicit and worth noting**: not prepper/survivalist framing — "I really believe in community, and I really believe in people looking after each other... when we are feeling vulnerable ourselves, we've got nothing to give." The point of personal resilience, in his framing, is to be able to help others, not to retreat from them.

## Background, for context only

Started as a builder in Australia, worked internationally (Kenya, Iceland, Malawi) before settling in Ireland in 2014. Renovated a derelict 1970s stone cottage near Ennistymon (bought €81,000 in 2016, €40,000 renovation). RTÉ commissioned *Build Your Own Home* in 2020; now filming seasons 3-4. Second book now published. No direct business connection identified.
