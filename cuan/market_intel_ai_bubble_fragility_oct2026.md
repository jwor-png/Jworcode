# Two linked pieces: a live "dress rehearsal" for an AI bubble burst, 17/18 Oct 2026

## 1. Katie Martin, "The Long View" — the dress rehearsal

**Core argument:** the past week gave a live preview of what a bursting
AI bubble in markets might actually look like — triggered not by hard
economics but by "vibes" (sentiment), against a backdrop every
investor already knows is fragile: a highly concentrated global stock
market trundling almost entirely on AI-theme speculation, eye-watering
borrowing often routed through balance-sheet structures rating
agencies struggle to assess.

**What actually moved this week:**
- **South Korea's chip-heavy stock market sank 8% in a day.**
- Bonds issued by pure-play data-centre companies took a beating;
  **credit default swaps (CDS) on some issuers rocketed higher** —
  Martin's own rule of thumb: "whenever you hear the letters C, D and
  S in one sentence, something bad is happening."
- **Boring, non-tech stocks (FTSE 100) outperformed** — a classic
  flight-to-safety signal.
- **US government bonds rallied** (10-year yields pulled back toward
  5%) despite inflation data not actually improving.
- **The dollar weakened almost 1%** since midweek — read as a sign
  investors think an AI implosion could threaten the "miracle" of US
  growth itself.
- **Surveys of professional investors now place AI exuberance and the
  hyperscaler debt binge at the top of worry lists.**

**What specifically rattled the market this week, per the piece:**
Broadcom's own CEO warning that chip investors are treating markets
like a "casino"; CoreWeave's ambition to tap insurers for funding
"because that's all the money in the world"; and the failure of
efforts to list data-centre companies on Australian and Asian stock
markets (see below).

**George Saravelos (Deutsche Bank)**, quoted directly: systemic risk
could stem from "a safety event, a failed [stock market] listing, or
disappointing revenues... concentration risk is immense, and it is
this... event risk that is most underpriced in markets at the
moment."

**The "what to watch for" checklist Martin gives:** a weaker dollar,
stronger bonds, a hammering for markets hooked on the semiconductor
boom, and a preference for old-economy stocks. Her own closing line:
"the AI trade has lived by the vibes, and it will die by the vibes."

## 2. "AI valuations in spotlight after Australian data centre IPO debacle triggers meltdown" (Nic Fildes)

**The trigger:** Firmus (an Australian data-centre operator backed by
Nvidia and Blackstone) pulled its planned ASX listing a week after
issuing debt, which would have valued the company at close to
A$4.44bn (~$50.5bn/A$ context implied). Investors balked amid concern
about AI-related debt and "huge levels of debt" across the sector —
Firmus's own attempted IPO failed due to weakness and delays, with
shareholders blaming its "necloud" business for the share price
tumble.

**Wider fallout named:** Lambda Labs, Nscale and others bled this week
amid rising concern about global AI-related stocks; CoreWeave pulled a
planned bond issuance; Singapore-listed AirTrunk and Australia's
AI-data-centre investment narrative came under scrutiny.

**Named investor background:** Firmus co-founder **Oliver Curtis**
(son of former Macquarie banker Nick Curtis) is a figure with a
chequered past — convicted of insider trading in 2016, served 12
months in jail — now rebuilding via Firmus and an earlier venture,
Sharon AI, alongside co-founder Tim Rosenfield. Firmus raised debt via
Blackstone and Goodman Group, with CDC Data Centres and others among
established players in the space; the company reported A$82m net
profit on nearly A$500m revenue year to June (per the piece, not
independently verified here) and had projected debt-to-earnings
ratios around six times, with plans to raise up to $500m in further
construction debt.
