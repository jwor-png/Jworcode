## The figures (share of batch, "Before" vs "Now", per Y Combinator's own "State of Startups in 2026" data)

| Metric | Before | Now | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard tech share of batch | 8% | 20% | 2.5x |
| Solo founders | 5% | 19% | 3.8x |
| Full-stack, end-to-end companies | 10% | 25%+ | 2.5x+ |
| End-of-batch monthly revenue | $8K | $20K | 2.5x |
| Robotics | 1% | 7% | 7x |
| Defense | 1.5% | 5% | 3.3x |
| Industrial manufacturing | 4% | 10% | 2.5x |

## Cross-reference

Several points directly relevant to live Cuan threads:
- **"Full-stack, end-to-end companies" jumping from 10% to 25%+** is
  structurally the same bet Tairseach is making — one shared
  intelligence core ("the spine") powering ten integrated components,
  rather than a single point-solution tool. Worth citing this stat
  directly in Tairseach's own investor conversations as evidence the
  "full-stack over point solution" model is now the dominant YC
  pattern, not a contrarian one.
- **Solo founders nearly quadrupling (5%→19%)** and **robotics up 7x**
  are both notable structural shifts — AI tooling is making it
  possible for one person to build what used to need a team, and
  hard-tech/physical categories (robotics, defense, industrial
  manufacturing) are all growing 2.5-7x as a share of the batch,
  a reversal of the software-only era YC was known for.
- **General relevance to ODIN's own positioning** — ODIN sits in the
  AI-infrastructure space; this data is useful macro context for how
  the investor pool YC itself touches is actively re-weighting toward
  hard tech and AI-native full-stack companies.
