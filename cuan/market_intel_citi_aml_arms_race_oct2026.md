# "We're in an arms race with the bad guys" — Brian McKenzie, Citibank Europe

**Core warning:** financial services firms risk falling behind in an AI
"arms race" with criminals because of the regulatory burden they alone
carry. **Brian McKenzie**, head of AML (anti-money laundering) at
Citibank Europe PLC (formerly head of financial crime at AIB, and
before that at Barclays Frankfurt), speaking at an AML conference
hosted by law firm A&L Goodbody: criminals are already using AI to
launder money without having to "go through different governance
committees and risk assessments and approvals" that regulated firms
are subject to. Direct quote: **"We're in an arms race with the bad
guys, effectively... We need to just keep pace with them on that."**

**The counter-balance — responsible AI use still required:** McKenzie
was clear financial firms "couldn't abdicate responsibility to a black
box," and that AI deployment had to be "done responsibly with the right
level of controls" — while also saying responsible AI use had already
helped "clear away a lot of the noise" for firms and helped staff
identify individuals presenting risk to their systems.

**Regulator's more cautious counterpoint:** **Derville Rowland**, the
former Central Bank of Ireland deputy governor and now an executive
board member of **AMLA** (the EU's new financial crime watchdog),
cautioned she was "sceptical" about companies' over-reliance on AI even
though it could "take out the noise" for firms. Her own review of AML
failures at a number of global financial services firms, prior to
taking up the AMLA role, found that many stemmed from the **incorrect
handling of data**: "in complex systems, you have to make sure that
you're really thoughtful about what you think you should be looking
at, and have you got that right."

**Relevance:** a named, credentialed regulator-vs-practitioner tension
directly usable for Meridian's AI Strategy & Governance positioning —
the "arms race" framing is a sharp, reusable line, and Rowland's
data-handling-root-cause point is a strong governance argument (AI
amplifies existing data-quality failures rather than fixing them).
