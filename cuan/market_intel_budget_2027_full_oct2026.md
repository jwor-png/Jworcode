# Budget 2027 — full confirmed picture

Source: Irish Independent's dedicated Budget 2027 supplement, 7 Oct 2026, pages 2-15 in full. This supersedes the pre-budget speculation pulled via web search on 7 Oct (WebSearch/WebFetch could not reach any Irish news domain or gov.ie from this environment — network egress blocked entirely, confirmed, not a search-indexing gap). Everything below is the actual announced package. Overall size: current and capital health allocation alone €29.1bn; total voted expenditure €125.6bn; overall State spend ~€134.7bn.

## Income tax, USC, PRSI

- **40% rate entry threshold up €2,500**, from €44,000 to €46,500 (single person).
- **Personal, PAYE and earned-income tax credits each up €125**, from €2,000 to €2,125.
- **USC 2% band threshold up €1,600**, from €28,700 to €30,300.
- **PRSI rose to 4.35% this month**, rising again to **4.5% next October** — this is the clawback that eats into every gain below. Costs a single individual roughly an extra €75/year once fully in effect.
- **Net effect:** single person on €50,000 better off ~€691/year; couple on €100,000 combined better off ~€1,382/year (also cited as up to €1,400 for middle-income couples elsewhere in the coverage).

## KPMG household verdicts (modelled, weekly gain after all changes incl. PRSI)

- Married couple, 3 kids, €77k income: **€8/week (€401/year)**.
- Married couple, 1 child, €44k income (Home Carer credit): **€8/week (€425/year)**.
- Co-habiting couple, €138k combined: **€31/week (€1,625/year)** — the biggest gainer shown.
- Retired couple, €45,168 income: **€26/week (€1,332/year)**, incl. the €10/week pension rise.
- Single mother, €55k: **€13/week (€683/year)**.
- Single renter, €150k: **€13/week (€691/year)**.

## Housing

- **Help-to-Buy threshold up €5,000, to €35,000.**
- **Stamp duty abolition for first-time buyers was proposed but dropped** — Help-to-Buy increase was the actual delivered measure instead. Analysis (Mark Keenan) argues this was the more sensible choice for supply (keeps builders focused on new-build family homes rather than subsidising all purchases), though a first-time buyer case study (Ambrose/Tighe, Balbriggan) notes stamp duty relief would have helped more given it hits late in the buying process after the deposit is already found.
- **€5.6bn total housing allocation**: €3bn social housing (11,250 new units), €1bn affordable housing supports (targeting 15,000 new-home starts), €1.5bn water/wastewater infrastructure (flagged as one of the most effective enablers of unlocking sites for new homes).
- **Rent tax credit up €150 (single, to €1,150) / €300 (couple, to €2,300).**
- **Rent-a-Room allowance up €2,000, to €16,000**, now explicitly linked to modular garden/back-garden homes on site — removes the rule requiring tenants to live inside the host's own home. Analysis flags this as a genuine "hidden gem," particularly relevant for asset-rich/cash-poor older people and anyone with serviced back-garden space, though notably **no measures for smaller landlords** generally, despite rental supply continuing to fall.
- **New derelict property tax proposed at 7%**, backed by "new lists" and direct council/Revenue Commissioner action.

## Welfare, pensions, disability

- **State pension rises €10/week, to €309.30/week (over €16,000/year)** from January — first time above €300. Government's manifesto target is €350 over the lifetime of the government; Minister Dara Calleary says he's "still determined" to hit it and wants €20 increases in future budgets.
- **Reaction mixed-to-critical:** Age Action wanted €20; Alone wanted €15 on the Living Alone Allowance (got €3, to €25/week) and criticised the budget for falling short given 80,000 older people living alone are in poverty. One pensioner case study called the increases "a token gesture."
- **First-ever Cost of Disability payment: €500 lump sum, January 2027**, for 240,000 long-term disability payment recipients (Disability Allowance, Blind Pension). Structure not yet finalised — to be co-designed with disabled people and advocates; possible future development into a permanent/weekly benefit. Disability Federation of Ireland welcomed it but noted it fell short of the weekly €55 payment disabled people's groups sought (Social Justice Ireland had proposed restoring a €400 disability support grant removed in Budget 2026, worth ~€2,860/year if combined with a weekly payment).
- **Fuel Allowance up €5/week, to €43/week** (income threshold to qualify also raised, to €641/week). ~460,000 recipients will get over €1,200 next year. Single pensioners (66+) also benefit from an improved means test (upper limit raised to 60% of the couple's limit, from 50%).
- **Living Alone Allowance up €3/week, to €25** — first increase to this specific payment in five years.
- **Core working-age welfare rates up €10/week** across the board (Jobseeker's, Carer's, Disability Allowance). SVP called this inadequate to meaningfully reduce poverty and wanted €15/week.
- **Child Support Payment up €6/week** — SVP and One Family both said this falls well short of what's needed; **Child Benefit remains frozen at €140/month**, with no increase since 2011 cited directly by one family case study.
- **100% Christmas bonus** (double week) for 1.4 million long-term social welfare recipients, including pensioners, in December.
- **Carer's Allowance income disregard up**, to €1,150 (single) / €2,300 (couple).
- **€119m fund secured for the Cost of Disability payment**, described as a "key Programme for Government commitment" by Minister Dara Calleary.

## Health (€29.1bn current + capital allocation, up €1.8bn)

- **Described as having "few eye-catching initiatives"** — focus was on practical pressures rather than headline giveaways. A hoped-for cut to the €80/month medical-card-excluded prescription charge did not materialise.
- Free contraception extended by two years, to age 37.
- 1.8 million additional home support hours (191,000 of which for disabled people).
- ~€7m for GP access improvements, particularly out-of-hours services to ease A&E burden.
- €18m for children's dental and orthodontic care; IDA welcomed this but stressed the two-year oral health action plan still needs to be finalised with dentist engagement.
- 33,000 extra hours of free counselling for men, young people and the LGBTQ+ community; targeted suicide-prevention investment for the Traveller community; record mental health investment incl. ADHD pathways in CAMHS.
- Capital budget progresses long-delayed surgical hubs in Cork, Dublin, Galway, Limerick, Waterford, plus the National Maternity Hospital relocation.
- **Context the budget was announced against:** HSE facing an overspend of €750m+ this year (another Exchequer bailout likely); 47 patients on trolleys waiting 24+ hours on budget day itself (9 of them over 75); INMO reported 1,100+ patients on trolleys as the budget was finalised. IMO president Prof. Matthew Sadlier said the government "needs to be honest" that the budget means reduced funding for services, longer waiting lists, continued overcrowding. Irish Hospital Consultants Association said acute hospital funding (40% of HSE spend) is "simply inadequate." ESRI estimates 6,500 additional inpatient beds needed by 2040 — "Budget 2027 remains silent" on 2027 acute bed delivery.

## Business, SME, hospitality

- **Labelled one of the "most business-friendly" budgets for years.**
- **Capital Gains Tax cut from 33% to 31%** — welcomed as "a very positive surprise" (Baker Tilly). Entrepreneur Relief lifetime limit left unchanged at €1.5m — several commentators (Grant Thornton, BDO) said a more meaningful increase here would have sent a stronger signal on supporting scaling Irish-owned businesses.
- **Employer PRSI threshold up, from €552 to €600/week** — saves employers €650-700/year per employee below the threshold, explicitly positioned to offset the minimum wage rise.
- **Minimum wage up from €14.15 to €14.94/hour**, effective 1 January 2027.
- **€360m national training fund over three years** (€150m targeted at skills resilience, incl. adapting to AI in the workplace) — AmCham called for accelerated utilisation of the fund.
- R&D tax credit changes: increased subcontracting thresholds for third-level/third-party work.
- **ISME (SME representative body) warned of a "growing structural imbalance" between indigenous enterprise and multinational-led growth** — said the budget "missed an opportunity again" to strengthen competitiveness/resilience of Irish-owned businesses specifically, despite welcoming some measures.
- **9% VAT rate for hospitality retained** (from last year's budget) — welcomed, but a named hotelier (Mario Puchowski, Longcourt House Hotel, Newcastle West, Co. Limerick, 39 bedrooms) said minimum wage increases will still force price rises; "when minimum wage goes up, everyone is asking for more money." 270,000 people employed in hospitality nationally per his estimate.
- **€15m support fund for rural pubs** — welcomed by the Vintners' Federation (VFI) as "much-needed breathing space," though below the €75m originally sought and specifics of delivery not yet explained. Context: 2,200+ pubs closed since 2005, 86 in 2025 alone. Excise duty on alcohol left unchanged.
- €3m allocated to Enterprise Ireland for "Start-Up Ireland," a national entrepreneurship initiative/single-entry point for founders.

## Fiscal risk — the macro picture (Donal O'Donovan analysis)

- **Core argument: Budget 2027 is "the biggest bet in the history of the State"** — spending plans assume Ireland keeps winning the corporation tax lottery indefinitely.
- Corporation tax take expected to hit **€39bn next year** (up from €6.6bn a decade ago — more than sixfold), vs income tax (~€41bn) and VAT (~€25bn), both of which have "only" roughly doubled in the same period and are directly tied to real domestic economic activity (people working, goods/services bought). **Corporation tax is "completely divorced from what happens within the Irish economy"** — it reflects global corporate decisions (Eli Lilly, Microsoft, Apple all paying billions here) to tax global revenues in Ireland, not Irish economic activity.
- **Risk flagged directly: three firms now account for nearly half of all corporation tax paid** (Irish Fiscal Advisory Council data) — concentration risk that used to sit across a handful of sectors now sits in a handful of products/companies.
- Explicit warning: if Donald Trump brings US corporate profits home "at the stroke of a Sharpie," or competition/recession/corporate takeovers intervene, the tax haul could evaporate — and six in seven euros of the corporation tax windfall are already being spent as if the flow continues (hiring teachers, raising pensions), locking in future spending commitments against a revenue source nobody controls.

## Other items of note

- National public emergency alert system to be implemented next year (direct-to-mobile alerts during major incidents).
- Capital gains tax cut (33%→31%) already covered above.
- Heritage donations tax relief threshold raised to €8m aggregate/year, credit remains 80% of market value for approved heritage item donations.
- Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) hub ambition — public consultation launching; positioned alongside existing US tech/Guinness as a new economic identity strand.
- TaxSaver commuter ticket scheme under review given post-Covid hybrid-working patterns reducing its value to many users.
- College fees cut by only €150 (to ~€2,300) — student unions (AMLÉ, Students Ireland) called this inadequate against €500+/year fee inflation; new SUSI grant income band introduced (€120k-€150k) for families with 2+ in college, worth €500/grant per eligible student.
- School capitation funding up €17m; Deis Plus programme +€2m; 2,339 additional Special Needs Assistants (28,000 total); funding for 1,353 extra special-education teachers and 1,420 more higher-education places in health/social care.

## Generational reaction (Irish Independent staff panel, 20s-70s)

Six journalists gave personal reactions, worth holding as qualitative colour rather than data:
- **20s (Philip Cowman):** budget "never going to be a game-changer" for this cohort; renter's credit and modest Help-to-Buy increase welcome but minor; generation remains structurally reliant on "the Bank of Mam and Dad."
- **30s (Emma Nolan):** gains feel like "going into one hand and out the other" against rent/grocery inflation; childcare cap welcomed but Child Benefit stagnant since 2011 is "disappointing."
- **40s (Kirsty Blake Knox):** cost of living, not housing, now the top concern per a Sunday Independent/Ireland Thinks poll; EV/green grants seen as poorly targeted at people without large cash reserves.
- **50s (Gareth O'Connor):** "Generation X sandwich generation" — caring for both adult children and ageing parents; wants targeted tax breaks/structural supports for family carers specifically.
- **60s (Barbara Scully):** mortgage-free but has no private pension — "an extra tenner a week in the state pension is neither here nor there."
- **70s (Liam Collins):** "a weak cheer" for the €10 pension rise; notes Ireland ranks 10th in Europe for state pension generosity but spends only 3% of GDP on it (vs 15% in Italy); flags Ireland's wealth is unevenly distributed to the already-comfortable, while an emergency dental filling cost him "two-thirds of my pension... in an hour."
