# "Give me a crash course in... getting enough protein" — Shauna Bowers

Source: Irish Times explainer column. VERIFIED mainstream press piece citing named sources (Safefood 2019 research, Food Safety Authority of Ireland 2021 guidelines, National Adult Nutrition Survey II 2024, Glanbia company accounts).

## What it says

- Protein helps build, maintain and repair muscles, organs, skin, hair and nails, via amino acids. The surge in protein-labelled products (wraps, ice cream, yoghurt, cheese) is driven partly by online fitness/wellness influencers promoting protein for fat loss and satiety (keeps people fuller, reduces snacking, helps stay within a calorie deficit).
- **Recommended intake:** general population 0.8g protein per kg body weight/day (a 60kg person needs ~48g); higher for those doing significant exercise/strength training. FSAI's 2021 guidelines for older adults at risk of frailty, sarcopenia or undernutrition recommend 1-1.2g/kg/day to preserve muscle mass — more protein-dense than the general adult diet.
- **Are we eating enough?** Yes, per the National Adult Nutrition Survey II (2024): Irish adults get 18% of total energy from protein. Similarly, a US government report found nearly 90% of Americans already get enough protein in their diets — described as a worldwide phenomenon of adequate-to-high intake despite marketing suggesting a gap.
- **Are protein products actually healthier?** Not necessarily — Safefood research (2019) found marketing of these products leads people to believe they're healthy alternatives, but 77% of high-protein bars surveyed were high in saturated fat and a source of added salt/sugar; high-protein yoghurts, quark, dairy drinks and smoothies surveyed were also a source of added salt/sugar.
- **Commercial success:** Kilkenny-based nutrition group Glanbia reported a 7% revenue jump for H1, including a 25% spike in revenue for its Optimum Nutrition brand — protein's marketing success is "undeniable" per company accounts.
