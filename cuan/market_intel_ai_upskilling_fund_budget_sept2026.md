# Market intel — €300m AI upskilling fund bid ahead of the Budget

**Source:** Irish Independent, Aisling Moloney, undated clipping
(captured 28 Sept 2026, ahead of this year's Budget). REPORTED —
government-source reporting, not independently verified by Cuan.

## What it says
Higher Education Minister **James Lawless** is understood to be pushing
for a **five-year AI upskilling fund worth €300m** in this year's
Budget — a proposed **€60m annual allocation over five years**, sourced
from the **National Training Fund (NTF) surplus** (currently ~€2bn),
explicitly avoiding further pressure on taxpayer funds. The NTF is
financed via a 1% levy on employer payroll, supporting apprenticeships,
enterprise-focused higher education, upskilling and labour market
research; it had a €1.77bn surplus at end of 2024, expected to reach
~€2bn by end of 2025. The National Training Fund (Amendment) Act 2025
already unlocked €1.5bn from the fund over six years to 2030 — this
€300m AI-specific bid would sit within/alongside that.

**Purpose:** financing further/higher education courses and curriculums
to train graduates in AI, keeping Ireland "ahead" in AI upskilling and
reskilling. Lawless is due to bring a **National Skills Council
blueprint** to Cabinet this week, urging a "systemic" and "nationwide"
response to rapid technological shifts across higher and further
education/training. Funding is also expected to expand the
**Government's AIReady.ie website into a national public AI-training
platform** — the primary target audience is higher/further education
students, preparing the workforce for an AI-driven economy.

**Employer sentiment cited:** Ibec's director of policy and public
affairs, **Fergal O'Brien**, said there is "a real sense of frustration"
among employers about the €2bn NTF surplus sitting unused despite
paying into it via payroll levy: "we're seeing this massive demand, in
particular for AI upskilling, but for other training and lifelong
learning as well... because the fund has been pre-financed, then the
tax has been already collected, then any spending coming out of the
fund shouldn't have to compete with other spending priorities."
