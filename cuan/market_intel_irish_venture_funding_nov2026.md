# "Venture funding to Irish tech firms hits record low as AI grabs investors' cash", Adrian Weckler, newspaper (John's photo, 17 Nov 2026; same page as the Grafton report). Figures as reported, UNVERIFIED; some figures in the article overlap or may refer to different periods (quarter versus nine months).

- Venture funding into Irish tech companies is at a decade-long low as AI draws most available cash. July to September: venture capital cash fell 45pc to 114m euro, the lowest third quarter in 10 years. International investment into Irish start-ups fell 76pc (IVCA data) as foreign VCs back large AI firms.
- Funding here has shrunk almost 40pc since 2023 for the first nine months; Ireland is set to finish the year below 1bn euro for the first time since 2020.
- Richard Watson (chairperson, IVCA): international VCs fell 76pc to 35.5m euro from 146.7m in the same quarter last year; the IVCA has warned about "overdependence on international investors". Hardest hit: Irish start-ups raising 1m to 5m euro; the number of deals in that range fell by over half to 12 from 30.
- Sarah-Jane Larkin (director general, IVCA): deals of 1m to 3m euro down 70pc to 10.7m; 3m to 5m rounds down 55pc to 15.7m (a later quote says 3m to 5m funding fell over a half, 56pc, to 37.2m over nine months, and 1m to 3m deals fell by over a third to 48.7m; rounds across both categories fell from 65 to 40 this year).
- Sector shares of money raised, first nine months: life sciences 163.2m (24pc); fintech 154.2m (22pc); software 110.6m (16pc); cybersecurity 91.4m (13pc).
- Many Irish VC firms are trying to launch new funds backed by the Government's Enterprise Ireland Seed and Venture Capital Scheme; they find it hard to get matching private money (institutional investors, pension funds). Enterprise Ireland's support is welcomed.
- Silver lining (Watson): Ireland has less exposure to AI stock market swings because there are no large frontier-model builders investing billions in data centres here. "AI is here to stay and Ireland has many companies applying it to solve real industry problems in healthcare, financial services, manufacturing and construction... These businesses will require normal levels of venture-scale funding rather than the extraordinary sums being invested by global model developers."
