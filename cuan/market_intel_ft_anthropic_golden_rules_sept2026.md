## What it argues
Responding to a recent essay by Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO), Foulis
argues Anthropic's stated ideals break from decades of accepted
globalisation orthodoxy in two ways:
1. **Governments should recommend "golden rules" for how far businesses
   pursue non-core diversification** (geographic reach, ownership
   structure, minimum "non-core" activity limits) — explicitly aimed at
   making it harder for companies to become "too big to fail" or
   politically unaccountable.
2. **Businesses should proactively outsource, not vertically integrate,
   to reduce sprawl and control.**

Foulis frames this as Anthropic (and its "AI safety" positioning
generally) proposing rules that would constrain exactly the kind of
sprawling, vertically-integrated, state-adjacent conglomerate structure
that companies like BYD, Reliance, Tata, and (implicitly) the Chinese
tech giants have used to build resilience and national-champion status —
and asks whether Anthropic's own scale ambitions are consistent with the
restraint it's recommending for everyone else.

**Historical framing used:** ownership concentration in world equity
markets was far higher in 1914 (83% cross-border/non-core ownership) than
in 1970 ("the end of the ideal of the perfect company" per the piece's
framing) — used to argue that today's push toward sprawl, state-backed
national champions (BYD, Huawei, Chinese car exports), and "own your
supply chain" thinking (SpaceX's vertical integration cited as a US
techno-nationalist example) is a reversion to an older, more
concentrated, less "flat world" pattern, not a new phenomenon. Also cites
historical capital-control precedents: Tsarist Russia's reliance on
French bond investment (72% of French firms' foreign equity in Russian
government bonds by 1914) as a cautionary parallel for capital dependency
across geopolitical lines.

**REPORTED, not independently verified beyond the column itself:** the
1914/1970 ownership percentages, the Tsarist Russia bond figures, and the
framing of Amodei's essay's actual content are all as characterised by
Foulis — this entry has not independently read Amodei's original essay
to verify the columnist's characterisation is fair.
