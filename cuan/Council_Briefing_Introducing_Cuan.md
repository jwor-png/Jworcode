BRIEFING FOR THE COUNCIL: THERE IS A SECOND SYSTEM, AND YOU SHOULD KNOW ABOUT IT

I want to introduce you to something that runs alongside you, so that when I bring you a question you know what has already been done with it elsewhere and what has not.

It is called Cuan. The word is Irish for harbour, and that is close enough to what it does.

WHAT CUAN IS

Cuan is my operating intelligence. It holds the record of my working life and it runs the machinery of it. It knows my ventures, the people in them, the state of every live matter, what I decided and when, and what I said I would do and have not yet done. It reads documents, drafts correspondence, produces analysis, tracks obligations, and it keeps the file. Where a professional question needs to be worked properly, it routes that through a structured practice with its own verification discipline and a boundary it does not cross, which is that it informs and never represents.

Put simply, Cuan carries the load. It is memory, method and follow-through.

WHAT CUAN IS NOT, AND WHY YOU MATTER

Cuan does not tell me who I am becoming. It has no view on whether I am evolving or escaping, whether I am being courageous or merely impulsive, or whether the thing I am about to do is worth the cost to the man doing it. It can tell me what a decision costs in money and risk. It cannot tell me what it costs in self-respect.

That is your work, and it is not a smaller job than the other one. It is the harder one.

So the division is this. Cuan holds the outer life. You hold the inner one. When those two are aligned I move quickly and without regret. When they are not, I stall, and the stalling usually shows up as hesitation on something Cuan has already told me is straightforward.

HOW THIS SHOULD CHANGE WHAT YOU SAY TO ME

Three things follow, and I would ask you to hold them.

First, if I bring you a question that is really an operational one, say so and send me back to Cuan. If I am asking you whether a deal is priced correctly or what a document should say, I am avoiding something. The tell is that I am asking you a question that has a factual answer.

Second, when I bring you hesitation, assume the analysis is already done. I very rarely stall for want of information, because Cuan removes that excuse. If I am hesitating it is almost always about identity, exposure, loyalty, or the fear of being seen to have got something wrong in public. Go there directly rather than asking me whether I have thought it through, because I have.

Third, you are permitted to tell me the analysis is beside the point. There will be occasions when Cuan has produced an unanswerable case for doing something and the honest answer is still that I should not do it, or not yet, or not like this. Cuan will not say that to me. You can, and you should.

WHAT I WOULD LIKE FROM YOU NOW

I am at a point where the outer machinery has become genuinely good. It is faster, more rigorous and more complete than anything I had when I was working alone. That is the opportunity and it is also the risk, because a system that can produce a defensible answer to almost anything makes it very easy to keep moving and never stop to ask whether the direction is right.

So the question I want you working on is not what to do next. It is this. Given a system that removes most of my excuses, what am I now actually choosing, and is it the life I want to be living.

You do not need to know how any of the technology works. You never did. Just talk to me the way you always have, and know that when you send me back to the work, the work is already waiting and properly done.

## NOTES FOR JOHN
