## What the scheme is
The **Starter Home Purchase Scheme** (formerly the **Local Authority
Affordable Purchase Scheme**) is a national scheme, administered
locally by each local authority, designed to help first-time buyers
bridge the gap between their own mortgage/savings/deposit capacity and
the full market price of a newly built home. This notice covers one
specific development — **Oscar Traynor Woods, Dublin 17** — run by
**Dublin City Council**, but the mechanism itself is the general
national model, not unique to this site.

## How eligibility/pricing is calculated (the general mechanism)
A buyer's **purchasing capacity** is calculated as the combined total
of three things:
1. **Maximum mortgage capacity** — 4x gross household income.
2. **A minimum 10% deposit** of the purchase price (can include Help
   to Buy scheme support).
3. **Relevant savings** — any savings in excess of the combined sum of
   the required deposit amount plus €30,000.

Apartments are sold below full market value, with the actual price
paid linked to the applicant's income, savings, and mortgage capacity
— each unit has an indicative **minimum and maximum purchase price**
tied to its market value (see worked figures below).

**Applicant Household Gross Income limit example (this scheme):**
typically €92,981 or less, based on 85.5% of the property's market
value divided by 4 — i.e. the income ceiling scales with the specific
property's market value, not a single fixed national figure.

## The Affordable Dwelling Purchase Arrangement (ADPA) — the key
structural mechanism
Purchasers enter an ADPA with the local authority: **the council takes
an equity share in the dwelling equal to the difference between the
property's market value and the price actually paid by the
purchaser**, expressed as a percentage of market value. This equity
share can later be bought out ("redeemed") by the homeowner making
payments to the council over time. This is the core mechanism worth
remembering for understanding any similar scheme elsewhere in the
country — it is a **shared-equity model**, not a simple discount or
grant.

## Eligibility priority rules (general scheme structure)
Per the "Scheme of Priority for Affordable Dwelling Purchase
Arrangements":
- Priority order for oversubscribed schemes is based on **date and
  time of application** (i.e. a "first come" queue once eligible).
- Applicants are strongly advised to have **Mortgage Approval in
  Principle (MAIP)** before applying.
- If oversubscribed, households of 2+ people are prioritised for
  3-bedroom homes specifically.
- **First-time buyers only**, subject to exceptions (e.g. the Fresh
  Start Principle, which can extend eligibility to certain previous
  owners in defined circumstances).
- A minimum of **30% of units in a given scheme** are reserved for
  applicants who are, or have been, living in that local authority's
  administrative area for at least 3 years — a **local-connection
  quota**, worth noting as it will vary by which council/area a scheme
  sits in.

## Worked example — Oscar Traynor Woods specifics (for reference, not
generalisable pricing)
95 one/two/three-bed apartments, this final phase. Market values range
from €335,000 (1-bed) to €435,000 (3-bed with ensuite); purchase price
range roughly €251,000-€413,250 depending on unit type and the
buyer's own purchasing capacity calculation; typical gross household
income limits range from ~€71,600 (1-bed) up to ~€92,981 (3-bed
ensuite). Applications open online via
**affordablehomes.dublincity.ie**, portal opens **Wednesday 23
September 2026 at noon**, closes **Wednesday 14 October 2026 at noon**.

## Why this matters as general knowledge (per John's framing)
The specific Dublin scheme numbers won't apply outside Dublin City
Council's area, but the **mechanism itself (income-linked pricing tied
to a formula, mortgage-capacity + deposit + savings-above-threshold
purchasing-capacity calculation, and the shared-equity ADPA structure)
is the general national template** used by local authorities around
the country under the Affordable Housing Act 2021 framework — worth
knowing as a reference model when this topic comes up elsewhere
(different county, different scheme name locally, same underlying
mechanics).
