## 4. "How can I sell my business in the most tax-efficient way possible?" — Colm Sheehan (Crowe Ireland), "A Question of Money," Business Post, 4 Oct 2026

Q&A column, Colm Sheehan (Corporate Finance Partner, Crowe Ireland), answering a 59-year-old insurance brokerage owner planning a sale within a few years, wanting to maximise after-tax retirement proceeds. Two distinct considerations named: (1) maximising gross disposal value, (2) managing the resulting tax liability efficiently.

**Maximising gross disposal value — five steps:**
1. Run a well-managed M&A process — a competitive process with multiple buyers creates tension and maximises value; engage advisers with specialist knowledge of the specific sector.
2. Prepare high-quality financial/operational information (financial statements, management accounts, client retention stats, compliance records, commission/income analysis) — reduces buyer uncertainty, supports higher valuation.
3. Develop a strong management team — businesses overly reliant on the owner carry more perceived risk; demonstrating the business can perform post-departure increases buyer confidence and broadens the pool of potential acquirers.
4. Maintain a clear growth strategy — buyers pay for future earnings, not just historical performance; a well-articulated plan (organic growth, cross-selling, recruitment, acquisitions, digital capability) supports a premium valuation.
5. Address potential risks before the sale — issues found in due diligence (regulatory compliance, customer concentration, insurer/supplier dependency, contractual arrangements, cyber security, governance) reduce value or delay completion if not addressed in advance.

**Managing tax liability:**
- **The tax outcome varies significantly depending on how a sale is structured — a share sale is often more tax-efficient for the owner than an asset sale**, though the optimal approach depends on specific circumstances, corporate structure, and buyer requirements.
- Planning years in advance provides greater flexibility and can significantly improve after-tax proceeds retained.
- **Retirement Relief and revised Entrepreneur Relief** can substantially reduce the tax burden on disposal — availability depends on age, shareholding, period of ownership and level of involvement in the business; reviewing eligibility well in advance allows time to address anything that could limit access to these reliefs.
- Pension contributions made before a sale can be a tax-efficient way to extract value from the business while improving retirement security — significant pension funding opportunities may exist depending on company structure and the owner's circumstances, particularly where pension provision has been limited historically.
- Overall framing: the best retirement outcome isn't just the highest sale price — it's ensuring proceeds are extracted and structured in the most tax-efficient manner possible.
