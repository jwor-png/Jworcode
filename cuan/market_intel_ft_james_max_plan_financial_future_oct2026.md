# "How much should you plan for your financial future?" (FT Opinion, James Max, Sat 24 Oct 2026)

Source: Financial Times, Saturday 24 October 2026, Opinion. Captured by photograph from John, 26 Oct 2026. Personal opinion column, UK context (sterling), no statistics; quotes and figures as printed, not independently verified. ## What it says

- **Premise:** the writer (56) has spent his adult life trying to become financially secure, with pensions, investments and property, but finds the future has other plans. Unbudgeted costs keep arriving: a hearing test leading to hearing aids costing about 5,000 pounds (he had guessed a few hundred), mice that ate electric cables and led to ceiling repairs and redecoration, then blocked drainpipes, a garage leak and rotted garden doors. "The cost of maintaining the ageing guy and his ageing pile is never-ending."
- **Against the tidy retirement model:** advice treats retirement as having a set date and a defined financial outcome, but things go wrong all the time. His father lived to 91, but vascular dementia meant two years in residential care; living long is not the same as living well or independently. At a wedding abroad one guest died unexpectedly; friends and family have faced cancer.
- **His conclusion:** plan and save, but do not deny yourself life's pleasures to fund an indefinite future; most of his predictions about how his life would turn out have been wrong. His "20 per cent rule" (offered as a personal habit, not advice): increase the budget by a fifth for the things that always exceed it, continue to plan, invest and maintain, and keep the concerts, holidays, cars and experiences. "The trouble is, [my future self is] expensive to run, his hearing is going, and the house needs more work."
