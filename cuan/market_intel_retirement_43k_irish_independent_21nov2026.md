# Irish Independent, Sat 21 Nov 2026, page 34, Indo Money: "Why do we need 43,000 euro annually to 'live comfortably' in retirement?" (Sinéad Ryan, Your Money)

Source: photographed newspaper page sent by John (21 Nov 2026). Figures as reported, not verified by Cuan.

## What it says
- **Poverty and pensions:** ESRI 2024 poverty report: 8.5pc of under 17s in consistent poverty against 2pc of over 65s. Policy aim: a universal state pension benchmarked at about a third of average wages.
- **State pension history:** IR£24.50 (31.11 euro) a week in 1980; IR£96 (121.89 euro) in 2000; 309.30 euro a week for 2027 (up 894pc against inflation of 367pc). Programme for Government: 350 euro a week by 2030. CSO consumer price index: what cost 100 in 1980 costs 446.10 today.
- **Pensions gap:** four taxpayers support each pensioner now, which could fall to 2 to 1 by 2050. 1.06 million pensioners in 2022, rising to 2.34 million by 2070, while the working-age population rises only from 3 million to 3.19 million. Governments ruled out raising the pension age to 70 (2011 legislation) and used small PRSI increases instead. My Future Fund (auto-enrolment) is a first step.
- **Expectations:** financial planner Eoin McGee no longer counts the state pension for anyone under 45. Royal London Ireland research (Mark Reilly): an adult needs about 3,575 euro a month (42,900 a year) to "live comfortably" in retirement; with maximum state provision of about 16,000 a year the pension fund needed is about 565,000 euro.
- **Time and compounding (Michelle O'Keefe, Goodbody Wealth Advisory):** 300 euro a month from age 25 gives about 445,000 at 65; the same from age 45 gives about 122,000: a gap of about 323,000 although the early starter paid only twice as much. "If you're already past 25, don't be discouraged": start now.
