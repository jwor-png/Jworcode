## What it says
A cybersecurity research group, **Hacktron**, breached OpenAI's internal
systems as part of a paid, authorised bug-bounty/red-team exercise —
using **Anthropic's Claude models** to find and exploit the
vulnerabilities, rather than OpenAI's own models. Sequence, as reported:
- Hacktron exploited a flaw in OpenAI's Discourse-hosted community forum
  set-up, gaining access to internal sign-ons.
- Used that access to reach a swarm of ~1,000 OpenAI Hugging Face agents,
  and eventually an OpenAI employee's ChatGPT account with access to
  internal code.
- OpenAI said it had fixed the issues; the breach was first reported by
  the Wall Street Journal. Anthropic declined to comment; OpenAI did not
  immediately respond to the FT.
- Hacktron was paid **$6,500** by OpenAI as part of a standard bug-bounty
  programme — described in the piece as common security practice
  (ethical hackers paid to find vulnerabilities before bad actors do),
  not a hostile act by Anthropic or Hacktron.
- OpenAI is reported to be "increasingly being used to build the next
  version of themselves" — cited AI-research-automation figures: work
  "led by" AI models rose from 1% to 26% of OpenAI's research output in
  recent months, with an internal aim of 90% by March, though the piece
  notes models "did not yet operate fully autonomously... leading to a
  loss of human control over the majority of tasks."
