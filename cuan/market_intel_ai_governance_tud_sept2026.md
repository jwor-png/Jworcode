## Opening framing — boards and AI risk

**Six things every board should do, per McGrath:**
1. Put appropriate AI/digital/cyber governance systems and policies in place.
2. Assign clear executive accountability for AI governance.
3. Maintain an AI inventory and classify material AI risk uses.
4. Assess board AI competence and AI training needs.
5. Review material AI activity, risks and assurance regularly.
6. Talk to your PI broker/insurer regarding cover for AI-related designs/products.

Core line: "Governance starts with verification, evidence and human accountability."

**Twelve questions for directors relating to AI:**
1. What are we legally required to do?
2. How do we operationalise the obligation?
3. Can we prove we comply?
4. Can we trace everything back to its source?
5. What could go wrong, before it does?
6. Are we seeing early signs of trouble?
7. Is the governance process actually being followed?
8. Who carries responsibility and legal liability?
9. What does this policy actually mean for our teams?
10. Are we aligned with the latest regulatory expectations?
11. What does the Board need to know right now?
12. Can we formally attest that we are compliant?

**Five "human gates"** — only humans should approve entry through:
1. Legal interpretation approval — is our reading of our obligations correct?
2. Design approval of automated controls — are the controls fit for purpose?
3. Risk acceptance approval — do we know and accept the risks involved?
4. Deployment approval — is this system cleared to go live, and where?
5. Regulatory attestation approval — can we formally certify to the regulator?

**Survey evidence cited:**
- BoardPro survey: 79% of boards use AI at least weekly; 44% of boards
  have no agreed position on AI; only 2% have a formal governance
  framework. Real quotes from boardroom directors: "We don't know what
  we don't know... even if we could see it, I'm not sure we would know
  what to do anyway" (Director/Trustee, Australia health/social
  services); "the governance scaffolding is still in draft" while
  "ungoverned AI use is already happening" (Board Secretary, NZ charity).
- Workiva 2026 Midyear Executive Benchmark Survey, "The Verification
  Gap": 1 in 4 of those surveyed say an AI error has already reached
  the board or investors.

**Recent AI cyber event cited:** the Hugging Face cyber incident (not
detailed further in the deck). Quote from Lian Jye Su, Chief Analyst,
Omdia (Irish Examiner, 18 Sept 2026): AI agents have become "more
determined to resolve complex tasks through inter-agent collaboration,
knowledge sharing, deception and concealment."

## EU regulatory landscape — full map

**AI-specific (EU 1 of 3):**
- **EU AI Act** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) — in force 1 Aug 2024;
  prohibited practices and AI literacy since Feb 2025; GPAI since Aug
  2025; Art. 50 transparency since 2 Aug 2026.
- **Digital Omnibus on AI** (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) — in force 27
  July 2026; defers Annex III high-risk duties to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex
  I to 2 Aug 2028. Note: the 2 Aug 2026 date many compliance calendars
  still carry has moved for high-risk systems only — transparency,
  prohibitions and GPAI duties were NOT deferred.
- **GPAI Code of Practice & Commission Guidelines** — voluntary
  soft-law route to demonstrating compliance for general-purpose AI
  models, policed by the EU AI Office.
- **Cloud and AI Development Act** — COM(2026) 502, proposed 3 June
  2026, not yet law (EU compute capacity, data-centre build-out,
  sovereign cloud/AI offer).
- **Apply AI Strategy / AI Continent Action Plan** — non-binding
  Commission communications on adoption, skills, infrastructure.
- **Product Liability Directive (recast)** (Directive (EU) 2024/2853)
  — software and AI systems expressly in scope as "products." Member
  State transposition due **9 Dec 2026** — Ireland's transposition
  status not yet confirmed in the deck.
- **AI Liability Directive** (COM(2022) 496) — WITHDRAWN by the
  Commission; no harmonised EU AI liability regime, national tort law
  and the PLD apply instead.
- **Machinery Regulation** (Regulation (EU) 2023/1230) — applies from
  **20 Jan 2027**; the Digital Omnibus moved the Annex I application
  date to 2 Aug 2028.

**Cyber security and operational resilience (EU 2 of 3):**
- **NIS2 Directive** (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) — transposition was
  due 17 Oct 2024; Ireland referred to the CJEU on 8 July 2026 for
  non-transposition; board-level accountability under Art. 20.
- **Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)** (Regulation (EU)
  2022/2554) — applies since 17 Jan 2025; ICT risk framework, incident
  reporting, resilience testing, third-party Register of Information.
- **Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) — Products** (Regulation (EU)
  2024/2847) — vulnerability and severe-incident reporting to ENISA
  from 11 Sep 2026; full application and CE marking from 11 Dec 2027.
- **Critical Entities Resilience (CER) Directive** (Directive (EU)
  2022/2557) — the "twin of NIS2"; Irish transposition still
  outstanding.
- **Cybersecurity Act** (Regulation (EU) 2019/881) — in force; ENISA's
  permanent mandate and EU cybersecurity certification framework.
- **Cyber Solidarity Act** (Regulation (EU) 2025/38) — in force;
  European Cybersecurity Alert System, Cybersecurity Emergency
  Mechanism, EU Cybersecurity Reserve.
- **eIDAS 2 — European Digital Identity** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1183)
  — phased; every Member State must offer an EUDI Wallet by 24 Dec
  2026, regulated sectors must accept it from late 2027.
- **Digital Omnibus (data and cyber strand)** — COM(2025) 837,
  proposed, still in negotiation; would amend GDPR, ePrivacy, the Data
  Act, NIS2 and DORA.

**Data, privacy and digital platforms (EU 3 of 3):** GDPR (in force,
under active amendment via the Digital Omnibus), ePrivacy Directive,
Data Act (applicable since 12 Sep 2025), Data Governance Act, Digital
Services Act (fully applicable since 17 Feb 2024), Digital Markets
Act, European Health Data Space (phasing from 2027), Open Data
Directive. Also flagged as relevant by sector: Financial Data Access
(FiDA) proposal, Interoperable Europe Act, Terrorist Content Online
Regulation.

**Sequencing — what's coming and when:**
- **2 Dec 2026:** AI Act new Art. 5 ban on AI generating intimate
  imagery/CSAM; Art. 50(2) AI-content marking due for systems already
  on the market; AI Office of Ireland information powers start.
- **9 Dec 2026:** Product Liability Directive transposition deadline.
- **24 Dec 2026:** every Member State must offer at least one
  certified EUDI Wallet.
- **2 Dec 2027:** AI Act Annex III high-risk systems (recruitment,
  credit scoring, education, essential services) become fully
  regulated.
- **11 Dec 2027:** full Cyber Resilience Act application; products
  that can't demonstrate conformity can't be placed on the EU market.
- **Beyond the horizon:** 2 Aug 2028, AI Act Annex I obligations for
  high-risk AI embedded in regulated products (medical devices,
  machinery, vehicles).

## Ireland-specific

**AI Office of Ireland** — established under the AI Regulation Act
2026, Paul Byrne appointed CEO, announced 30 July 2026 (with Minister
Peter Burke and Minister of State Niamh Smyth).

**Regulatory org chart (EU level → Ireland level):** European
Commission/EU AI Office, European AI Board (Member State
coordination), Scientific Panel, Advisory Forum all sit above the AI
Office of Ireland, which acts as central coordinating authority and
single point of contact. Below it: **15 National Competent
Authorities** covering sectoral oversight/market surveillance/
enforcement — Central Bank of Ireland, Coimisiún na Meán, ComReg,
Commission for Railway Regulation, Commission for Regulation of
Utilities, Competition & Consumer Protection Commission, Data
Protection Commission, Health & Safety Authority, Health Products
Regulatory Authority, Health Service Executive, Marine Survey Office,
National Transport Authority, Workplace Relations Commission, plus
the Minister for Enterprise/Tourism/Employment and Minister for
Transport. Of these, **13 are Market Surveillance Authorities (MSAs)**
and **4 are Notifying Authorities (NAs)**, with ComReg and HPRA
holding both roles (hence 13+4=17 role-instances across 15 bodies).
Separately, **9 Article 77 fundamental-rights authorities** exist as a
distinct role (An Coimisiún Toghcháin, Coimisiún na Meán, Data
Protection Commission, Environmental Protection Agency, Financial
Services & Pensions Ombudsman, Irish Human Rights & Equality
Commission, Ombudsman, Ombudsman for Children's Office, Ombudsman for
the Defence Forces) — some bodies (DPC, Coimisiún na Meán) hold both
an NCA and an Article 77 role.

**Key distinction:** Competent Authorities ask "is this AI system/use
complying with the AI Act?" — a formal regulatory/enforcement role.
Article 77 Authorities ask "is this use of AI interfering with
people's fundamental rights?" — they support their existing mandate,
are not the general AI regulator, but can access documentation and
request technical testing through the market-surveillance authority.

**Irish legislation in force today:** Irish Regulation of Artificial
Intelligence Act 2026 (No. 31 of 2026, commenced in part 31 July 2026,
establishes the AI Office of Ireland and the AI Act sanctions regime);
Data Protection Act 2018; Irish Digital Services Act 2024; Online
Safety and Media Regulation Act 2022; Coimisiún na Meán's Online
Safety Code (Part A 19 Nov 2024, Part B 21 July 2025 — age assurance
for pornography/gratuitous violence, parental controls, content
rating); EU Artificial Intelligence (Designation) Regulations 2025
(S.I. No. 366 of 2025, designates the 15 NCAs); NIS1 (still operative
pending NIS2 transposition); DORA.

**Legislation still in progress:** National Cyber Security Bill 2024
(Ireland's NIS2 transposition vehicle — places the NCSC on a statutory
footing, Head 28/Art. 20 gives management board approval and oversight
of cyber risk measures personal liability, draft penalties up to €10m
or 2% of turnover for essential entities; general scheme published Aug
2024, not enacted, Ireland referred to the CJEU 8 July 2026); CER
Directive transposition (overdue since 17 Oct 2024); recast Product
Liability Directive transposition (due 9 Dec 2026). McGrath's own
framing: "Legislative delay is not a grace period. NIS2 duties already
reach Irish firms through EU customers' contracts."

**Strategy, policy and guidance:** Digital Ireland: Connecting our
People, Securing our Future (National Digital & AI Strategy, 18 Feb
2026, 20 objectives/90 deliverables); National Cyber Security Strategy
(successor strategy committed for 2026); NCSC Cyber Governance
Guidance for Boards (published 7 July 2026 — see below); AI Regulatory
Sandbox (AI Act Art. 57, under establishment, now under the AI Office
of Ireland, ahead of the Dec 2027 high-risk deadline); Sectoral AI
Adoption Strategy (committed for 2026, sector uptake targets, AI
Sector Champions, an Enterprise Ireland AI Adoption Roadmap); Public
sector AI guidance (AI Advisory Unit, National AI Cyber Risk
Assessment); Central Bank of Ireland DORA materials (supervisory
expectations, Register of Information system guide). McGrath's own
note: "Guidance is not law, but it is the yardstick a regulator will
use after an incident. Board minutes should show it was considered."

## NCSC & NIS2 — implications for directors ("Management Boards")

National Cyber Security Centre guidance, **"Guidance on Cyber
Governance For Management Board Members In NIS2 entities,"** published
**7 July 2026**, acknowledged to the Institute of Directors in the
deck. Minister for Justice, Home Affairs and Migration Jim
O'Callaghan quoted: "Cybersecurity has evolved far beyond a technical
challenge handled in server rooms; it is now a fundamental boardroom
priority... NIS2 requires accountability at the highest level of
executive management."

Article 20 of NIS2 makes cyber-risk governance an explicit management
obligation: "Member States shall ensure that the management bodies of
essential and important entities approve the cybersecurity
risk-management measures taken by those entities in order to comply
with Article 21, oversee its implementation and can be held liable for
infringements by the entities of that Article." The Board is
responsible for ensuring effective internal control systems including
financial, operational and compliance controls and risk management,
reviewed annually.

**The guidance helps directors to:**
1. Understand NIS2 and their responsibilities as a Management Board member.
2. Recognise the business implications of cyber risk.
3. Ask the right questions to drive informed oversight and decision-making.
4. Establish an effective cyber resilience strategy for organisational preparedness.
5. Determine next steps to deliver resilient and secure services amid an evolving threat landscape.

## AI law and the construction industry — direct relevance to Meridian's sector work

**Role-based action table, per the deck:**
- **If you design** (engineering, architecture, QS practices) —
  already live: list where AI already sits in your design software,
  including features your vendor switched on without telling you; keep
  certification/sign-off with named people and record that the check
  happened; determine clearly where you're obliged to label
  AI-generated images/text; ask your insurer, in writing, whether your
  PI policy covers AI-assisted work.
- **If you build** (main contractors, site operations) — from **2 Dec
  2027**: recruitment screening, task allocation and productivity
  monitoring become high-risk (find out what you're already running);
  data protection and employment law apply to these tools today, not
  in 2027; check safety-monitoring products for emotion or fatigue
  detection — this is **already banned**; certain workplace
  emotion-recognition uses are prohibited subject to the AI Act's
  medical/safety exception.
- **If you make or modify** (plant owners, specialist subcontractors,
  offsite/modular manufacturers) — from **20 Jan 2027**: identify
  anything you place on the market, integrate or modify that uses
  machine learning in a safety function; treat a digital retrofit as a
  modification — fit AI guidance or collision avoidance to a machine
  and **you may become its manufacturer**; from Dec 2026 the Product
  Liability Directive treats software, including AI, as products
  (Ireland must transpose by 9 Dec 2026).

**Board questions for a construction company specifically:** Where is
AI used in our company, and who owns each use? What have we certified,
signed-off or warranted on the back of AI-assisted work? Which dates
apply to us, and who is tracking them? Does our insurance cover us for
AI-assisted work?

**Where it actually bites — four instruments reshaping digital/
software/AI-enabled products:**
- **Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, 20 Jan 2027** — safety
  components with fully/partially self-evolving (machine-learning)
  behaviour used to ensure safety functions are listed in Annex I Part
  A and require third-party conformity assessment — internal control
  will not do. A substantial modification, digital as well as
  physical, could make the modifier the manufacturer.
- **Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853, 9 Dec 2026** —
  treats software and AI as products however supplied; strict
  liability, rebuttable presumptions of defect, court-ordered
  disclosure of technical documentation; no escape where the defect
  stems from failure to supply updates within your control.
- **Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110** — applicable in
  stages since 8 Jan 2026; creates the legal basis for a
  machine-readable construction **Digital Product Passport**, intended
  to interoperate with BIM; duties arrive product family by product
  family via delegated acts; near-term effect is on data, not
  compliance.
- **Cyber Resilience Act (EU) 2024/2847** — live today; vulnerability/
  incident reporting to ENISA began 11 Sep 2026, full obligations and
  CE marking from 11 Dec 2027; relevant to building management
  systems, access control, smart metering, sensor networks and
  connected plant.

**NIS2 reaches construction firms through the supply chain** even
though Ireland hasn't transposed it — NIS2 clauses are already arriving
in contracts from energy, water, transport and health clients.

**Separately flagged, government procurement angle:** the Office of
Government Procurement's BIM mandate for public works contracts under
the Capital Works Management Framework reached **Milestone 7** (design
of projects >€5 million) from 1 July 2026; **Milestone 8** follows on 1
January 2027.

## UK and USA governance approaches (comparator material)

**UK — no AI Act, a deliberate choice, not a gap:**
- Five non-statutory principles (safety/robustness, transparency/
  explainability, fairness, accountability/governance, contestability/
  redress), applied by existing regulators (FCA, PRA, Bank of England,
  ICO, Ofcom, CMA, MHRA) in their own sectors — outcomes-based and
  technology-neutral, contrasting with the EU's risk-tiered horizontal
  regime.
- The principles themselves carry no direct legal force; binding
  obligations come from law of general application (UK GDPR, the Data
  (Use and Access) Act 2025 on automated decision-making, equality/
  consumer/product/online safety law) plus sector rulebooks (e.g. the
  FCA Handbook).
- Direction of travel: no AI Bill; DSIT's Blueprint (Oct 2025)
  replaced the AI Growth Lab with licensed sectoral sandboxes; a
  Regulating for Growth Bill expected before Parliament around late
  2026 (cross-economy sandboxing powers, not an AI Act); Parliament's
  Treasury Committee criticised a "wait-and-see" stance in Jan 2026.
- Scope caution: FCA/PRA expectations bind authorised financial
  services firms only; for every other UK company the binding
  constraints are data protection/equality/consumer/product law. A
  UK-headquartered group placing AI-enabled products on the EU market
  is still caught by the EU AI Act regardless.
- **FCA's specific expectations** (confirmed it will NOT write
  AI-specific rules): four existing binding obligations apply —
  Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR, using AI does not
  transfer accountability to the model/vendor/IT function), Consumer
  Duty (bias, poor explainability and AI-shaped customer journeys are
  all in scope), governance/systems/controls (proportionate oversight
  of model risk, data quality, testing, human review, explainability),
  operational resilience (concentration in a handful of model/cloud
  providers is "the live systemic concern"). The 6 July 2026 Mills
  Review (FCA's forward look to 2030) made seven recommendations
  covering the regulatory perimeter, autonomous/agentic AI, and
  accountability for redress.
- For the board: "ask who is the named accountable individual, what
  they are accountable for, and what evidence reaches the board.
  Automation does not dilute responsibility."

**USA — no federal AI statute, policy vs. law in open conflict:**
- Federal layer is policy, not law: Congress has passed no
  comprehensive AI statute; direction set by executive action
  (America's AI Action Plan, July 2025; Executive Order 14365,
  "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for AI," 11 Dec 2025, created
  a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state laws). Pre-emption
  of state AI law is proposed, not achieved — as of Sept 2026 no
  federal statute or court ruling has displaced any state AI law. NIST
  AI Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001 remain voluntary but
  useful evidence of structured governance.
- State layer is where duties actually sit: Texas (TRAIGA/HB 149, in
  force 1 Jan 2026, prohibited uses with a safe harbour for NIST-
  aligned firms); California (SB 53 frontier-model transparency, AB
  2013 training-data disclosure from 1 Jan 2026, AI Transparency Act
  operative 2 Aug 2026, automated decision-making rules phasing in
  from 1 Jan 2027); Illinois (AI in employment decisions from 1 Jan
  2026); Utah (consumer disclosure); New York City (bias audits for
  hiring tools); Colorado (its 2024 AI Act was **repealed before it
  ever took effect** and replaced in May 2026 by a narrower
  transparency regime, SB 26-189, effective 1 Jan 2027).
- Volatility is the point: roughly 1,500 AI-related bills introduced
  across 45 US states by March 2026 (not all will be enacted).
  Colorado's repeal-and-replace of "one of the country's most
  prominent state AI laws" before its principal requirements ever took
  effect is the headline illustration.
- **Board accountability, near-term exposure is not an AI statute —
  it's fraud, discrimination, disclosure and oversight law applied to
  AI:** Delaware's Caremark line of cases requires directors to make a
  good-faith effort to put an information/reporting system in place
  for mission-critical risks (and since the 2023 McDonald's decision,
  that duty reaches officers too — directors aren't expected to
  understand the model, they're expected to show the board was
  informed); SEC's Cyber and Emerging Technologies Unit treats
  overstated AI claims ("AI washing") as an enforcement priority, with
  private securities litigation alleging AI misrepresentation rising
  sharply; FTC Act Section 5 reaches deceptive/unfair practices
  regardless of AI involvement, alongside unchanged sectoral law
  (employment discrimination, fair credit reporting, HIPAA, state
  insurance rules); NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 are voluntary but
  now "evidentiary, not merely good practice" — what a regulator,
  plaintiff or acquirer will measure an organisation against.
- For the board: "the question a US court or regulator will ask is not
  'was the model correct?' but 'what system did the board put in place
  to know?' That evidence has to exist before the incident."

## Standards and international guidance

- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** — AI Management System standard. Flags AI-
  specific management considerations beyond classical IT systems:
  automatic decision-making that can be non-transparent/non-
  explainable; data-analysis/machine-learning-driven design (versus
  human-coded logic) changing how systems are developed, justified and
  deployed; continuous-learning systems that change behaviour during
  use requiring special ongoing oversight.
- **OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible AI** (revised May
  2026) — a detailed cross-mapping table (Step 2: identify/assess
  actual and potential adverse impacts) showing how it aligns with the
  EU AI Act, EU DSA, EU CSDDD, ASEAN Guide, Australia's AI Adoption
  guidance, Canada's Code of Conduct, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 42005,
  Japan's AI Guidelines for Business, Korea's AI Basic Act, Singapore's
  AI Verify Testing Framework, UK DSIT's AI Assurance Framework, UN
  Guiding Principles, and the US NIST AI RMF — i.e. a genuine
  Rosetta-stone document across international frameworks.
- **MIT AI Risk Initiative / MIT FutureTech — "Prioritization of Risks
  from Artificial Intelligence: A Delphi Study of 272 International
  Experts"** (June 2026). Ranks risk categories by mean severity
  (Business as Usual vs. With Pragmatic Mitigations) and separately by
  catastrophic-risk percentage. Highest catastrophic-risk categories
  (Business as Usual → With Mitigations): dangerous capabilities (22%
  → 12%), weapons & cyberattacks (21% → 12%), power centralization
  (18% → 11%), environmental harm (17% → 12%), inequality &
  unemployment (17% → 11%), AI misalignment (17% → 9%), competitive
  dynamics (17% → 7%). Lowest: transparency & interpretability (8% →
  5%), unequal performance (9% → 5%). The study's core finding
  visualised: pragmatic mitigations meaningfully reduce catastrophic-
  risk percentages across essentially every category, not just the
  highest-risk ones.
