# Business Post batch 5, 11 Oct 2026

## 1. "The AI-equipped banker is much better than someone who's not using the tools" — Laura Trimble, CEO, HSBC Ireland (The Big Interview)

- Trimble, HSBC Ireland CEO since 2022, reports a marked **increase in
  interest from Asian firms** (China, India, elsewhere) looking at
  Ireland for European expansion — "there was a lot more interest...
  versus times when I'd been two, three years ago" — a trip to China
  in May found firms "definitely starting to see a little bit more
  interest in companies in other parts of the world, like Asia."
  Official data backs this: Ireland hit its €100bn two-way-trade-with-
  Asia target two years ahead of schedule (2023).
- **HSBC's own AI/staffing framing, directly relevant:** "Our view is
  not that AI will replace jobs here," Trimble insists, describing the
  bank's approach as **"carrot versus the stick."** Core line, headline
  quote: **"I think it's much more clear to clients now versus, say,
  three, four, five years ago, actually what HSBC does, doesn't do,
  what's the strategy, what do we want to offer for our clients."**
- **On AI specifically:** "the AI-equipped banker is much more
  productive... because you can imagine, ultimately, the first person
  is going to be fundamentally much more productive than the second
  person [who isn't using the tools]. For us, it's about fuelling
  growth." Morgan Stanley forecast cited: **more than 200,000 jobs in
  European banking could come under threat by 2030** from AI, but
  Trimble frames HSBC's own approach as productivity-and-growth
  focused rather than headcount-reduction focused — "I would expect...
  that we will be able to grow with our clients, availing from better
  technology, which by extension probably means you don't need to
  increase headcount to such a degree," rather than active replacement.
- **Context on HSBC Ireland's own restructuring:** under CEO Georges
  Elhedery (Lebanon-born, appointed 2024), HSBC has aggressively
  restructured to eliminate complexity, cut costs and reorient around
  the activities/geographies that are most profitable — merged or
  exited some operations, withdrawn entirely from investment banking
  in the UK/US/Europe, sold its hedge fund administration business.
  HSBC Ireland now has ~280 staff in Dublin, down from 400 in previous
  years.
- Trimble: "I don't see any particular risk around the US corridor...
  Dublin has many, many benefits as a business location."

**Cross-reference:** another named, senior financial-services executive
reinforcing the "AI augments productivity, framed as growth not
replacement" positioning already well tracked across this whole AI
market-intel thread — directly consistent with Meridian's own
governance-first framing and useful as a citable example for Velocity
AI/Ambrion AI training content given it's a globally recognisable bank.

## 2. "'Completely incorrect': Echelon rejects data centre's 'sweetheart deal' claim" (Donal MacNamee)

Direct rebuttal story, tightly linked to yesterday's Vincent Boland
data-centre-opposition column (`market_intel_business_post_batch4_oct2026.md`)
— genuinely useful as the "other side" of that argument.

- **Cormac Nevin, Director of Power, Planning and Utilities at
  Echelon** (data centre operator), rejects claims — reported by
  utilities and lobbying/opposition entities — that data centres are
  getting a "sweetheart deal" on their energy bills, calling the
  suggestion "completely incorrect."
- **Nevin's core counter-argument:** decisions on how the cost of
  expanding the grid gets spread across consumers are made by the
  energy regulator, not by data centre operators — "the cost of
  'building for a few very large users' is spread across the common,
  captive base of ordinary consumers, rather than changed to the
  users whose demand drives it" is the complaint he's rejecting as a
  mischaracterisation of how regulatory decisions actually work.
- **Transparency criticism levelled back at opponents:** Nevin
  accuses "industry associations or opposition entities" lobbying on
  this of "challenging these and doing a very good job at fostering
  the narrative that it's getting caught up in the average price of
  power" without proper fact-checking — "there's a few very large
  users whose consumers are paying for it, that is incorrect."
- **On secrecy allegations specifically:** Nevin pushes back on
  "secretive" characterisations of the industry, noting Echelon has
  made complaints about noise levels in the area public, engaged with
  residents in west Dublin (Clondalkin) directly, and "as an industry,
  it's been very secretive in the past" is itself an inaccurate,
  outdated claim — citing recent reports, including a September MEP-
  commissioned report (Lynn Boylan, Sinn Féin) that Nevin says did
  not specifically mention data centres but has been used in recent
  backlash against data centre facilities regardless.
- **Specific, concrete example given:** Echelon is at the centre of a
  live noise-complaints dispute in Clondalkin (west Dublin) related
  to its Dublin10 data centre, with Eirgrid, Uisce Éireann and local
  residents all engaged in the dispute; Nevin described proposed
  new rules on compliance with noise standards as "international
  discourse" such that Ireland risks over-restricting operators
  beyond what's typical internationally.

**Cross-reference:** directly extends yesterday's Boland data-centre-
opposition column — this is the **industry's own direct rebuttal**,
giving Tairseach/Shane a genuinely balanced two-sided view of the live
data-centre-land debate rather than only the critical framing. Worth
holding both pieces together when assessing the data-centre-land
thesis: real local opposition exists and is organised, but the
industry's own position is that the regulatory/cost-allocation
criticism specifically is being mischaracterised.

## 3. "Unclear if €1bn scale-up fund will seed a new generation of Irish champions" (Donal MacNamee)

- **The new scheme:** Finance Minister Paschal Donohoe (via Budget
  2027) announced Ireland's "biggest ever investment in scaling," to
  be delivered in coordination with Enterprise Ireland — **ISIF
  (Ireland Strategic Investment Fund) will allocate an average of €333
  million per year until 2030** toward this, administered as part of
  a new Strategic Investment Fund structure aimed at "creating an
  environment in which Irish businesses remain rooted in Ireland but
  can scale-up and grow internationally."
- **The structural problem the piece identifies, stated plainly:**
  "the problem, then, is to everyone... the question is whether more
  state backing for Irish companies represents the best solution" —
  Ireland's venture-capital environment is already "pretty clear" that
  most active Irish investor backing is thin: **Enterprise Ireland is
  already the most active venture capital investor in Europe, having
  backed more than 2,000 deals between 2016 and the first half of
  2026** — i.e. state capital is already doing most of the heavy
  lifting, raising the question of whether more of the same solves a
  genuinely different problem (scale-up capital, not seed capital).
- **Named data points on the funding gap:** a 2025 report commissioned
  by the Department of Enterprise found Ireland's private equity
  investment in Ireland struggles to reach the growth-capital stage
  that founders need — the Global Startup Ecosystem Index ranks
  Ireland **16th out of 100 countries** on fostering start-ups, a
  "good job at stimulating entrepreneurship," but with **a significant
  obstacle for Irish founders with ambitions of growing** once past
  the earliest stages.
- **A separate named document, the "funding gap" study:** identified
  a **€1.1bn "funding gap" for Irish companies** struggling to raise
  the private equity investment they need to become global growth
  companies, with ambitions.
- **Named early-stage investors/market-watchers quoted:** Fiona Kelly
  and Fionan Leonard, founders of independent early-stage VC Baseline
  — "it's not clear how its [the new fund's] exit constraints are
  likely to become more acute over time, as the data — rising interest
  rates also likely to become harder to come by all contribute" to
  Irish funding constraints being different, often faced by all
  company owner-founders rather than by investors specifically.
- **Historical framing cited directly:** CRH's and Ryanair's own
  "national champion" status is argued (per the piece) to show
  "Ireland's scaling process — the country struggles" with — is where
  the next generation of businesses needs support; Ireland has "plenty
  of culture and national champions" historically but it's unclear
  whether state capital alone can replicate that for the current
  generation of founders.
- MacNamee's own closing framing: Harris/Donohoe still has "plenty
  more to do to prove he can create his world-class national
  champions."

**Cross-reference:** directly relevant background for **ODIN and
Tairseach's own funding environment** — this is the structural, Irish-
specific funding-gap context behind why ODIN is pursuing international
(US, Saudi) capital rather than relying on Irish institutional
sources, and why Tairseach's own raise explicitly already targets
international conversations (per the one-pager already logged: "America,
Australia, the Middle East and Germany"). The €1.1bn funding-gap figure
and the Enterprise Ireland "most active European VC investor" fact are
both strong, citable data points worth holding alongside the existing
Enterprise Ireland AI Summit figures.

## 4. "UCD launches Ireland's first AI research institute" (Laura Roddy)

- **University College Dublin** launching Ireland's first
  **university-led AI research institute**, officially launching
  Thursday at the **European AI in Science Summit**, held at UCD as
  part of Ireland's presidency of the Council of the European Union —
  1,000 scientists, policymakers and technology leaders expected,
  with Higher Education Minister James Lawless and Enterprise Minister
  Peter Burke both attending.
- **Focus areas:** health, climate change, science and agricultural
  discoveries. An AI design studio will be established where computer
  science engineers work alongside scientists, ethics experts and
  academics across disciplines.
- **Named leadership:** led by **Robin Burke**, Professor of Computer
  Science at UCD (formerly University of Colorado Boulder), whose
  research focus is personalised recommendation systems, algorithmic
  fairness and transparency. **Orla Feely**, UCD President: "AI was
  beginning to change not only what we can discover, but how discovery
  itself happens... universities have an important role in ensuring
  that capability reaches researchers across every discipline."
- **Funding structure:** core operations funded by the university;
  public research bodies, philanthropic organisations and industry
  partners will fund individual projects and PhD programmes. UCD has
  not disclosed total investment in the initiative.
- **Existing UCD AI use cited as a base to build on:** improving
  diagnosis of pre-eclampsia during pregnancy, advancing weather
  forecasting and flood prediction.
- Burke's own framing: "our model is about bringing AI expertise
  directly alongside researchers. A scientist, clinician, agricultural
  researcher or humanities scholar can bring us a research question
  and work with experts to explore how AI can help them answer it."

**Cross-reference:** general Irish AI-ecosystem awareness, relevant
background for Meridian's AI Strategy & Governance domain and the
agribusiness sector lens specifically (UCD's named agri-discovery
focus area) — a concrete, citable example of Ireland's institutional
AI investment to hold alongside the Enterprise Ireland Summit figures
already logged.
