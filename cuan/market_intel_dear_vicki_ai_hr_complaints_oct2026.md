# "Dear Vicki" — AI-generated HR complaints, 19 Oct 2026

**The question:** an HR professional at a large Dublin firm reports a
rise in workplace grievance claims generated via ChatGPT after staff
disputes with managers — "10 pages of mumbo jumbo... cut-and-pasted
out of a chat with a bot," one-sided, framed favourably to the
complainant. Asks whether to issue a company-wide notice discouraging
AI use for this purpose.

**Vicki O'Toole's answer, key points:**
- The real issue is the volume/pattern of grievances, not that people
  are using ChatGPT to write them — a sudden rise in complaints
  warrants investigation regardless of drafting method (which
  department, which manager, working-conditions changes).
- **A complaint can't be dismissed simply because it was drafted with
  AI** — nor is an AI-generated argument, however persuasive, proof of
  wrongdoing or a substitute for proper legal advice; AI can only
  reflect the one-sided account fed into it.
- Recommends against banning/discouraging AI use outright — instead,
  review the grievance procedure itself for clarity/accessibility,
  encourage employees to explain the issue in their own words, and
  keep managers addressing disagreements promptly rather than letting
  them fester into formal complaints.
- Explicit framing: "AI may be adding to the HR department's
  workload, but it's unlikely to be the real problem."
