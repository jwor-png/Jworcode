## 3. The core point

A supermarket owner who plans to hand the business to family, or sell it, usually meets the legal process cold. The buyer's solicitor raises **requisitions on title** after the contract is agreed, when most of what they ask can no longer be fixed cheaply or quickly. Succession planning (who inherits, when, with what tax position) and requisitions readiness (is the property and the business in a state a buyer's solicitor will accept) are normally handled separately, by different advisers, at different times.

The value is in the overlap. Each of the following is a legal-readiness gap that also changes a financial or succession decision:

| Gap | Legal side (requisitions) | Financial and succession side (DSB's picture) |
|---|---|---|
| Boundaries and title defects | Buyer's solicitor requires them resolved or the price is reduced | A defect can change the basis of a valuation, and can block a gift or transfer to a successor |
| Planning and building control compliance for the site and any extension | Compliance certificates and permissions are asked for | Missing documents reduce the number of buyers, and can delay a transfer to family |
| Family law and spousal consents | Consents may be required before a sale or transfer | A refusal or a dispute undermines the succession plan itself |
| Lease terms and symbol-group (franchise) agreements | Assignment and change-of-control conditions | Limits who can buy, and when; links directly to timing and value |
| Employee transfer | Buyer's solicitor asks about transferring staff | Cost and timing; Irish TUPE equivalent applies |
| Environmental history (forecourts) | Environmental enquiries | A potential liability that may reduce price |
| Tax history, VAT and capital allowances | Tax clearance and history asked for | Succession and sale both depend on a clean history |

## 4. A readiness checklist, in plain language, for a client to use years before a sale

**Important:** the categories below follow the standard Law Society style of requisitions on title and the categories John's Julianstown file used. The actual Julianstown document (Micheál Glynn & Co., Replies to Requisitions on Title, Folio 72142F, Co. Meath, August 2023) was **not readable by Cuan** (it is a PDF attachment on John's Gmail thread "Julianstown Contract Deposit", which Cuan's tools cannot open). The categories must be checked against that document before this goes to Ger.

1. **Title and boundaries.** Does the owner hold clean registered title? Do the physical boundaries match the map? Are there rights of way, wayleaves or encroachments?
2. **Planning and building control.** Is every building and extension permitted, with commencement and completion certificates on file?
3. **Environmental.** For forecourts and older sites: tank registration, testing history, any contamination reports. (Irish statutory position **UNVERIFIED**: this needs a legal check before it goes further than "get an environmental survey done".)
4. **Tax.** VAT history, capital allowances claimed, tax clearance, any outstanding liabilities.
5. **Family and spousal.** Whether spousal or family consents are needed, and whether any family member holds a right or claim over the property.
6. **Services and wayleaves.** Water, drainage, electricity and access rights.
7. **Litigation and judgments.** Any pending claims or registered judgments.
8. **Statutory compliance.** Fire safety, licences, food and weights compliance, as they apply.
9. **Employees.** Contracts, accrued service and the position under S.I. No. 131/2003 (the Irish regulations on transfer of undertakings), including the 30-day consultation requirement. (**VERIFIED**: Irish Statute Book and WRC guidance.)
10. **Leases and franchise agreements.** Term, assignment, change-of-control and any consent the symbol group requires. (Musgrave's actual transfer terms **UNVERIFIED**: no published agreement found.)
11. **Goodwill and business assets.** What is being sold separately from the property, and how it is documented.
12. **Directors and company secretary.** Statutory duties that bear on a sale: accurate registers, filings up to date, authority to sign.

## 5. Where the readiness picture and the succession picture meet

The point Ger can use with a client: **the same document gap can hurt the sale price, the succession plan and the tax position at once.** Examples to verify with real client files, not claims about any named business:

- An unresolved title or boundary issue that would change the valuation basis and also hold up a transfer to the next generation.
- A franchise or lease condition that restricts who the owner can sell to, and so affects the realistic price range and the timing of a family handover.
- A spousal or family consent that is missing, which neither the valuation nor the succession tax plan would flag.
