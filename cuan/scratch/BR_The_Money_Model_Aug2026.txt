    THE LESSON FROM THE WINNERS
    One side pays commission where the value lands. Every transaction carries a small technology fee
    that funds the machine. And subscriptions come later, where the data earns them. A market like this
    has never existed in barbering. We’re building it, and new markets learn new prices when the value
    is obvious. The entry point, though, is always set by what the trade will carry on day one.



The floor is Johnny’s
And that entry point comes from real conversations across the trade, held over months, not from a
spreadsheet: shop owners are slow to pay for systems. Twenty-five to thirty-five pounds a month is what
a shop will carry as a membership today. And a barber won’t pay a penny simply to exist on a staffing
platform. That read is built into every number in this document. The model starts where the trade stands,
and grows into what the platform proves.

    T H E N U M B E R T H AT FA C E S I N V E S T O R S
    Every price in this model stands on its cost line. And from the first live booking we track one number

    commission with the technology fee clears its direct costs several times over; the pilot turns that
    sentence into a measured figure. A model that knows its own margin per transaction is a model an
    investor can fund with confidence.

And here’s the part worth sitting with: the same engine that costs money mints the second asset. Every

moving, and who shows up. The cost line and the data asset are one machine seen from two sides, and
both compound from the first week.

How the Republic earns
Four layers, in the order they switch on. The first two carry the pilot. The third matures
with the data. The fourth arrives with scale.

1    Commission on the covered day
     The spine. The shop pays when the chair is filled and the day is done, a percentage of the day rate.
     Urgency carries a premium, because an empty Saturday chair costs a shop five to seven hundred
     pounds in lost takings, and saving that day is the most valuable thing the Republic does. The commission
     prices the day saved, not the software used.
       Standard 10%       Urgent cover 18%         VIP house call 22%
       Opening dials · settled with the trade at visit one


2    The technology fee
     The Uber mechanic, native to us. A flat pound or two on each booked day, and it funds the intelligence
     that made the match. It lands on a day the platform has already paid for: a barber carries it only when the
     Republic has put a full day rate in his pocket, and a shop sees it beside a chair that would otherwise have
     sat empty.
       £1 to £2 per booked day       Tested with the trade before it’s set


3    Membership where it earns its place
     No barber pays a membership for staffing. That holds, permanently. It’s Johnny’s read of the trade and
     it’s built into the architecture, because the supply side is the scarce side and the Republic pays barbers,
     it never taxes them for existing. The shop side starts inside the twenty-five to thirty-five a shop already
     carries, and it matures into something no one else can sell: the intelligence subscription. What a chair
     should cost on a Saturday in this postcode. Who’s reliable before you’ve ever met them. Where demand
     moves next. When the data density is there, that’s what a shop pays monthly for.
       Shops £25 to £35 a month         Barbers £0, always, for staffing


4    The layers that come with scale · Phase Two onward
     Coaching and the credential under Johnny’s name, mentor days booked through the platform, corporate
     venues, brand partnerships in front of a verified professional audience. All real, and all priced later, after
     the exchange proves itself. They go on the record now as the expansion surfaces round two buys into,
     never as the money that carries year one.


    THREE QUESTIONS FOR THE TRADE · JOHNNY’S GROUND
    Only Johnny can ask these right, privately, owner to owner and barber to barber. Asked over the
    coming weeks, they price the dials before a line of payment code gets written. One: would you pay
    thirty pounds, today, to save a six-hundred-pound Saturday? Two: a couple of pounds on a day
    you’d never have had, fair or not? Three: would you pay monthly to know what a chair should cost,
    and who actually shows up?

    T H E S PA C E T H AT ’ S O U R S
    Every one of them books appointments, runs a diary, or fills generic shifts. Not one grades craft with
    an expert’s eye. Not one runs verified day cover for the chair. And not one holds an intelligence
    layer on the trade itself. The fees above prove the trade already pays for technology, worldwide,
    every month. The ground we’re taking is the ground none of them stand on, and it’s empty.


What the pilot puts on the table
Round two is bought on measured facts, and the pilot is built to produce exactly four: the fill rate and the
speed to fill. The margin on a covered day. How much of the trade rebooks through the platform. And the
engine’s reach, the same architecture re-aimed at hairdressing, at high-end bar staff, at every trade where
an empty seat costs real money and cover is worth paying for. Barbering proves the machine, with the
strongest name in the industry behind it. The machine is the investment.
