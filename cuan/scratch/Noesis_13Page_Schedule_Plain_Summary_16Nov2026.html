The 13-page schedule in plain language

Noesis group: agreements, insurance and banking, version 2 of 12 Nov 2026, sent to Shane 12 Nov. For John only. Not legal, tax or insurance advice. Items marked UNVERIFIED in the schedule still need a solicitor, broker or the bank.

What it is for

A checklist of the paperwork, protection and banking the group needs in place before the Asterial investment arrives, so nothing is rushed once the term sheet lands.

1. Agreements: who signs what

  • Each person gets written terms: Shane, you, Pat Carroll, Tom Stapleton, Diarmuid Hudner, then staff, freelancers, advisers and the introducer. Typical documents: service agreement, director letter, IP assignment, conflicts declaration.
  • IP assignment matters most. Anything built for the group must be assigned in writing and signed. Shane's ODIN IP is the key one.
  • Any deal between a director and the group (for example Shane's ODIN consideration, or Pat's firm's fees) must be declared and approved by someone independent.
  • Group-wide policies: intragroup services, IP licence, signing limits, conflicts, sanctions, data protection, AI use, whistleblowing, compliance calendar.

2. The investor side

  • The London law firm acts for the investor, not for Asterial. Asterial needs its own solicitors.
  • A UK investing company owned by a Saudi family still counts as a non-EU investor under Ireland's investment screening rules; ask for the full ownership chain. Irish counsel decides whether a filing is needed.
  • The term sheet should name the investing company, any guarantor, proof of funds and a funding timetable, and name who may speak and sign for the investor.

3. Insurance

  • A company cannot fully promise to cover its directors' liability, so a directors and officers (D&O) policy is the real protection. One group policy, every company insured, new companies added automatically, you covered for outside roles.
  • Also: cyber, crime and payment fraud, professional indemnity for AI errors, employment claims, key person, public liability.
  • Bind the cover before completion, ideally before the group trades or hires. Marsh Dublin (Mark Doherty) is the proposed broker.

4. Banking

  • Each company has its own accounts, resolutions and minutes. Asterial holds the investment; Noesis and Fabrica AI first to open accounts.
  • Order: incorporate, file beneficial ownership, then apply. Banks want original documents and a face-to-face meeting.
  • Twelve board resolutions (R1 to R12): board rules, open accounts, signing matrix, treasury policy, payment controls, independent reviewer, intragroup agreements, related-party items, subscription and allotment, standby bank, receipt of funds, monthly treasury item.
  • Proposed roles: Shane signs; Pat prepares payments and reports; Tom runs tokens and fraud controls; you chair, certify minutes and review; no one releases a payment to themselves.

5. Who is whose employee

Shane and Tom: Noesis and Asterial. Pat and you: Noesis only. Diarmuid: Asterial only. Asterial needs its own secretary.

6. Order of work

  • Now: broker, employment solicitor, Asterial's own solicitors, ask the London firm for the investor details, approach banks.
  • Before the term sheet: draft letters and IP assignments, D&O quotes, Irish investment-screening advice, resolutions R1 to R7.
  • At the term sheet: name the investor entity, agree funding and insurance terms, resolutions R8 to R10.
  • Before completion: sign everything, bind insurance, test the accounts with a small payment.
