## Like-for-like comparison traps

1. **Fitzgerald Power's headline saving is real but the firm is
   materially smaller** (~100 people vs Mazars' 950+/BDO's/RSM's
   international scale) — the cheaper price likely reflects genuinely
   lower overhead and a boutique/SME-audit specialism, not
   necessarily reduced service, but bench depth for a complex,
   multi-entity, cross-border group is the fair question to test
   directly rather than infer either way.
2. **BDO's tax-fee discrepancy (€16,250 vs €17,500+5%) needs
   resolving before BDO's total can be trusted at face value** — could
   go either direction.
3. **RSM's headline total already includes their most generous
   "no-charge for ad-hoc advice" framing**, which is a genuine value
   point but not separately costed, making true cost-of-service
   comparison harder than the raw totals suggest.
4. **Mazars' iXBRL exclusion and RSM's onboarding fee are both
   realistic extras that would apply on top of any multi-year total**
   — worth asking both firms to quote an all-in figure rather than
   comparing headline totals as if they were final.
5. **BDO's explicit first-year "unaudited opening balance" flag on
   two entities is actually useful honesty, not a weakness** — but
   it's unclear whether the stated €72,500 audit fee already reflects
   that extra first-year work or whether it will be billed as a
   change/overrun once work starts. The other three proposals are
   silent on this point rather than explicitly addressing it, which
   isn't necessarily better — it may just mean it isn't priced in and
   would surface as a surprise later.

## What's missing from Dave's criteria, worth weighing separately

- **Audit rotation/independence governance** — Mazars is the only one
  that proactively addresses it (partner rotation already built into
  their proposal). Worth asking the other three directly about their
  own rotation policy for UHL specifically, not assuming parity with
  Mazars on this point.
- **Group consolidation experience specifically** — all four quote
  consolidation fees, but only Mazars and RSM explicitly reference
  multi-entity/complex-group audit experience in their own words;
  worth asking Fitzgerald Power and BDO to speak to this directly
  given UHL's post-Expert-Hardware group structure.
- **Continuity through an auditor change** — none of the three
  challengers (Fitzgerald Power, BDO, RSM) addressed handover risk
  from the incumbent (Mazars) in what was extracted; worth asking
  directly, since a change this year would be a first-year transition
  regardless of firm chosen.

## Omitted/unasked — surfaced 13 Oct 2026, against a stated May 2027 AGM requirement

**Confirmed, not a discrepancy:** John, as Chair of the Audit & Risk
Committee, confirms this is a genuine cycle change directed by the
UHL Chairman (Eoin McGettigan) — the AGM moves from the prior
September pattern (2026 AGM held 30 Sept) to **May 2027**. This is
now the hard deadline every firm's completion timeline must be tested
against. **None of the four firms was tendered against this date** —
it needs putting to all four directly as a named requirement, not
inferred from generic "agreed timetable" language.

**Criteria in the Committee's own assessment document that NONE of
the four proposals actually answered:**
1. **Professional indemnity insurance level/cover** — zero mentions
   in any of the four, despite being named explicitly under "Risk
   management and service continuity."
2. **Business continuity / disaster recovery arrangements** — same
   criterion, same silence across all four.
3. **References from comparable clients** — "References and track
   record" carries real weight in the evaluation table; none of the
   four named a contactable referee or comparable-client case study.
4. **Conflicts of interest / independence — Mazars and Fitzgerald
   Power are silent on this entirely.** Only RSM and BDO address it
   (BDO has a dedicated section). Notable gap for Fitzgerald Power
   specifically, given they're the firm actually under live
   consideration on price.

**Gaps specific to the May 2027 AGM requirement, if confirmed real:**
5. **Only Fitzgerald Power gives an actual calendar commitment**
   (first week Feb through 15 April, weekly status updates). The
   other three (Mazars, BDO, RSM) speak only of "agreed timetables" in
   the abstract, with no dates pinned down — none has actually
   committed to a date that would guarantee a May sign-off.
6. **No firm specifies a penalty or service credit if the timeline
   slips** — the one failure mode that would actually breach a hard
   AGM deadline, and nobody was asked to address it.
7. **Transition/handover risk from the incumbent (Mazars) is
   unaddressed by all three challengers** — a change of auditor in
   the same cycle as a hard AGM deadline is precisely when transition
   risk is most likely to cause delay.

**Action, not just observation:** this should be the lead question put
to all four firms at the Teams presentations — "Can you commit, in
writing, to audit sign-off in time for a May 2027 AGM, and what would
you need from management by what date to guarantee it?" Of the four,
only Fitzgerald Power has volunteered a calendar specific enough to
test against this (first week Feb–15 April); the other three need to
be pushed to the same level of specificity before the fee comparison
can be treated as comparing equivalent commitments.

**The real deadline is tighter than "May":** UHL's AGM notice
requirement is a minimum of 21 days between signed-off accounts and
the AGM itself (confirmed by John). So the actual audit-completion
deadline sits roughly three weeks before whatever date in May the AGM
is held — effectively late April/early May, not "sometime in May."
Hold each firm to that specific figure, not the looser monthly target.

## Bottom line for the Audit & Risk Committee discussion

Not a simple lowest-price decision — per the Committee's own
assessment-criteria document: *"A proposal should not be selected
solely because it has the lowest fee... independence and audit
quality should be treated as minimum requirements rather than areas
that can be offset by a low price."* Fitzgerald Power's saving
(~€30-36k/year vs the other three) is large enough to deserve serious
consideration, but given UHL's scale (post-Expert-Hardware, ~200
stores, €500m+ turnover, NI exposure, ongoing group consolidation
complexity), the honest next step is testing Fitzgerald Power's depth
directly in the Teams presentation rather than assuming either way,
and resolving BDO's tax-fee inconsistency before including BDO's
total in any like-for-like ranking.
