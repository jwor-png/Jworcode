# "Are you waiting to have your child assessed for ADHD?" (Irish Independent, Health & Living pp30 to 31, Mon 26 Oct 2026; Eleanor Flegg)

Source: Irish Independent, Monday 26 October 2026, pp30 and 31. Captured by photograph from John, 27 Oct 2026. Figures and quotes as printed; not independently verified. General health-service and health-tech reference; no personal inference is drawn from John sending it.

## What it says

- **Waiting times and routes:** ADHD assessment waiting lists range from one to three years depending on where you live. Two routes: (1) the HSE through CAMHS (free; the "postcode lottery"; referral from a GP, assessment by a multidisciplinary team including a consultant psychiatrist or clinical psychologist); (2) private assessment, about 600 to 1,500 euro (a clinical psychologist can diagnose but not prescribe; a consultant psychiatrist can also prescribe and manage medication). Medication can only be started by a psychiatrist after formal diagnosis.
- **Scale:** ADHD accounts for one third of CAMHS referrals. At ADMiRE, a specialist ADHD service at the Linn Dara CAMHS centre in South Dublin, referrals rose 400% between 2020 and 2025. The condition affects 5 to 7% of people worldwide but only 1 to 2% of Irish children are diagnosed (Dr Jane McGrath, Trinity College Dublin, consultant child and adolescent psychiatrist with the HSE). She attributes the rise to awareness, reduced stigma and chronic under-diagnosis. On screens: they do not cause ADHD but do matter for behaviour and sleep.
- **No national standard pathway:** there is no national standardised clinical pathway for assessment and treatment in Ireland; the HSE has implemented a national clinical programme, and a model of care is under development. Diagnosis rests on interviews and information from family, teachers and old school reports; there is no blood test or scan.
- **The app:** in 2018 McGrath created a step-by-step pathway for ADMiRE. In 2023 ADMiRE received 50,000 euro from the Public Service Innovation Fund to digitise it. **Zendra Health** (Irish company; twin brothers David and Thomas Coleman from Cork, working together nine years; Thomas CEO, David CTO) built **Zendra Navigator**, co-designed with the HSE, clinicians, parents and young people. It has been in use at Linn Dara CAMHS for a year; the platform is licensed to the public health sector (HSE in Ireland, NHS in the UK) and used by public and private clinics in both countries; private clinics pay a monthly subscription by size; free to patients, parents and families.
- **What it does:** replaces the paper-based protocol: sends families lists of tasks and information requests (entered securely by parents, teachers and the young person), auto-plots height, weight, blood pressure and heart rate to flag trends and link them to medication prescribing, reduces paperwork. It does not reduce clinician time per visit but speeds the diagnostic process. Being adapted for autism and other neurodevelopmental pathways (a private autism assessment costs 1,000 to 2,000 euro, per AsIAM).
- **Results:** at Linn Dara, Navigator sped up ADHD diagnosis by 33%, but the waiting list is unchanged because referrals keep rising.
- **Comment:** Ken Kilbride (CEO, ADHD Ireland): CAMHS is under-resourced and operates two waiting lists (immediate care and the rest); ADHD is not a mental health challenge and needs a "neurodiversity one-stop-shop"; the funding gap dates to 2008 and the real issue is the lack of clinicians. HSE: demand has increased significantly; a focus is clear, consistent pathways; cases not needing CAMHS can be coordinated by the GP or primary care. More than 80% of children with ADHD have a co-occurring condition (autism, dyspraxia, learning difficulties, language disorders, anxiety), but autism and ADHD are assessed separately. Adults: CSO Healthy Ireland report (2025): 2% of adults had an ADHD diagnosis but 9% thought they might have it (world average 5 to 7%).
