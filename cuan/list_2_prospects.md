## Why 22, not 100

Roughly 65 targeted searches were run across CeADAR/EDIH case studies, the Google–Bank of Ireland SME AI Academy, Enterprise Ireland/LEO grant and digital-transition programmes, sector press, and industry awards — specifically hunting for genuine, checkable AI evidence per company rather than sector-inferred prospects. Only 22 real, named companies with a traceable AI-relevant source came out of it.

Reaching 100 at this evidence bar would require either:
1. **Direct access to non-public sources** — CeADAR's full ~90-company member list, Enterprise Ireland's Digital Transition Fund grantee register, or LEO regional grant-approval records — none of which are published in searchable form, or
2. **Accepting a materially higher share of sector-reasoned entries** — which is exactly the padding this revised approach was meant to avoid.

Two names were investigated and excluded on inspection: Buymedia (Digital Transition Fund success story) is itself an AI-powered ad-tech platform — a peer, not a buyer, per the exclusion criteria. Healthdaq and WorkJuggle, genuine CeADAR case studies, are themselves software/platform businesses selling to others — excluded as peers. Glenhaven Foods had strong AI evidence but was acquired by Sweden's Scandi Standard in 2026, making it a multinational subsidiary — excluded on ownership grounds.

## Research notes

**What worked well:** CeADAR's EDIH-for-AI "Test Before Invest" case-study series was the richest vein of genuine evidence (Atomic, Pearl & Dean Ireland, Trinity Finances — named, dated, technically specific AI projects). The Google/Bank of Ireland "SME AI Academy" surfaced several small but genuinely AI-adopting businesses with named tools and named owners. Kepak's AI governance rollout — independently reported, explicitly referencing EU AI Act risk classification — is the strongest single find in either list and speaks directly to the governance/compliance angle.

**Where coverage was thin:** insurance, financial services beyond fintech, construction, logistics, and law produced essentially nothing usable — extensive searching returned only generic industry-trend content or large multinational examples that fail the exclusion criteria. Regional spread is weak (Leinster/Munster-heavy); no verifiable AI-specific evidence was found for a genuinely Ulster-ROI or Connacht-based company beyond OpenHive's multi-location footprint.

**Evidence mix vs. List 1:** the ratio delivers on the brief — 45% AI-specific here vs. ~19% in List 1 — but volume is the real shortfall, not quality.

## Caveats before outreach
1. Several entries are quite small (BKultured, McNally Family Farm, Lahinch Surf Shop, OpenHive) — genuine AI evidence, but arguably below the mid-market bar for a governance/board-education pitch. Worth a judgement call: prospects, or proof points to cite when pitching others.
2. Kepak and Dawn Meats are large private companies, not mid-market — included because ownership is a reachable family/private structure; flag before pitching as "SME."
3. Trinity Finances' exact office location is unconfirmed — verify before outreach.
4. All adjacent-signal and sector-reasoned rows should get a fresh verification/LinkedIn check before outreach — current trading status wasn't independently reconfirmed beyond the cited source.
