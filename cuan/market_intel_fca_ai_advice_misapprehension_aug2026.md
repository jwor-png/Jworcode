# "Young Britons think AI advice is regulated" — FT

Source: Financial Times, Madeleine Wright, 29 August 2026, citing the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), Lloyds Banking Group's 2025 survey, Fairview Investments (Ben Yearsley), Willis Towers Watson (Mark Atkinson), Nottingham University (Prof John Gathergood), the CFA Institute (Rhodri Preece), the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS), and the World Economic Forum's Global Retail Investor Survey 2024. VERIFIED as reported — a mainstream financial-press piece citing named regulators and researchers, not independently re-checked by Cuan against primary FCA data.

## What it says

- Almost half of young adults in the UK mistakenly believe financial information generated by AI is regulated, per the FCA — a gap between actual regulation and what people believe, which is growing as AI tool use for financial decision-making increases.
- Among 18-24-year-olds, 76% have used AI for personal finance, compared with 47% of 55-64-year-olds (Lloyds Banking Group 2025 survey).
- 44% of 18-40-year-olds surveyed by Fairview Investments thought AI chatbots were regulated, and 32% wrongly thought they would get compensation if the AI advice went wrong.
- FCA-regulated firms must flag when AI tools are being used for "narrow point" information to help a decision, but a clear risk-information gap exists between those who can afford regulated advice and those who cannot.
- Thirty-eight per cent of 18-28-year-olds surveyed by Wisr Alliance said they were willing to trust AI investment returns within a year — a sign young people take a short-termist approach to investment, misunderstanding realistic timeframes (per Prof John Gathergood, Nottingham).
- Gen Z investors are notably impatient: per the World Economic Forum's 2024 Global Retail Investor Survey, 30% of Gen Z expect significant investment returns in less than six months, compared with 20% of millennials, 15% of Gen X, 9% of boomers (narrowing with age). One in five Gen Z and 15% of millennials expect quick returns within a year.
- Gen Z and millennials are also more likely than older generations to invest in complex/unregulated products — crypto, sizeable returns on social media "stories," asset classes and derivatives, alternative investments — per Rhodri Preece (CFA Institute).
- FSCS: only regulated financial advice/institutions are protected; AI chatbot advice or unregulated "financial influencer" guidance carries no compensation protection. Cited case: an individual lost £85,000 to bad financial advice from a UK-regulated institution that went out of business after April 2019 (FSCS protection scope limitation cited as a comparator case, not an AI-related loss itself).
