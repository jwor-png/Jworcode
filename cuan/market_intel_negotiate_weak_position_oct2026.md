# "How to Negotiate From a Weak Position" — business__infographics

## What it says

Seven-part cheat sheet for negotiating without strong leverage:

1. **Understand your true position** — be honest about leverage before negotiating: who needs the deal more, who has better alternatives, more time, more information, who can walk away more easily. A relative-leverage gauge runs from "they need you more" to "you need them more." With no BATNA, focus on creating value, managing perception, and finding alternatives before making commitments.
2. **The Hormozi Value Equation** — Perceived Value = (Desired Outcome × Certainty) / (Time Delay × Effort). To increase value: deliver a better outcome, raise certainty (reduce their risk). To reduce friction: shorten time to result, reduce effort required on their side.
3. **The 3 layers of negotiation** — Show (visible: some constraints you can reveal, reasonable interests/goals); Manage (partially visible: non-critical weaknesses, flexible trade areas); Protect (hidden: no BATNA, true limits, internal pressure or deadlines — never reveal these).
4. **Which weaknesses to show (and hide)** — safe to show: a stated preference, non-critical constraints, high demand elsewhere. Never show: no alternatives/no BATNA, internal pressure, desperation.
5. **Create leverage without a BATNA** — even with no real alternative, you still have levers: information (find something they don't know), time (slow things down or create urgency), competition (introduce optionality even early-stage), tradeoffs (swap across price/scope/timing/terms), risk reduction (make their decision easier/safer), reframing (shift perspective from price to total value).
6. **Difficult situations — what to say instead:** "Can you give us a chance?" → "What would make this worth considering for you?"; "We can do it cheaper" → "Is there anything your current option doesn't handle well?"; "That's our best price" → "If we adjust X, would you be open to Y?"; "We need this deal" → "We're committed to making this work. What would need to change on your side?"
7. **Build a BATNA in parallel** — always be creating options even during the negotiation itself: explore alternatives, test the market quietly (soft conversations), keep multiple options alive, improve your offer based on what you learn.

**Core reminders:** perception is power, don't reveal you have no alternatives; create value instead of just asking for a lower price; trade, don't concede — every give should get something back; if you must show a weakness, show a controllable one; the goal isn't to win this negotiation at any cost, it's to stay in a strong position over time.
