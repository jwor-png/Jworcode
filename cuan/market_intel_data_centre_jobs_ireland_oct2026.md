# "Almost 5pc of Irish job ads are now linked to data centres"

Source: print article, John Burns, citing Indeed (Pawel Adrjan, senior director of economic research), the ESRI, and Bitpower (consulting company). REPORTED — figures as attributed to named sources, not independently re-verified by Cuan.

## What it says

- Almost 5% of job ads in Ireland now relate to data centres, the highest proportion among 10 European countries analysed by Indeed — reflecting US hyperscaler AI investment.
- Data-centre job postings rose sharply from early 2026, now over twice early-2020 levels, following the Government's December decision to lift a four-year effective moratorium on connecting new data centres to the electricity grid.
- Taiwan microchip/processor imports more than quadrupled in six months due to a few multinationals' activity; ESRI noted non-pharma exports exceeded pharma exports in Q2 2026.
- Data-centre job postings are above pre-Covid levels in all 10 countries analysed, while other tech-role postings remain below pre-Covid levels. Just over half of data-centre postings are for IT infrastructure, software development and project management.
- Indeed: data-centre-related searches account for 1 in 2,000 of total Irish searches in August, more than double the share of any other country analysed — "fits the bill for a country where AI adoption is already among the highest in the world."
- European data-centre roles pay substantially more than elsewhere, at levels similar to the US and Canada — reflects Europe racing to catch up on AI infrastructure; the European Commission has proposed tripling EU data-centre capacity within 5-7 years.
- Ireland has 108 operating data centres (Bitpower), 9 under construction, 38 with planning approval.
- Data centres used 23% of Ireland's electricity last year (up from 5% in 2015), expected to exceed 30% by 2030 — considerable political opposition exists due to energy-supply demands.
