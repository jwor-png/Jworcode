# "Over 3,600 firms struck off this year as CRO steps up enforcement" — Irish Independent

By John Mulligan. The Companies Registration Office (CRO) has
significantly escalated involuntary strike-off enforcement in 2026.

## Key facts

- **3,600+ companies struck off since the start of the year**, across
  sectors (hospitality, car sales, pharmaceuticals, property) — mostly
  for **failing to file legally required documents**, most often
  **annual returns**, for years.
- Some struck-off firms were already in liquidation/receivership, or
  effectively dormant.
- **CRO has been targeting up to 1,000 companies a week** for
  potential strike-off action over failure to file annual returns on
  time.
- **The process, step by step:** warning email to the company's
  registered email address → **10 weeks later**, an involuntary
  strike-off notice by registered post to the company's registered
  office, and by ordinary post to directors and the company secretary
  at their home addresses → struck off **two months after** the
  notice, unless remedial action (typically filing the late annual
  returns) is taken.
- **Scale of the CRO's own mailing operation:** a near-€600,000
  contract to print and post 216,000 letters (54,000 registered,
  162,000 ordinary) — undertaken because an **IT issue had blocked
  automated involuntary strike-off for over a year**, i.e. this is a
  backlog-clearing enforcement push, not routine-pace activity.
- **Consequence of involuntary strike-off:** the company **ceases to
  exist as a legal entity**, and any assets legally become **property
  of the State**. The CRO may also notify the **Corporate Enforcement
  Authority**, and directors of struck-off firms could face
  restrictions or disqualification by the High Court.
- Peak week so far: **190+ firms dissolved in the week of 28 August
  2026.**
- Named examples: Waterford News & Star Ltd (est. 1909, struck off
  this year, had been in receivership since 2013); WB Crawford
  (Dublin) Ltd (est. 1923 by Arthur Crawford, struck off July 2026 —
  2007 turnover €12.6m, went into receivership 2009).
