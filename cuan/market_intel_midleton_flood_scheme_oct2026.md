# "Midleton flood relief scheme to go to planning soon" — The Echo

Source: The Echo, Paul Hosford, 2 October 2026, citing Cork East TD James O'Connor and OPW figures. VERIFIED mainstream press report.

## What it says

- The long-awaited Midleton flood relief scheme plan will go to An Coimisiún Pleanála for review by year end, per TD James O'Connor — not yet submitted to planning as of this report, expected "before the end of the year."
- Storm Babet (October 2023) damaged 600 homes and 300 businesses in Midleton; the OPW overall damage cost estimate is between €180m and €200m.
- Scheme cost: estimated €55m, expected to protect 750 properties including all homes and businesses affected.
- Flood protection works seek a comprehensive solution addressing fluvial and tidal flooding vulnerabilities.
- Separately: in the northern side of town, a Tír Chluain area of Midleton flood-gate works (€5.8m for Part 8, received planning approval January 2026) are nearing completion — flood gates delivered, 94% of ground investigations complete, works expected to begin March 2027, completing by 135 homes on the north side by date given.
- OPW approved €5.8m to the Storm Babet flood relief fund for Cork County Council; over 80% of allocated individual property protection funds have been spent to date, remainder due mid-November.

## Update, 20 Oct 2026 — "Still no planning application for Midleton flood relief scheme" (The Echo, Amy Campbell)

**The pattern of slippage continues, and has worsened.** Minister of
State at the OPW, Kevin 'Boxer' Moran, said earlier this month that
Cork County Council will formally lodge the planning application "at
the end of the year" — the same "end of year" framing already logged
above from the 2 Oct article, **but this is now the third stated
deadline in succession**: previous OPW minister Kieran O'Donnell had
said early 2026; in July the same minister (or office) said September;
now "end of year" again. As of this article, **there is still no
planning application lodged at all**, more than three years after
Storm Babet (18 Oct 2023).

**New complication, not previously logged:** the council has found
**traces of asbestos** in the area where they were planning to dig,
further delaying this "most straightforward" of the various Midleton
flood-protection elements (the one where all land needed was already
council-owned).

**Community reaction:** ~500 people attended a recent protest in
Midleton over the delays. Caroline Leahy (resident, home severely
damaged in 2023, protest organiser) quoted: "At this point, we don't
have any trust in the system that anything will ever be done to
protect our homes." ~600 homes and ~300 businesses remain impacted;
outlying villages won't see protection until the main Midleton scheme
itself is finished, even where some homes have flooded three times.
Ms Leahy features in a documentary, *Ireland Under Water*, which aired
20 Oct on Virgin Media One, examining whether planning/housing/flood
policy is keeping pace with climate change.
