## 1. Career break and pension impact (Charlie Weston, "Your Questions")
Reader query: mother of two, 15 years full-time work, considering a
5-year career break. Shane Tobin (CEO, True Financial, Portlaoise)
answers: a 5-year break can cost missed personal + employer pension
contributions plus lost investment growth. Example given: stopping
€200/month personal contributions for 5 years means €12,000 less
invested; if the break runs age 35-40, those missed contributions could
otherwise have grown to ~€46,000 by 65. If the employer also pays
€200/month and stops during the break, the combined gap could reach
~€92,000. Practical steps recommended: get written confirmation from
employer/pension administrator of what stops/continues; distinguish
maternity leave, additional unpaid leave and career break (different
treatment); check PRSI record and state pension caring supports
(qualifying periods caring for children under 12 or a dependant can
protect state pension entitlement, though don't replace workplace
contributions); on return, confirm payments restart and employer
contribution is reinstated.

## 2. Health insurance switching (same page, Dermot Goode, HealthInsuranceIreland.ie)
Confirms switching health insurer is safe even with underlying medical
conditions, provided continuity of cover — full credit for prior
membership if switching from your renewal date, immediate cover if 5+
years continuous membership. Caveat: the "two-year upgrade rule" means
any *new, additional* benefits in the new plan aren't accessible for
existing conditions for 2 years; existing conditions remain covered at
the previous plan's benefit level in the interim. No venture tie,
general household-finance reference.

## 3. As the ECB hikes rates, is it time to fix your mortgage? (Sinéad Ryan, "Your Money")
ECB raised rates by 0.25 points on 10 September 2026, citing Middle
East conflict-driven inflation pressure remaining "well above target
for an extended period." ~70% of Irish mortgages are on a fixed rate
(one to five years); 94% of new mortgages in July were fixed, the
highest on record. Mortgage switching up 36.1% year-on-year per
Banking and Payments Federation Ireland. Advice: start with your own
bank for a requote based on current LTV; check green-loan eligibility
if BER improved; compare non-bank lenders via brokers (they can have
higher rates but more flexible/niche products); use CCPC and Bonkers.ie
comparison sites. Example cited: €200k mortgage on a €500k house, 18
years remaining, 4% variable — at least four lenders currently beat
that fixing for three years (Avant Money 3.45%, AIB 3.5%, PTSB 3.65%,
MoCo 3.7%). Also flagged: review life/home insurance at the same time,
since one in four mortgage holders mistakenly believe they must buy
insurance through their mortgage lender (per broker Gallagher research)
— it is not a requirement and often not the most competitive option.

## 4. French central bank chief warns of debt "stranglehold" (Donal O'Donovan)
Bank of France governor Emmanuel Moulin warned France must avoid a
sovereign debt crisis and should not expect the ECB to fix French
budget issues; French 10-year borrowing costs hit 4.7%, the highest
since the 2008 financial crisis, amid a global bond sell-off, budget
concerns, and France's seismic April-May 2027 presidential election
with far-right/far-left candidates prominent. Moulin said reaching for
ECB support "reflected flawed reasoning" — fixing the deficit sits with
national governments and parliament, not the ECB. General
macro-economic reference, no direct venture tie.
