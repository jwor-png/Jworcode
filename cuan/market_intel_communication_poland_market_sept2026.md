## 1. Gina London — "How to tell your team how much they're valued... and have them believe you"
Practical framework for executive presentations that land, built around
eight numbered principles (paraphrased):
- **Find your money lines** — every presentation should have several
  "money lines" (memorable, quotable phrases) that deserve extra
  emphasis, not delivered at the same flat energy as everything else.
- **Let your voice carry the message** — vocal variety (pace, pitch,
  emphasis, pauses) helps listeners distinguish the important from the
  filler; watch "ums" and "uhs," which dilute confidence.
- **Remember who's in the room** — tailor the message to the specific
  audience and what becomes important to *them*, not a generic version
  of the presentation.
- **Start with the punchline** — lead with the achievement/conclusion,
  then take the audience through context and process, rather than
  building up to it.
- **Prefer to get there faster** — if presenting something extraordinary
  or accomplished, lead with that achievement rather than the backstory.
- **Audience isn't bored yet, but might be soon** — executives often
  repeat the same material to different audiences; find a fresh way in
  each time rather than running the identical script.
- **Hearing your name and a specific contribution recognised by a
  senior leader** is something people remember long after the rest of a
  roadshow/presentation fades — personal, specific acknowledgement beats
  generic praise.
- General framing: presentation skill signals to an audience "how much
  care and attention" a speaker gives to communicating meaning, which in
  turn shapes how much the audience trusts/believes what's said.

## 2. Enterprise Ireland — "Booming Poland the perfect launch pad for Irish firms"
**John Cosgrave**, EI's Overseas Manager for Poland/Ukraine/Baltics.
Promotional/partner content framing Poland as a growth market for Irish
firms:
- Poland's economy is "booming," acting as a hub for Central/Eastern
  Europe supply chains and Ukraine reconstruction/reconstruction-adjacent
  business, with GDP growth converging toward Western European
  standards.
- Recent EI-supported Irish trade activity: Warsaw Agro Show (one of
  Europe's largest agricultural expos, up to 120,000 visitors) —
  Irish agri machinery/equipment firms exhibited (JFC, Abbey Machinery,
  McHale, Ireland's Samco among named stands); a separate EI trade
  mission to the Polish agri sector.
- DCN (Data Centre Nation) Expo, Warsaw, early October — Enterprise
  Ireland exhibiting alongside Irish companies in cloud/data
  infrastructure-adjacent sectors.
- Named sectors Poland is building capacity in: life sciences,
  engineering, education, consumer retail, smart manufacturing and
  renewable energy — cited as relevant to Irish companies seeking
  Warsaw-market entry points, local offices/support, and market
  research.
- Notable named projects/employers: a new nuclear power station in
  Pomerania; construction/materials firms (Smurfit WestRock, Zeus
  Packaging, CRH, PM Group, Kingspan, Tzaogas as printed) already
  active in Poland; Taiwanese Foxconn/Ingrasys-scale industrial park
  development near Wrocław; Intel's originally-intended (per the
  clipping, "originally intended for") plant near Wrocław; Warsaw's
  first commercial nuclear plant construction starting this year; a new
  "mega-airport" being built between Warsaw and Łódź.
- EI Poland/Baltics team hosting an Irish client network dinner in
  Warsaw on 12-13 November, alongside the Rebuild Ukraine event
  (attracting up to 10 Enterprise Ireland client companies).
- Cosgrave's advice for Irish firms entering Poland: most people speak
  English which "makes it straightforward for Irish businesses to build
  relationships," but a local office/on-the-ground presence and local
  introductions are important for credibility and navigating the
  market; EI can support market research and local introductions
  (enterprise-ireland.com/en).
