# "7 founder truths" — Instagram carousel, 12 Oct 2026

All 7 slides captured. Generic founder-advice content, but several
points land directly on live, founder-stage ventures already tracked.

1. **Win or learn, never win or lose.** "Founders run one bad beta
   test and shut the whole product down. Maybe the test was run
   wrong, maybe the spend was too small, but either way the result
   was data and they treated it as a verdict." Do this: write down
   what the failure taught before allowing any decision about the
   product.
2. **Problems are the job.** "You get paid to solve problems, so
   problems landing on your desk every morning is the work
   functioning correctly. Founders who expect a clean week are the
   ones who quit in month four." Do this: stop reading resistance as
   evidence you picked the wrong business.
3. **All of it is your fault.** "The agency that took your money, the
   product nobody wanted, the team member who stole from you... that's
   the only version of the story where you have any control." Do
   this: after every mistake, isolate the part that was yours and fix
   that one thing before the next round.
4. **Work one constraint at a time.** "At any given moment there is
   exactly one bottleneck stopping the business from growing. It isn't
   your website, your manifesto or your HR documents." Do this: read
   funnel data every week, find the single worst drop-off, work on
   nothing else until it opens.
5. **Raise before you build.** "Elon raises hundreds of millions
   before anything exists. Money arriving that early means the idea
   was compelling enough and the founder had enough conviction to sell
   it with nothing to show." Do this: get good enough at explaining
   the business that someone would fund it on the explanation alone.
6. **Money is the moat.** "Founders hunt for a technical moat when the
   real protection is capital. With money you can outspend, outgrow
   and outlast every smaller competitor in the category." Do this:
   treat fundraising as a growth strategy, not the thing you do when
   you run out of cash.
7. **Stop grading your own homework.** "What got you here will not get
   you where you're going. First-time founders don't have the
   instincts of someone who's done it before, and reading your own
   numbers with first-time instincts is how you talk yourself into the
   wrong six months." Do this: put your proforma, your niche and your
   beta results in front of someone who has launched already.
