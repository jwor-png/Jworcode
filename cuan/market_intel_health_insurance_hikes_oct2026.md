# "Seven ways to beat the health insurance hikes" — Gabrielle Monaghan

Source: Sunday Independent Money section, citing named sources (Patrick Brennan, head of health and wellbeing strategy at NFP Ireland; David Allen, managing director of Letsbuyhealthcare.com; Brennan quoted again on cross-border care). VERIFIED mainstream personal-finance feature.

## What it says

**Context:** health insurance premiums have risen sharply. Weighted average premium for Q2 2026 reached **€1,950**, up from €1,448 in Q2 2022. Average person pays €1,448 by comparison noted for context (figures as stated in the photographed text, some sideways/partially legible). Number of people with private health cover dropped by 1,663 in September alone, per the Health Insurance Authority.

**Seven ways to beat the hikes:**

1. **Downgrade to a basic plan** — may allow immediate savings on premiums from opting for lower cover; self-paying for certain scans can save money versus a comprehensive plan covering everything.
2. **Mix self-pay with the public system** — access to healthcare through your GP (consultations, tests, X-rays/scans) are free or low-cost through the HSE for many; public hospitals provide inpatient/outpatient services, though waiting lists can be long. Patrick Brennan (NFP Ireland): "Ireland has a range of healthcare supports available, many of which are underutilised or poorly understood." Losing access to healthcare benefits if your financial circumstances improve in the future could face a penalty — a Lifetime Community Rating loading applies (introduced 2015) to encourage people to take out cover younger — a 2% loading for every year you're uninsured over 34 when you re-join, up to 13 years before cover lapses for more than three months, after which it may be more affordable to re-enter without penalty depending on circumstances.
   - **Private consultant/scan example:** might pay €250-€350 for a private consultant or scan; with health insurance you might get 50-75% back depending on your plan, so "you're paying out of pocket anyway" in some cases.
3. **Claim tax relief** — once you've paid for a GP, private consultant, or non-routine dental treatment, you can claim back **20%** of health expenses from Revenue, in real time via Revenue's MyAccount portal (no need to wait until the tax year ends) — seen as an increase in tax credits rather than a refund.
4. **Build up a health nest egg** — set up a health-specific savings account (instant-access, interest-bearing, via a neobank) and pay out of pocket for consultant visits/diagnostics. A cooperative, **Letsbuyhealthcare.com**, has taken this further — lets anyone buy €50 "healthcare coins," then book individual treatments online with private hospitals, GPs and consultants through its portal. David Allen (managing director): buying vouchers rather than saving of your own accord means you're committed to saving for healthcare — "because the product is structured so that the vouchers can only be used for healthcare, it means you can't raid it if the boiler breaks down, or you want to go on holidays." Targets "the one million people in the 'twilight zone' who can neither afford health insurance nor qualify for a medical card." Day-case treatments (gallbladder removal, hernias, tonsils, cataracts) account for 70% of private hospital activity and are the main use case — "you then rely on the public system for the more serious stuff that can happen, such as cancer or a heart attack, which is where you want to be anyway" (Allen).
5. **Get a GP card** — if you've had to let health insurance lapse, you may be entitled to a free GP visit card, saving €50-€80 per visit depending on location. Means-tested — HSE considers income, monthly expenses, personal circumstances. Children under 8 also entitled to free GP visits, including out-of-hours GP services.
6. **Travel abroad for certain treatments** — unlikely to save enough for major procedures like a knee replacement (~€14,000), but under the **EU Cross-Border Healthcare Directive**, public patients can bypass Irish waiting lists by travelling to another EU country and reclaiming the cost from the HSE (e.g. knee/hip replacements). Patrick Brennan: "Many consumers are unaware that they may be entitled to travel elsewhere within the EU for certain medical treatments and seek reimbursement from the HSE under the Cross-Border Healthcare Directive... While there are restrictions and administrative requirements, it can provide another option where waiting times in Ireland are lengthy." Companies like Healthcare Abroad Ireland and Trasna liaise with the HSE/clinician, handle paperwork, scheduling, travel, post-operative care; some providers can help arrange a credit-union loan for initial costs. Example: Healthcare Abroad teamed up with a Spanish hospital group in 2022 for routine surgeries for Irish patients in Alicante.
7. **Get a cash plan** — HSF Health Plan pays money back on GP/consultant/dentist visits, medical tests, day-case surgeries, physiotherapy. Individual plans start at €18.78/month. Run by a charity (the Hospital Saturday Fund), so not subject to multiple price increases a year like standard insurers. Includes HSF Assist: 24-hour GP advice line, virtual doctor, counselling service. Online claiming available via app.

## Background context (Monaghan's framing)

Sláintecare, Ireland's "glacially slow" universal-healthcare reform programme, is "starting to reap benefits for patients" — introduction of public-only consultant contracts has reduced the "morally dubious practice" of private consultants operating in public hospitals. Private-sector waiting times have been growing too, due to a shortage of private consultants and an ageing population driving demand. Self-insuring (paying entirely out of pocket) is flagged as high-risk if major hospital treatment is needed — "self-paying can work well for consultations, diagnostics and lower-cost procedures, consumers should remember that major hospital treatment can become prohibitively expensive if funded entirely out of pocket" (Brennan).
