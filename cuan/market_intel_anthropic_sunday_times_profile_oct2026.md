# "How Anthropic went from zero to $2 trillion in five years" — Danny Fortson

Source: The Sunday Times Business section. VERIFIED — named journalist, a profile piece drawing on named sources/quotes (Dario Amodei, Jared Kaplan, employees). **Confirms the $2 trillion figure** that appeared in the earlier, badly-photographed FT Weekend piece (`market_intel_anthropic_ipo_weirdness_oct2026.md`) — the companion "AI anxieties" summary logged alongside that piece had instead suggested ~$1tn; this Sunday Times profile, clearly legible, confirms **$2 trillion** is the figure being reported for the IPO target, correcting the earlier note that $1tn was more reliable.

## What it says

**Scale and speed:** Anthropic went from a zero valuation at founding to a targeted **$2 trillion IPO valuation in five years** — "the problem of all the problems here is that it's happening very fast," per a quoted Anthropic insider. Revenue context cited: Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate jumped from $1bn to $9bn in the past year alone (12-month period), with a next jump to $40bn+ targeted.

**Founding story:** Dario Amodei (43, Anthropic co-founder and CEO), along with his sister Daniela Amodei (president) and co-founders including Jared Kaplan (chief scientist) and Jack Clark (co-founder, head of policy), left OpenAI in 2021 over disagreements about the pace and safety of AI development. Described as having a "cultish" Silicon Valley belief system around AI's transformative potential and risk, with a central tenet that humanity is "a few serious decades from disaster," and that Anthropic's founders see themselves as among "the few at the frontier" tasked with saving humanity from the very technology they are building — the article frames this as the company's own "total paradox."

**S1 filing (IPO registration document) detail:** filed last week, ~$4.6bn in revenue for the year (filing shows $3.2bn to $4.6bn range depending on section read — sideways photo, treat as approximate), expenses far higher. Described by the article as "one of the largest" filings in SEC history by page count — "6,000-page document," compared to roughly 2,600 pages for Reddit's. Anthropic's valuation is reported to be worth more than the combined value of Ireland's largest publicly traded companies (comparator used by the piece for scale, exact Irish companies not specified in legible text).

**Explicit "existential risk" warning in the S1 itself** — the filing reportedly discloses the risk that Anthropic's own AI systems could pose catastrophic or existential risks, described by the article as highly unusual for an IPO prospectus to include. This directly corroborates the "AI anxieties" summary from the earlier FT Weekend capture (same claim, now independently confirmed via a second, clearly-legible source).

**Culture and pace:** article describes extreme internal pace — compared to building "a skyscraper in a weekend." Cites employees' own accounts of gruelling hours, references to "monks and coaching people to end their lives" (likely a garbled OCR of a longer quote about burnout/extreme dedication culture — **this specific line is unreliable, flagged as a legibility issue, not to be relied on verbatim**).

**Key product/history milestones referenced:** ChatGPT's November 2022 launch described as catalysing the AI boom broadly; Claude (Anthropic's own model/chatbot) and the company's positioning on "safety-first" development versus faster-moving competitors; a reference to Claude Code as a specific product generating significant revenue growth (consistent with Cuan's own infrastructure, which runs on Claude).

**Risk/criticism framing:** the piece situates Anthropic within a broader "doomerism" narrative (consistent with the earlier Richard Waters FT piece already logged) — employees and critics quoted suggesting the pace of development itself is the central risk, more than any single capability. One quote, paraphrased given photo legibility: concerns raised internally about pushing out large numbers of enterprise deals and partnerships at a pace that outstrips the company's own ability to evaluate safety implications.
