## Reference: "Budget must scrap Carer's Allowance means test, Government warned" (Irish Independent, Mary Regan and Cónal Thomas, captured 2 Oct 2026)

Source: VERIFIED mainstream press report, citing Family Carers Ireland
(CEO Sharon Foley) and government budget sources ahead of Budget 2027.

- **Carer's Allowance** is a weekly social welfare payment to those
  caring for a person due to age, disability or illness. Current maximum
  rate: €270/week for carers under 66 caring for one person, €308 for
  carers over 66 (higher rates for those caring for two or more people).
- **Means-tested** — Department of Social Protection assesses all of the
  applicant's income sources; the payment rate can reduce depending on
  weekly income.
- **Income disregard thresholds** (the income level before the payment
  starts reducing) rose significantly in last year's Budget (2026): from
  €375 to €1,000/week for a single person, and from €750 to €2,000/week
  for a couple.
- The Programme for Government committed to **scrapping the means test
  entirely**, but due to last year's already-significant increases, a
  further rise in the income disregard in Budget 2027 (due the following
  Tuesday, as reported) is in doubt — sources suggest any changes this
  year will be "modest."
- Family Carers Ireland (which represents 624,000 people caring for loved
  ones) is calling on the Government to increase Carer's Allowance to
  €325/week and abolish the means test — says not increasing the
  disregard would be a government "failure."
- **Only 4% of Carer's Allowance recipients currently receive Fuel
  Allowance**, per Sharon Foley (Family Carers Ireland CEO) — directly
  relevant detail, since Fuel Allowance mechanics are already logged in
  `market_intel_fuel_allowance_household_benefits_sept2026.md`.
- Separately reported: a new Cost of Disability payment (Minister Dara
  Calleary) could be worth €400-500 as a one-off lump sum; a flat €10
  increase to core welfare payments (pension, Carer's Allowance,
  jobseeker's) is understood to be under consideration; the Irish
  Independent separately reported a likely €5/week Fuel Allowance
  increase plus two bonus payments over winter.
