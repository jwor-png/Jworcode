Author: Eibhlin Fitzpatrick, Community Manager, Circular.ie (Ireland's national hub for circular innovation, action and solutions). Photo is tilted with a shadow; text mostly legible, right-hand edge cut off.

## What it says
- The circular economy as a community-level movement: food-sharing projects, reuse and vintage shops, repair cafes, toy libraries, rural development groups, social enterprises turning textile waste into training and jobs. Often started by parents or volunteers; some small businesses make it a livelihood. Examples named from Cobh, Clonmel and Cahirciveen.
- **What helps:** grants to tell their story or build the service; social support from a strong network; funding that values prevention and reuse, not just recycling; recognition of volunteers' time; media treating these stories like any business success; local authorities and development agencies treating circular initiatives as part of their infrastructure.
- The gap is connection between initiatives, not shortage of them. Circular.ie's work: a grants programme, the **Full Circle Awards**, and **Connecting Communities** events (one held this week at St Peter's, Cork city). Partnership with the **Design and Crafts Council Ireland for Irish Design Week 2026**.
- **Event:** Fri 13 Nov 2026, **Reimagining Hospitality Textiles**, a hands-on workshop at **Castle Hotel Macroom**, led by **Ailis Crowley, founder of FASH Forward** (Cork), exploring how discarded hotel towels can be turned into new products by embroidery and customisation.
