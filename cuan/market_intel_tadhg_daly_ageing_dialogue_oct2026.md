# "Finding our collective voice on age old crisis" — Tadhg Daly

Source: opinion column by Tadhg Daly, CEO of Nursing Homes Ireland, in national Irish press. VERIFIED as a named, on-the-record opinion piece by a credible sector CEO; figures cited are his own (ESRI, government economic statement), not independently re-checked by Cuan. **Author is a personal friend of John's**, flagged specifically for that reason alongside John's own stated "special interest in ensuring our elderly are looked after."

## What it says

Core call: the government should convene a **national dialogue on ageing**, modelled on Ireland's past social-partnership approach (employers, unions, community groups, government), to build national consensus on how Ireland supports people as they age and what resources that requires.

Key figures cited:
- Government's 2026 summer economic statement: age-related costs (healthcare, long-term care, pensions) projected to rise from €35bn this year to €97bn by 2065 — roughly trebling in 40 years.
- Population aged over 85 projected to increase 162% by 2045.
- More than one million people will be over 65 before the end of this decade (within three years).
- Nursing homes already operating at 95% occupancy; the ESRI estimates Ireland will need **an additional 1,000 nursing home beds every year until 2040** to meet demand.

Framing: this is "not a question of numbers... about choice, dignity and community" — people needing nursing home care should have the choice to receive it close to family and community wherever possible, which requires long-term capacity planning, not just bed numbers. The challenge extends beyond healthcare to housing, transport, retirement income, age-friendly communities and access across the full continuum of care. Notes the Commission on Care (established 2023) has yet to publish findings. Closing argument: the cost of inaction is higher than the cost of planning — without action, Ireland risks "sleepwalking into one of the biggest social and healthcare challenges in its history," forced into costlier, poorer-outcome reactionary decisions. Frames ageing as "not a crisis... a success story," but one requiring preparation and ambition.
