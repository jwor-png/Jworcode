# Market Intelligence — "Dairy farmers are getting bored — so where
should they invest next?" (Mike Brady, Brady Group)

Newspaper clipping (Dairy/Finance section) photographed by John, 9
Sept 2026. By Mike Brady, agricultural consultant and managing
director at Brady Group.

## Core thesis
Post-2015 milk-quota abolition, Irish dairy farmers spent over a
decade on an expansion playbook — more cows, more land, better
infrastructure. That work is largely done: debt is down, cash is
strong, and the sector's new problem is **boredom** — the excitement
and ambition that expansion once supplied has nowhere left to go.
Successful farmers are now asking "what's next?" rather than "how do I
grow the herd?"

## Why "more cows" isn't the answer anymore
Nitrates regulations, water-quality concerns, emissions targets,
planning restrictions, and maintaining social licence to farm all now
constrain unlimited herd expansion. Long-term land leases at
reasonable prices are scarce. The article's framing: "the era of easy
growth appears to be well and truly over" — progressive farmers are
shifting from thinking about cow numbers to thinking about **capital
allocation**.

## Six directions farmers are investing instead
1. **Build new houses on-farm** — housing crisis + relaxed rural
   planning legislation lets farmers use existing project-management
   experience to build rental housing, particularly within commuting
   distance of population/employment centres.
2. **Renewable energy** — solar farms, rooftop solar, battery storage,
   wind, energy partnerships; dairy farms are well positioned given
   high electricity demand. Also emerging: biomethane projects using
   slurry/grass silage, leveraging Ireland's grass-based system.
3. **Forestry** — afforestation grants make marginal or hard-to-sell
   land a viable long-term investment.
4. **Adding value to milk** — farm shops, artisan dairy products,
   specialised nutrition products, local food brands, milk vending
   machines. Explicitly framed as a way to recapture the "excitement
   of expansion" through brand-building rather than volume.
5. **Technology and data** — direct AI relevance: "The dairy farms of
   the future will be more digital than physical. Artificial
   intelligence, automated systems, animal monitoring technologies and
   predictive analytics are rapidly transforming dairy farms." Not
   every technology delivers value — the best systems improve
   decision-making, reduce labour requirements, and improve animal
   performance. Key line: **"the next gains will come from better
   information rather than more infrastructure."**
6. **Personal investment** — after years of debt reduction and
   reinvestment, some farmers are reaching a stage where the best
   investment is personal: family home improvements, children's
   education, hobbies, employing more labour to free up time. Brady's
   framing: there's nothing wrong with stacking these goals, or with
   enjoying the rewards of decades of hard work.

## Closing thesis (direct quote, strongly relevant)
"The most prosperous farms of 2035 may not be the ones milking the
most cows or producing the most milk. They may be the ones whose
owners recognised that the next great opportunity lay beyond the
milking parlour... the value of your investment down as well as up."
And on point 5: **"The winners of the next decade will not be those
with the largest herds but those who make the smartest technology
decisions."**
