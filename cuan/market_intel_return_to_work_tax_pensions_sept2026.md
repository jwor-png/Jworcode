# Market Intelligence — "Tax, pensions and supports — what to know
going back to work after having children" (Sinéad Ryan, Irish
Independent Indo Money, 4 Sept 2026)

## Core theme
Practical tax/pension/state-support guidance for a parent (framed
around stay-at-home mothers) returning to work, full or part-time,
after time out of the workforce raising children. Expert quotes from
Sarah McGurrin (Head of Employee Benefits, NFP Ireland) and Jennifer
Sweeney (Tax Director, EY Ireland).

## Key facts
- **Framing advice:** "Don't panic about years missed, take stock and
  consider all your options" — not everyone returning to work needs to
  immediately maximise pension/savings/mortgage overpayment; start
  small and review as circumstances change.
- **Tax bands (2026):** single person's standard rate band €44,000 (20%
  up to that, 40% above). Married couple, both earning sufficiently:
  combined standard rate band €88,000 (€44,000 each). Single-income
  couple: €53,000 standard band for the earner, with the non-earning
  spouse's unused €35,000 band non-transferable.
- **Employee/PAYE Tax Credit (€2,000)** — a spouse returning to work
  part-time may qualify for this, giving the household an additional
  tax deduction of the same amount.
- **Home Carer Tax Credit** — a returning spouse can earn up to €7,200
  gross without affecting the couple's entitlement to this credit,
  reducing on a sliding scale up to €11,100/year. Cannot claim both the
  increased dual-income standard rate band and the Home Carer Tax
  Credit in the same tax year — a genuine either/or choice worth
  modelling before assuming both apply.
- **My Future Fund (State auto-enrolment pension):** all workers aged
  23-60 earning over €20,000/year are enrolled automatically.
  Contributions from employee and employer, with a State top-up,
  initially 1.5%/1.5%/0.5% respectively based on salary. Applies across
  total income from all employments — someone with more than one
  part-time job earning a combined €20,000+ is enrolled even if no
  single job alone reaches that threshold.
- **State Pension (Contributory):** funded via PRSI, payable from age
  66. The Homemaker's Scheme and Home Caring Periods "recognise certain
  periods spent caring for children," subject to conditions — worth
  requesting a PRSI contribution statement from the Department of
  Social Protection (mywelfare.ie) on return to work, to have caring
  years properly disregarded rather than counted against the record.
- **Time off:** no automatic legal right to time off for routine
  childcare disruption (a sick child, a training day) beyond whatever
  the employment contract specifies. Statutory right to paid "force
  majeure" leave under the Parental Leave Act 2019 for family crises
  (e.g. illness of a close family member) — capped at 3 days in a
  single year, or 5 days across 3 years.
- **Childcare:** no direct tax deduction for childcare costs. Couples
  can instead avail of the Universal Childcare Subsidy or means-tested
  supports.
- **Self-employment/starting a business supports** for those returning
  from homemaking: Back to Enterprise Allowance (BTWEA — retains a
  reducing % of welfare payments for up to 2 years); Short-Term
  Enterprise Allowance (STEA) for jobseekers; Enterprise Support Grant
  (ESG, up to €2,500) for new full-time businesses; Local Enterprise
  Office (LEO) advice; Microfinance Ireland loans (€2,000-€50,000) for
  those who can't secure a bank loan.
- **Qualifications:** Tuition Fees Relief on full-time third-level
  courses — relief on fees between €1,500/€3,000 (disregarded amount,
  single/part-time student) and up to €7,000 per person per year.
- **General household-finance note:** where a household receives
  means-tested supports, returning-to-work income should be assessed
  for its effect on the *overall* household result, not evaluated in
  isolation — supports may reduce or be lost entirely once a second
  income starts.
