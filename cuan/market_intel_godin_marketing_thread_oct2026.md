# "9 content/marketing lessons from Seth Godin" — Threads thread

Source: Threads (thecreator.amit), 9-part thread attributed to Seth Godin's ideas, captured 8 Oct 2026. **Platform itself labels this "AI content"** — treat as a generated/derivative summary of Godin's published thinking, not Godin's own words, and not independently verified against his actual books/blog in this note. 8 of 9 points captured.

## The 8 points captured

1. **Aim for a small audience, not a big one.** You only need enough people who truly care. Practical steps: write down the 100 people you want to help, describe their exact problem in one line, make everything for them. "A small group that cares beats a big crowd that scrolls past."
2. **Speak to "people like us."** Humans join groups that share their beliefs and habits. Example given: target "creators who have a product but no buyers," not "anyone who wants to make money online" — say who you're for in your bio and every post.
3. **Ask permission, don't interrupt.** Give one reason to follow, make your best posts easy to find, never DM a pitch to a stranger. "People pay attention to what they asked to hear from."
4. **Be worth remarking on.** If nobody would tell a friend about your post, it's too safe. Write the sharper version of the idea, add one example nobody else uses, cut the lines that sound like everyone else. "Safe content gets scrolled. Remarkable content gets shared."
5. **Show up for years, not weeks.** Pick a posting rhythm you can keep, treat each post as practice, judge the pattern after 90 days, not 3 posts. "Trust comes from showing up long enough."
6. **Change something for them.** Good marketing moves people from where they are to where they want to be — name where they're stuck today, name where they want to be, show the first small step. "People follow whoever helps them move."
7. **Know when to quit.** Godin's "the dip" — the long, hard stretch between starting and getting good. Quit things that won't get better, push through the ones that will, decide before you start, not in the middle. "Quitting the wrong thing is smart. Quitting the right thing is expensive."
8. **Serve first, grow second.** Answer every comment, solve one real problem per post, care more about one reader than 1,000 views.
