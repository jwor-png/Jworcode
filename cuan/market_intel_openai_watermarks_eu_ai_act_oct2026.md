# "OpenAI to add invisible watermarks to ChatGPT text in EU to meet AI Act rules"

Source: Instagram, mindwired.ai. UNVERIFIED as a primary source — a social-media news summary account, not independently confirmed against OpenAI's own announcement or press coverage by Cuan. Treat the specific system name ("textGrain") and implementation detail as REPORTED pending independent verification.

## What it says

- OpenAI is reportedly rolling out **invisible watermarks for eligible ChatGPT and Codex-generated text in the EU**, as part of its response to the **EU AI Act**.
- The system, described as called **"textGrain,"** does not add a visible label. Instead, it subtly changes the statistical pattern of words chosen by the model, creating a machine-readable signal that can later be detected.
- **Explicit limitations stated:**
  - Does NOT prove who wrote the text.
  - Does NOT reveal the prompt used.
  - Does NOT identify the user.
  - Does NOT tell you whether the content is true.
  - It only signals that OpenAI's systems *may* have played a role in generating the passage.
- **Access is expected to be limited:** detector access restricted to approved researchers and expert organisations; API customers can opt in, rather than it being enabled by default.
- The post frames this as potentially "one of the biggest shifts in AI provenance yet" — moving from visible labels/disclaimers toward invisible, statistical signals embedded directly in generated text.
