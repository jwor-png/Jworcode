# Dormant Account Notice — BPFI

Source: statutory BPFI notice, VERIFIED — primary legal/regulatory notice text, not a secondhand summary.

## What it says

- Under the Dormant Accounts Act 2001, financial institutions must identify accounts with no customer transaction for 15 years and designate them dormant.
- Institutions must make reasonable effort to contact dormant account holders to reactivate the account via a customer-initiated transaction; once a transaction occurs, the dormancy clock resets to zero.
- Where the account holder is deceased, the legal/personal representative may reactivate the account in the normal manner.
- If no transaction is initiated by 31 March next, funds transfer to the National Treasury Management Agency (NTMA), which manages them on behalf of the State. **Transferred funds remain the property of the account holder and can be reclaimed at any time** from the original holding branch or Head Office, per the "Guide to Dormant Accounts" booklet.
- Institutions must write to all dormant account holders except where: the balance is below €100; the institution has been instructed to hold correspondence; or previous correspondence was returned "gone away."
- Anyone believing they hold a dormant account should contact their branch/Head Office to reactivate and de-list it. Fraudulent claims are an offence under the Act.
- If the original institution no longer exists, see bpfi.ie for individual institution helplines.
- Full BPFI member institution list included in the notice (all major Irish-licensed banks).
