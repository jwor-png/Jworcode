on state support for high energy costs" (Charlie Weston, Irish
Independent, Monday 7 September 2026)

Newspaper clipping photographed by John, 9 Sept 2026. John's own
framing: to understand the scheme, how money is being lost by
eligible people not claiming it, and **the opportunity to deal with
the people** — i.e. a possible business angle, not just reference

## The scheme and the scale of the problem
- **The Household Benefits Package (HBP)** is worth up to **€580 a
  year** to an eligible household: **€35/month** (~€420/year) toward
  electricity or natural gas, plus a **free TV licence** (normally
  €160/year to renew).
- Over-70s qualify **automatically, without a means test**. Under-70s
  can also qualify, but only if they meet certain conditions (e.g.
  living alone, or with only a dependent spouse/certain qualifying
  others) and are subject to a means test.
- **Scale of the shortfall, per a Department of Social Protection
  analysis released under Freedom of Information (obtained by Zak
  Carolan, peoplespocket.ie):**
  - Around **560,000 households** are receiving the benefit nationally.
  - As many as **116,000 households with at least one person aged 70
    or over are NOT receiving the Household Benefits Package**, despite
    automatic eligibility.
  - Separately, around **389,000 households with at least one person
    aged 70+** are not receiving the electricity/gas element
    specifically.
  - **More than 100,000 households with someone over 70** may be
    missing out on the TV licence element.
  - The Department itself cautions these figures "may be an
    overestimate," since in many cases another household member
    (rather than the over-70 individual) may already be the named
    claimant — so the true scale of genuinely unclaimed entitlement is
    uncertain, though still described as "a huge number."
- **Why this happens, per Zak Carolan (peoplespocket.ie):** the
  package is not well understood — "nobody rang [some claimants'
  parents] and told them it existed... that's the whole problem" — no
  proactive outreach model exists; the onus is entirely on individuals
  to know about and apply for the scheme themselves.
- **How to claim:** apply online via MyGovID and the Household Benefits
  Package account, or by completing a paper application form and
  posting it to the Department of Social Protection.
- **Wider context cited:** the Department says it runs periodic
  national "Benefits and Entitlements" awareness campaigns (last ran in
  2022 and 2024) and separately points out that "billions of euro" in
  unclaimed benefits and tax refunds go unclaimed every year across
  the whole benefits/tax system in Ireland and the UK generally, not
  just this one scheme.

## Why John flagged this — the "opportunity to deal with the people"
John's own framing points at a genuine market gap rather than pure
reference reading: a large, poorly-targeted, purely self-service state
entitlement (no proactive outreach, means-tested eligibility rules that
are hard for an older person to self-assess, an online-first
application route via MyGovID that itself presents a digital-literacy
barrier for exactly the older cohort meant to benefit) sitting
unclaimed at real scale (potentially 100,000+ households). This maps
directly onto **Golden Generation's stated audience and mission** — a
concrete, quantifiable "help older people navigate a system that
wasn't built with them in mind" opportunity, distinct from Golden
Generation's existing AI-upskilling/succession-planning angles but
squarely in the same demographic and same "AI/digital tools helping
an underserved older cohort" thesis.
