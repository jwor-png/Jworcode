# "One thing I would never do... The family law solicitor" (Sunday Independent, Life, p27, 18 Oct 2026)

Source: Sunday Independent, Life, 18 October 2026, p27 (series by Etain McGuckian; interviewee Karen Tobin, family law solicitor, Comyn Kelleher Tobin, Cork). Captured by photograph from John, 27 Oct 2026. General newspaper advice, not legal advice; figures as printed and not independently verified. Logged as general legal-practice and negotiation reference; no personal inference is drawn from John sending it.

## What it says

- **The one thing she would never do:** agree to a separation or settlement without professional legal advice, however amicable things seem. Even someone only thinking about it should have one consultation: "You can bind yourself up in something unintentionally without the benefit of legal advice" (for example an access arrangement that does not work long term, a maintenance agreement that becomes unworkable when circumstances change, pension arrangements, or joint enterprises such as farms and businesses).
- **Why:** people do not always make their best decisions at times of high emotion. Knowing the options and what the outcome will look like (two households, children in two homes) lets you decide with information, without committing to anything. A 2024 ESRI study is quoted: 37% of married mothers became economically vulnerable after divorce.
- **Manage expectations:** she asks clients "Where do you see yourself in five years' time?" to move them from what happened yesterday to what is achievable. Family law is as much about managing expectations as knowing the law. Being right and getting what you want are not necessarily the same thing.
- **No-fault system:** Ireland operates a no-fault divorce system, so something a person finds deeply hurtful may have no bearing on the legal outcome. She advises weighing the financial and emotional cost of litigation against the likely outcome, while accepting that litigation can sometimes be needed to achieve a fair result.
- **Two versions of every story:** be practical about what the process will look like and remember that someone else may have a different narrative.
- **Mediation first:** solicitors are professionally bound to recommend mediation. Timing: if a case goes to court, 12 to 18 months; if agreement is reached, potentially six to eight months. Her closing point: decisions made in the worst moments of our lives shape the ordinary moments that come afterwards.
