## 1. Landowners paid €60m in first year of zoned land tax (RZLT)
Hundreds of farmers around the country have paid a combined **€60m** in
the first year of the Residential Zoned Land Tax (RZLT), which places
full responsibility on landowners to go through a two-step process to
gain an annual exemption on land zoned residential, often without their
knowledge. Dublin City had 907ac liable; the next-largest amounts were
Limerick (492ac), then Meath and Kildare (474ac liable, split between
them). The IFA has objected to RZLT since it was first announced in
2021, campaigning for a permanent solution that removes farmland from
the scope of RZLT. The tax was introduced 2022 to encourage landowners
to build houses on land that is zoned residential rather than raise
revenue, but the IFA says it remains "hugely unpopular" with farmers,
who should check updated zoned land maps for 2026 and verify land use
sooner than raising revenue.

**Separate agreements item (same page, Irish Independent):** Glenveagh
Properties (senior director Eoin Hughes) confirmed a partnership-style
land-acquisition initiative to identify lands suitable for housing
delivery through landowner partnerships, generating "a number of sites
with the potential for development." In 2025 alone Glenveagh entered
partnership agreements covering just over 100ac in two large towns and
is actively seeking further opportunities with owners of sites of 10
acres or more.

## 2. Budget may deliver farm succession shake-up (Margaret Donnelly)
More incentives to encourage Succession Farm Partnerships are
understood to be on Agriculture Minister **Martin Heydon's** wish list
ahead of the upcoming Budget. **CSO data cited: 38% of Irish farmers
are aged 65+, only 4% are under 35.** Heydon is understood to be
looking at overhauling the model to encourage earlier farm transfers —
only around **3,500 farm partnerships** currently in operation.

Succession Farm Partnership mechanics: the asset owner must commit to
transfer a minimum of **80% of agricultural assets** to the successor
between the end of year 3 and end of year 10 of the plan, with an
annual income tax credit of **€5,000 for five years** available to
participants.

A generational-renewal report launched at last year's Ploughing
Championships made 31 recommendations, including removing the 3-year
minimum requirement for Succession Farm Partnerships, reducing
administrative burden in setting them up, and updating eligibility
criteria for the tax credit.

**EU angle:** Agriculture Commissioner **Christophe Hansen** posted on
LinkedIn after visiting the National Ploughing Championships in Offaly,
framing generational renewal as a personal commitment since the start
of his mandate, and citing Ireland's finance/inheritance/taxation
measures as offering "valuable lessons" for the rest of Europe. Nearly
9 in 10 Irish farms are engaged in livestock production.

## 3. MEPs hit impasse over CAP's place in next EU budget (Euractiv — Eddy Wax, Victoria Becker, Maria Simon Arboleas)
MEPs are deadlocked over where the Common Agricultural Policy (CAP)
sits in the EU's next seven-year budget (2028-2034 MFF), after
socialists and far-right groups rejected the European Commission's
proposed structure. The Commission wants to fold agriculture, cohesion
and other major spending areas into **one national plan per country**.

Closed-door European Parliament agriculture-committee talks broke down
abruptly, per three parliamentary staffers. **S&D negotiator Cristina
Maestre** is pushing to keep CAP's own governance via national CAP
plans rather than folding it into the broader national plan structure;
joined in opposing the new structure by Patriots for Europe, European
Conservatives and Reformists, The Left, and Europe of Sovereign
Nations. EPP, Renew and Green negotiators back the Commission's new
architecture. Maestre: "The proposal to integrate the CAP into mere
chapters... weakens agricultural policy and dilutes its financial,
regulatory and political autonomy." One MEP called the clash "the
first wheel that's fallen off the wagon."

Commission proposal would ring-fence **€300 billion** for farmers'
income support, with other agricultural/rural development measures
funded from the wider pot. Lead MFF negotiator **Carla Tavares** (S&D)
does not share Maestre's position, saying the only workable majority is
one built with pro-European groups, per the European Parliament's April
interim report (negotiated with EPP's Siegfried Mureșan). For now, the
split leaves the agriculture side of talks without an obvious way
forward.
