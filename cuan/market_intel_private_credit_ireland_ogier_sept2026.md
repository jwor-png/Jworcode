# Market intel — "New era of private credit in Ireland" (Ogier, Business Post "Future of Banking")

**Source:** Business Post commercial-content feature, "Focus on: Future
of Banking," Penny Gray, 27 September 2026. **Marked "Commercial
Content"** — Ogier-sourced claims treated as REPORTED/promotional, not
independently verified by Cuan.

## What it says
**Jennifer Dobbyn (partner, investment funds, Ogier)** and **[first
name illegible] McClenaghan (head of investment funds team, Ogier)**
discuss Ireland's funds/private-credit market. Key figures cited:
- Net assets of investment funds domiciled in Ireland: **$6.3 trillion**
  at end of 2025.
- Funds serviced in Ireland: **$8.2 trillion**.
- Ireland's share of the European funds market has risen from **14% to
  21%** over the past decade.

**Regulatory driver:** the Central Bank of Ireland's updated **AIF
Rulebook**, effective May 2026, aligning Ireland's framework more
closely with the revised European AIFMD II rules. Historically, Irish
direct lending funds faced a 25% borrower concentration limit, a 200%
leverage cap, and restrictions on combining loan origination with other
investment strategies — these restrictions have now been superseded by
a pan-European framework, described by McClenaghan as "actually
spearheaded by the Central Bank," which "was very flexible and
accommodating in terms of granting derogations and permissions to funds
over the course of last year to early adopt these more flexible loan
origination requirements."

McClenaghan flags the ILP (Irish Limited Partnership) structure as
particularly significant — a structure only available since 2020,
which has seen a 225% increase in ILPs lodged in the last 12 months,
just over 50% of which are engaging in direct lending.

Dobbyn: the updated AIF Rulebook also removed a previous prohibition on
certain Irish funds acting as guarantors for third-party obligations or
granting security to third parties — Ogier frames this as
simplifying fund finance structures, reducing transaction costs and
shortening negotiation timelines, important as funds become more deeply
integrated into the wider financing ecosystem. Dobbyn on Ireland's
positioning generally: "Ireland's a very attractive jurisdiction to do
business from a funds perspective... We are based in an EU common law
jurisdiction, which is certainly a great advantage for Ireland,"
citing a skilled English-speaking workforce, speed to market, regulatory
infrastructure and established relationships with international asset
managers as the combination driving this.

**Next stage flagged:** fund tokenisation — named as a current Irish
policy-direction priority, with Irish funds already beginning to
tokenise units in money market funds.

McClenaghan's overall framing: the combination of regulatory reform,
private credit growth and technological innovation (tokenisation) points
to a financial services sector "becoming more diverse and flexible,"
with "good buy-in and good appetite" and a "progressive and
accommodating" message from the Central Bank.
