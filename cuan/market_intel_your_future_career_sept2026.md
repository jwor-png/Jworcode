# Market Intelligence — Mediaplanet "Your Future Career" Supplement, Sept 2026

Newspaper supplement (8 pages, `yourcareer.ie`), photographed by John,
9 Sept 2026. Career/skills/apprenticeship market intelligence — logged
per the standing Cuan → Meridian capture protocol since it touches
several live domains (Ambrion/Velocity AI training, Golden Generation,
Meridian's talent/adoption lens).

## Front page framing
"Your Future Career" — cover feature is Jones Engineering's
apprenticeship programme ("At Jones Engineering, an apprenticeship is
only the beginning" — Paddy Kenna & Lauren O'Rourke).

## Ways to future-proof your career (Keith Sunderland, Registrar,
Engineers Ireland)
- Ireland facing a critical engineer shortage: **22,300 additional
  engineers needed over the next decade**; several engineering roles
  on the national Critical Skills Occupations List; engineering in
  the top 15 fastest-growing jobs (Future of Jobs report 2025).
- Pay (Engineers Ireland 2026 Salary Survey): graduate engineers
  ~€39,000; 6-10 years experience ~€59,000; Chartered Engineers
  ~€64,000; engineering directors €100,000-€135,000. Chartered (CEng)
  or Fellow (FIEI) titles add €5,000-€30,000.
- Apprenticeship route (QQI-FET Level 5/6, PLC courses) offered as an
  alternative pathway to a Level 7/8 engineering degree.

## Supporting apprentices (WWETB / Suir Engineering)
- Suir Engineering: 320 apprentices, mostly electrical/instrumentation,
  runs an "Apprentice Centre of Excellence" in Citywest with dedicated
  mentors — CEO John Kelly cites improved safety, retention, and
  learner outcomes as a direct result.
- Waterford & Wexford ETB (WWETB) provides 1,000 Phase 2 apprenticeship
  training places annually across 14 trades, with academic/personal/
  wellbeing supports and training advisors monitoring progress.

## Jones Engineering advertorial
264 apprentices currently across four-year mechanical/electrical
programmes; Apprentice Centre in Carlow being expanded. Lauren
O'Rourke (Electrical Estimator, qualified with distinction, now
training toward quantity surveyor) and Paddy Kenna (apprentice 25
years ago, now Construction Manager) as case studies. Line worth
noting for Meridian/Golden Generation talent messaging: "You earn
while you learn, and with that experience behind you, you can
genuinely go anywhere."

## New apprenticeship programmes introduced in Ireland (Minister James
Lawless TD)
- Nine new programmes introduced in 2026 across business/operations,
  social care, paramedicine, robotics/automation, engineering.
- Government target: **12,500 annual apprenticeship registrations by
  2030**, two-thirds in craft/construction (Programme for Government).
- New **Level 6 Business and Operations Apprenticeship**, steering
  group chaired by Lidl, opens Civil Service entry via mainstream
  apprenticeship for the first time; includes an Irish-language
  pathway (5 places, inaugural cohort). A degree-level **Spatial
  Planning Apprenticeship** is in development.

## Dublin Bus driver recruitment (advertorial)
Recruiting full-time and part-time drivers; part-time minimum 2
days/16 hours a week (every Sunday + one other day); pay €22.90/hr
rising to €26.51/hr over a six-year scale. Framed around flexibility
and career-changers (banking, technology, healthcare backgrounds cited
among current drivers). 8% of Dublin Bus drivers are female.

## Europass / QQI (National Europass Centre for Ireland)
Free EU-wide platform for CVs, skills-matching, job/course search
across Europe — positioned around Ireland's current EU Council
Presidency and its stated competitiveness/productivity agenda. Open
to non-EU nationals too. Includes a digital-skills self-assessment
tool and a job/skill trends explorer.

## Great Place to Work Ireland — trust and performance
Cathal Divilly (Business Partner) and Marina Rivas (Marketing & Brand
Manager): Ireland's Best Workplaces show materially higher trust
scores than the national average (e.g. 88% vs 61% say management is
approachable; 81% vs 52% say management genuinely responds to
suggestions; 84% vs 61% say staff give discretionary effort). Central
claim: organisations that perform best under uncertainty are the ones
that already invested in trust before it was tested — relevant
framing for any Meridian/Ambrion pitch around culture/AI-adoption
readiness.

## Tertiary pathway into ID Nursing (WWETB / SETU)
1+4 model: QQI Level 5 Nursing Studies at Waterford College of Further
Education, then BSc (Hons) Intellectual Disability Nursing at SETU —
CAO-points-free, first year fully funded (uniforms, books, manual
handling/first aid training included). Student case study: Alisha
Collier, who has dyslexia and said she "never thought nursing would be
an option" for her. WWETB Tertiary Manager Alan O'Gorman: "The best
route for each individual might not always be straight." Second year
of the programme; ambition to expand across Waterford/Wexford and
beyond.

## KCETB — lifelong learning / regional talent (Dr Lindsay Malone, CEO)
South East framed as a growth region (investment, population,
sectoral expansion in engineering/construction/advanced manufacturing/
healthcare/digital/green economy) needing a "regional talent
ecosystem" connecting education, employers, communities, government.
Explicit line: the "one qualification for a 40-year career" model is
obsolete as skills evolve faster — relevant to Golden Generation's
succession/knowledge-capture thesis and Ambrion's upskilling pitch.

## AHEAD Ireland — WAM Programme (disability employment)
Deirdre Moore, Employment Manager: WAM secured 68 new positions for
disabled graduates across 34 employers in 2025; provides a Needs
Assessment meeting, mentor-matching, and disclosure support for every
placement. Currently recruiting for Civil Service, Bord Bia, ESB
placements (open through September); recruitment for 2027 already
being planned as "similarly ambitious and widespread."

## An Garda Síochána recruitment (advertorial, Garda Dylan Hogan)
Recruitment content — training in Templemore, BA in Applied Policing
on qualifying, diversity of roles cited (Road Policing, Detective
Unit, Drugs & Organised Crime Bureau). Personal/human-interest framing
rather than data — noted for completeness, no direct cross-reference.

## Graduate recruitment outlook (Gavan O'Brien, MD, gradireland)
- Hiring "more selective," AI "undoubtedly" having an impact, but
  demand holding up in technology services, life sciences, financial
  services, public sector, sustainability.
- Employers prioritising **skills over specific degrees** — direct
  relevance to Ambrion/Velocity's skills-based training pitch.
- Salaries have stabilised post-pandemic; total reward broadening
  (structured training, certifications, mentoring, progression paths).
  SMEs "investing more consistently" in graduate intakes, often
  offering faster responsibility than large multinationals.
- Direct quote worth keeping: "Employers are hiring for potential and
  mindset as much as for experience."
