# Guillaume Princen (Anthropic, head of EMEA) on safety, IPO and the OpenAI rivalry

**IPO status — new precision on timing and valuation:** Bloomberg
reported (Wednesday, per the piece) that Anthropic is set to begin
**formally marketing an IPO as soon as the week of 9 November 2026**.
The company has told prospective investors **third-quarter revenue was
$16.8bn (€15bn)** and that it was profitable on an adjusted basis. Some
investors put a fair valuation at **$1.8 trillion to $2 trillion**
(Bloomberg), though analysts noted growth likely slowed from the
previous quarter. Separately cited: annualised revenue for the company
was **$1bn at the start of 2025 and $10bn by the end of the year**; as
of May, annual run-rate revenue had passed **$47bn**. (Figures as
reported — the run-rate figure in particular is notably larger than
other revenue figures in the same piece and should be treated with some
caution pending independent confirmation.)

**Princen's framing — safety as "the DNA of the company," not
OpenAI-rivalry:** declined to discuss the IPO in detail; instead
emphasised Anthropic's "responsible scaling policy" governing how and
when models are released (citing the staged release of "Mythos" in
April as an example) and said the company wants frontier labs and
governments to move from voluntary to binding safety commitments,
and for third-party testing of frontier models — explicitly saying
"companies should not be able to choose their own evaluators." Anthropic
has a multi-year partnership with **Accenture**, which includes
appointing the consultancy to evaluate its AI models — a notable detail
given Princen's own point about third-party evaluation.

**On OpenAI specifically:** asked directly about the rivalry, Princen
said "we have a lot to do. We're very busy," noting four model launches
in the past four weeks alone, and that safety and customers are the
company's two priorities. OpenAI CFO **Sarah Friar** gave a similar
response days earlier, saying her company "doesn't pay too much
attention to what its rival is up to." AI cyber tools were separately
claimed by Anthropic to now be "more powerful than any human cyber
expert" at patching long-unfixed software vulnerabilities.

**Dublin build-out — confirms and extends the previous office-space
entry:** Dublin was Anthropic's first port of call when it expanded to
Europe, opening an office at the end of 2024. The Business Post had
already revealed Anthropic is hunting for new Irish premises to house
**up to 1,000 employees**; a further 200 jobs for Ireland were announced
in March. Princen: **"talent was the key draw,"** noting Ireland ranks
highly on AI-ready employee base. Rival OpenAI also announced a further
200 jobs for Ireland the same week (on top of 250 confirmed earlier
this year).
