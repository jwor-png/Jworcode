# Market intel — "Ireland near 'a turning point' on data centre delivery"

**Source:** print business feature, Emer Walsh, undated clipping
(captured 27 Sept 2026), reporting from the Construction Industry
Federation (CIF) annual conference, Croke Park, "last week." REPORTED —
named-source conference commentary, not independently verified by Cuan.

## What it says
Ireland is described as "now well-equipped to deal with growing demand
for data centres, but Ireland's construction market may come at the
cost of higher energy bills," per a senior industry executive speaking
at the CIF conference.

**Darragh Martin, strategic partnership manager, Weltec Engineering:**
Ireland "may be an upshot" of higher energy prices "but we do not have
the same kind of heavy industry as other large European countries... We
don't have a large steel industry. On an overall economy, industrial
infrastructure to support that. This is our industrial revolution."
General framing: Ireland's economy is more digital/data-centre-weighted
than traditionally industrial, changing the energy-cost calculus versus
peer EU economies.

**Shane O'Neill, partner, Arthur Cox (construction and engineering):**
offered clarity on public bodies' obligations when deciding on such
developments; said Ireland is "on the cusp of a turning point" for data
centre delivery, "more positive than in previous years despite
setbacks." Ireland "did not have" sufficient ICT structure/foundation
to attract data-centre investment before recent government
acknowledgement of the importance of "single service export," and
recent decisions have started to give clarity on planning approval and
grid/power purchase agreement arrangements.

**Michelle Wallace, chief operations officer, Digital Infrastructure
Ireland:** said the data-centre construction pathway is "more positive
than in previous years," despite recent setbacks, with government now
acknowledging the sector's economic importance; called for further
support (ICT structure/foundation, planning clarity, power purchase
agreement clarity) to secure future data-centre investment, warning "if
we do not have that, we start to lose it."

**Context/friction cited:** a recent Killala, Co. Mayo data-centre
planning refusal — Sally Rooney (name as printed) objected on both the
regulations and headline direction, with the courts understood to be
considering what direction the regulations are going in; described as
"a complex topic" needing further lift/clarity on planning approval.
