## 1. "Traynor shores up property duo with Salesforce payout" (Inside Track, Nick [surname not captured])

group Fin to Salesforce this year, paid about €3.1bn to buy Fin and

## 2. "Artificial intelligence is great, but it can't solve the risk conundrum" (Darren Gillen, Quilter Cheviot Europe)

- Core argument: Ben Graham's "trinity of risks" (business risk,
  financial risk, valuation risk) still applies — AI doesn't resolve
  the probability of permanent capital loss, even as hyperscaler capex
  ramps up.
- **Hyperscaler capex figures:** Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Alphabet
  cumulative capex on building data centres forecast at **$5.1tn
  between 2022 and 2029** (per AlixPartners analysis).
- **Off-balance-sheet financing risk flagged directly:** hyperscalers
  increasingly financing data-centre build-out via circular deals,
  credit-default swaps, and asset-backed arrangements with insurance
  companies rather than straight capex on their own balance sheets —
  "financial risk... that were asset-light with high returns on
  capital and large moats can only be defined as speculative
  undertakings," per the piece's own framing.
- **Off-balance-sheet debt has "ballooned to $1.7tn"** by one
  estimate, from net cash position five years ago, with Torsten Slok
  (Apollo chief economist) forecasting growth to $2.3tn by 2028.
- Five of the biggest AI-related tech stocks are forecast to double
  tech-related capex spend between 2025 and next year, "while analysts
  forecast that revenue could roughly double that."

## 3. "The 27-year-old whose $5.6bn AI firm is wooing City lawyers" (Daniel Woolfson, Business Correspondent)

**Legora** — Stockholm-based legal AI company founded by **Max
Junestrand** (27, started the company just three years ago, after a
background in AI/legal tech research). Now valued at **$5.6bn**
(if not a paper billionaire, he's close) after an April funding round;
**$550m raised this year alone**, following a recent funding round
that roughly doubled valuation from the April figure, taking it close
to $200m in annual recurring revenue — the industry's second-largest
legal AI firm by that metric.

- **What Legora does:** AI tools for law firms — contract review,
  due diligence document analysis, drafting assistance — explicitly
  positioned to "make lawyers more efficient," not replace them.
  Quote from Junestrand: "We're not going to change that... It's not
  going to change the job, as a junior, spend three hours formatting
  documents, because the AI can do that."
- **Named law firm clients/adopters:** Taylor Wessing, White & Case,
  and others among "many of our customers" — described as big law
  firms across London adopting the tools.
- **Also well-funded competitor landscape named:** Harvey (a
  competing legal AI company) is referenced as part of the same
  wooing-City-lawyers wave.
- Junestrand's stated ambition: make Legora a "category-defining
  champion" in legal AI.

**Cross-reference:** **directly relevant to Jurgen** (Meridian's own

## 4. "The human side of AI transformation" (PwC Ireland, Aisling Curtis, Partner and Strategic Alliances Lead)

Commercial-content feature, timed to Dublin hosting the **International
AI Summit** this week.

- **The AI value gap, PwC's own 2026 AI Performance Survey (global
  and Irish executives):** roughly **20% of organisations capture 75%
  of the total economic value generated by AI** — the remaining 80%
  are "trapped in a cycle of point solutions and minor efficiency
  gains," focused on personal productivity (summarising emails,
  speeding up drafting) rather than operating-model redesign.
- **Curtis's framing of the fix:** "Productivity alone isn't a
  strategy unless you decide what to do with the time saved. Are you
  redeploying that capacity into new product development, a better
  customer experience or top-line growth?" — worked example: an
  insurance company using a chatbot for FAQs (a "quick win") versus
  the top 20% of leaders redesigning the entire claims-handling
  process end to end (triage instantly, eliminate unnecessary steps,
  speed up pay-outs) — the latter is "not adding a tool, it's an
  operating-model transformation."
- **No enterprise runs on a single platform** — the real blocker to AI
  adoption is legacy systems and data silos that don't talk to each
  other across SAP/Salesforce/Workday/Microsoft etc.
- **Governance framed as an accelerant, not a brake:** "governance not
  as a bureaucratic speed bump, but as something that can accelerate
  adoption" — trust is explicit: PwC's own research shows
  outperforming organisations are "nearly twice as likely to operate
  within formal Responsible AI frameworks."
- **Diversity-in-leadership argument, directly tied to AI quality:**
  "AI models are built on data and human inputs. If you don't have
  diverse voices, backgrounds and perspectives in the boardroom and on
  development teams, you risk embedding blind spots and bias into the
  systems running your business."
- Closing framing: "You don't need to be a computer scientist to lead
  an AI-powered company" — growth mindset, curiosity, willingness to
  step outside comfort zone are what's actually required.
