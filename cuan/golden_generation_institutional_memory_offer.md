## The problem, as I understand it

Owner-led and family-owned SMEs carry decades of undocumented tacit knowledge — pricing logic, supplier relationships, informal decision rules, customer history, "the reason we do it this way" — that exists only in the owner's head. When the owner retires, exits, or dies, that knowledge is usually lost or takes years to painfully reconstruct. This is a well-known succession-planning problem in general terms.

**What I have NOT verified:** whether this is an actively felt pain point for any specific company on your prospect list, or in the Irish SME market specifically. That's an inference from "family-owned, founder-led" status, not a confirmed need. Flagging this because it's the single biggest assumption the whole idea rests on.

## Why AI changes the economics of solving it

Before AI, capturing this kind of institutional knowledge meant expensive manual consulting (structured interviews, a written knowledge-management binder) that rarely got done because the cost-to-value ratio was poor and the output was usually a static document nobody opened again. What's different now: AI-assisted capture (voice note or interview, transcribed and structured) plus a queryable, living knowledge base makes both the capture and the ongoing usefulness genuinely different in kind, not just cheaper.

Worth noting: this is structurally close to how you already work with me — voice note or dictated input, structured into something usable. If it works for running your own operation, that's a reasonable (not proven) signal it could work as a client offer.

## What the offer would actually be

A three-phase engagement, deliberately scoped so it doesn't require new software to be built before the first client:

**Phase 1 — Capture.** Structured interviews/voice notes with the owner and key long-tenured staff: how decisions get made, key relationships and their history, informal processes, pricing logic, risk knowledge, "things only I know."

**Phase 2 — Structure.** AI-assisted organisation of that material into a navigable, searchable, updateable knowledge base — not a static document that gets filed and forgotten.

**Phase 3 — Governance (natural upsell into Ambrion).** Advise on how the resulting knowledge base connects to succession planning, IP protection, and — where the business is also adopting AI tools — EU AI Act-adjacent governance. This is where the offer stops being generic "knowledge management" and becomes something only a governance-literate AI advisor can credibly deliver.

This uses all three entities coherently: Golden Generation for the framing and relationship (leadership legacy, second-half-of-career), Shane's technical side for Phase 2's actual system, Ambrion for Phase 3's governance layer. Meridian isn't needed here and shouldn't be named, per the standing framing rule.

## Open questions — what needs to be true before this goes further

1. **No validated demand yet.** This rests on an inference from one social media post about a US-focused YC document, not on anything an actual Irish SME owner has said. Needs testing in real conversation before being built out or pitched.
2. **Differentiation from existing knowledge-management/succession consulting** (Big 4 practices, boutique continuity consultants) isn't yet sharp. "AI-native" needs to mean something concretely different in the delivery, not just be a label on an old service.
3. **Succession is a sensitive conversation.** Owners can be resistant to discussing anything that implies exit or mortality. Positioning needs real care — this is a genuine execution risk, not just a marketing nuance.
4. **Data sensitivity is real.** Capturing an owner's most sensitive commercial knowledge (pricing, supplier terms, customer relationships) into an AI system raises legitimate security/IP/governance questions. This cuts both ways: it's exactly the kind of thing that needs Ambrion's governance credibility to do safely, but it's also a real risk if the engagement isn't handled with rigour from day one.
5. **Delivery capacity.** This requires actual interview and structuring work beyond identifying and approaching companies. Worth deciding early whether early clients get delivered personally by you and Shane, or whether this needs a delivery partner.

## Recommended next step — not a commitment

Test the concept in two or three real conversations with prospects already on the list, framed as an exploratory question rather than a finished pitch, before investing further in building it out. That validates real demand cheaply and keeps this consistent with "curate opportunities, don't collect them" rather than over-building a concept that hasn't been market-tested.
