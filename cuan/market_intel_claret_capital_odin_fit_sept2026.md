## What the article actually says
- **David Bateman's Claret Capital Partners** has closed **Fund IV**
  at **€575m**, exceeding its original €500m target — €440m of that in
  commitments to Claret European Growth Capital Fund IV, plus a
  further €135m through affiliated discretionary-management
  operations. Final close achieved after raising **€297m** at a prior
  close in 2022.
- **What Claret actually does — this is the critical fact:** Claret
  "focuses on companies across the technology, life science and
  impact sectors" and **"offers businesses an alternative to raising
  money by selling shares to investors — ing to companies, offering
  loans as an alternative to raising money by selling shares to
  investors."** In plain terms: **Claret is a growth-debt (venture
  debt) lender, not an equity investor.** It lends money to already-
  revenue-generating companies as an alternative to a further equity
  raise, rather than buying equity itself.
- Money comes from institutional investors: pension plans, insurance
  companies, family offices, other institutional and public wealth
  investors, "alongside co-investors for continuing to place their
  trust in our ability to find unwavering trust in our approach."
- Portfolio character: **32% of Claret's prior fund (Fund III) capital
  was deployed in AI technology and technology-enabled customer
  service operations**, plus companies backed through fund
  reconciliation software, cloud-based operations. Named portfolio
  companies: EdgeTier (customer service AI), Fund Recs (cloud
  reconciliation software), Cyrora, Endomag (acquired by Hologic),
  Bilberry Pharma, Cinclus Pharmaceutical, Inventiva (biotech).
- Fund III deployed over €1.5bn since 2013 across 210+ companies,
  through private wealth investors and institutional/public
  investors, mostly across Europe. Claret is London-based, with a
  growing presence in Paris and a new Berlin office.
- Fund IV will be used to continue commercial real estate/software,
  tech/life science/impact-sector growth lending, including "pension
  plans, insurance companies, family offices and other institutional
  and public wealth investors."

## Fit analysis against ODIN's actual raise
Per `ventures_dossier.md` (ODIN Business Plan, July 2026, prepared by
Shane McCarthy): ODIN's live raise is **€20m at a €100m pre-money
valuation**, structured as a straightforward **equity** round —
capital deployment plan includes 31% to tokens/API capacity, alongside
build-out costs, with ODIN explicitly positioned as pre-revenue (the
"Funding Reality" pack makes the case that pre-revenue is not a
weakness given the scale of the IP base).

**Core mismatch, flagged directly rather than glossed over:** Claret
Capital is a **debt** fund, not an equity fund. Its entire model is
lending money "as an alternative to raising money by selling shares"
— i.e. it serves companies that want to avoid dilution and can service
debt repayments from existing cash flow or revenue. ODIN, as
described in the Business Plan, is **pre-revenue** and is explicitly
raising **equity**, not debt. On the facts in this article alone,
**Claret is not a natural fit for ODIN's current raise** — a pre-
revenue company with no debt-service capacity is not the profile
growth-debt lenders like Claret typically underwrite.

**Where there could be a fit, if things change:**
- If ODIN reaches a stage with contracted/recurring revenue (even
  early), a **growth-debt facility from a fund like Claret could
  become a genuine option to extend runway without further diluting
  Shane/John's equity** — worth revisiting at that point, not now.
- Claret's own portfolio shows real appetite for AI-enabled
  technology/customer-service businesses (EdgeTier, 32% of Fund III
  into AI/tech-enabled ops) — so the sector fit is right even though
  the instrument (debt vs equity) currently is not.
- Ambrion AI or Velocity AI, if either reaches steady contracted
  training/service revenue, may in time be a more natural candidate
  for this kind of lending than ODIN itself, given they're closer to
  an operating-revenue model than ODIN's IP-and-raise structure.

## Bottom line for John
This is good sector-relevant market intelligence — confirms real,
large-scale institutional capital (pension funds, insurers, family
offices) actively flowing into AI/tech growth lending in Europe right

ODIN's current pre-revenue equity raise.** Worth keeping on file as a
later-stage option (debt facility once revenue exists) rather than
pursuing now. Flagging this distinction explicitly rather than
suggesting an approach that wouldn't fit ODIN's actual funding need.
