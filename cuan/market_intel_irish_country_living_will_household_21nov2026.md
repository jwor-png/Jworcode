## 1. Legal, p28: Aisling Meehan (Agricultural Solicitors and Tax Consultants), seven steps to make a will
1. **Check what you own:** a spouse does not automatically own half; joint tenancy (passes to the survivor whatever the will says) versus tenants in common (passes under the will); transfers between spouses are generally tax exempt; check the registered owner at landdirect.ie or the Registry of Deeds.
2. **Know who has rights:** testamentary freedom, except a spouse or civil partner's legal right share (one half, or one third where there are children; can be renounced); children have no automatic entitlement but can apply to court under the moral duty rule; an unmarried partner inherits nothing unless the will provides. Intestacy with spouse and children: spouse two thirds, children one third.
3. **Decide who gets what:** no need to list every item; if children are young, leave to the spouse and use a discretionary trust until the youngest reaches a set age.
4. **Choose executors, guardians and trustees:** one executor is enough, two give a back-up; guardians and trustees if children are under 18.
5. **Sign it properly:** solicitor satisfied of free will; a doctor's certificate may be needed with age; never let a beneficiary or their spouse witness.
6. **Know where the last will is:** no central register of wills; keep a copy and tell the executors.
7. **Review it:** marriage generally revokes an existing will; review when circumstances change; property abroad may need a separate will in that country.

## 2. Money Mentor, p29: Philip O'Connor (ifac head of farm support): managing farm and household expenses
- Work out the household budget from 6 to 12 months of statements (include occasional costs); separate household and business spending; treat it as paying yourself a wage via an agreed regular monthly transfer; budget what the business can afford (seasonal income, tax, loan repayments, planned investment); review regularly rather than relying on the overdraft.
