# Market intel — Citi's Brent McIntosh: €570m Dublin HQ commitment + "AI journey"

**Source:** Business Post, "The Big Interview," Donal MacNamee (with
Fergal Phillips photography), 27 September 2026, p.20. REPORTED —
named-executive interview; not independently verified by Cuan.

## Citi's Dublin commitment
**Brent McIntosh**, Citi's Chief Legal Officer and Corporate Secretary
(joined Citi 2021; prior career: US Treasury Dept general counsel/
undersecretary for international affairs 2017-2019, White House deputy
assistant to the president 2006-2009), interviewed in Dublin for the
Eurofi Financial Forum (a major EU-presidency summit). Citi has
operated in Ireland 60+ years and is about to open a new headquarters
at **Waterfront South Central, the docklands** — purchased from Johnny
Ronan's RGRE in 2023, expected to open by end of 2026. McIntosh frames
it as "a €570 million commitment to Dublin" and "a tribute to Europe,"
calling Dublin "truly an international financial hub."

## Regulatory context (Eurofi Financial Forum)
Discussion centred on EU banking-regulation reform push amid concern
over European competitiveness (referencing Mario Draghi's 2024
competitiveness report). European Commission has committed to
"simplifying" bank regulation (legislative package expected early
2027), against a backdrop of US Federal Reserve-announced capital
requirement cuts. McIntosh pushes back on the "EU vs US regulatory gap"
narrative as overstated, says "I think we're all committed to a level
playing field" but doesn't think the current EU regulatory regime
creates that level playing field either — naming duplicative
requirements and inconsistent national interpretation of standardised
rules (not capital requirements) as the biggest friction point for
scaling a business across EU geographies.

## Citi turnaround under CEO Jane Fraser
Citi has cut "tens of thousands" of jobs, reduced bonuses, refocused
around a "handful of core business lines," reduced management layers,
which McIntosh says produced "significant improvements" in financial
performance. Ireland-specific: ~100 jobs eliminated as part of the
global restructuring; McIntosh says Dublin's situation is "much
improved" post-programme.

## "The AI journey" (final section, most directly relevant)
McIntosh describes Citi executives as "often more bullish on the
benefits of AI than rank-and-file staff, who are more fearful for the
future of their jobs than filled with excitement about its
productivity-unlocking capabilities." Citi has worked to bring staff
onto its "AI journey" — McIntosh's framing: "once you demystify it,
people come to realise that in the same way that the search engine made
their life a whole lot easier, AI can make your life a whole lot
easier." Notably candid on limits: as a regulated institution, "we
don't have licence to adopt [AI] in a way that's not thoughtfully done,
and that our regulators are uncomfortable with. So we're bringing them
along on our journey, too" — i.e. regulatory buy-in is treated as a
gating constraint on AI adoption pace, not an afterthought.
