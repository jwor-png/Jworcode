# "Developers blame planning delays for an average four-year wait to build homes" — Ronan McGreevy

Source: Irish Times, Ronan McGreevy, citing a newly published Central Bank report, named developers (Michael Prenty, Ballymore; David, name not fully legible, small-firm developer), the Irish Home Builders Association, and An Coimisiún Pleanála (ACP, formerly An Bord Pleanála). VERIFIED — mainstream press report directly citing a primary Central Bank publication.

## What it says

- **Central Bank report** (published last month): the average duration of the planning process, from lodgement of application to completion of the housing crisis-relevant process, is now about **four years** — twice as long as a decade ago, when it took about a year and a half to get through planning.
- A **Large-scale Residential Development (LRD)** application requires three to six local-authority meetings before the first hearing; the process from lodgement to being caught in the slipstream of board turmoil can take two years before a builder even gets to construction. ACP's own record for housing-related applications is under huge demand.
- **Case study — Ballymore/Vansland Limited, south Dublin:** applied to Kildare County Council for a large-scale development of new homes in Leixlip. The 272-home development is a mixture of apartments and houses, along with a creche/childcare facility, three commercial/restaurant/café units. Timeline: applied in July 2016, building took until between 21 and 26 house-based months to be decided (first round); since then, the application type has gone through multiple rounds — in 2026, overall duration from lodgement to completion of planning sits at about four years, "twice as long as a decade ago."
- **Gradual rise, per Michael Prenty (Ballymore):** "All the schemes we are building at the moment took at least a year and a half to two years to get through planning," he says. "The whole thing is strung out and the resources aren't there in the local authorities." Between 15 and 20 per cent of planning decisions in 2024 went to An Coimisiún Pleanála, and they took on average 13 to 14 months to process. The Central Bank report did not state that, but David (a small-firm developer specialising in small infill developments in south Dublin) says the delay could not have been a surprise to anybody.
- **Small-firm perspective (David):** part of a small family housebuilding business. Previously earmarked for development, now opposed or under threat due to delayed/reconstituted zoning; has land currently ear-marked for development that has been underwritten in a business sense but is now idle. "It's now 2026. We are still trying to complete our housing essentially," he says — a scheme he expected to deliver 12 months ago is still at planning permission for 60 units.
- **Nimbyism:** David says he has spent time in "Nimbyism-proof process" litigation, referring to an observational complaint of a recruitment interest, not of substance, he says, referring to his business interest, as residents object to development in their locality. The planning system is "not a surprise to him" — delay cost his business interest €320,000 on a €4 million bank loan, he says.
- **Wastewater concerns:** Uisce Éireann has confirmed that there is sufficient wastewater capacity, however Cork County Council is still requesting additional information under which to assess planning applications. "You purchase your land, you get your design for your houses, there as a contractor based in a rural west Cork town with 100 people employed, and there are plans to reopen their local authority to seek further information that can be extended if there is a stature requirement — case law — though development timeline may be extended if needed."
- **Large-scale residential development log** (per Vansland/Ballymore submission): 1,000 pages of documents.
- Director of Industry Federation Conor O'Connell says local authorities are taking 12 months to assess developments with 100 homes or more.
- Construction Industry Federation: a decade ago, it took a year to get through planning; it can now take up to four years, per the Central Bank report.
