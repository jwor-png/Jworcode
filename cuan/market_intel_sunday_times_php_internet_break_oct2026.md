# "Is the internet about to break?" (The Sunday Times, Business, p7, 25 Oct 2026; William Turvill)

Source: The Sunday Times, 25 October 2026, Business section p7. Captured by photograph from John, 28 Oct 2026; the page is photographed sideways with shadow and glare, so some lines are partly legible. Figures and quotes as printed; not independently verified. Standfirst: "After vulnerabilities emerged in the code at the heart of the internet, fears grew over the threat posed by hackers. Now bosses must act."

## What it says (legible parts)

- **The story:** chief information security officers (CISOs) worldwide are frustrated; Brian Brackenborough (formerly of Channel 4 and the BBC) and others believe it is high time boards and executive teams took their technical colleagues' warnings more seriously. The PHP episode "could have a long-tail effect over many years".
- **What PHP is:** the programming language underpinning up to 70 per cent of all websites, little known to ordinary internet users until last week. It was created in 1994 by Rasmus Lerdorf (Danish-Canadian programmer). It is open source and powers WordPress. It runs a very large share of sites, including Wikipedia, some BBC outlets, Nintendo and several UK government sites.
- **Who maintains it:** the PHP Foundation, whose executive director and only full-time employee is Elizabeth Barron (Cincinnati, Ohio). "We accomplish much with a very small staff and a very limited budget." The Foundation's income this year is just over 800,000 dollars (about 600,000 pounds), from 566 donors, and funds Barron and 14 contractors; Barron says PHP is "incredibly underfunded". The XKCD "all modern digital infrastructure" cartoon (a block tower resting on a small project maintained by one person) is cited.
- **The flaw:** the PHP vulnerability was due to be announced on Wednesday afternoon but leaked about six hours early; the panic was caused by a group of researchers, including employees of OpenAI and the Google-owned cybersecurity firm Wiz, who used AI to identify a hacking vulnerability inside PHP. Two critical flaws were found. A hacker who takes control of a website's PHP code "can take full control of the web server" (Issy Richards, Darktrace); damage could include taking a site down, locking it for ransom, and gaining personal details, passwords and sending phishing emails. Systems had to be patched within hours; fast-moving bots probe for unpatched sites immediately; some websites were taken down temporarily while patches were applied.
- **Wider pattern:** Darktrace's Richards: "It feels like a watershed moment." AI is lowering the cost of finding holes, for attackers and defenders. Microsoft's Patch Tuesday record (partly legible: 1,413 patches in a recent month, breaking an earlier record of 974). "For all the good guys, there will be bad guys seeking holes to exploit." Even where good guys find flaws, there is no guarantee the bad guys have not found the same ones.
- **Board points:**
  - Pippa Begg (Board Intelligence): "The PHP [saga] is one we should have seen coming. Many legacy code bases are more at risk, and boards should have an outsized focus on this in their risk reviews."
  - Rinki Sethi (former CISO of Twitter): CISOs need to translate cyber threats into business terms and what the impact could be to the business; this is about making it real.
  - Brackenborough's test: how many other areas does your company depend on absolutely, yet have no seat at the top table? The IT or security lead is often represented by the chief financial officer or chief operating officer, not by someone who understands the risk.
