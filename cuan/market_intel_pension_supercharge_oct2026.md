# "Seven simple ways to supercharge your pension before October ends" — Sinéad Ryan

Source: Irish Independent "Your Money" column, Sinéad Ryan. VERIFIED mainstream personal-finance column, citing named advisers (Sinéad Cullen/LifeCraft, Eoin McGee, Standard Life research, Tilda research). Timely given the 31 October pension-contribution deadline for 2025 tax-year relief.

## The seven steps

1. **What do you have?** Most people have had multiple employers — contact former employers/pension scheme administrators for benefit statements; check PRSI contributions via mywelfare.ie to see if the maximum state contributory pension (~€300/week, rising) is funded.
2. **What do you need?** Sinéad Cullen (LifeCraft): the old 50-60% pre-retirement-income replacement rule is a starting point, not the plan — "the better question is: what will your life actually cost, and what do you want it to look like?" Tilda research found actual retirement income mattered more to quality of life than simply replacing a fixed percentage of previous earnings.
3. **Mind the gap** — use the Pensions Authority's gap calculator (pensionsauthority.ie) or a financial planner to see the difference between what's already funded and what's needed; especially important for the self-employed, since CSO data shows only 57% of self-employed people have any private pension funding, compared with 68% of employees — a gap set to widen as auto-enrolment currently only covers employees.
4. **Educate yourself** — is your Approved Retirement Fund (ARF) big enough? Do you need an annuity? What's your Normal Retirement Age (NRA)? Citizensinformation.ie, CCPC.ie and PensionsAuthority.ie are cited as good public resources; professional investment advice (financialbroker.ie, fpireland.ie) is also recommended.
5. **How long do you have?** Not as long as you think — a 45-year-old has 240 pay days left. Self-employed people might work longer, but it's "no guarantee, especially in physically demanding jobs" (echoes the Golden Generation/succession material already tracked re: physically demanding occupations and retirement timing).
6. **Claim back Thursdays** — a 40% taxpayer working Monday-Friday effectively works almost two days a week for tax; making a pension contribution claims back Thursday's income via tax relief. Revenue age-related relief limits: a 45-year-old can invest 25% of income into a pension tax-relieved; over 55 it's 35%. Even via AVC (occupational schemes), PRSA, or another qualifying arrangement, this makes a big difference to the final pension pot — every €1,000 put in costs €600 (at the top relief rate) but delivers the full €1,000 invested value immediately.
7. **Plan for retirement, not just your pension** — a good financial planner starts with "what are your dreams for retirement?", not a technical question. Retirement can now last 20, 25 or even 30 years — travel, hobbies, grandchildren, volunteering should be planned for, not just money. Per Standard Life research: "one of the most enduring worries is the fear of not having enough [money]" and clients' "virtual exclusion of their future self" from present-day decisions is a common "dissonance" (Eoin McGee). "Health has to sit alongside wealth" (Ms Cullen) — financial planning should reduce fear, not create it, and provide for tomorrow without forgetting to live for today.
