# Cantillon column, Irish Times — two items, 17 Oct 2026

## 1. "AI ecosystem may be booming, but at what cost?"

**Headline figures:** Almost 182 deals across Irish AI companies in 2025
(a new record), per Pitchbook. Total invested in Irish AI companies this
year at a record €256 million, near 75 deals so far across 2025. Almost
€1 billion has been invested in Irish AI companies since Enterprise
Ireland's latest figures began tracking (framing somewhat ambiguous in
the clipping — worth checking the full Pitchbook/Enterprise Ireland
release before citing the headline €1bn figure externally).

**Context:** this ran the week of the International AI Summit (same
event as the McGrath keynote already logged — see
`market_intel_ai_act_summit_mcgrath_oct2026.md`). Protesters demonstrated
against data centres outside the Summit on Wednesday — photo caption
references this directly, tying the investment boom to the
data-centre/energy backlash thread already tracked.

**The "at what cost" angle — labour market:**
- CWU (Communication Workers' Union) event, earlier in the week: Christopher
  Paye (country director for the Stepstone Group in Ireland) warned of a
  narrowing skills gap and entry-level hiring decline as companies use AI
  to augment or replace human workers — "narrowing opportunities for
  entry-level workers," risk of degrading on-the-job skill development.
- Separately, Irish Times interview cited: second quarter 2026 figures
  found a 16% decline in entry-level hiring in occupations where AI is
  commonly used to augment human workers.
- Pitchbook's own report flagged a parallel risk: AI companies creating
  their own future skills gap by chasing AI utopia while underinvesting in
  junior/entry-level staff needed to grow and justify funding rounds.
- Named raise comparators in the clipping: Wayflyer (2025 blockbuster
  raise, performance highlighted), Series C round — figures given as
  €162m, €121m and €20.1m across different companies/rounds (clipping
  layout makes attribution between specific companies and figures
  unclear — do not quote these as matched to named firms without
  checking the original article).

**Relevance:** directly on-topic for Meridian's AI Strategy & Governance
work and the Ideas & Opportunities lens — Irish AI investment is at a
record, but the entry-level/skills-pipeline risk is now a live,
union-flagged and press-flagged theme, not just an abstract concern.
Sits alongside the already-logged McGrath "ahead of the curve, help
needed" Summit piece and the TUD AI governance conference material as

## 2. "Carelessness comes to insure..." (companion item, same page)

**Core point:** Irish homes are significantly underinsured. Insurer
Outsurance estimated that many homeowners could be undervaluing or
underinsuring the cost of rebuilding their homes by about €83,000 on
average. Example given: if rebuild cost is actually €600,000 but cover
is only €400,000, the shortfall (roughly two-thirds cover) leaves the
homeowner carrying €200,000 of risk themselves after a claim (flood,
fire, storm). Advice: check rebuild-cost valuations (not market value)
and make sure cover matches actual rebuild cost, not an outdated or
assumed figure.

**Relevance:** general household-finance/property-risk reference —
flagged in case it's useful for Bantry or any other property-cover
review; not tied to a specific tracked venture.
