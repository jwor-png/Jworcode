# Vincent Boland (The Big Picture, p23): "Anthropic's rush to the stock market raises some concerns"

Source: Business Post, 1 Nov 2026, page 23 (opinion column; two photographed spreads, tilted, with shadow; wording and figures as read and partly legible). Boland's argument is a critical opinion, not a news report. Cuan runs on Claude, so it is logged neutrally and with the source's slant stated.

## What it says

- **Framing:** in January Dario Amodei wrote a 20,000-word essay on the perils and potential of AI, arguing it is so powerful that humankind could be wiped out if we are not clever enough to use it wisely. On Monday Anthropic filed a **335-page** proposed listing document (the column counts about **240,000 words**). Boland's line: "AI could kill us all, but on the other hand, would anybody like to buy our stock?" The Nasdaq debut is expected soon and "sure to be a blockbuster"; SpaceX's June listing at about a **$1.75 trillion** valuation set the record. Anthropic is "AI pure and simple", the first company at the technology's pioneering edge to list, which is why the IPO matters.
- **His conclusion:** Anthropic and its "clique of hyper-sensitive, hyper-controlling founders" are "too immature for the stock market". Four factors:
  1. **An unfinished tool:** the prospectus has **94 pages of risk factors**, including that AI systems in the wrong hands "pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity"; he contrasts Henry Ford making sure the wheels did not fall off before selling cars.
  2. **Structure ("Five classes"):** the IPO creates at least **five classes** of common stock; Class A goes to outside investors with voting rights, but the document is silent on how many shares and at what valuation, and new shareholders would have essentially no say because of the other classes, which entrenches rather than reduces the founders' control ("par for the course for Silicon Valley and its cult of the founder"). It is a **public benefit corporation** ("Anthropic PBC"), not a corporation as investors usually understand the term, with a self-described "novel capital and corporate governance structure". The two key people are Dario Amodei and his sister **Daniela**, who chairs the board, is president and runs day-to-day operations: "buying shares in Anthropic is a bit like buying shares in the Amodei family."
  3. **Language ("Self-aggrandisement"):** the document refers to its "mission" 78 times, "humanity" 21 times and "history" 37 times ("mostly hype"). No industry has been more submerged in mystification and overstatement than AI.
  4. **Need to raise capital:** the document does not say how much the IPO will raise; reports suggest up to **$100bn**. The last private raise valued the company at just under **$1 trillion**, so a stake sale of about 10% may be ahead. Yet at the end of June it held **$130bn** in cash, equivalents and short-term investments, about six times the end-December figure. Revenue in the six months to June was **$16bn** against just over $1bn a year earlier. The column says Anthropic had a net loss of "$80 billion in 2025" (as read; other reports say first-half 2026, see below).
- **Why the rush ("Adults in the room"):** first-mover advantage as the first AI company to list; the IPO is "so hedged with qualifications and caveats that it is hardly an IPO at all". The potential **$2 trillion** target valuation mentioned "may or may not be worth" it. Silver lining: the debut could mark Anthropic's coming of age and the end of the era of AI hype, "in an industry badly in need of a few grown-ups".
