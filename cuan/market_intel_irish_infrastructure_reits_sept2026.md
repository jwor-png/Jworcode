## Investor Finn column — "Why I want to back Irish infrastructure"
Personal-finance columnist (writing anonymously as "Investor Finn")
describes reviewing their portfolio and, while nervous about US
equities near-term, wanting exposure to **Irish infrastructure ETFs**
specifically. Key points:
- Motivation: growing up watching delays and cost overruns on Irish
  infrastructure (National Children's Hospital, MetroLink, Transport
  Infrastructure Ireland projects), the columnist wants to "put their
  money where their mouth is" and back Irish infrastructure/savings
  rather than just complain.
- Notes infrastructure ETFs have historically returned ~8% per annum,
  though warns of high management fees and rising borrowing costs as
  live risks; flags that project cost overruns/delays are the central
  risk to watch (echoing the "boring things are the ones that last"
  theme in the adjacent regulatory piece already logged).
- References a Google search suggesting an Irish infrastructure fund
  from Merrion could be one entry point (unverified by the columnist
  themselves, presented as their own research trail, not a
  recommendation).
- General framing: "one of my biggest concerns is what happens if the
  project starts to overrun on budget or there are mistakes made" —
  echoed as the central risk of the asset class.
- Explicit disclaimer printed with the column: opinions/information are
  general and educational only, not financial/investment advice, and
  the columnist has no relevant qualifications to give such advice.

## Adjacent feature — Irish REITs/infrastructure financing (Fergal Phillips)
**Paul Kilcullen (CEO, BNY Mellon Ireland):** sees an opportunity to
create investment products channelling international/domestic capital
into Irish critical national infrastructure directly, allowing
long-term investors exposure to development, operational and long-term
assets. Advocates matching long-term investment vehicles/structures
with appropriate government policy and tax incentives so private
capital can be tapped for infrastructure without over-reliance on
public financing. Notes there's currently "a lot of discussion" but a
recognition that additional capital channels are needed.

**Daryl Byrne (CEO, Euronext Dublin):** referenced context on Irish
REITs (property-focused Real Estate Investment Trusts) — the regime was
created via 2013 legislation against a backdrop of housing/construction
demand, "at the time there was a real concerted effort to get a REIT
regime going," but few new REITs have listed since; suggests the
current REIT structure "hasn't resulted in a sustained number of REITs
coming to market" despite "nothing to shout about" fanfare when
introduced, and that a similarly designed regime is needed to
accelerate infrastructure capital specifically (not just property).

**GDI Management Group** referenced as the entity listed on the Irish
stock exchange (context around REIT listings), with Ires REIT cited as
chief executive-led context for the property-REIT comparison.
