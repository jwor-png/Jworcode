# "Just six derelict sites acquired by agreement with owners last year" (Sunday Independent, 25 Oct 2026)

Source: Sunday Independent, News and Business section, 25 October 2026, p5 (continued from page 1; Housing). Captured by photograph from John, 26 Oct 2026, turned sideways and partly legible (glare and shadow). Figures are as printed and not independently verified. Anything not clearly readable has been left out and is marked below.

## What it says (legible parts)

- **Headline claim:** only six derelict sites were acquired by agreement with owners last year.
- **Collection record:** local authorities have struggled to impose a solution or collect the money levied. Notices or demands were issued in respect of 4,112 sites, but in the majority of cases the councils did not secure a solution or collect the money. Almost half of local authorities failed to collect any derelict levy last year, although every county had a share of the 2,611 registered derelict sites at the end of 2025.
- **Register movement (as printed, partly legible):** 2,140 sites on the registers at the start of 2025; 864 added during 2025; 389 removed; 2,611 on the registers at the year end. (The article's own numbers are not fully readable here, so treat the arithmetic as unchecked.) Levies imposed during the year are given as about 12.6m euro, of which only about 1.07m was received.
- **New tax:** a new tax will be collected by the Revenue Commissioners. Amounts already levied under the current system stay the responsibility of local authorities.
- **Examples named in the text:**
  - Waterford is owed about 420,000 euro after collecting more than 70,000.
  - Galway City and Galway County collected no levies last year, despite placing more than 150 sites on their registers.
  - Dublin City Council received about 458,000 euro of the 2.8m euro it levied during 2025.
  - Limerick City and County Council, described as more proactive than most, collected just 116,597 euro of the dereliction fines it imposed last year, although it collected more than 500,000 euro of levies imposed in earlier years, leaving about 3.8m euro outstanding.
  - Cork City is owed more than 6m euro after collecting just over 400,000 euro.
- **Government position:** Finance Minister Simon Harris, earlier this month, called dereliction "a scourge in many of our towns and cities and quite simply an unacceptable spectacle in the depths of a housing crisis". One key government measure is the Vacant Property Refurbishment Grant, introduced in July 2022.
