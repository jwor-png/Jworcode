# Sunday Independent Life (15 Nov 2026), pp15 to 17: Maura Carey, "The Seven Deadly Feelings" (three photographed pages). Personal reference; opinion piece, not evidence.

Author: Maura Carey, IACP-accredited psychotherapist, clinical supervisor and doctoral researcher (Cherry Blossom Therapy). Graphics by Clare Meredith.

**Argument:** behind our reactions often lie feelings we would rather not admit to; we explain them away and stop questioning them. Seven feelings:
1. **Begrudgery:** the Irish talent for cutting people down; envy is not begrudgery (wanting something is not wanting them to lose it); success close to home can sting because it reminds us of an abandoned ambition; we rarely know what we are comparing (we see the house, not what it took).
2. **Shame:** goes to who we are, not what we did; people conceal ordinary difficulties (a struggling child, financial problems, a ended relationship); can make people withdraw or over-achieve; the difference between "I did something wrong" and "something is wrong with me"; we compare our struggles with others' presented lives; "we don't talk about shame".
3. **Anger:** often the end of a longer story (the sink of dishes is about who carries the household); not always a mask: it can be a justified signal that a limit has been crossed; shaped by how anger was handled growing up.
4. **Dread:** hypervigilance from earlier experience; ask whether you are recognising something present or responding to something familiar from the past.
5. **Anxiety:** the strength of a feeling does not prove a prediction; take unease seriously without treating it as proof.
6. **Resentment:** the dependable one in the family who is never asked whether they are managing; small requests become permanent arrangements; may reflect a genuine imbalance; ask whether you agreed because you wanted to or because saying no felt selfish, and whether you have told people what you need.
7. **Denial:** we spot these in others but not in ourselves; acknowledging a feeling is not letting it dictate behaviour ("I can admit I'm jealous without becoming begrudging"); "the next time you find yourself defending a reaction... ask what you're finding so difficult to acknowledge."
