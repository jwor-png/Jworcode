# Market Intelligence — "Parents selling family homes to adult
children to get them on property ladder" / "Shift in the 'bank of mum
and dad' as older couples seek to downsize" (Laura Lynott, Irish
Independent, Thursday 3 September 2026)

Newspaper clipping photographed by John, 9 Sept 2026, for his own
understanding and the knowledge base. General personal-finance/
property market intelligence.

## Core trend
Mortgage brokers report a new pattern: rather than the traditional
"bank of mum and dad" model (parents gifting a deposit from savings),
some parents are now **selling their own family home directly to their
adult child**, using the proceeds to both release equity for
themselves and help the child get a foothold on the property ladder —
often at a below-market price rather than through the open market.

## Key facts and figures
- **Illustrative scenario cited (Ross Craigie, Hegarty Financial
  Management):** a Dublin couple in their mid-30s (public sector
  workers) are currently buying a parental home valued at ~€650,000
  for **€400,000** — the parents gift €250,000 in equity to the child,
  who then takes out a €250,000 mortgage to complete the purchase. The
  parents use the cash to downsize to a smaller home or apartment.
- Mr Craigie describes this as "not the case any more" being unusual —
  he's seen "around a couple of grand" (i.e. a couple of instances,
  transcription ambiguity in the clipping) of gifts of this kind, and
  it's become **"more common."**
- **Why parents are doing this, per brokers:** genuine financial
  planning reasons on both sides — it lets parents release equity from
  a family home that may be larger/more expensive than they now need,
  while providing a secure, straightforward way to help an adult child
  buy, avoiding the open market entirely for the child (selling "to
  your child will make it easier... you will have to put off starting
  a family, generally speaking, in which to raise their family" —
  addressing declining birth rates as a backdrop concern too, per
  Aisling McNamara, mortgage sales director, Mortgage123).
- **Central Statistics Office data cited:** Ireland's birth rate
  dropped from 18.2 (2015) to a lower figure last year (exact 2025
  figure not clearly legible in the clipping) — cited as part of the
  broader context/motivation for this shift, alongside declining
  affordability generally.
- **Typical age of first-time buyer in this arrangement: 30-40s**
  (broader than the traditional younger first-time buyer cohort),
  reflecting a "hassle-free" way to buy a home viewed by some as a
  secure, less risky purchase route than the open market.
- **Considerations flagged by brokers:**
  - Parents should understand tax/legal issues before agreeing such an
    arrangement — specifically **gift/inheritance tax implications**
    and **stamp duty considerations**, since a below-market sale can
    still trigger tax exposure depending on the size of the gift
    element relative to Capital Acquisitions Tax thresholds.
  - Some parents structure this as an early inheritance-planning
    mechanism — passing on an adult child's inheritance while they're
    alive and can see the benefit, rather than "receiving it later" (a
    line directly echoing sentiments already logged elsewhere in
    John's own family-succession material).
  - Not always disclosed to banks/lenders as a related-party
    transaction — flagged as a practical/compliance nuance worth being
    aware of, not something the article recommends.
  - This mechanism can work in either direction of price: sometimes the
    parent sells at a genuine discount to help the child; in other
    cases (not detailed fully in the clipping) parents structure the
    sale near market value and simply gift part of the proceeds back.
