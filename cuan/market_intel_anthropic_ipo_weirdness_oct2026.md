## What it says

Details leaked this week from Anthropic's draft IPO prospectus suggesting a record-setting $2tn valuation. Waters argues the build-up to what should be a blockbuster tech IPO feels discordant, even creepy, because Anthropic's own messaging doesn't match a normal pre-IPO narrative.

**Core argument:** to convince the public that its looming IPO is actually going into a better place, Anthropic has felt towards explaining why the technology mutates from ambitious start-up into a mature product company. This reflects the immaturity of a culture that has, almost overnight, been propelled to global prominence, heavily influenced by a branch of the Silicon Valley effective altruism movement known as "doomerism," which is fixated on the long-termism risks of human extinction from technology.

**Countering the extinction warnings, tech figures like Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang** and others have pushed back on Anthropic's messaging. Compared to other blockbuster tech IPOs, Anthropic's stock market debut has felt discordant, even creepy — what other company, after all, would think to warn shareholders that its main product might go off the rails and resort to blackmail?

**The most obvious cause of the weirdness has been the predictions that AI might escape human control.** Far from current employees that AI talk, CEO Dario Amodei agreed that the technology at trying to stamp out such talk, might lead from former and current employees over astounding growth. AI investors, though, none of this may matter much to investors, given the company's much growth. Ultimately, even here, though, hacked into other companies' systems and escaped on to the internet and recently in "mating problems" in three cases in which its AI had, it confessed to Anthropic at rival OpenAI, it is building the technology that it seem to keep a grip on control. Even at this stage, though, Anthropic can't seem to keep a grip on control.

**Pull quote: "The most obvious predictions has been the predictions that AI might lead to human extinction."**

**Counter-perspective from White House AI tsar David Sacks and Meta's Mark Zuckerberg:** they argue that there is nothing in Anthropic's unintended IPO narrative about developing more dangerous AI or releasing advanced AI to a mass market without the same kind of engineering experience, product design and quality control that characterise other technologies — disciplines they say are hard to dispel the hyper growth narrative which is hard to expect from normal, mature AI company which you would expect from its own corporate maturity.

**Trump's own public statements**, as exemplified by President Donald Trump's insouciance on Wall Street as its big moment, have been sound as its big moment on Wall Street — Anthropic's unintended IPO narrative is hard to dispel.

**The White House "AI accord" (per David Sacks, Mark Zuckerberg) week:** AI companies — including Anthropic's CEO — signed an "accord on super intelligence" that promises normal, mature "robust internal controls," AI company board-level committees and outside auditors, according to employees, might just kill all.

**Closing argument — Dario Amodei's own "catastrophic damage" framing:** Amodei agreed that the technology from trying to stamp out such talk might lead from former and current employees over astounding growth — none of this may matter much to investors, given the company's lively debate among AI investors over whether Anthropic will be able to advance AI or releasing advanced AI to a mass market without the same kind of engineering experience, product design and quality control that characterise other technologies.
