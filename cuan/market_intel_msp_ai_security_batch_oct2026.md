# Three further MSP commentary pieces, same 18 Oct Business Post feature as Ekco

**Triangle Technology Services (Brendan Healy, services director,
founded 1999, 120 staff):** cyber recovery (backup/data management)
and AI are now the two main areas of client demand. Notable line on
contract structure: "change should be built into contracts... that
one-off licence for the hardware" model is being replaced by
architect-led, evolving arrangements — "if that doesn't disappear in
year one of a five-year contract... that's nonsense." Argues the MSP
relationship needs a clear rules-of-governance/data-mining framework
from the outset, not bolted on later.

**Landmark Technologies (Ken Kelleher, managing director, founded
1998, 24 staff):** reframes the MSP pitch away from cost-saving toward
risk reduction and specialist access — "a firm of 40 people can
justify one IT person, but they can't be a security specialist, a
cloud specialist, a cyber compliance specialist and an AI specialist."
Key line: **"security is often an afterthought and we're helping to
put that front of mind."** Describes MSPs evolving from "managed
service provider" to **"managed intelligence provider"** as they
start supplying AI agents that do work for clients directly. Advice to
buyers: "ask the MSP about their own security — if they aren't able to
describe their own controls, they shouldn't be providing AI agents to
your business."

**Datapac (Karen O'Connor, general manager):** argues managed IT
services shouldn't be treated as "set and forget" — relationships
should be reviewed for continuous improvement (service levels,
response times, security posture) rather than just renewed on
autopilot. Explicit tie to cybersecurity: "the threat landscape is not
static, and neither can the measures used to protect an organisation."

**Relevance:** same theme already captured in full via the Ekco piece

supporting detail (the "managed intelligence provider" reframing in
particular is a sharp, reusable line) but don't change the core
picture. Logged briefly rather than as full separate entries.
