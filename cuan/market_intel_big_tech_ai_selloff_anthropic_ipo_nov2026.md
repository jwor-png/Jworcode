## What it says

- **The theme:** a grim few days for tech, with investors seemingly losing patience with the vast sums spent on AI infrastructure. The writer says it is too early to call a dotcom-style bubble burst.
- **The sell-off:** on the FT's count, Big Tech lost a combined **$900bn** in market value in recent days as firms signalled more capital expenditure. Hyperscalers' capex came to **$207bn** in the third quarter against **$113bn** a year earlier (the FT's figures). The companies all said they plan to keep spending even if investors are unhappy. Alphabet's CFO Anat Ashkenazi: "We continue to expect our capex to increase significantly in 2027."
- **Alphabet:** shares closed **down 12.6% on Thursday**, wiping nearly **$500bn**, its biggest one-day fall since it went public in 2004. It missed on revenue and earnings for the first time in nearly four years; core search growth slowed to 12%. Michael Spencer (AI Supremacy) called it "maybe the most disappointing earnings in its history". Jeffrey Wlodarczak (Pivotal Research): this may be the first quarter AI is "seriously" hurting search. Angelo Zino (CFRA): the fall "probably represents a buying opportunity".
- **Amazon:** shares down about **7%** on Friday; earnings below forecast, data centre build-out ate into cash ("capex just absolutely swallowed it", Dave Wagner, Aptus Capital Advisors).
- **Meta:** shares down nearly 7% last week after results; it spent **$34.9bn** on data centres and equipment in the quarter, more than operations generated. Zuckerberg: "It's going to be very important to get the math on all of this to work out." Gene Munster (Deepwater) says Meta's personal AI agent, Muse, is losing more money than any other Meta product.
- **Microsoft** fared better: revenue up 19%, Azure up 48%, capex below forecast, free cash flow **$18.6bn** (about double expectations).
- **Intel:** shares fell up to **30%** on Friday on a memory-chip shortage as data centre operators absorb supply, a weak final-quarter forecast, and no PC recovery until mid next year. Intel employs just under 5,000 in Ireland. **Leixlip** is central to its recovery (Intel committed an additional 5bn euro in July; the plant makes the Xeon chips used in AI data centres, where sales were up 78% in the quarter), so the Irish operation sits on the right side of the AI boom "even if the rest of Intel is paying for it".
- **Cloud:** Google Cloud revenue doubled to $30.7bn; AWS grew 39% to $46bn, its fastest in almost five years; Andy Jassy says AWS could be a trillion-dollar revenue business within five to ten years. But free cash flow is negative at Alphabet (-$9.1bn in Q3) and Amazon (-$42.4bn over 12 months to September).
- **Funding:** the giants are turning to lenders and investors. Alphabet sold $28.7bn of bonds and $12.4bn of new shares in Q3. Meta announced a new class of non-voting shares so it can raise capital without diluting voting power (Zuckerberg keeps roughly 61% of the votes).
- **Anthropic:** the sell-off is "badly timed" for Anthropic's IPO, which is aiming to complete by mid-November. Reuters reports it is seeking to raise as much as **$100bn**, which would surpass SpaceX's record **$75bn** flotation in June. Its filing warns compute and infrastructure spend will keep rising and remain its biggest cost; it has committed more than **$600bn** to computing deals with partners including Amazon and Google. Investors expect it to seek **$1.5 trillion to $1.75 trillion** on floating. OpenAI is also aiming to list, reportedly at up to **$1.25 trillion**.
- **Comment:** Matt Kennedy (Renaissance Capital, to Reuters): "Anthropic is entering an IPO market that is far more challenging than the summer." Lukas Muehlbauer (IPOX): its IPO "will establish a benchmark for valuing leading AI labs".
- **Dublin:** Anthropic already employs over 100 people in Dublin, announced a further 200 jobs in March and is looking for premises for up to 1,000 staff. OpenAI also has a Dublin base and said this month it is creating a further 200 roles. The writer says their flotations will test how much faith investors still have in the AI revolution.
- **Sidebar "The numbers":** $900bn wiped off Big Tech; 7% fall for Meta and Amazon; $500bn and 12.6% for Alphabet; 30% for Intel.
