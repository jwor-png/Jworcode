# "Farm succession made easier, but will the next generation want to take over?"

Source: Irish Independent Budget 2027 coverage, 7 Oct 2026. Includes news report, farmer case studies, and an "Analysis" column by Margaret Donnelly.

## What it says

**The demographic headline:** nearly 38% of Irish farmers are aged 65 or over; just 4% are under 35. EU Agriculture Commissioner Christophe Hansen, speaking at the National Ploughing Championships: "there can be no long-term future for European agriculture without a new generation of farmers."

**Budget 2027 measures (Agriculture Minister Martin Heydon):**
- Tax credit for newly registered Succession Farm Partnerships **increased from €5,000 to €10,000**.
- The **three-year holding period** to transfer assets under the scheme has been **removed**.
- Fuel Income Support Scheme for farmers/agricultural contractors extended a further five months.
- SEAI grant aid for solar panels on shed roofs (battery storage not yet included — flagged as a gap by a named farmer).
- TAMS (Targeted Agricultural Modernisation Scheme) budget increased to €93m, against a sector ask of €100m.
- €31m fertiliser fund (an effective rebate), average farmer spend €15-20k/year, rebate ~€1,000.
- VAT flat rate for farmers to increase from 4.5% to 4.8%.
- Agri-taxation measures worth ~€470m annually overall, per Minister Heydon.

**The reaction — welcomed but called insufficient:**
- **Macra (young farmer organisation):** "fixing a flaw in an existing scheme is not the same as investing in succession" (Josephine O'Neill, president). Macra proposed a separate €5m pilot Succession Scheme to support 100 farm families, CAP-supported.
- **ICSA president Edmond Phelan:** removing the three-year holding period and raising the tax credit are "positive steps," but "much more needed to support generational renewal." Key line: **"the starting point has to be viable farms... the next generation will not take over if there is no sustainable income from farming."**
- **ICMSA president Denis Drennan:** the core issue isn't the schemes themselves, it's that young people aren't committing to farming and food production at all.
- **Scale of actual uptake flagged as the real problem, not the tax mechanics:** only **162 active Succession Farm Partnerships currently registered** nationally — a strikingly low number given the 38%-of-farmers-over-65 backdrop. Karol Kissane (ifac, head of economics): the three-year transfer rule meant partnerships "had not seen the volume it should" — "things can change in three years... so there was a lot of uncertainty" for anyone considering it.

**Margaret Donnelly's analysis column — the sharper underlying point:** Budget 2027 has removed some procedural obstacles to succession, but has it addressed the actual reason young people may not want to take over the farm in the first place? Well-paid 9-to-5 jobs make a seven-day-a-week farming career unattractive to many, while farming "on a promise" (the hope of eventually inheriting) is risky. Her conclusion: it could take years before anyone knows whether the combined national/EU supports are enough to make farming an attractive, viable career choice for the next generation — but "the sector can't afford to wait a generation to find out."
