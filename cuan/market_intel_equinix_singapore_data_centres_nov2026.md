# Vish Gain: "Equinix chief: Ireland can learn from Singapore's policy on data centres"

Source: Business Post, 1 Nov 2026 (interview with Peter Lantry, managing director of Equinix Ireland, by Vish Gain). Captured by photograph from John, 1 Nov 2026, page sideways with shadow; figures and quotes as read. Interview with a data-centre operator, so a commercial perspective.

## What it says

- **The argument:** Ireland should learn from Singapore and build more data centres in Dublin as a matter of "urgency". Singapore has more political will to build data centres to support its economy but lacks space and renewable energy; Ireland has the space and the renewable potential but lacks the political will. Lantry: "Singapore is very jealous of Ireland because we have the space to build out renewable power, that supports a data centre build out."
- **Singapore's approach:** it decided it still needs more data centres despite little space, will build out a generation station to support them, and runs an **auction** to decide what kind of data centres best support the economy; operators submit papers explaining why they should get access to power. Equinix has been successful in **both rounds** held so far.
- **Equinix in Ireland:** Lantry has led the Nasdaq-listed company's Irish arm since February 2023; Equinix (led globally by Irishwoman Adaire Fox-Martin) provides multinationals with data centre co-location; all **eight** Irish sites are in Dublin. It recently moved its Irish headquarters from CityWest to about 5,500 sq ft at 1 George's Quay (60 employees, a 1.8 million euro investment) amid demand for its services in the AI boom.
- **Opposition:** Lantry sees a "complete lack of understanding" of data centres' purpose: "They're looking at a building and they're looking at what that building uses rather than what it does." Customers include insurance, utility, pharma, health science and finance firms and central banks. **Data centres accounted for 23% of Ireland's electricity consumption in 2025**, which has put the sector under greater public scrutiny. He argues data centres can also provide flexibility and support grid stability.
- **AI demand:** "The amount of capacity and connectivity that is being driven by AI is significantly more than has traditionally been needed in the past." Infrastructure constraints are a "major barrier in place for future generations". Utilities fear backlash from the public and from **judicial reviews** on grid investment. Ireland needs to become much better at moving quickly.
