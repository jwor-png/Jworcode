## 1. "I'm planning to install a home office. Will insurance cover all the equipment?" (Ian Kennedy, chief marketing officer, OUTsurance)
- Depends on the policy and on who owns the equipment and how it is used. Many home policies cover some office equipment but with limits and conditions.
- A laptop you bought yourself may be treated differently from one supplied by your employer (which may fall under the employer's insurance).
- Ordinary remote working at a desk is usually fine; **freelance work, meeting clients at home or storing stock** may need a different product. Declare expensive tech or specialist equipment separately. Review contents cover generally: a home office can push contents value above the original sum insured.

## 2. Self-employed retired couple: pension funds jointly bought two properties (60/40 and 50/50); what happens on a death? (Glenn Gaughran, Independent Trustee Company)
- A surviving spouse can often hold on to jointly pension-bought property; it depends on how ownership is structured and whether the pensions are in retirement (ARF, vested PRSA) or pre-retirement.
- Where the deceased is in retirement and the property sits in an ARF or vested PRSA, ownership can be **transferred tax-free to an ARF in the surviving spouse's name** provided they are the beneficiary; determined by the terms of the will (or intestacy rules). Result there: 100% of the property in the survivor's pension, with full entitlement to the rental income. Seek financial and legal advice.

## 3. "Can shops increase their prices before a sale?" (CCPC)
- No. A business cannot mislead consumers by raising prices before a sale. It must display the sale price and the **"prior price": the lowest price in the 30 days before the sale** (example: "was 700, now 500" requires no sale below 700 in the previous 30 days). Misleading advertising and false information are illegal.
