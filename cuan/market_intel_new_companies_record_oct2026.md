# "Record number of new companies established since the start of year" — John Mulligan

Source: Irish Independent Business, citing CRIFVision-net (Christine Cullen, managing director) and the Companies Registration Office. VERIFIED mainstream press report citing a named credit-risk analyst's own data.

## What it says

- **Almost 22,000 new companies registered in Ireland so far this year** — the highest ever for the first nine months of any year, 8% higher than the same period in 2025.
- Vision-net labels these "start-ups," but the reality is more varied: some are holding companies for existing businesses or to acquire specific assets (property, aircraft lessors creating new companies to own clutches of jets, companies preparing to list notes on the stock market). **A large number, though, are genuinely people branching out on their own** — plumbers, electricians, restaurant operators, people setting up their own tech businesses.
- **Sectoral growth breakdown:** IT sector strongest at **+54% year-on-year** ("reflecting continued activity within Ireland's technology and digital economy"); motor sector **+23%**; construction **+15%**; manufacturing **+11%**.
- Christine Cullen: growth "was not confined to the main urban centres, with increased start-up activity recorded across the entire country" — also described as pointing to "the resilience of the Irish business landscape."
- **Companies Registration Office noted during summer that new company creation was at one point running 30% ahead of 2025 levels** — had to allocate extra staff to process formation applications.
- **Parallel indicator cited:** CRIFVision-net reports a significant reduction in recorded commercial and consumer judgments alongside the formation surge — value of commercial judgments fell 21% year-on-year to €31m in the first nine months, volume down 20%; volume of consumer judgments fell 24% year-on-year — read together as "a further indication of resilience across the Irish business and consumer landscape."
