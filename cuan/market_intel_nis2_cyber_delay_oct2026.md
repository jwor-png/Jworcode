# "Delays to new law leaves firms exposed to cyber threats" — Carlo Salizzo

Source: Irish Independent Business, Opinion column, Carlo Salizzo. VERIFIED — named columnist, citing the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) and European Commission referral action.

## What it says

- **Ireland has not yet transposed the EU's second Network and Information Systems Directive (NIS2) into Irish law** — it was due in October 2024, now a year overdue. This leaves Ireland exposed to potential EU fines and is "a bad look" given Ireland currently holds the EU presidency, with security stated as one of its own priorities.
- **Scale:** the NCSC estimates **over 3,000 Irish organisations** will be covered by the new law once transposed, not counting customers/suppliers of those entities in critical sectors — energy, food, manufacturing, transport, healthcare, banking.
- **Business impact of the delay:** uncertainty is actively affecting corporate decisions — internal procedures, incident response policies, contractual terms, and who to hire for senior security roles. The author argues the delay "has in turn impeded investment," including affecting decisions by multinational companies that might otherwise locate cybersecurity operations in Ireland.
- **NCSC's own position:** established 2011 (Department of Justice), significantly expanded following the high-profile 2021 HSE cyberattack, now 90+ employees. Unlike counterparts in most EU countries, its powers of intervention and investigation are "significantly curtailed" in the absence of NIS2 — **it has never been put on a statutory footing as an independent body**, something NIS2 transposition would fix.
- **EU enforcement action:** in August, the European Commission referred Ireland, France, Spain and the Netherlands to the EU Court of Justice for failing to transpose NIS2. The author's framing: the point isn't avoiding legal action/fines, it's "the need to effectively protect Ireland, Irish organisations and companies based here from cyber attacks."
- **Competing legislative priorities acknowledged:** NIS2 competes for technical drafting resources against other priorities like the EU AI Act, even as cyber risk increases alongside growing technology adoption.
- **Closing line:** "We need a robust defence and we need it as soon as possible."
