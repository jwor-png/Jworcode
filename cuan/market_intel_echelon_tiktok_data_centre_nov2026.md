# Business Post: "Echelon must pay TikTok 100m euro over shelved Irish data centre plan" (Laura Roddy, Industry Correspondent)

Source: Business Post (the article says "the Business Post can reveal"). Issue date not visible on this page; other clippings photographed in the same sitting are confirmed as the Business Post of 1 Nov 2026, so this is very likely the same edition. Captured by photograph from John, 1 Nov 2026; page photographed sideways with shadow, so wording and figures are as read and flagged where unclear.

## What it says

- **The payment:** Irish data centre firm **Echelon** will have to pay **TikTok 100 million euro** in compensation after TikTok pulled the plug on its second Irish data centre, per accounts filed last week for TikTok's Irish operations.
- **The write-off:** TikTok's Irish operations wrote off **65.8 million dollars (58 million euro)** in impairments on the Echelon data centre in 2025: **45.7 million dollars** on data centre equipment and **20.1 million dollars** on the fit-out of the Tropical Fruit Warehouse, which is now leased to OpenAI. The payment results from a **termination agreement made in January** between TikTok and Echelon, the firm led by **Niall Molloy**.
- **Background:** TikTok announced its first Irish data centre in 2023 (K2 Strategies' campus, Blanchardstown, Dublin; gone live), then a second at Echelon's campus in Clondalkin, Dublin, known as "Dub 10". In 2026 TikTok called it quits on that second site. It opened a data centre in **Hamar, Norway, in 2025** and plans a third in **Kouvola, Finland, by the end of 2026**.
- **Accounting treatment:** the accounts note that TikTok expects to receive the 100 million euro in 2026, but as the agreement was concluded after the reporting date, it is a non-adjusting event, so no amount is recognised in the 2025 financial statements.
- **Carbon:** TikTok's UK accounts for 2025 say its carbon emissions were similar to 2024, helped by opening the Norwegian data centre; the article contrasts Norway's cleaner grid with Ireland's.
- **Comment:** An **IDA** spokesman said Ireland "remains an important location for data centres and digital infrastructure", but access to secure, scalable and increasingly renewable energy is critical to competitiveness. An **Echelon** spokesman said it "should not be an issue of choosing between digital investment and climate objectives". The Department of Enterprise said Ireland "remains an attractive and dynamic hub of investment". Echelon's campus, just off the M50, can provide over 90 megawatts across three data centres; AWS agreed in 2023 to lease one of them, per the accounts. TikTok and Echelon declined to comment.
