# AI agency blueprint — Threads thread, 21 Oct 2026

**Claim, unverified:** poster says he started an AI agency "broke,
with no clients and no experience," now runs a "$3M+/year agency"
with 3 people, AI doing work he'd otherwise have hired for.

**The four-step framing, useful regardless of the revenue claim:**
1. **Pick a niche with no idea how to implement AI** — charge an
   established operator (his example: a $7M/year, 50-staff law firm)
   for what they can't build themselves; "you get paid for what
   they'll never build themselves."
2. **Sell it before you build a single thing** — sell the offer first,
   build only what's already been paid for, rather than building
   speculatively and trying to bend the market to fit it afterward.
3. **Sell the outcome, then put a number on it** — "nobody's paying
   for the AI, they're paying for what it gives back." Price against
   what the problem already costs the client, not against the
   technology itself (his example: a wellness studio closed on $20k
   once shown what wasted admin was actually costing them).
4. **Build one agent per job, not one that does everything** — narrow,
   single-purpose agents outperform a generalist one; "you can be good
   at anything, but you can't be good at everything — same goes for
   AI."

**Relevance:** generic growth-marketing content, revenue figure
unverified — but the framing (price against the cost of the problem,
not the technology; sell the outcome before building; narrow
single-purpose agents) is directly usable positioning language for
Meridian/Velocity AI/Ambrion AI's own sales conversations, consistent
with similar material already logged (the Startup Channel Map, Dear
Vicki's client-profitability columns). Nothing here changes strategy,
just reusable phrasing.
