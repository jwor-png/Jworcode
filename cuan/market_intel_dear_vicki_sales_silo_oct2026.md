# "How can I make my sales team realise the business is not just all about them?" — Dear Vicki, Vicki O'Toole

Source: Irish Independent Business, "Dear Vicki" advice column. VERIFIED mainstream press advice column, named columnist.

## What it says

**The question:** reader runs a large engineering company in the south-west, multiple divisions, where sales has "almost become a company all to itself" — staff identify with the department rather than the firm, compete with other departments, blame other sectors when things go wrong, try to grow their own budget at others' expense, and hoard information that should be shared across the team. The reader describes this as bad for morale and says the company is "suffering, as sales seems to have lost sight of the overall strategy."

**Vicki's diagnosis:** the issue is cultural, not a sales-performance problem — relocating desks or running social events won't fix a mindset problem; people who see their department as competing with others won't share information just because they had a nice team day out.

**Her own experience, cited as the basis for the advice:** joining her husband's family packaging business, she learned each department's processes and found that despite working toward a common goal, team members often viewed the same problem from different perspectives (e.g. purchasing hesitant to introduce a product sales wanted to push; sales wanting lower prices while purchasing worried about order volume at that rate) — not negligence, just each department viewing it solely through its own lens.

**The fix she implemented as managing director — restructured "sales meetings":** rather than starting with the sales agenda, meetings began with the head of accounts, then purchasing, so ideas/concerns from those functions were raised before the sales discussion — surfacing problems in the room rather than letting them get kicked down the line, and giving each department visibility into how its part fit the bigger picture.

**Her recommendation for the reader:** bring heads of the different divisions together regularly with the overall company strategy as the shared agenda; every department needs to understand the pressures and priorities of the others, and how its own decisions affect the wider business. **Key line, pull-quote in the article:** "Every department needs to understand the pressures and priorities of the others." Information cannot be kept within one department if another part of the business needs it.

**On incentive structures — the sharpest point in the piece:** "I would also look carefully at what is being rewarded. If people are measured purely on their own department's performance, it is hardly surprising if they protect their own patch." Recommends consistent language from leadership — "our" customers/results/problems, not "my department" and "your department" — since people will follow that lead.
