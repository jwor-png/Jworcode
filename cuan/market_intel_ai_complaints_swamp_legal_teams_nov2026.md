# Eoin O'Hare: "Complaints using AI swamp firms' legal teams"

Source: newspaper business page photographed by John on 1 Nov 2026; publication not named on the photo, likely the Business Post 1 Nov 2026 edition (same sitting as the other clippings), unconfirmed. Photographed sideways with shadow; wording as read.

## What it says

- **The pattern:** Irish in-house legal teams are being flooded with increasing amounts of irrelevant and lengthy submissions aided by AI: customer complaints, subject access requests, and employee complaints and grievances generated by AI, "framed in very legally sophisticated language".
- **Eir (Sorcha Nic Mhurchú, general counsel):** "So much of that AI-assisted material is irrelevant and just not arising... you end up going back and having to figure out, line by line, what is relevant. So that adds a lot of volume and time to any response, and that's a reality for everybody, not just us."
- **Dentons report:** the international law firm says the use of AI in correspondence is placing a "significant burden" on the legal departments of Irish firms. Flipdish's Máire Neary (vice president of legal and people) says firms will keep seeing significant volumes of irrelevant AI-associated material in grievances and complaints, with implications for how HR is resourced and trained; HR will need a more legally informed perspective.
- **Wayflyer (Deirdre McCarthy, chief legal and people officer):** people spend a lot of time trading red lines because they are putting contracts into their AI tool; AI-produced content "sometimes looks good, but may not always be good", so firms are cognisant of the need for oversight and project risk.
- **Eir (Aideen Delaney, commercial and corporate solicitor):** AI is "here to stay"; she does not expect it to change the structure of the legal department but it may change the shape of the day-to-day and time allocation; "responsible adoption is probably where we are".
- **Other items on the same page (not logged in detail):** AerCap raised 2026 guidance after a record quarter ($2.4bn revenue, $14bn to deploy); Danske Bank is considering its future role in Northern Irish retail banking (Fionn Thompson).

## Relevance (Cuan's read)

- **Meridian and Jurgen (legal AI):** evidence that AI is both creating volume for legal and HR teams and being adopted by them; supports an angle on triage, relevance-filtering and verified summaries, with human oversight (the "informs, does not represent" line).
- **Noesis governance and John's own matters:** the same effect will appear in complaints, subject access requests and claim correspondence (for example AHL and UHL matters); a reason to keep a verified, source-tagged record and a clear human sign-off.
- **Velocity AI and Ambrion AI training:** a ready example for HR and legal audiences on the cost of unreviewed AI-generated correspondence.
- **Caveats:** a single press report quoting in-house lawyers; no data on the scale of the increase.
