# Market intel — "Did you not get my message? How to avoid inbox anxiety"

**Source:** Sunday Independent, Business/Comment p.10, 30 August 2026,
Gina London ("The Communicator" column). REPORTED — columnist's own
framing plus a cited Appspace survey; not independently verified by
Cuan.

## What it says
Appspace survey of 1,000 UK employees (2026 workplace report): 72%
felt out of the loop on important workplace information during the
previous year; 81% said their organisation's communication lacked
consistency across channels; 20% said they missed critical updates
because they were overwhelmed by too many messages/channels; another
24% said they've never had more important information to communicate,
while buried in the wrong channel. Separately cited: over a quarter of
respondents said they missed critical updates specifically sent by
email.

London's argument: channel proliferation (email, Teams/Slack, intranet,
project-management tools, WhatsApp, text, Google Drive notifications,
Instagram DMs, LinkedIn messages) means recipients no longer know where
to look, and senders wrongly assume sending equals communicating.

## Her three fixes
1. **Agree the channel before blaming the recipient.** Ask direct
   reports/colleagues/clients how they prefer to communicate and how
   often — don't assume, don't "auto-adopt" a workplace's default
   system. Where there's no explicit agreement, do a periodic reset:
   "How do you prefer I contact you?"
2. **The burden of clarity is on the sender.** Consider length,
   structure, deadline, and required action explicitly — a subject line
   like "Please get me the Widget report by Tuesday" beats a buried ask.
   State if action is required, say so, be crystal clear about the
   deadline and who owns the next step.
3. **Take control of your own communication traffic.** Set rules for
   which channels you check and when; batch or bury (don't
   proactively monitor) low-priority channels; unsubscribe from what
   you don't need; agree "reply-by" windows rather than treating every
   channel as always-on; turn off notifications that don't deserve
   interrupt-level attention.
