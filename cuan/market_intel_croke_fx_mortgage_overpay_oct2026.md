# "Your financial questions answered" — Liam Croke, Making Cents

Two reader Q&As. Third Liam Croke column now logged in Cuan (after the

## Q1 — $200,000 held in dollars: convert to euro and invest now, or wait for the dollar to strengthen?

Croke declines to call the FX move and instead lays out why timing it
is genuinely hard right now:
- EUR/USD moved from above 1.18 to 1.16 in days — euro weakened on
  Middle East conflict/energy-cost inflation fears; dollar strengthened
  as a safe haven on US energy independence.
- This runs **against** 2026 consensus expectations (USD weakening
  vs EUR, on the ECB being closer to the end of its rate-cutting cycle
  than the Fed, with the Fed expected to cut twice this year).
  Complicating factors: unclear how much rate-cut expectation is
  already priced in; **Kevin Warsh's nomination as the new Fed chair**
  — a Trump appointee but with a history of hawkish/strong-dollar
  views, muddying the "Trump wants a weaker dollar" assumption.
- Core lesson, stated plainly: accurate short-term FX forecasting
  requires simultaneously forecasting rates/inflation/trade
  balances/public debt for **both** economies, plus unpredictable
  geopolitical shocks (Greenland, Middle East this year) — Croke
  doesn't give a buy/wait answer, just the factors.

## Q2 — €400,000 mortgage (25yr, 3.47% variable, €1,996/month): overpay by €300/month, or save/invest it instead?

Full worked comparison over the 25-year term:
- **Overpaying €300/month on the mortgage:** cuts the term by 4 years
  9 months, saves **€41,710 in interest**. The overpayment itself
  effectively drops the mortgage's real rate from 3.47% to **2.69%**.
  After the mortgage clears early, redirecting the freed-up €1,996/
  month repayment for the remaining 57 months at an assumed 4% annual
  return adds a further **€11,298** — **total outcome: €53,008.**
- **Saving/investing €300/month instead, mortgage untouched, 35
  years, 4% annual return:** accumulates **€154,239**, of which
  **€64,239 is interest/growth** — i.e. a **€11,231 better outcome**
  than the overpayment route, but entirely contingent on actually
  achieving a **net** 4% return every year.
- **The real bar to clear:** to beat the mortgage-overpayment route,
  an investment needs a **net** return above 3.47%/year — which,
  after tax and investment management fees, Croke estimates requires
  a **gross** return of roughly **6.67%**. Possible, but "you would
  have to invest in high risk accounts" to target that.
- **Croke's own framing, worth holding as a general principle:**
  overpaying a mortgage is a **guaranteed** return at the mortgage's
  own rate; investing instead is a **variable, risk-bearing** return
  that must clear a meaningfully higher bar once tax/fees are
  accounted for — "the return is a big factor" in the actual decision,
  and sometimes people reasonably choose the lower-return route anyway
  for non-financial reasons (saving liquidity, risk tolerance).
