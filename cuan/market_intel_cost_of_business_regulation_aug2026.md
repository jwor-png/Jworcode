# Market intel — "Avalanche of regulations is crippling business"

**Source:** Sunday Independent, News/Business p.3, 30 August 2026, Sarah
Collins. REPORTED — quotes from named business-body figures and a
government-commissioned forum report; not independently verified by
Cuan.

## What it says
The Government's Cost of Business Advisory Forum (24 business reps, 23
state agencies, 14 government departments, set up June 2025 under
Enterprise Minister Peter Burke) published its final report on Friday
28 Aug 2026, with 63 recommendations on energy, insurance, planning,
water, legal, tax and banking. Central finding: "shocking" and outdated
laws are adding to a cost-of-business crisis driven by rising wage and
utility bills, with calls for a cultural and regulatory audit of
non-wage costs.

Key figures/claims cited:
- SFA survey (summer 2026): businesses with fewer than 50 staff have
  seen costs rise more than 44% in three years (David Broderick, SFA).
- A restaurant selling alcohol can require up to 23 licences/permits;
  childcare facilities up to 33; nursing homes 45 (Sarah Collins'
  reporting on the forum's findings).
- RGDATA (Tara Buckley): food retail is "the most regulated, licensed
  and inspected sector"; grocery margins of 2-3% are being squeezed by
  22% energy cost rises and double-digit water charge increases.
- Report calls for a cut in energy/water tariffs and reduction of a new
  €1,800 fee for retailers to sell tobacco and vapes.
- Wages were reportedly kept off the forum's agenda despite the Low Pay
  Commission's recommended 5.6% minimum wage increase — flagged as a
  gap by RGDATA.
- Restaurants Association of Ireland (Adrian Cummins): called licensing
  requirements "nonsense," some dating to before the foundation of the
  State; welcomed the forum as the first real government attention on
  business costs in a long time.
- ISME (Neil McDonnell): report will be seen as a "test of government
  resolve" — large businesses doing well, small businesses struggling to
  stay profitable; "delivery will be a test of government credibility."
- Dublin Chamber (Aebhric McGibney): report reflects a renewed
  government focus on costs but "you would expect them to implement it."
- One anonymous forum participant flagged new Revenue real-time
  reporting demands on staff gifts (e.g. an Easter egg, flowers for a
  new baby) as disproportionate, with suspicion it may be a precursor to
  taxing such gifts.
