# Dear Vicki (Vicki O'Toole), newspaper p.25: "Additional costs are killing my business, what can I do to stop us going bust?" (John's photo, 16 Nov 2026; no note). Opinion column, not evidence.

**Question (anonymous Irish B2B services owner):** auto-enrolment, PRSI increases, statutory sick pay and other payroll costs hit small firms harder than large; raising fees risks losing business to competitors.

**Advice (Vicki O'Toole, formerly of JJ O'Toole; her own account of the 2002 plastic-bag levy, when 80pc of the business was plastic and she reversed the mix to 80pc paper; 2008 and Covid also tested it):**
1. Know your numbers, especially gross margin; turnover alone tells you little; more business is not necessarily better business.
2. Cost each service (time and labour); find which clients and contracts are profitable.
3. Examine how work is done: senior time on administration, duplication, unnecessary reporting; standardise tasks; small efficiencies need no expensive technology.
4. Review the service offering: be clear what the fee includes; charge separately for work outside the original agreement.
5. Talk to clients and suppliers about what they value; streamline reporting, agree realistic turnaround times.
6. Closing line: you cannot keep doing things the same way and expect a different result.

## Addendum (17 Nov 2026): Dear Vicki, "Is it right that my employer insists I show up at the office three days a week?" (John's photo; opinion column, UNVERIFIED)
- **Question:** a bank official whose employer expects three office days a week with attendance monitored although the job can be done from home.
- **Vicki O'Toole's answer:** no single answer suits every organisation; being physically present does not automatically mean being productive. At JJ O'Toole there was no remote working before Covid; during the pandemic everyone except the warehouse worked from home and the business continued. Her children's examples: a VP in aviation based in Dubai who worked from her home office for two summer months; a partner in a UK law firm who always works remotely; an actuary in Dublin whose firm allows part-time remote. Some roles need presence (her daughter, a nurse manager). Office presence helps with quick questions, learning from experienced colleagues and social aspects. Employers should consider the role and the person, not a blanket rule; some people take ownership and need little supervision, others need structure. "The real question isn't 'Where are you working?' but 'Are you doing your job?'"
