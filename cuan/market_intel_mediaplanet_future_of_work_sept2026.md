# "Future of Work" — Mediaplanet supplement

Source: Mediaplanet, an independent sponsored-content supplement ("who take sole responsibility for its content") distributed with an Irish print newspaper. Each article is written by or sponsored by a named company/professional body — treat as REPORTED expert commentary with a commercial interest, not independent research, except where a named survey/study is cited.

## Contents

**1. "What Ireland's most resilient organisations are doing differently" — Eimear Byrne, Managing Director, SD Worx Ireland (written by Bethany Cooper)**
Per SD Worx Research Institute: 62% of Irish organisations are exploring AI use, but only 47% report significant outcomes. Per the HR and Payroll Pulse 2026 report (SD Worx): 60% of Irish organisations consider workforce planning a critical/high priority; 78% of employees say payroll is accurate/reliable, yet 45% of organisations reported payroll-related compliance issues or audit findings in the previous year. On the EU Pay Transparency directive (due to be transposed into Irish law by June 2026, date missed): 68% of employers say they're prepared, but only 41% of employees understand what it means. One in four Irish employees report being dissatisfied at work; 43% are dissatisfied with work-life balance. Core message: "AI is not a trend that organisations can wait out... the challenge is making sure it delivers measurable value and is genuinely connected to business strategy."

**2. "HR's role in building a resilient AI-driven workforce" — Meg Dunphy, HR Policy and Engagement Manager, CIPD**
World Economic Forum estimate: 39% of workers' core skills will change by 2030. Gartner: only 1 in 50 AI initiatives delivers transformative value. Per CIPD's "HR Practices in Ireland 2026" research: AI/automation/technology are the leading drivers of organisational change for 65% of organisations, while 91% are experiencing skills shortages. Core message: "Technology alone doesn't create value; how we redesign work around it does" — HR's role is workforce planning, organisation design, skills development, leadership and performance. CIPD HR Awards applications open until 21 October.

**3. "Is your office strategy built for what happens next?" — Joe McGinley, CEO, Iconic Offices (written by Sarah Brown, sponsored content)**
Three considerations for workplace strategy: (1) plan for change, not certainty — AI is "less a story about needing fewer desks and more a story about needing greater flexibility around the decisions businesses are making"; (2) match commitment to certainty — weigh the full financial commitment (fit-out, furniture, technology, rates, maintenance) against how certain the requirement actually is; (3) create a workplace worth the commute — as AI handles more individual tasks, physical workplaces must deliver value through collaboration, mentoring, problem-solving and culture ("the office has to earn the commute").

**4. "Who is designing the future of work?" — Maria Souza, Head of Human Experience & Innovation, CPL**
Key argument: as automation decisions accumulate across an organisation one by one, they collectively reshape roles and where human judgement sits — "people can hold the same job title... and increasingly perform very different work." Employee judgement (what to hand to AI vs retain) gradually reshapes capability across a workforce; trying to control every variation would miss valuable applications emerging from experimentation, but there's a difference between creating room for experimentation and allowing work to evolve without direction. As AI agents take on longer parts of a workflow, entering the work only at exceptions risks missing the experiences that build expertise and judgement. Calls for a multidisciplinary team (technology, the work itself, the people doing it, business context) to deliberately design how work evolves, "otherwise we could make hundreds of good automation decisions and still end up with an organisation we would never have designed." Closing line: "Human judgement deserves to be designed into the future of work, rather than discovered in the work that remains."

**5. "Key learning and development trends shaping work in 2026" — Sinéad Heneghan, CEO, Learning & Development Institute (L&DI)**
Per the latest L&DI Member Survey (158 organisations): L&D is becoming increasingly central to workforce capability, leadership, transformation and future readiness. Key trends: (a) AI demands discernment — members report growing concern about ethics, risk and governance alongside AI's opportunities; L&D's task is twofold, using AI effectively while building the wider workforce's AI literacy, confidence and judgement; (b) human skills still matter — leadership/management development remains the strongest learning priority, followed by soft skills and resilience, then AI/data/digital capability — organisations are not choosing between human and technical capabilities, they need both; (c) investment brings accountability — 62.5% of respondents expect L&D spend to increase next year, yet only 43.8% measure results beyond participant reaction (87.5% measure reaction) and just 10% use a formal ROI methodology; (d) from intent to impact — the 2026 outlook includes continued resourcing pressure, financial constraints, skills gaps and change fatigue alongside accelerating tech adoption; the stated opportunity for the L&D profession is to move from strategic intent to demonstrable impact.

**6. "Investing in an inclusive, resilient workforce" — Caroline Shanahan, Executive, Technology Ireland**
Citing Ibec's Budget 2027 submission: calls for deploying the €2bn surplus in the National Training Fund (financed by a 1% levy on employer PRSI contributions) to finance supply-side, non-inflationary upskilling supporting enterprise AI/digital preparedness. Calls to expand AI skills training nationally via Skillnet Business Networks, Springboard+, micro-credentials/"Skills to Advance," an AI skills fund for foundational competencies for all graduates, and iVET (initial Vocational Education and Training) modules in secondary schools. Also calls for addressing gender imbalance in STEM, a national STEM Passport for Inclusion rollout, and P-TECH programmes to more learners. Notes Ireland's lifelong learning participation rate is slightly above the EU average (+2%) but more is needed; Ireland currently holds the Presidency of the Council of the EU.

**7. "Why AI can't replace human expertise in payroll and accounting" — Jennifer Kelly, Partner, Accounts and Business Advisory Services, Azets Ireland (written by Tony Greenway, sponsored content)**
Per Azets' "Fresh Perspectives on Payroll" report: 69% of Irish executives believe payroll errors are as damaging as a Revenue audit scandal; 37% are struggling with legislative updates. Core message: AI accelerates routine data tasks but hasn't changed the fundamental role of the accountant — qualified, experienced people are still needed to prepare accounts and advise clients; human oversight remains essential for payroll compliance (Employment Regulation Reporting, pension auto-enrolment, Gender Pay Gap reporting) given the reputational risk of payroll error. Azets does not use AI to process payroll directly, citing data sensitivity. "A hybrid approach, combining smart automation with expert and experienced oversight, will always be best for business."

**8. "The next AI challenge is redesigning work" — Allison Hodgson, Director, CIPD in Ireland (written by Elizabeth Meager)**
Core argument: simply introducing AI technology won't deliver full opportunity — organisations need to look at the entire system of work and the people within it. Cites McKinsey research: for every $1 invested in an AI technology platform, organisations should invest $3 in redesigning jobs/processes/work structures and $5 in building human capability and change readiness — "the value of AI depends on what happens around the technology." For Ireland, where 91% of organisations report difficulty filling vacancies, building a workforce that can learn and adapt alongside AI is framed as critical to competitiveness. Recommends a human-centred starting point: understanding people, their skills, and a gap analysis, before considering blended human/AI-agent teams and new questions around performance management, quality assurance and accountability.
