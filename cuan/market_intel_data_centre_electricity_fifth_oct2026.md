# AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft now ~19.7% of Ireland's electricity usage

**The headline figure:** the four hyperscalers combined now account for
approximately **19.7%** of all electricity used in Ireland. Data-centre
electricity usage overall rose **10.8% in 2025**, against roughly 2%
growth across other sectors — based on a Business Post investigation
grounded in EPA documents.

**Estimated household cost:** Friends of the Earth-sourced figure cited
in the piece puts the knock-on cost to an average household at roughly
**€40/year**, cumulatively around **€360 over nine years**.

**Grid connections under pressure:** the CRU (Commission for Regulation
of Utilities) has paused a significant number of new grid-connection
applications — cited as a concrete sign the network is already
stretched by existing hyperscaler demand, separate from any new
build.

**AWS's backup power switch:** Amazon/AWS is switching backup diesel
generators at its Irish data-centre campuses over to **HVO (hydrotreated
vegetable oil) biodiesel** — framed as a response to scrutiny over
emissions and fuel use at these sites.

**Political backlash:** Sinn Féin MEP **Lynn Boylan** has called for a
pause on further data-centre development pending clearer answers on
grid capacity and emissions impact.

**Geographic concentration critique:** the piece repeats the now-familiar
criticism that data-centre capacity is heavily concentrated around
**Dublin and Meath**, intensifying local grid strain rather than
spreading it.

**Companion piece, same by-line, confirms and sharpens the figures —
"Tech giants tried to hide data centre energy use" (Donal MacNamee,
Business Post):** Google and Microsoft **unsuccessfully tried to keep
their Irish data centres' electricity usage confidential**, via
requests to the EPA when filing industrial emissions licence
applications (which also cover permission to run backup diesel
generators on-site in grid emergencies). The EPA rejected the
confidentiality requests; Google told the EPA that releasing the
figures would "lose its market edge" and raised "commercial
sensitivity." Precise numbers from the EPA documents:
- The four companies (Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon Web Services)
  have a **combined market cap of $13 trillion**.
- Their four Irish data-centre operations accounted for **18.4% of the
  country's total electricity consumption in 2024, rising to 19.7% in
  2025** — confirming the headline figure above.
- **AWS alone used 11% of total metered electricity consumed by the
  country last year.**
- Confirms the **10.8% rise in data-centre electricity consumption
  between 2024 and 2025, against ~2% for the non-data-centre
  category** — the grid-strain gap is real and growing, not a one-off.
- A Microsoft spokeswoman said the company's data centres (operating
  in Dublin since 2009) are "operated," that information on energy
  usage is commercial, and that the public should have access to
  information "in order to be able to fully participate in the
  decision-making process relating to the issuing of an industrial
  emissions licence for their activity" — a notably open position
  relative to Google's.
