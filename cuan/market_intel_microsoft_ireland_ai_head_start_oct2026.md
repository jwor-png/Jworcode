# "Irish business has an opportunity to make the most of its AI head start" (Business Opinion, Catherine Doyle, Oct 2026)

Source: Irish newspaper Business section, opinion piece by Catherine Doyle, general manager of Microsoft Ireland. Captured by photograph from John, 26 Oct 2026 (newspaper and exact date not visible on the page; likely the 24 to 26 Oct editions). Figures are as printed and not independently verified. Only this article was reviewed; the second-hand EV article on the same page was excluded at John's request.

## What it says

- **Adoption figures (attributed to Microsoft's Global AI Diffusion Report):** Ireland is Europe's leading adopter of AI and third in the world, up from fourth three months ago. 49.9% of the working-age population now use AI, nearly three times the global rate.
- **Workers (the "2026 work trend index", a nationally representative survey of 1,000 workers in the Republic; the article does not say who ran it):** 89% say AI has improved the quality of their work; 65% believe AI skills are now essential to compete in the jobs market.
- **The gap:** only 40% say they have had enough AI training or guidance; only 44% say their employer provides the tools they need; nearly half say they would turn to free or publicly available tools if their employer did not provide a suitable alternative.
- **Her risk argument:** without trusted technology, practical training and clear guidance, employee enthusiasm "quietly turns into a security and compliance risk".
- **Organisations' questions (her words, paraphrased):** what will it cost, how do I prove a return, how do we move quickly without compromising security or trust.
- **Her anecdote:** an Irish customer began with AI for its sales team and widened to how the whole organisation works. What changed: leaders used the tools themselves rather than being briefed; a single agreed focus rather than scattered pilots; a clear view of success before starting.
- **Her three actions for leaders between now and year end:**
  1. Give people tools you trust and a clear written rule on what data can go into them.
  2. Replace generic AI awareness sessions with training built around what each team actually does.
  3. Pick one workflow, not one task, to redesign around AI, then measure what changed.

## Cuan's read (assessment, not verified fact)

- **Source bias:** a vendor executive, writing about adoption of tools her company sells. The three recommendations are sensible, but the headline numbers are Microsoft's own and the method behind "use AI" (any use, or regular use) is not given. The worker survey is a sample of 1,000 (a margin of roughly 3 points), and the article does not name its publisher. Treat the figures as indicative, not settled.
- **What is solid regardless of source:** the training-and-tools gap (40% and 44%) and "nearly half would use free tools" are the useful points, because they describe shadow AI use, which is a governance and data-leakage issue and not a technology issue.
- **Consistent with earlier logged data:** ifac's Food and Agribusiness Report (AI use 21% to 48%, 75% with no AI governance policy), Indeed's 14.9% AI job ads, and the SD Worx and Gartner "adoption without value" statistics already in `market_intel_agrifood_report_testing_network_sept2026.md`, `market_intel_ai_summit_stats_batch_sept2026.md` and `market_intel_mediaplanet_future_of_work_sept2026.md`.
