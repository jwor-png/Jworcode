# AI "resellers" / implementation partners — FT Money, 17/18 Oct 2026

**Core argument:** AI boom excitement has concentrated on hardware
providers (Nvidia, TSMC) and large language model developers (OpenAI,
Anthropic). The hyperscalers (Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon Web
Services) now need to recoup billions invested in AI infrastructure —
their priority has shifted to increasing adoption rates among public
and private sector customers, not just building capability.

**The spend forecast, the single most citable figure:** broker **Peel
Hunt believes AI will push long-term technology spending from about
10% of operational expenditure budgets to between 20 and 30%.**
Government and corporate tech spending is already rising as
organisations recognise AI's value goes beyond automating mundane
tasks.

**Why "resellers" matter, per the piece:** many organisations see the
advantages of agentic AI or moving to the cloud but are wary of
choosing the wrong solution — giving them a powerful incentive to use
implementation partners/resellers (named examples: **Bytes
Technology, Softcat**) rather than go direct to the hyperscalers.
These resellers help businesses integrate new technology, handle
licensing, and manage cybersecurity — "a bridge for the hyperscalers
that cannot easily reach these customers."

**Named proof point:** Bytes Technology helped deploy **Microsoft 365
Copilot to half a million NHS staff**, aimed at reducing
administrative time so staff spend more time on patient care.

**Durability argument:** reseller relationships with customers tend
to be long-term and secure; each new technology shift adds complexity
and more customer budget, reinforcing the model rather than
disrupting it.

**Relevance:** this is effectively independent, named-analyst
validation of the exact commercial position Meridian/Ambrion AI/
Velocity AI occupy — the "bridge" layer between frontier AI
capability and organisations wary of choosing/implementing it
themselves. The 10%→20-30% opex-share forecast is a strong, citable
macro figure for prospecting conversations and Meridian's own
positioning material; the NHS/Copilot deployment is a concrete,
large-scale proof point worth having on file as a comparable case
study (half a million users, admin-time-to-patient-care framing).
