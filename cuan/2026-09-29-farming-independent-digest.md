# Farming Independent digest, 29 September 2026

Summaries are paraphrased from the print articles. Figures are as reported in each piece and should be checked against the source before use in client work.

## 1. Farm succession: CAP retirement package

- **Author:** Catherina Cunnane
- **Context:** INHFA AGM, Sligo
- **Summary:** Farmers called for CAP to put a stronger focus on succession, including a dedicated retirement or severance package so older farmers can step back and younger farmers, roughly aged 25 to 30, can take over. Older farmers say they cannot afford to hand over the land while the state pension alone would not support them. Speakers argued the economics of transfer have shifted since earlier generations and that young people need access to land early to build a future. Calls were made to reintroduce an early retirement scheme.
- **Voices:** John Moran (Mayo cattle and sheep farmer), Anthony Walsh (Crossmolina), Jazmine Williams (Sligo).
- **Relevance:** Succession is an under-served advisory need in rural Ireland. Fits Meridian Property & Development and Finance & Restructuring.

## 2. Poultry diversification: 1,200 hens

- **Author:** Darragh McCullough (mixed farm enterprise, Meath)
- **Summary:** Converted a three-bay hay shed for about €40,000 using second-hand equipment to house 1,200 free-range hens. Sells direct to the public through vending machines at the farm shop, with wholesale customers lined up for surplus. Expects sales of around €100,000 a year, at a premium over packer prices. His view is that husbandry know-how, not the kit, separates a profitable flock from a break-even one. Also notes CCTV setup teething problems.
- **Relevance:** Diversification economics, direct-to-consumer model, small-scale enterprise viability.

## 3. Budget 2027: five farm asks

- **Author:** Eddie Punch (former ICSA general secretary, farms in Clare)
- **Summary of asks:**
  1. A plan to counter fertiliser price increases, including topping up the EU crisis reserve allocation.
  2. Further fuel supports with simpler rules; carbon tax increases seen as politically difficult given fuel prices.
  3. Capital Acquisitions Tax reliefs to be farmer-proofed, thresholds brought in line with inflation, and low thresholds for transfers outside direct family addressed.
  4. Solar panels on farm sheds funded outside TAMS, which is over-subscribed.
  5. A permanent solution to Residential Zoned Land Tax for working farmers.
- **Wider points:** Farm tax reliefs such as agricultural relief, young farmer stamp duty relief and long-term lease relief are under constant pressure from the Department of Finance. Concern that non-active farmers and investors using agricultural relief push up land prices. Final year of the current CAP regime limits room for change.
- **Relevance:** Tax and policy context for farm transfer and property advice. Update after Budget day.

## 4. Legal advice: personal guarantee over family home and farm

- **Author:** Mary Frances Fahy (solicitor and tax consultant, Fahy Neilan Solicitors, Ballaghaderreen)
- **Summary:** A reader signed a guarantee over the jointly owned family home and hobby farm for her husband's business loan, without independent legal advice. The business is now in arrears. Advice given: banks can obtain possession orders on family homes, so act early; engage MABS; use a properly regulated financial adviser; have a solicitor review the signed documents for defects; expect a long process, possibly involving sale of the debt to a fund. Options to explore include debt write-down, restructured repayments and personal insolvency arrangements.
- **Relevance:** Guarantee exposure remains a live risk for rural families and SME owners. Fits Legal & Governance and Finance & Restructuring.

## 5. Six farming lessons from Australia

- **Author:** Mike Brady (managing director, Brady Group; Chair, Nuffield International Farming Scholarships)
- **Summary:** Drawn from a visit to the Northern Territory.
  1. Resilience as a core business strategy: plan for below-average years, hold reserves, build contingency.
  2. Scale is not everything: efficiency, management skill and cost control matter more.
  3. Innovation from necessity: remote monitoring and data tools should be treated as essential, not optional.
  4. Focus on market opportunities: value creation over volume.
  5. Water as a strategic asset: storage, drainage and soil health.
  6. People as a vital resource: skilled labour, education and succession planning.
- **Relevance:** Strategic framing for agri clients. Succession theme links to article 1.
