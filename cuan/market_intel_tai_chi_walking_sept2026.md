# Market intel — "Tai chi walking: What is it and are there any benefits to it?"

**Source:** The Echo, Wednesday 30 September 2026, Camilla Foster,
interviewing Rebecca Bossick (physiotherapist, co-founder and clinical
director, One Body LDN). REPORTED — expert commentary in a consumer
press piece, not independently verified by Cuan.

## What it says
Tai chi walking is a slow, deliberate walking practice distinct from
normal walking: rather than momentum-based stepping, weight is
consciously shifted fully onto one leg (the "full" leg, the other
"empty") and balance established before the other foot moves. Once the
movement pattern is mastered, diaphragmatic ("belly") breathing is
layered in.

**Benefits cited by Bossick:**
- Accessible for all ages, children to elderly adults.
- Builds lower-body strength (legs, intrinsic foot muscles, glutes) and
  engages the core/postural spine muscles through balance and control.
- Improves balance and proprioception (body-position awareness),
  particularly useful for fall prevention.
- Stress reduction — the required focus on balance/breathing/alignment/
  weight distribution trains working memory, attention and distraction-
  filtering; its meditative quality may reduce anxiety/depressive
  symptoms by activating the parasympathetic nervous system.
- Low-impact, joint-protective compared to higher-impact exercise like
  running.
- Group sessions add a social element and guided instruction.
- **Increases confidence specifically in older populations** — Bossick
  notes people whose confidence has been knocked by a fall or
  unsteadiness often rebuild it through practising in a controlled
  environment, with knock-on improvements to function, independence and
  quality of life.

**Getting started:** recommended to begin in a group setting to watch
and observe before joining in; alternatives include finding a local tai
chi master, one-to-one sessions, or a physiotherapist who can teach the
practice.
