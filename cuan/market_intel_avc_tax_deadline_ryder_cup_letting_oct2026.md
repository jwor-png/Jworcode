## 1. "How topping up your pension now could unlock a Christmas tax refund" (Gabrielle Monaghan)

- **Deadlines (as printed):** 31 October is the annual deadline for filing tax returns; the pay-and-file deadline for those filing online is 18 November. An additional voluntary contribution (AVC) before the deadline can reduce the 2025 tax bill, with relief at the highest rate (40% for higher-rate payers).
- **How to make AVCs:** PAYE workers can top up an occupational scheme, or, if the scheme does not accept AVCs, the employer must give access to a personal retirement savings account (PRSA); you can also open your own PRSA (through an adviser, provider or insurer; watch the charges). Check with payroll whether relief is given at source or must be claimed from Revenue. Self-employed people make an AVC to a PRSA before the deadline and claim relief on Form 11.
- **Claiming relief (Mairead O'Driscoll, tax adviser; Shaun O'Connor, Insight Private Clients; Paul Murray, Quest Retirement Solutions):** PAYE workers can claim through Revenue's MyAccount tax return; the AVC certificate or receipt from the provider is needed. Murray's example: a 10,000 euro AVC costs about 6,000 euro net because 4,000 is saved in income tax.
- **How much (age-related limits, % of earnings, up to a legal cap):** under 30: 15%; 30 to 39: 20%; 40 to 49: 25%; 50 to 54: 30%; 55 to 59: 35%; 60 and over: 40%. Example: a 42-year-old earning 60,000 can get relief on 25%, up to 15,000; if 5% (3,000) already goes in through work, an extra 12,000 can be put in.
- **Cautions:** keep an emergency fund of at least six months' net pay; do not use savings needed for a house deposit; AVCs are generally locked until retirement; check where PRSA contributions are invested ("lifestyled" defaults may not suit); do not put in more than you can afford. Quoted illustration: 250 euro a month into an AVC for 25 years at 6% a year could reach about 450,000 euro (an illustration, not a forecast).

## 2. "Your questions answered" (Anne O'Doherty, Xeinadin Ireland, head of life and pensions)

- **Renting a four-bed house for the Ryder Cup (reader in Co Limerick, 65, widowed former teacher):** short-term lets of 25,000 to 30,000 euro are being advertised near Adare. The whole 25,000 would be taxable rental income. The rent-a-room exemption (16,000 euro, up from 14,000 in the budget) does not apply, because it excludes short-term guests. The article's illustration: on a 30,000 pension plus 25,000 letting, about 8,550 euro extra combined tax (income tax, USC and PRSI, 4.4% PRSI on the letting if under 66); at a 40,000 pension about 10,550; at 50,000 about 12,100. A widowed person in 2027 gets a 46,500 standard-rate band at 20%.
- **Downsizing and the family home (five siblings inherited it 10 years ago; probate value 500,000, now about 675,000):** each sibling's capital acquisitions tax (CAT) Group B threshold is 44,000 (up from 40,000 in the budget) and is a lifetime cumulative threshold since 5 December 1991; the small-gift exemption is 3,000 per donor per recipient per year. Capital gains tax is now 31% (down from 33%) with a 1,270 annual exemption. Siblings living in the home as their main residence get principal private residence relief on their one-fifth share; non-resident siblings would have a chargeable gain based on the date-of-death value (500,000) against the sale price, reducible by costs and unused losses.
