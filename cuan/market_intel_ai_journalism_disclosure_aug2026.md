# Market intel — Adrian Weckler: journalists, AI, and disclosure standards

**Source:** Sunday Independent p.12, 30 August 2026, Adrian Weckler
column. REPORTED — columnist's own account plus cited outlet policies;
not independently verified by Cuan.

## What it says
Weckler has been trialling an AI-detection tool, **Pangram**, claimed
99% effective at detecting AI-generated text (Copilot, ChatGPT, etc.).
Running it against LinkedIn/X posts and professional writers' work
flagged a surprising number of "AI-generated" snippets, articles and
columns from named professional writers and public figures.

**The Druckenmiller case:** US billionaire Stanley Druckenmiller wrote
a Wall Street Journal op-ed ("Let The Bond Market Speak") criticising
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. Social media sleuths ran the
1,367-word piece through Pangram, which concluded it was AI-generated
(Weckler's own run of it concluded "100% of this text is AI").
Druckenmiller admitted using AI when asked by a reporter: "Of course I
used AI... I write everything using AI now for the same reason I use a
calculator when I do math problems." The WSJ's editorial page editor,
Paul Gigot, defended publishing it: "AI is a fact of modern life...
people will use it to assist in their work and their writing... The
question for us is whether what we publish from contributors reflects
an author's original argument, and if the author has the standing and
credibility to make it." WSJ's position: they'd had a relationship with
Druckenmiller for years and don't doubt the op-ed reflects his genuine
opinion.

**FT/Hausmann case:** earlier in August 2026 the FT discovered an op-ed
by Harvard professor Ricardo Hausmann had been AI-condensed before
submission (Pangram found the FT's own appended disclosure paragraph
79% AI-written). The FT's editorial code explicitly prohibits AI use in
the writing process.

**Irish outlets' stated policies, as cited:**
- *Irish Times*: does not accept letters to the editor written or
  modified by AI (stated bluntly in its letters guidance). Has
  previously been the victim of a hoax AI-written op-ed.
- **Mediahuis** (publisher of the *Irish Independent*, *Sunday
  Independent* and other titles): "We do not publish AI-generated or
  AI-modified content without a 'human in the loop' at some point in
  the publishing process." Discloses AI use for summaries or altering
  reader experience (e.g. personalisation) via disclaimers/labels/
  watermarks "where appropriate." Confirmed AI is used to suggest
  headline options for sub-editors, who choose whether to use them.
  Mediahuis Ireland's former publisher previously had to apologise
  after AI made up quotes from experts to validate an argument in
  published content (embarrassing prior incident, not fully detailed).
- **RTÉ**: currently forming its own AI rules; a spokesperson said any
  AI use will align with public service values, comply with editorial
  standards, have "appropriate human oversight," be transparent, and
  never undermine audience trust.

**Weckler's own stated position:** uses AI weekly, sometimes daily, for
research — likens it to using Google, cutting through
unfriendly websites, speeding up information-gathering, sniffing out
spokesperson contact details, bouncing ideas around (which he equates
to reading widely on a subject). Draws a hard line at letting AI write
for him: "Sorry, no. As ChatGPT might put it: that's not just poor
form, it's a deal-breaker." His argument: writing requires critical
thinking, otherwise it's "just a large-language hallucination" or
equivalent to retweeting someone else's opinion. Default AI writing
produces what he calls "the equivalent of editorial spam," with
detectable "tells" that, once spotted, discount both the writer and the
publishing outlet in the reader's mind.
