# Musgrave Group PLC acquires four SuperValu stores (Nally/Nally & Casey) — Renatus, 18 Oct 2026

**Deal:** Musgrave Group PLC has agreed to acquire Express Check-Out
(Trim) Unlimited Company — Steven Nally's four-store SuperValu group:
Nally's SuperValu Celbridge, Nally & Casey's SuperValu Maynooth, Nally
& Casey's SuperValu Kilcock, Nally's SuperValu Dunboyne. Consideration
undisclosed; subject to CCPC approval. Target does not report turnover/
EBITDA.

**Musgrave context:** Irish food retail/wholesale/foodservice group,
founded 1876, family-controlled. FY Dec'25: turnover ~€5.5bn, EBITDA
~€228.9m.

**Renatus's read — relevant to UHL/AHL's own buying-group model:**
Musgrave normally runs SuperValu as an independent-operator network
(unlike company-operated Tesco/Dunnes) — their own annual report calls
the independent retail model "at the heart of our identity." This deal
and two recent comparables show Musgrave actively managing the balance
between direct ownership and independent ownership within that network:
- Bought Guinan's SuperValu (Carrick-on-Suir) intending to find a new
  independent operator for it (i.e. direct ownership as a *bridge*, not
  an end state).
- Sold Knocklyon to Pettitt's in July (moving a store *back* to
  independent ownership).
- Now acquiring the four Nally stores directly.

**Relevance:** a live, named case study of a parent group actively
tuning the direct-vs-independent-ownership mix inside a buying-group/
symbol-group structure — directly comparable to the hardware-sector
question UHL/AHL sits inside (and to the Meridian → UHL "Specialist
Affiliate Group" brief already logged in `board.md`/`ventures_dossier.md`
— the independent paint/decorating buying-group concept sent to Paul
Candon). Worth having in mind as a precedent/talking point if that
UHL conversation progresses — shows a major Irish retail group's
playbook for when to buy a member store outright vs keep it independent.
