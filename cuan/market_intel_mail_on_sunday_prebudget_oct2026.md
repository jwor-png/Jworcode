# Irish Mail on Sunday, "Countdown to Budget 2027" — 4 October 2026

Source: pre-budget coverage, two days before the actual Budget (6 Oct). Useful mainly as a **cross-check against the confirmed outcome already logged in full** (`market_intel_budget_2027_full_oct2026.md`).

## HSE €1bn overspend — the row behind the scenes (John Lee, John Drennan)

- **Health Minister Jennifer Carroll MacNeill and Public Expenditure Minister Jack Chambers "closed off" the Department of Health's 2027 budget**, but only by agreeing a **€1bn supplementary budget** will be needed to cover this year's shortfall — described by a Cabinet source as "the worst overrun in a decade."
- A senior Department of Finance official, anonymously, was considerably blunter: **"The HSE went on a hiring spree over the summer that they had no permission to do... The new regionalisation of the HSE appears to be a complete f***ing mess."**
- **Genuine, named interdepartmental conflict**: a PAC (Public Accounts Committee) hearing on the €1bn overspend was described by one member as icy — "like East and West Berlin in the Cold War." A source said "a significant power struggle is developing between the department and the HSE," with the HSE "utterly isolated."
- Context from the companion "€3bn spending timebomb" piece (27 Sept): Department of Finance told the paper HSE spending levels are "not sustainable"; HSE response said this doesn't explain the full scale of the overspend, implying the department bears some responsibility too.
- **This matches and sharpens the €750m+ HSE overspend figure already logged** in the confirmed Budget 2027 file — this source gives a more specific €1bn figure and the internal blame-game detail behind it.

## €100 arts/culture vouchers for 16-year-olds — "snubs older voters" (Kate Lynch)

- Scheme cost €7-8m: every 16-year-old gets €100 to spend on cultural activities (cinema, theatre, live music) — these 16-year-olds will be first-time voters at the next general election, prompting accusations the Coalition is "simply trying to buy the support of future first-time voters."
- **Age Action Ireland strongly critical**: Camille Loftus (Head of Advocacy and Public Affairs) — "if you were to give a voucher, why would it only be for younger people? Why not also for older people?... with a rising cost of living and an ongoing energy crisis, it's important that we direct available resources to those who can't afford the basics."
- **Political risk flagged directly**: Loftus noted older people vote at a higher rate than any other group, "by some margin," and some lifelong Fianna Fáil/Fine Gael voters are considering switching allegiance, feeling "taken for granted." **Did this scheme survive to the actual budget?** Not mentioned in the confirmed Budget 2027 coverage already logged — worth noting as an open question, possibly dropped or simply not covered in the Independent's own supplement.

## Budget 2027 winners/losers scorecard (pre-budget predictions)

- **Predicted winners:** Fine Gael/Simon Harris — framed as having "robbed the old Charlie McCreevy mantra of tax cuts and a few presents for the middle classes, lock stock and barrel."
- **Predicted losers:** Fianna Fáil (Jack Chambers seen as generating friction with big-spending departments); Sinn Féin and the Social Democrats, criticised for failing to articulate a clear alternative fiscal policy of their own.
- **Pre-budget predictions worth checking against the confirmed outcome:**
  - Expected €2,000 increase in the higher-rate tax threshold, settling around €1.2bn package cost — **confirmed outcome was a €2,500 increase to €46,500**, close to but larger than this prediction.
  - A possible second tier of means-tested Child Benefit, which the ESRI estimated could lift 40,000 children out of poverty for just under €700m — **did not happen**: confirmed Budget 2027 coverage already logged shows Child Benefit remained frozen at €140/month with no increase.
  - Social welfare increase predicted as "a miserly €7.50 or a prudent €10" — **confirmed outcome was €10**, at the higher end of this prediction.
  - A rural package tied to Agriculture Minister Martin Heydon's "increasing political clout" — **loosely consistent** with the confirmed TB compensation increase and Succession Farm Partnership tax credit doubling already logged, though this piece predicted it in vaguer terms.
  - "State-backed Savings and Investment Account (SIA)" scheme for "thrifty savers" flagged as a likely small-scale (~€50m) sweetener — **not mentioned** in the confirmed Budget 2027 coverage already logged; unclear if it featured.
