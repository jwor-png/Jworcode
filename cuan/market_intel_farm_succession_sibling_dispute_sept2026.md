# Market intel — "My brother's marriage changed everything on our family farm"

**Source:** Irish Independent, Farming p.4, Tuesday 22 September 2026,
"Legal Advice" column by Mary Frances Fahy (solicitor and tax
consultant, principal of Fahy Neilan Solicitors, Ballaghaderreen, Co.
Roscommon). REPORTED — an anonymised reader letter and a named
solicitor's professional advice; not independently verified by Cuan,
and not a real named case John is party to.

## The situation described
A reader farms a dairy enterprise in partnership with her brother; no
formal partnership agreement exists. Their elderly father has long
stated his will leaves the farm equally between the two siblings once
he dies (he himself only inherited when his own father died — he is
"quite traditional" and won't transfer the farm while alive). This
worked while both siblings pulled equal weight, but since the brother
married (his wife has no farming background), he has significantly
reduced his hours and involvement, leaving the reader doing the bulk of
the physical work while still splitting profits 50/50. She fears that
if she "walks away," her father could change his will to leave
everything to her brother, and she'd have no formal claim despite years
of work and investment.

## Fahy's advice (key legal/practical points)
- **Danger of an unwritten promise:** as things stand, the father could
  change his will "on a whim," leaving both siblings exposed; the
  reader is "lucky" relations with her brother have been fine so far,
  but that's not a legal protection.
- **Proprietary estoppel:** if the father amends his will to leave the
  farm solely to the brother, Fahy believes the reader would have
  grounds to contest it on this basis — she relied on the father's
  repeated promise and acted to her detriment (years of work,
  investment), and a court could find it unjust to deny her a share.
- **Quantum meruit** (Latin, "as much as is deserved"): an equitable
  contract-law remedy where someone did work/made investments on the
  assumption of compensation, even with no formal agreement — the
  reader would need to show good faith and that her brother benefited
  from her work, and be able to quantify the value of that work.
- **A farm partnership as a practical alternative:** Fahy suggests
  proposing to the father that he consider a formal farm partnership (a
  tax-efficient, "modern way" of running multi-family farms) rather
  than an outright lifetime transfer, addressing his reluctance to
  transfer while still respecting the succession intent — direct
  overlap with the Succession Farm Partnership tax-credit mechanism
  covered in `market_intel_farm_land_tax_succession_cap_batch.md`.
- **Practical fixes before litigation:** consider hiring help for the
  milking (tax-deductible, cost shared by both siblings) so the reader
  can reclaim family time; consider a student placement or Department
  of Social Protection internship scheme if funds are tight; propose a
  weekend/holiday milking roster with Farm Relief Services cover when
  neither sibling is available, plus a contingency fund for illness or
  emergency — framed as good business practice, not just a family fix.
- **Overall recommendation:** expensive litigation is not the solution;
  the reader should put family first, try to resolve the tension
  directly and amicably with her brother rather than assume the worst,
  while being aware of her legal position (estoppel/quantum meruit) if
  it does come to that.
