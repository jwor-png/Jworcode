## Editorial framing (Vanessa Hartley, Head of Google Ireland)

"Upskilling is the key to Ireland's AI future." Since 2015, Google
claims to have trained over 21 million Europeans in digital/AI skills
and worked with 500+ startups. Cites: Ipsos research finding 8% AI
adoption growth potential to boost Ireland GDP by up to €45bn; INCO and
Chance (Google.org-funded) research finding nearly 25% of entry-level
roles now require AI skills, and 74% of SME employers struggle to find
qualified candidates; NewFutures:AI curriculum built with INCO and

across Europe.

## Making AI Work — headline stats (all Google/third-party sourced, as cited)

- **€45bn** — GDP boost potential in Ireland from timely, widespread
  generative AI adoption over 10 years (Implement Consulting study).
- **11%** of SME leaders believe generative AI tools "will never create
  much value" for their organisations, vs just 1% of leaders in large
  businesses — the "pessimism gap."
- **74%** of SME employers find it "difficult" or "very difficult" to
  recruit candidates with AI skills (INCO report).
- **4%** — 2025 European Central Bank survey found firms making
  significant AI use are 4% more likely to take on additional staff in
  the near term (not a jobs-destruction signal, per the magazine's
  framing).

## Ireland's AI adoption — the uneven picture

**20%** of Irish enterprises used AI technologies in 2025 — Ireland sits
roughly mid-table on the chart shown (below the ~19.95% EU average line
is marked, Ireland appears essentially at the average). Leaders: Denmark
42%, Finland 37.8%, Sweden 35%, Belgium/Luxembourg/Netherlands/Austria
30-34.5%. Laggards: Romania 5.2%, Poland 8.4%, Bulgaria 8.6%.
**Company-size gap:** large enterprises 55% AI use vs medium 30% vs
small 17%. **63%** of enterprises in information/communication use AI —
the highest sector share, vs 11% in construction and transportation/
storage. **170 million** new jobs potentially created by new
technologies until 2030 (World Economic Forum whitepaper). **15%** of
adult workers in the EU received AI-related training in the previous
year (training gap). **91%** of employees with disabilities or
neurodivergent traits view AI-powered tools as helpful assistive
technology, 85% believe it could create a more inclusive workplace
(Ernst & Young survey).

**Ipsos's six human-centric adoption barriers** (their framing for what
businesses/workers must navigate): (1) Is AI relevant to me? (2) What's
the value of AI? (3) What are the risks? (4) What are the rules? (5) Can
I trust AI? (6) Do I have the capability to use AI?

## "Rethink Your Processes" — James Manyika (Google SVP, Technology &
Society) and Ben Armstrong (Executive Director, MIT Industrial
Performance Center)

Key framing points, useful for Ambrion/Meridian positioning:
- Most studies "underestimate" jobs gained vs jobs lost — jobs whose
  demand will grow because of AI, and genuinely new job types that
  "didn't exist before 1945," are routinely undercounted.
- The biggest factor is job *change*, not creation or destruction —
  ILO concluded augmentation affects more than two-thirds of all jobs.
- Manyika: educational institutions made a mistake equating computer
  science with entry-level software engineering training — coding
  became "just one piece of a much larger foundational curriculum," and
  that broader range of skills needs re-emphasis now that AI handles
  more of the mundane coding work itself.
- Armstrong on AI startups vs mature companies: startups build AI into
  their products from a greenfield position; mature/established
  organisations must integrate AI as a tool into an existing workflow —
  "re-tooling" — a materially harder problem, and the one that needs
  rethinking rather than cost-cutting.
- Manyika on what's next: the intersection of AI with quantum computing,
  life sciences, and materials science — one Google model reportedly
  identified 2.2 million new crystal/materials candidates that didn't
  exist before.
