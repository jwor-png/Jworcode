# Gary Connolly (Sunday Times Ireland, Money): "Are we in an AI bubble? The only honest answer is, we don't know" (25 Oct 2026)

Source: The Sunday Times (Irish edition), Money section, 25 October 2026, column by Gary Connolly, investment director at Davy. Captured by photograph from John, 28 Oct 2026, taken sideways with shadow; some figures partly legible and flagged. Opinion column by a wealth manager; the closing lines mention his firm's products.

## What it says

- **Bubbles are identifiable only in hindsight:** he has pushed back on the idea that bubbles are identifiable in real time; a bubble definition usually implies price detached from fundamental value, which requires agreeing a valuation model. Price-based definitions (a big run-up followed by a crash) are less contentious. Part of the problem is that bubbles are rare, which makes the question hard to answer from data.
- **The evidence (Roger Ibbotson, CFA Institute paper, US stock market data back to 1792, "232 years"):** a boom is a reliable predictor of future volatility but not of market direction. Booms are common. Booms are more likely to be followed by a further boom than by a crash. Of episodes where the market rose at least 50 per cent over three years, on 37 per cent of occasions the market gained at least another 50 per cent within five years; a full reversal happened on only 13 per cent of occasions (figures as read from the photo; partly legible). Even very large multi-year run-ups in the US equity market have been more likely followed by further gains than by full reversals.
- **Caveats he makes:** survivor bias (the US is exceptional; the US market survived wars, crises and regime changes); the small sample means the conclusion is in "anecdote" territory. The bear case is easily constructed and compelling, but the most intellectually honest conclusion on whether this is a bubble is "we don't know".
- **His view:** hype phases are different in character, and AI is different now; with global equities up about 17 per cent year to date in euro terms, you are being paid to bear the ambiguity; if investors are cautious, he says he has bonds or money-market funds on offer (a sales line, not a finding). A boom tells you more reliably that the range of possible outcomes has widened than which direction comes next.
