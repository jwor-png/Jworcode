## The ongoing issues register

*Each issue: what it is, the evidence John himself surfaced, why it persists, and what working through it looks like. Status is John's to set. Nothing here is a commitment until John and (where commercial) Shane confirm it.*

### 1. Signal fragmentation
**What it is:** John's strategic thinking is scattered across notebooks, voice notes, WhatsApps, late-night thoughts, meetings and random captures, with nothing catching, structuring, ranking or orchestrating it.
**Evidence:** a single day's notebook pages held AI architecture, client follow-ups, governance, funding, people, property and personal reminders all at once. The thread named this the core problem: "valuable strategic cognition scattered ... with nothing capturing it."
**Why it persists:** John's cognition is associative and parallel, not linear. Every linear tool he has tried fails him, so signal keeps leaking back out to paper and memory.
**Working through it:** Cuan becomes the single capture layer (photo, voice, typed). The handwritten spread stays as the analogue front end; Cuan ingests it and structures it against the taxonomy. Test: one photographed page in, a structured brief out, nothing dropped.

### 2. "I'm not the best at my own personal management"
**What it is:** John's own words. He makes notes to reach out to people and stay on top of his responsibilities, but the follow-through is inconsistent.
**Evidence:** "These are things I make notes of to reach out to people ... I'd like to be on top of my own personal management, but I'm not the best."
**Why it persists:** the load is very large and there has been no continuity system holding the open loops between one day and the next.
**Working through it:** the Memory Layer plus the Relationship Stewardship function. Open human loops re-surface gently until closed, so being "not the best" at admin stops costing John the relationships and responsibilities he actually cares about.

### 3. Losing visibility on people (relationship guilt)
**What it is:** John feels genuinely responsible when he loses sight of people who matter, and it weighs on him when he finds out late.
**Evidence:** he learned only on meeting Johnny Hall Mulcahy's wife that Johnny had recently had open-heart surgery. He was concerned and disappointed not to have known. The grief and illness follow-ups (Dave Dagan, John Hannon, Michael O'Driscoll) sit in the same layer.
**Why it persists:** no system tracks silence gaps, only tasks. People fall quiet and nothing flags it.
**Working through it:** a Relationship Stewardship function that watches for silence gaps and reconnect opportunities, handled with care and the right tone (a call, not a text, where it matters). This is the layer John most wants to never fail again.

### 4. Overload becomes avoidance
**What it is:** when the daily list grows long, John subconsciously avoids it. A sheet of 15 urgent items becomes something he turns away from.
**Evidence:** the thread named it directly: a 15-item urgent sheet becomes "executive guilt infrastructure." John does not need more guilt, he needs clarity and continuity.
**Why it persists:** his mind generates far more signal than any single day's linear execution capacity, so any honest full list is always overwhelming.
**Working through it:** the CRITICAL 3-to-5 rule. Cuan never surfaces more than three to five genuinely important actions a day, parks the rest visibly so nothing is lost, and tags by type (Human / Commercial / Governance / Personal / Strategic) rather than by escalating urgency marks.

### 5. High-velocity context switching
**What it is:** within seconds John's attention moves between AI architecture, a client follow-up, funding, a person, governance, an operational fix and a personal reminder.
**Evidence:** the single biggest characteristic the thread found across the notebook pages. It is why linear systems fail him.
**Why it persists:** it is how his mind works, not a flaw to be trained out. The closing instruction in the thread was explicit: do not tidy up John's note-taking style, build around his cognition, not against it.
**Working through it:** the Intelligence Layer does the sorting after capture, so John never has to context-switch into "organise mode." He thinks the way he thinks; Cuan does the classification.

### 6. Too much surface area for two humans
**What it is:** the commercial form of the same overload problem. The distribution infrastructure John built is excellent but cannot be run manually by two people.
**Evidence:** Derry Gray's independent read (30 June): "You built a distribution infrastructure ... but you're trying to run it manually. Two people can't execute a 91 target, 3 wave, 6 sector outreach campaign while also building products, running content, and doing everything else." Filed in full in `meridian/sales_orchestration_live.md`.
**Why it persists:** linear outreach scales with hours, and John and Shane do not have the hours.
**Working through it:** shrink the target list, 4 to 6 products on dead-simple one or two pagers, find distribution and network leverage so outreach is not linear, run focused 2-hour blocks. This feeds Build Decision 2 (narrow Diagnostic-first front door). The John/Shane four-question agenda is the next move.

### 7. Things falling through the cracks
**What it is:** the cumulative cost of issues 1 to 5. Without continuity, items that matter quietly disappear.
**Evidence:** the worked notebook decode (`captures/2026-06-30_notebook_page.md`) pulled 3 immediate human follow-ups, 2 property actions, 1 AHL governance action, 1 parked personal item and 6 contacts out of one messy page, several of which were overdue.
**Why it persists:** capture without orchestration. John writes it down, but nothing carries it forward or ranks it.
**Working through it:** this is the whole point of the engine. The measure of success is simple, nothing important falls through, and John knows it.
