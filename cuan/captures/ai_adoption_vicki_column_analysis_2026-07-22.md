# "Dear Vicki" AI-adoption column — analysis + UHL training implication

*Captured 22 July 2026, from John. Article shared by John for analysis. Columnist: Vicki (per John, background heading up the JJ O'Toole family packaging business in Limerick before selling; John rates her credibility highly). Source-tagged: article text REPORTED from the photograph.*

## The reader's question
An 18-staff logistics and services SME in Munster: hard to recruit good admin people, wages rising, banks reluctant to lend for software/tech, no time to plan or implement. "Where does a small business like ours even start with bringing AI into our systems?"

## Vicki's answer — assessment: sound and honest, adopt into the base
Strong, accurate points:
- **"AI is a tool, not a strategy. It should solve a real problem, save time, or improve how you serve customers. If it doesn't, question whether it's worth doing at all."** (The key line. Matches Shane's "discipline is the product.")
- Look at existing people, skills and processes first; efficiencies often come from improving what is already done, not new tech.
- AI as an investment in strengthening the business, not just another cost.
- Don't buy tech for the sake of it because everyone else is.
- Use your own team's natural tech interest to own small improvements; bring in an **external AI/digital specialist on a short, focused basis** (most SMEs don't need a full-time expert). NB: this describes John and Shane's exact model.
- Funding: alternative lenders, Enterprise Ireland and Local Enterprise Office supports are underused.
- "AI will undoubtedly change how we work, but it isn't a magic wand." The strength of any business is its people, supported by good processes and the right tech.

## The gap in the article (= John and Shane's opening)
The column gives philosophy but **no practical worked examples**. "Start with your biggest frustration" is true but abstract. That gap is exactly where a paid engagement begins: two or three named, worked examples with proof of implementation elsewhere. The article validates John and Shane's approach and then stops where their value starts. It is not a competitor; it is corroboration.
