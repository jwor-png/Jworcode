## THE ARGUMENT

Sleep better, eat better, move more, do yoga, do pilates, do resistance training, more protein, track your health, more water, less processed food, hot and cold therapy. **His point is that it is exhausting even reading those words**, and that it is too much to add to an already busy life, so people do not start at all. It becomes another thing on a list, and the list gets forgotten as life gets in the way.

He writes it for anyone sitting there overwhelmed, unmotivated, down on themselves and stuck, and his case is that **it does not have to be hard or dramatic and does not have to be another thing on the to-do list.** Getting healthier is done through simple changes and simple swaps, by being aware of your day and the choices inside it, and none of it involves joining anything or going anywhere.

**The line worth keeping:** choosing health over poor health, independent ageing over dependent ageing, strength over weakness. Choice.

---

## THE LISTS

**Food.** A healthier breakfast. Less coffee or tea during the day. Fill half your plate with vegetables. Chocolate maybe once a week. A large two litre bottle of water at the start of the day, finished by bedtime. No caffeine after 4pm. Eat one new vegetable each week. Cook one new meal each week.

**Movement.** Park the car further away. Stand more instead of sitting. Take the stairs rather than the lift or escalator. Do more house cleaning. Lift the groceries. Have a car free day. Meet that friend for a walk instead of cake. Next time you walk past the outdoor gym machines, use one.

**Lifestyle.** Go to bed and get up at the same time each day for a week. Write down five simple goals to achieve each week. One big food shop on Sunday to set up the week. Spring clean your room, your house, your fridge and your car. Smile more during the day and carry out a random act of kindness for someone each day. Reflect on what you have and how lucky you are, rather than yearning for other people's lifestyle. Call that friend you have not spoken to in a while.

## THE THREE THAT FIT JOHN'S EXISTING PRACTICE

Not a plan, just the overlap with what is already running.

- **Write down five simple goals each week.** This is the weekly version of the daily gratitude questions and it would sit naturally alongside them on a Monday.
- **Reflect on what you have rather than yearning for other people's lifestyle.** This is almost word for word the second gratitude question, "what is one thing that is actually going well right now".
- **Call that friend you have not spoken to in a while.** This is the first gratitude question turned into an action, "who in my life am I glad is there, and have I shown it recently".

The rest are mechanical and need no system.
