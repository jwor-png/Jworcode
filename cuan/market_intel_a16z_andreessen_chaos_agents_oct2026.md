Source: Instagram carousel from a16z, screenshotted by John 27 Oct 2026 (about three hours old at capture). A summary slide of a Marc Andreessen argument, which in turn endorses Replit's Amjad Masad. Secondary social summary; not the original conversation, and no data.

## What it says

1. **A chaotic entrance is one of the best tells:** one of the best signs that a technology will change the world is that it makes a chaotic entrance. Then comes Andy Grove's question: who will let chaos reign, and then rein in the chaos?
2. **PCs:** like ChatGPT a few years ago, people brought PCs to work because they got things done (some claimed on procurement forms they were buying a typewriter, an oscilloscope or a desk). A 1983 article warned of "shadow" data processing departments. Smart leaders saw the value and built practices that kept the productivity intact.
3. **Spreadsheets:** non-programmers could suddenly model a business; many sheets were hard to audit, full of formula errors and understood only by their author. Locked cells, version histories and audits reined them in, and they have been indispensable since.
4. **Email:** the mailroom once decided who could reach whom; email let anyone contact anyone, sparking fears about secrecy and leaks. For a minute, requiring a manager's approval to send an email seemed reasonable. Permissions and access controls let the benefits through.
5. **Agents today:** most agents run in chat windows, personal accounts and one-off scripts; in Replit's words, "Nobody decided what they could touch." Replit's idea is an "Agent Habitat" where bottom-up enthusiasm stays alive and the chaos is reined in "in just the right amount".
6. **Two lessons from past waves:** someone has to rein in the chaos and build a habitat where the behaviour can thrive; and the chaos itself signals something valuable, behaviour that creates deep alignment inside the company.
