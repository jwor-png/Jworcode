# Business Post batch 6 + Threads prompts, 11-12 Oct 2026

## 1. "Innovation minister: AI will 'hit the boardroom' of Irish firms" (Oisín Gaffey)

**James Lawless**, Research and Innovation Minister (qualified
barrister), speaking at the launch of the Business Post Legal 100.

- **Core warning, directly quotable:** AI is "coming for the middle
  and maybe even the top... it's hitting the boardroom potentially as
  much as it is the factory floor, and it is threatening to replace
  the professional services." Technology previously "hit the factory
  floor" (entry-level, manufacturing); this wave is different —
  reaching middle management and professional services directly.
- **Specific risk to the legal profession, named directly:** a danger
  that skills young lawyers/solicitors/barristers typically develop
  early in their career get undermined by AI doing entry-level work.
  **Direct quote:** "In a successful organisation you need what we
  call the grey hairs, you need the people that have been around a
  while, have made the hard calls, have learned the hard way on the
  way up... with AI, if we were to pause graduate recruitment for a
  couple of years because the computer can do that entry-level work,
  at some stage you're not going to get the pipeline of grey hairs
  coming through... there's something lost along the way."
- **A sharper point — AI creates a new burden, not just savings:**
  junior staff using AI tools without enough oversight places "a
  greater onus on people up the chain to correct the work and to do
  extra homework checking" — "rather than saving work, you're actually
  generating more work along the tree, so it's not a fantasy by any
  means." At "current stage of maturity," Lawless said the available
  AI tools were "rarely right."
- **Separate, unrelated context from the same event:** Lawless also
  addressed the outdated criminal legal aid system (solicitors
  currently on strike over a new scheme introduced in July, replacing
  per-appearance fees with a flat €520 rate regardless of court
  appearances), "pulling behind" Justice Minister Jim O'Callaghan's
  reform efforts. Also discussed rural/regional access-to-justice
  structural issues (solicitors in Skerries/Bray can't easily leave
  practice to attend Dublin's Criminal Courts of Justice for a case).

**Cross-reference:** directly relevant to Meridian's AI Strategy &
Governance domain and specifically to **Jürgen** (Meridian's legal AI)
— a sitting government minister naming the legal profession
specifically as an AI-displacement risk, with a genuinely sharp
secondary point (AI creates more senior-review burden, not less, at
current maturity) that's a useful caution for how Jürgen's own output
should be positioned — reviewed and verified, not relied on raw, which
is already Meridian's standing position ("Meridian informs, it never

## 2. "All you need to know about the new Irish investment accounts" (Kathleen Gallacher and Megan O'Brien) — Budget 2027 explainer

Directly extends yesterday's IG/deemed-disposal piece
(`market_intel_sindo_money_batch3_oct2026.md`) with the actual
confirmed mechanics, worth treating as the primary reference now.

**At a glance (confirmed facts):**
- **€50,000 tax-free threshold** — no tax paid on account value up to
  this; **1% tax rate** applies to the value of the account **over**
  €50,000 (not a tax on profit — tax is based on the account's overall
  **value**, calculated daily, using the **average of daily values**
  over the year to determine if/how much tax is due).
- **No minimum contribution**, but a **maximum annual contribution of
  €12,000** — unused yearly allowances **cannot** be carried forward
  (per industry expectation, not yet explicitly confirmed by
  government).
- **Available from July 2027** (per Harris's Budget speech), though
  the **full €12,000 allowance is expected to only be available from
  the following year** — not explicitly stated in the budget, but
  understood from industry sources.
- **Eligibility:** Irish residents over 18 holding a PPSN.
- **Eligible investments:** shares, bonds, investment funds, insurance-
  based investment products — **cash, crypto assets and derivatives
  are explicitly NOT permitted.** No holding cash on deposit in the
  account except temporarily around a purchase/sale.
- **Providers:** MiFID-authorised service providers, regulated fund
  managers and insurance companies — expected distribution channels
  include financial advisers, credit unions and An Post. Providers can
  charge fees (no government cap set — Harris says he'll rely on
  competition to bring fees down).
- **One account per person** (may change in future, possibly extending
  to children's investment accounts eventually).
- **Existing investments cannot be transferred in** — must be sold,
  tax paid, then the cash reinvested within the new account.
- **No restriction on geography of investment** — not limited to Irish
  companies, despite the policy's own stated aim of channelling
  savings into investment.
- **No holding/lock-in period**; moving between providers will be
  facilitated "where possible" on a tax-neutral basis, with the
  provider responsible for the account at year-end calculating and
  remitting tax (all tax reporting/administration/payment sits with
  the **provider**, not the individual investor directly with
  Revenue).

**Named platform positions, as of this article:**
- **AIB and Bank of Ireland** likely to offer via Goodbody and Davy
  respectively.
- **Revolut Ireland** (3m+ Irish users) committed to launching savings
  and investment accounts — Malcolm Craig (Revolut Ireland head) flags
  a real concern: the accounts "could funnel capital into major US
  tech stocks at the expense of European and Irish start-ups,"
  arguing Ireland (and Europe generally) "need to think about... where
  is this money going to."
- **Trading 212** confirmed it will offer the accounts; **Lightyear**
  also committed, pushing for children's accounts to be added.
- **IG Group** (already logged yesterday) says tax administration
  won't be a problem for it in Ireland.
- **Etoro** still assessing; **N26** (German neobank) has "no immediate
  plans to participate"; **Trade Republic does not currently offer
  either ISAs or ISKs** (implying uncertainty on this too).
- **Monzo Ireland** (Elaine Deehan) "encouraged by the direction," says
  it's engaging with government, hopeful to offer the accounts once
  final details confirmed.

yesterday's IG piece — this is now the authoritative reference for

## 3. "Anyone with eyes can see AI capex boom is a fugazi" (Alan O'Sullivan, StockTake column)

Sharp, skeptical AI-bubble column — a genuinely strong addition to the
Anthropic/AI-infrastructure-risk thread already well tracked.

- **Core argument, via a 1997 film reference (Donnie Brasco):** "fugazi"
  — fake, a scam, "I want nothing to do with it" — O'Sullivan's framing
  for the current AI capex boom's accounting.
- **"Maths" section — the P/E ratio distortion:** AI-related capex
  issuers (Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and the rest) are trading at an
  average P/E ratio spread of **115bps versus 78bps** for the broader
  US investment-grade ecosystem — bulls will counter that, unlike the
  telecoms funding boom of 1999, hyperscaler spending is backed by
  "real self-preservation" and "there is a break" — but O'Sullivan's
  own view: "there is evidence that AI agents have already left the
  sandbox and they know it," arguing genuine motivation isn't
  commercial interest but self-preservation-driven panic spending.
- **Specific debt/capex figures cited:** Goldman expects gross
  hyperscaler debt issuance to reach **$420bn this year**, rising to
  **$700bn in 2027**; this would see even less than 1999 telecoms-
  bubble-era debt-to-cashflow ratios from the hyperscalers, which are
  "already becoming ratios of 'eye-watering'" per the piece.
- **"Messaging" section — markets are self-aware, not naive:**
  O'Sullivan argues the markets understand the AI-investment narrative
  has an accounting manipulation/euphoria problem (echoing 1990s
  internet-bust-era chip, fibre, data-centre, Cisco Systems-style
  overbuilding), and that both equity bulls and credit-market bears
  are "pricing in" a more skeptical, sobering risk premium now than
  earlier in the cycle, requiring a substantial yield premium to lend
  to the hyperscalers.
- **"Morality" section — closing framing:** "the tech masters of the
  universe are scrambling to push the existential fear button, while
  mainstream media gobble up the AI boom" — a direct, sharp media-
  critique angle worth noting given its tone is unusually blunt for a
  mainstream business column.

**Cross-reference:** a strong, well-argued third independent voice
(after Darren Gillen and the Bank of England FPC piece, both already
logged) converging on the same AI-infrastructure-financing-risk
thread — this one is notably more skeptical in tone than the other
two, worth holding as the "bear case" reference specifically for
Meridian's AI Strategy & Governance macro-risk framing, given it
makes the accounting-manipulation argument more explicitly than the
other two pieces did.
