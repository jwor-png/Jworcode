## 1. Technology, p.6 (Adrian Weckler): "It's a relief to come to a place where I do not have to hide what I want to work on"
- Professor Robin Burke, new director of UCD's AI Institute (Ireland's first university-led AI institute; formerly of the University of Colorado, Boulder), came to Ireland partly because of US political interference in AI fairness research: grants cancelled or downgraded for "fairness" keywords; "the word 'fairness' is a big red flag".
- The institute, within UCD, will help non-technical parts of the college (medicine, law, agriculture, arts) integrate AI into their studies and research; conduct independent research to "critically evaluate AI systems" and contribute to safety, ethics, regulation and European AI policy; and include a novel "AI design studio" placing specialist AI engineers alongside researchers. Already working in childhood cancer, maternal health, agriculture, climate and weather forecasting and cultural research.
- Burke is sceptical of the "hype": the idea that AI will kill us all looks "very far from anything really going on right now"; he sees "a very strange kind of marketing" (our product is so bad you must fear it). On Jacob Coxon (the engineer who resigned from Anthropic, as written) he agrees that companies "aren't taking their responsibilities seriously in a lot of domains where there are real impacts". "AI harms are already here"; better safety culture would be great; he sees no AI company with the deliberate safety culture required; the frontier is making AI "operated in ways that are in fact socially responsible". He worries about over-reliance on chatbots and will not use AI summaries; a study cited showed students' exam results fell the longer they had chatbot access; students are "rebelling a bit against AI": "you can send your robot to the gym, but you don't get stronger". UCD's institute positions itself as a "responsible instigator" applying AI to existing research areas rather than chasing the bleeding edge.
- **Use for John:** a respected academic voice on evaluation, safety culture and human oversight; supports the "AI driven, human led" and governance language in Shane's ODIN paper and the need for evidence behind capability claims; UCD's AI Institute and AI design studio are a possible independent evaluator or research partner (an idea only; nobody approached). ## 2. Lifestyle, p.34 (Meadhbh McGrath, Shopaholic): "13 expert tips to find the best secondhand buys on Vinted"
- Vinted launched in Ireland in 2024. Tips: train the algorithm by liking items; mid-market brands (Hobbs, Jigsaw, Me+Em, Joseph, Sezane, Sandro); beware "unknown" brands (often fast fashion passed off as vintage; reverse image search); search by image; save searches; look for "dupes"; look for charity shops' pages ("hospice", "Oxfam", "Red Cross", "Enable Ireland"); study the fabric label; check the seller's other items (a seller whose only item is high-end, or whose other listings are Shein and Temu, is a red flag); zoom in on photos (watch for cropped images and AI-generated pictures); chat to sellers and offer 5 to 20 per cent below the listed price; opt into email notifications; save on postage (type "Ireland" in the search bar; bundle purchases; choose a pick-up point; An Post, DHL, Drop2Shop or DPD).
