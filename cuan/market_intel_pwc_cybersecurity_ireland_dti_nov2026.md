# PwC Ireland (Moira Cronin): "Cybersecurity in Ireland: Turning investment into resilience" (commercial content)

Source: Business Post, 1 Nov 2026 (the page header reads "Business Post, 1, 2026"; marked COMMERCIAL CONTENT, i.e. sponsored by PwC Ireland). Author: Moira Cronin, digital risk and resilience partner at PwC Ireland. Based on PwC's 2027 Global Digital Trust Insights survey: 3,934 business and technology leaders across 71 countries. Captured by photograph from John, 1 Nov 2026 (tilted; figures as read and flagged). Vendor survey, not independent research.

## What it says

- **The gap:** only **17%** of Irish respondents say cyber risk is integrated into their enterprise risk management process, against **41%** globally. The article's message: judge cyber investment by the resilience it creates, not the budget it receives.
- **AI security:** AI-system attacks are the leading global threat identified, cited by **50%**; cloud threats **40%**; third-party breaches **34%**.
- **Irish investment gaps:** only **34%** of Irish respondents report investment in mitigating AI-enabled attacks (46% globally); **37%** in securing AI agents (45% globally); a further **23%** are not developing formal operational continuity plans related to cybersecurity.
- **Budgets and continuity (global):** **84%** plan to increase cyber budgets in 2027, yet only **39%** have fully formalised and integrated cyber continuity plans.
- **Autonomy:** Irish organisations take a more cautious approach to autonomous AI in cyber defence: only **6%** allow AI agents to act fully autonomously (22% globally); **37%** favour a "recommend, then confirm" model with human approval before action. Cronin recommends making the decisions explicit: where agents can act independently, where approval is required, and who is accountable when something goes wrong, and testing controls before expanding deployment. Autonomy should not be treated as a maturity target in itself.
- **Quantum:** only **6%** of Irish respondents are implementing quantum-resistant security measures (21% globally); **37%** exploring options; **20%** have not begun considering the risk. Advice: identify where sensitive data relies on cryptography that must last long term.
- **Third parties (global):** **91%** of organisations report material obstacles, including enforcing service-level agreements, planning for exit and portability, and understanding vendor concentration risk; supplier oversight should be part of resilience planning.
- **Five priorities for Irish leaders:** integrate cyber risk into enterprise decisions; strengthen supplier oversight; test operational continuity (rehearse recovery); set clear boundaries for AI agents (match autonomy to risk, with testing, monitoring and accountable human oversight); begin quantum-readiness assessments.
