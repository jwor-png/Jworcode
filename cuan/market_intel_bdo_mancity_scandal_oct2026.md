# "Man City scandal shines light on BDO and audit regime" — FT Weekend

**Directly relevant to AHL and UHL** — BDO is both Manchester City's
auditor in this scandal and AHL/UHL's own auditor (already extensively
tracked in `ventures_dossier.md`: AHL's FY2025 audit, the BDO →
BDO Ireland LLP merger, the ongoing Smyth claim correspondence via
Mark Doherty/Marsh). Worth reading this as background on the firm's
current regulatory exposure, not just a sport story.

## What happened

- An **independent Premier League commission found Manchester City
  artificially inflated its financial position by more than £900m
  over nine years**, as it became the dominant force in English
  football — using "sham" commercial deals to overstate income. The
  commission's report said City "filed misstated accounts and
  concealed the true state of its finances from its auditors and
  football regulators." **City rejects the findings and has launched
  an appeal.**
- **BDO was City's auditor throughout the period in question** — the
  UK's fifth-biggest accounting firm, and a self-described football
  "market leader" claiming to work with more Premier League and
  Championship clubs on deals, audits and tax advice than any rival.
  BDO won the Man City audit in 2007 when **KPMG resigned.**
- **BDO declined to comment**, citing legal and professional
  confidentiality requirements in the auditing sector.

## The regulatory exposure specifically

- **Regulators will now consider whether BDO did enough to examine
  the deals it was shown and exercise the "professional scepticism"
  expected of it.** The Financial Reporting Council (FRC) has powers
  to investigate BDO as the firm that audited the accounts, and can
  also examine the individual accountants who prepared City's
  financial statements.
- **A genuine structural gap flagged in the piece:** even a club of
  Manchester City's size isn't classified as a "public interest
  entity" (PIE) — a designation that would subject its audits to the
  tougher oversight applied to listed companies, banks and insurers.
  Because City is not a PIE, its audit arrangements are neither
  subject to nor consistent with the FRC's stricter rules on auditor
  rotation (at least every 10 years) and rotation of key audit
  partners (every five years).
- **BDO's audit quality more broadly has come under sustained
  scrutiny** — the FRC has criticised its work for **six consecutive
  years.**
- **Named BDO partners on the City account:** Julien Rye (first BDO
  partner to sign City's accounts, 2014, a football specialist who
  went on to serve a decade on the firm's 12-member partnership
  council — now retired as BDO partner but continues to work for the
  firm "on contract as an engagement quality reviewer"); **Stuart
  Wood** took over the City audit in 2017, only months after becoming
  a partner, and signed the club's accounts as recently as 2024 — he
  is now **BDO's head of audit for the north-west of England.**
- **A named critical voice:** Liam Byrne, chair of the Business and
  Trade Select Committee, has urged the FRC to probe the affair,
  explicitly comparing it to the Carillion collapse: "How could the
  established system of audit and regulatory scrutiny fail to uncover
  alleged misstatements of such a scale over nine years?"
- **A direct quote from a Big Four audit veteran, worth holding onto:**
  "This is pretty juicy stuff... and when things get political, it's
  all fair game," referring to pressure the regulator may feel to
  take action.
- **BDO's other named football clients:** Aston Villa, Burnley, Queens
  Park Rangers, Southampton, Celtic, Brentford and Cardiff City —
  together paying BDO £428,000 in fees in their financial years ending
  2025. BDO was paid roughly **£200,000 a year** for its audit and tax
  work for City Football Group from 2014, while Manchester City alone
  paid **£830,000** during the nine years at issue.
- **Wider Big Four context, same piece:** Deloitte, EY, KPMG and PwC
  are separately preparing to urge government to revisit which
  companies fall within the tougher PIE regime, as it consults on its
  corporate reporting reform bill — arguing the City case strengthens
  the argument for bringing more large private companies within PIE
  status (though one source noted this is also commercially convenient
  for the Big Four firms, since PIE audits can be charged at a
  premium).

## Cross-reference — why this matters for AHL/UHL specifically

Not a direct allegation against BDO's work for AHL or UHL — nothing in
this piece suggests any parallel issue there. But it's directly
relevant context given:
- **BDO is actively under FRC scrutiny for audit quality, confirmed
  for six consecutive years** — useful background when assessing how
  rigorously BDO is likely applying "professional scepticism" more
  generally across its client base, including AHL/UHL.

  Manchester City, are very unlikely to be classified as public
  interest entities, meaning they sit in the same lighter-oversight
  category this scandal has exposed as a genuine regulatory weak
  point (no mandatory auditor rotation, less stringent partner
