# "My advice if you want to start a business" — Seán Barrett, ReAble Labs

Source: The Echo (Cork), Opinion column by Seán Barrett, co-founder of ReAble Labs (with William Hartley). VERIFIED as a first-person opinion piece by a named founder, not independently fact-checked beyond that.

## What it says

ReAble Labs is an early-stage MTU/Trinity student venture developing a 3D-printed prosthetic hand designed around affordability, customisation and repairability — still at an early stage, working toward first fittings, with significant engineering, clinical and regulatory work ahead.

Core advice, drawn from the founders' own experience:

- **Having an idea and having a finished product are two very different things.** "The first version is rarely the answer." Development was iterative: build something, test it, discover what doesn't work, change it, repeat.
- **With a medical device, that iterative process is especially important** — something can work technically and still not be the right solution for the person who ultimately uses it. Visiting the National Rehabilitation Hospital reinforced that some of the biggest challenges in prosthetics are not purely technical — they're about the individual wearer, fit into clinical practice, and whether someone can realistically access and maintain the device.
- **Being a student can be an advantage, not just a gap.** You're already in a learning environment, surrounded by different expertise, used to encountering what you don't understand and figuring out how it works. What matters is recognising what you don't know and finding people who do.
- **Environment and support structures matter** — Student Inc. at MTU and the Hult Prize Global Accelerator (pitched for a $1m prize, didn't win it) gave access to people who could challenge assumptions and create momentum, even without winning.
- **Success can't ultimately be measured by competitions, pitches or headlines** — it will be measured by whether the product earns trust from clinicians and makes a meaningful difference to the person using it.
- **Closing advice to another student sitting on an idea:** don't wait until you feel completely qualified to pursue it — you probably never will. That doesn't mean rushing forward without understanding risks, particularly in a regulated area like healthcare; it means accepting that expertise can be built, collaborators can be found, and ideas can improve. Start with the problem, not the company. Build something small. Put it in front of people who will tell you what's wrong with it. Find people whose knowledge complements your own. Treat something not working as information, not defeat.
