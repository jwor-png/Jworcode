Export Anatolia (legal name **Anatolya Otomotiv Traktor Yedek Parca Ithalat Ihracat Ticaret A.S.**), founded **2001** by Yuksel Kangal and headquartered in Menemen, Izmir, is a genuine, substantial manufacturer and exporter of spare parts for agricultural tractors, combine harvesters and mowing equipment, trading through two named subsidiaries: **ARIMIS Seating** and **SDM** (sheet metal components). Company-reported scale: approximately 300 employees across the group, a roughly 7,500 square metre export facility, around 500 x 40-foot containers shipped annually, 98 percent export-focused, ISO 9001:2015 certified, running SAP Business One as its ERP, with a reported 96.7 percent order fulfilment rate and under 0.003 percent returns.

That last set of facts matters more than it might look. A company already running ISO 9001:2015, SAP Business One, and tracking fulfilment and returns to three decimal places is not starting from zero on data and process discipline — it already has the operational bones that make targeted AI adoption low-risk rather than speculative. The case for AI here is not "modernise a paper-based business." It is "you already measure what matters; a small number of AI applications sit directly on top of data you already collect."

Across all seven Meridian domains, the picture is a well-run, financially advantaged exporter with real global-market credibility, sitting on a specific and manageable EU regulatory position, and with two or three genuinely low-risk AI opportunities that map directly onto its existing quality and export operations — not a wholesale AI transformation, a handful of targeted moves.

**One correction carried forward from the prior briefing:** an earlier profile described a castings and foundry business. That was wrong. This briefing, and the company's own note, confirm the real product lines: tractor, combine and mower spare parts, seating, and sheet metal.

## 2. LEGAL, TRADE AND REGULATORY INTELLIGENCE

**The Customs Union advantage is real and current.** The EU-Turkey Customs Union (Association Council Decision 1/95) covers industrial goods including tractor spare parts, seating and sheet metal — tariff-free, quota-free movement into the EU and Ireland. This is a genuine, current trade advantage over non-Customs Union competitors, and does not remove product-safety or conformity requirements, which sit separately.

**The compliance framework that actually applies:**
- **Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230** (in force January 2027) governs machinery, safety components, and parts for machinery placed on the EU market.
- **Agricultural and forestry tractors and tractor-specific components fall under the separate Tractor Type-Approval Regulation (EU) No 167/2013.**
- **Tractor seats (ARIMIS Seating) sit in a well-established, EU-harmonised type-approval regime** under Regulation 167/2013 — the area of highest genuine regulatory exposure in the group, more demanding than simple CE self-declaration.
- **Sheet metal products (SDM)** fall under the Machinery Regulation's safety-component provisions only if structural or safety-relevant; otherwise may carry no independent CE obligation.
- As a non-EU manufacturer, the EU importer/distributor carries legal responsibility for verifying conformity; an EU Authorised Representative is generally needed where CE marking applies.

**Recommended action:** map the product catalogue against Regulation 167/2013 and the Machinery Regulation with a compliance specialist, ideally jointly with the EU-side importer. This is unrelated to AI regulation and should proceed regardless of any AI decision.

**The EU AI Act — the honest, reticence-relevant answer.** With no AI system currently in use, Export Anatolia has **low to no current exposure** under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. This is the single most important fact for a reticent owner to hear: adopting AI does not automatically drag the company into a heavy compliance regime. Real exposure only arises if AI is specifically adopted for:
- **Production-line defect detection** — could meet the high-risk threshold only if the AI system functions as a safety component under machinery law; a case-by-case determination at the point of adoption, not a blanket rule.
- **Customer-facing AI** (chatbots, automated quoting) — triggers only the light-touch Article 50 transparency obligation (tell users they're talking to AI), effective August 2026.
- **Supply chain/logistics AI** — generally low-risk as currently drafted.

The Act applies by what the AI system does and touches, not by company sector — so the compliance burden is proportionate to how far Export Anatolia actually goes, not a fixed cost of entry.

**Turkish domestic law.** KVKK (Law No. 6698) applies to any AI system processing personal data. Turkey's National AI Strategy (2021–2025) is policy guidance, not binding law — there is no Turkish equivalent of the EU AI Act today.

**Proportionate governance recommendation:** no compliance programme is warranted now. A short one-to-two-page AI use register only if and when a specific system is adopted, classified against the Machinery Regulation and AI Act thresholds at that point, with KVKK/personal-data hygiene as the nearer-term real obligation for any AI tool touching personal data.

## 3. AI STRATEGY AND ADOPTION — the core of this briefing

This section is written directly for a reticent owner. It ranks real, deployed applications by genuine payback against Export Anatolia's actual operations, not generic AI enthusiasm, and is explicit about where AI is not yet worth the money or risk.

**Addressing the reticence directly — what AI is not, here:**
- It is not a wholesale replacement of the 300-person operation or the engineering judgement that built a 25-year export business.
- It is not a mandatory compliance burden — section 2 above shows the AI Act exposure is genuinely low unless Export Anatolia chooses to go into higher-risk applications, and it doesn't need to.
- It is not an all-or-nothing commitment — every application below can be piloted on one line or one process before any wider rollout, and dropped if it doesn't pay.
- It is not proprietary technology risk — the strongest-fit applications below are bolt-on, commercially available tools layered onto data Export Anatolia already collects through SAP Business One, not bespoke software builds.

**Ranked by genuine fit and payback:**

1. **AI-powered visual quality inspection (highest, most proven fit).** This is mature, commercially available technology for metal component manufacturing — catching surface defects, dimensional inconsistencies and material flaws faster and more consistently than manual inspection. Directly relevant to Export Anatolia's spare parts and sheet metal lines, and directly complementary to an already-strong 96.7 percent fulfilment / under-0.003-percent-returns record: this is a business that already competes on quality, and this is the AI application that protects and extends that exact reputation. Proven, bolt-on, not a novel build.

2. **Precision-agriculture component opportunity (highest strategic upside, medium term).** As global tractor and machinery manufacturers make precision agriculture standard rather than an add-on, component manufacturers face a real choice: stay a commodity mechanical-parts supplier, or embed sensing and connectivity into components already being manufactured (frames, hitches, drawbars, seating with integrated sensors). Named, real suppliers already active here: HBK (custom force/torque sensors for agriculture), Hexagon's precision-agriculture machine-control portfolio, Sensor Solutions Corp. This is a genuine product-development opportunity for Export Anatolia's agricultural machinery and seating lines, best approached as a partnership with an established sensor manufacturer, not in-house AI development from scratch. This is the move that changes what Export Anatolia sells, not just how it makes what it already sells.

3. **Export operations — demand forecasting and logistics/quoting.** AI-assisted demand forecasting by market (relevant given the US/UK split) and AI-assisted export logistics and quoting are well-proven, low-risk, and typically available as modules within an ERP system Export Anatolia already runs (SAP Business One) rather than requiring bespoke development. This is the lowest-friction starting point of all, because the infrastructure is already in place.

4. **Energy and sustainability, if the solar and rainwater claims are confirmed as material.** The company's own note now states it "operates renewable electricity generation for part of its facilities and incorporates rainwater harvesting" — a firmer statement than the earlier unconfirmed claim, though still company-reported rather than independently audited at installed-capacity level. If material, AI-driven solar generation forecasting paired with production scheduling (running energy-intensive processes when generation peaks) is a genuinely strong fit, and strengthens the commercial sustainability case in section 4 below.

**What Meridian does not recommend:** no closed-loop, fully autonomous production AI, no broad "AI transformation" programme, no adoption of AI for its own sake in HR, admin or other back-office functions where the payback is unproven for a business of this size. The honest advice to a reticent owner is that the value here is narrow and specific, not broad.

**A proportionate first step:** a pilot AI-assisted quality inspection system on one production line, run in parallel with existing manual inspection for a defined trial period, measured against the existing fulfilment/returns benchmarks Export Anatolia already tracks. If it doesn't measurably beat those benchmarks, it doesn't get rolled out further. That is a low-risk, evidence-based way to test AI without committing the business to anything.

## 4. FINANCE, TRADE AND CURRENCY INTELLIGENCE

**The currency dynamic works in Export Anatolia's favour, managed correctly.** The Turkish Central Bank has held its policy rate at 37 percent through mid-2026 in a deliberate disinflation strategy, with annual inflation easing to around 32 percent and the lira on a managed depreciation path. Because Export Anatolia's costs are largely lira-denominated while export revenue is earned in hard currency, sustained lira depreciation structurally compresses the cost base relative to the export price list — a genuine, structural competitiveness advantage. The risks: imported input costs re-price with every depreciation leg, and the 37 percent domestic policy rate makes lira borrowing expensive — exactly why hard-currency trade finance is the standard tool for exporters in this position.

**Turk Eximbank is the natural financing partner.** Turkey's official export credit agency delivered USD 54.3 billion in total support in 2025, with 83 percent of the roughly 18,500 exporters it actively supports being SMEs — precisely Export Anatolia's profile. Relevant products: short-term and pre-/post-shipment export credit insurance, and financing raised against insured export receivables. A documented Eximbank relationship is also a credibility signal worth citing directly to larger European buyers assessing supplier resilience.

**The sustainability data is a genuine commercial asset with European buyers.** The EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive requires large in-scope companies to report Scope 3 (supply-chain) emissions, typically 70–90 percent of a company's total footprint. Export Anatolia itself is very unlikely to be directly in scope, but its larger European buyers increasingly are, and need supplier-level sustainability data to complete their own mandatory reporting. Given the company's own note now states renewable generation and rainwater harvesting are in operation, packaging this into a simple standing supplier sustainability fact sheet (installed capacity, annual generation, percentage of plant energy self-supplied) is low-cost to produce and directly answers what CSRD-affected buyers are now asking their supply chains for. This is a case where a modest AI application (forecasting/scheduling, section 3) and a finance/commercial opportunity (this fact sheet) reinforce each other.

**Value-building recommendations.** Diversification across spare parts, seating and sheet metal is a genuine strength — different demand cycles reduce group-level revenue volatility, worth stating explicitly in any financing conversation. Converting one-off export orders into formalised, multi-year framework agreements with larger buying groups is worth materially more than equivalent spot-order revenue. A documented treasury and FX policy (hedging large hard-currency contracts, preferring hard-currency trade finance over lira borrowing) is exactly the governance detail that improves how the business reads to any outside financial partner.

**The honest funding picture.** This reads as a strong candidate for debt-led growth capital — Eximbank export-investment credit or green/energy-linked facilities — rather than an equity venture story. It is a real, cash-generative manufacturing exporter, and any capital conversation should be framed on those terms.

## 6. BUSINESS TRANSFORMATION AND GROWTH

**The real transformation question is not technology, it is sequencing and ownership.** For a reticent owner, the most common reason a pilot AI project fails is not the technology itself but unclear ownership and no defined success measure. Export Anatolia's existing operational discipline (ISO 9001:2015, SAP Business One, tracked fulfilment and returns) means the business already has the habit of measuring things — the transformation task is to extend that same discipline to one AI pilot, not to introduce a new way of working.

**Recommended sequencing, deliberately narrow:**
1. **Pick one line, one process, one AI application** — the quality-inspection pilot in section 3 is the strongest candidate because it sits directly on an existing, already-measured KPI (returns rate).
2. **Name one internal owner** for the pilot, someone who already owns quality or production data today, not a new hire and not an external consultant running the show.
3. **Set the success measure before starting**, using Export Anatolia's own existing benchmark (under 0.003 percent returns) as the bar the AI system has to clear or beat, not a vague "see how it goes."
4. **Run it in parallel with the existing manual process** for a defined trial window, so nothing is put at risk while the tool is tested.
5. **Decide to expand, adjust or drop based on the measured result**, not on enthusiasm either way.

**Change-management honesty for a reticent leader:** the biggest real risk in AI adoption for a business like this is not the technology failing, it is a rushed, broad rollout creating disruption to a well-functioning operation for a benefit that hasn't been proven yet. The sequencing above is designed specifically to avoid that risk — small, measured, reversible, owned internally.

**Growth framing.** If the precision-agriculture component opportunity (section 3, item 2) develops, that is the genuine growth-and-transformation story for Export Anatolia over the medium term — moving from commodity component supplier toward a value-added, sensor-enabled product line, in partnership with an established sensor manufacturer. That is a multi-year strategic move, not something to conflate with the near-term quality-inspection pilot.

## OVERALL ASSESSMENT

Export Anatolia is a substantial, well-run manufacturing exporter with real operational discipline already in place (ISO 9001:2015, SAP Business One, tracked quality KPIs), a structural currency advantage, a specific and manageable EU regulatory position, and a stable, established production base. None of that changes because of AI — the point of this briefing is that AI is not a precondition for any of Export Anatolia's existing strengths, and adopting it narrowly and carefully can extend two of those strengths (quality reputation, and export-market positioning) without introducing the broad risk, cost or compliance burden that reticence about AI usually assumes comes with it.

**The single most useful thing to say to Yuksel directly:** the honest, evidence-based case for AI at Export Anatolia is narrow, not broad — one quality-inspection pilot on one line, measured against numbers he already tracks, with a clear stop/go decision at the end of a defined trial. That is a very different, much smaller commitment than "adopting AI," and it is the version most likely to be worth his time.
