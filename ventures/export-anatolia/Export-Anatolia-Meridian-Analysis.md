## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Export Anatolia (legal name **Anatolya Otomotiv Traktor Yedek Parca Ithalat Ihracat Ticaret A.S.**, Menemen, Izmir, Turkey) is a genuine, substantial manufacturer and exporter of **spare parts for agricultural tractors, combine harvesters and industrial mowers**, trading through two named subsidiaries: **ARIMIS Seating** (agricultural, industrial and turf seating) and **SDM** (sheet metal products). Company-reported scale: approximately 300 employees, two manufacturing facilities, a roughly 7,500 square metre export facility, around 500 x 40-foot containers shipped annually, 98 percent export-focused, with the US and UK as primary markets.

This briefing sits Export Anatolia inside its real global context: Turkey's genuinely strong position as an agricultural machinery manufacturing base, the trade advantages of the EU-Turkey Customs Union, the specific EU regulatory regime that actually governs its products (tractor and machinery component law, not the assumption first put to us), a live and growing set of AI applications directly relevant to component manufacturing and precision agriculture, a sober read of the Turkish macroeconomic environment and how it can be turned to genuine export advantage, and an honest assessment of where real value can be built in the years ahead.

One correction made plainly at the outset: an earlier profile circulating on the company described a castings and foundry business with sustainability initiatives including solar generation and rainwater harvesting. Independent verification found no public evidence connecting Export Anatolia to castings, manhole covers or foundry products, and could not confirm the solar or rainwater claims at company level, though the broader Turkish trend toward industrial solar self-generation is real and well documented. This briefing is built on what is verified about the actual business.

## 1. COMMERCIAL AND MARKET INTELLIGENCE

**Turkey's real position in agricultural machinery.** Turkey's domestic tractor market was valued at approximately USD 1.6 billion in 2024, and Turkey imported over a billion dollars of tractors that same year (52,180 units), confirming Turkey's standing as a major market on both the import and export side simultaneously. Turkey hosts significant domestic manufacturers, including Erkunt Traktor Sanayii, TurkTraktor, Uzel and Hattat Holding, alongside assembly presence from CNH Industrial, AGCO and John Deere. Turkish-made tractors are recognised as price-competitive against European manufacture, gaining real export traction in the 50 to 90 horsepower segment through proximity, cost and after-sales network strength. Component and spare-parts manufacturers such as Export Anatolia sit inside this genuinely substantial and growing industrial base.

**Ireland and the UK as markets.** Export Anatolia's stated primary markets, the US and UK, sit alongside a broader European opportunity. Worth noting for context: Ireland's water and wastewater infrastructure investment programme (Uisce Eireann, EUR 13.6 billion 2025 to 2029) illustrates the scale of demand-side investment cycles across Irish and European infrastructure and machinery-adjacent sectors generally, a useful data point on the strength of continued European capital investment even where it does not directly touch this company's product lines.

**Turkey's global foundry and casting standing** (context, not Export Anatolia's own business): Turkey ranks among the top five global casting producers by tonnage and has overtaken France and Italy in European rankings, with roughly 69 percent of Turkish casting output value exported in 2024. This confirms Turkey's broader manufacturing depth, relevant context for the credibility of Turkish industrial exports generally, even though it is not Export Anatolia's own product line.

**Verification note:** absolute Turkish agricultural machinery export values by product category were not found in open sources in this pass; a precise figure would need direct data from TUIK (the Turkish Statistical Institute) or the Machinery Exporters' Association.

## 2. LEGAL, TRADE AND REGULATORY INTELLIGENCE

**The Customs Union advantage is real and current.** The EU-Turkey Customs Union (Association Council Decision 1/95, in force since 1996) covers industrial goods, including tractor spare parts, seating and sheet metal components. This means tariff-free, quota-free movement of these products between Turkey and the EU and Ireland, a genuine and current trade advantage over non-Customs Union competitors. The Customs Union does not, however, remove product-safety or conformity requirements, which sit in separate legislation.

**The real compliance framework, corrected.** Export Anatolia's actual product lines sit under EU machinery and tractor-component law, not construction-product casting standards:

- **The Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230**, in force from January 2027, governs machinery, interchangeable equipment, safety components, and parts and components for machinery placed on the EU market.
- Critically, **agricultural and forestry tractors, and the systems, components and parts designed specifically for them, fall instead under the separate Tractor Type-Approval Regulation (EU) No 167/2013**, except for machinery mounted on tractors, which stays under the Machinery Regulation. Which regime applies depends on the specific part, not a single blanket answer.
- **Tractor seats specifically (ARIMIS Seating) sit in a well-established, EU-harmonised regime** with dedicated legislation on driver and passenger seats for agricultural tractors, consolidated within the Regulation 167/2013 type-approval framework. This is the area of highest genuine regulatory exposure in the group, a more demanding regime than simple CE self-declaration.
- **Sheet metal products (SDM)** fall under the Machinery Regulation's safety-component provisions if they are structural or safety-relevant (guards, panels, brackets), or may carry no independent CE obligation at all if they are non-safety-relevant general parts. This depends on the specific product list.
- As a non-EU manufacturer, the EU importer or distributor carries legal responsibility for verifying conformity before goods are placed on the market, and an EU Authorised Representative is generally needed where CE marking applies.

**Recommended action:** map the actual product catalogue against Regulation 167/2013 and the Machinery Regulation with a compliance specialist, ideally jointly with the EU-side importer who carries responsibility on that end. This is a genuine, current compliance domain, separate from and unrelated to AI regulation.

**The EU AI Act — an honest assessment.** The AI Act (Regulation EU 2024/1689) regulates AI systems specifically, not businesses by sector. As things stand, with no AI system currently identified as in use, Export Anatolia has **low to no current exposure**. This should not be inflated into a broad compliance narrative. Real exposure would arise specifically if AI is adopted for:
- **Production-line defect detection**, which could meet the high-risk threshold if the AI system functions as a safety component under machinery legislation, a case-by-case determination made at the point of adoption, not a blanket rule.
- **Customer-facing AI tools** (chatbots, automated quoting), which trigger only the light-touch Article 50 transparency obligation, telling users they are interacting with an AI system, effective from August 2026.
- **Supply chain and logistics AI**, generally low-risk under the Act as currently drafted.

The Act's territorial reach means EU rules apply to any AI system Export Anatolia deploys that touches the EU market, regardless of where the company is headquartered.

**Turkish domestic law.** Turkey's binding data protection law is **KVKK, Law No. 6698**, which would apply to any AI system processing personal data (HR, customer, or camera-based systems capturing identifiable people). Turkey's National AI Strategy (2021 to 2025) is policy guidance, not binding law, there is no Turkish equivalent of the EU AI Act imposing risk-tiered obligations today.

**The proportionate governance recommendation:** no compliance programme is warranted now, since no AI system is confirmed in use. The sensible step is a short, one-to-two-page AI use register only if and when a specific system is adopted, classified at that point against the Machinery Regulation and AI Act thresholds, with KVKK/personal-data hygiene treated as the nearer-term real obligation if any AI tool touches personal data.

## 3. AI STRATEGY AND ADOPTION

Real, deployed applications directly relevant to Export Anatolia's actual manufacturing operations, agricultural machinery components, seating and sheet metal, ranked by genuine payback rather than generic AI enthusiasm.

**The precision agriculture opportunity is the standout fit.** As global tractor and machinery manufacturers adopt precision agriculture as standard rather than an add-on, component manufacturers face a real strategic choice: remain a commodity mechanical-parts supplier, or move up the value chain by embedding sensing and connectivity into components already being manufactured, frames, hitches, drawbars, seating with integrated sensors. Named, real suppliers already active in this exact space include HBK (custom force and torque sensors for agriculture), Hexagon's precision-agriculture machine-control portfolio, and Sensor Solutions Corp. This is a genuine, low-risk product-development opportunity for Export Anatolia's agricultural machinery and seating lines, best approached as a partnership with an established sensor manufacturer rather than in-house AI development from scratch.

**Manufacturing quality control.** AI-powered visual inspection is now mature and commercially available for metal component manufacturing, catching surface defects, dimensional inconsistencies and material flaws at speeds and accuracy levels manual inspection cannot match, directly relevant to Export Anatolia's spare parts and sheet metal production lines. This is a proven, bolt-on technology, not a novel build.

**The energy and sustainability angle, if the solar investment is confirmed.** If Export Anatolia does operate on-site solar generation, as the original profile suggested but which is not yet independently confirmed, AI-driven solar generation forecasting and energy-management scheduling is a genuinely strong, differentiated fit: pairing production scheduling with solar forecasting lets energy-intensive processes run when generation peaks, reducing cost and strengthening the sustainability case described below. This is worth confirming and, if real, actively developing.

**Export operations.** AI-assisted demand forecasting by market (particularly relevant given the split between US and UK primary markets) and AI-assisted export logistics and quoting are well-proven, low-risk applications, typically available as modules within existing ERP systems rather than requiring bespoke development.

**A proportionate first step:** a pilot AI-assisted quality inspection system on one production line, alongside confirming and, if real, building on the solar/energy angle, before any larger investment in sensor-enabled product lines or closed-loop process AI.

## 4. FINANCE, TRADE AND CURRENCY INTELLIGENCE

**The currency dynamic works in Export Anatolia's favour, managed correctly.** The Turkish Central Bank has held its policy rate at 37 percent through mid-2026 in a deliberate disinflation strategy, with annual inflation easing to around 32 percent and the lira on a managed depreciation path. Because Export Anatolia's costs are largely lira-denominated while its export revenue is earned in hard currency, sustained lira depreciation structurally compresses the company's cost base relative to its export price list, a genuine, structural competitiveness advantage that has kept Turkish exporters price-competitive against European manufacturers for years. The risks that sit alongside it are real too: imported input costs re-price with every depreciation leg, and the 37 percent domestic policy rate makes lira-denominated borrowing expensive, which is exactly why hard-currency trade finance is the standard tool for exporters in Export Anatolia's position.

**Turk Eximbank is the natural financing partner.** Turkey's official export credit agency delivered USD 54.3 billion in total support in 2025, with 83 percent of the roughly 18,500 exporters it actively supports being SMEs, precisely Export Anatolia's profile. Relevant products include short-term and pre- and post-shipment export credit insurance, and financing raised directly against insured export receivables, a genuine way to convert Eximbank insurance into working capital while banking the currency advantage rather than leaving it to chance. A documented Eximbank relationship is also a credibility signal worth citing directly to larger European buyers assessing supplier resilience.

**The sustainability data is a genuine commercial asset with European buyers, confirmed or not.** The EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive requires large in-scope companies to report Scope 3, supply-chain, emissions, which typically represent 70 to 90 percent of a company's total footprint. Export Anatolia itself is very unlikely to be directly in scope, but its larger European buyers increasingly are, and those buyers need supplier-level sustainability data to complete their own mandatory reporting. A supplier who can readily provide credible energy-source and emissions data reduces the buyer's compliance burden and is a genuine, quantifiable procurement advantage over a competitor who cannot. If the solar generation and rainwater harvesting claims are confirmed, packaging this into a simple standing supplier sustainability fact sheet, installed capacity, annual generation, percentage of plant energy self-supplied, is low-cost to produce and directly answers what CSRD-affected buyers are now asking their supply chains for.

**Value-building recommendations.** Diversification across spare parts, seating and sheet metal is a genuine strength, different demand cycles reduce group-level revenue volatility, and this is worth stating explicitly in any financing conversation. Converting one-off export orders into formalised, multi-year framework agreements with larger buying groups is worth materially more to any future valuer than equivalent spot-order revenue, and should be tracked as a distinct, named category. A documented treasury and FX policy, hedging large hard-currency contracts and preferring hard-currency trade finance over lira borrowing, is exactly the kind of governance detail that improves how the business reads to any outside financial partner.

**The honest funding picture.** This reads as a strong candidate for debt-led growth capital, Eximbank export-investment credit or green/energy-linked facilities, rather than an equity venture story. It is a real, cash-generative manufacturing exporter, and any capital conversation should be framed on those terms. Where capital would be best deployed: further manufacturing capacity, additional self-generation capacity if the solar investment is confirmed and expandable, and market expansion support for longer, contracted relationships with European buying groups.

## OVERALL ASSESSMENT

Export Anatolia is a substantial, genuinely well-positioned manufacturing exporter sitting inside a real growth market, agricultural machinery components and precision agriculture adjacency, with a structural currency advantage it can bank rather than leave to chance, and a European regulatory environment that, correctly read, is a source of real trade advantage (the Customs Union) balanced against a specific and manageable compliance domain (tractor and machinery component conformity, concentrated most in the seating line). The AI opportunity that fits best is not generic automation but the precision-agriculture sensor and component trend directly relevant to its own product lines. The sustainability story, if confirmed, is not a soft add-on but an increasingly real commercial differentiator with European buyers now required to ask for exactly this data.

The recommended next steps: confirm and, if real, actively develop the solar and sustainability data into a sales asset; map the product catalogue against the correct EU tractor and machinery conformity regimes, with the seating line as the priority; formalise a treasury policy that banks the currency advantage; and pilot AI-assisted quality inspection and precision-agriculture component partnerships as the two most proportionate near-term technology moves.
