## PHASE 2 — THE DECISION

### The structural tension, which is the whole finding

Studio Sixty Eight is positioned for a **business and creator buyer**: product photography, brand content, launches, masterclasses. That buyer is overwhelmingly urban, and concentrated where brands, agencies and product companies are.

It is located in a village of about two hundred people, two hours from Cork city, in an economy that is seasonal by construction and whose principal attraction closes for four months of the year.

**The proposition and the location are pointing in different directions.** That is not a criticism and it is not a problem. It is a fork, and it is discoverable from public evidence alone.

### Candidate decision one: what is Studio Sixty Eight actually for? **[PRIMARY]**

**Why it appears to exist.** Three readings of the same facts are available and they are not compatible. Either the studio is a **destination** people travel to West Cork for, which is a hospitality and experience business competing with retreat venues. Or it is a **local utility**, in which case the addressable market is structurally small. Or it is **Sharon's own working studio**, with third-party hire as secondary income from an asset she needed regardless, in which case the content-creation positioning is ambition sitting on top of an owner-occupied space.

**Evidence.** The stated uses are B2B and creator-facing. The location is rural, seasonal and small. The offer to book "a photographer or a full creative team" alongside the space points toward a packaged destination experience rather than bare hire, which is a meaningful signal.

**What is at stake.** The three readings demand different money, different marketing, different pricing and different risk. **The characteristic failure is not choosing, and funding all three at a level sufficient for none.**

**Missing.** Utilisation, hire rates, the split between own use and third-party use, the tenure and cost of the premises, and what Sharon wants the business to be.

**Sufficient for Meridian?** **Yes.** It determines every other question.

### Candidate decision two: is the photography business's customer base local or visiting?

**Why it appears to exist.** Newborn and family photography is a repeat-relationship, local business. A permanent catchment of two hundred people plus Bantry and Castletownbere cannot sustain volume alone. Yet the business is listed on the Glengarriff tourism site and works the woodland and coastline, which points at visitors.

**Evidence.** Tourism listing and location-led imagery on one side, a thirteen-year local trading history and a family-repeat product range on the other.

**At stake.** Where marketing money goes, and whether pricing is set against local expectation or holiday willingness to pay.

**Missing.** The actual customer split. Only Sharon holds it.

**Sufficient?** Real, but subordinate to one.

### Candidate decision three: one brand or two?

**Why it appears to exist.** Two websites, two identities, two social presences, one person.

**Evidence.** Both trade publicly and separately from the same village.

**At stake.** Marketing efficiency, and whether the studio can ever trade without Sharon.

**Sufficient?** **Not yet.** It cannot be answered before decision one, because what the brands should be depends on what the studio is. **Meridian declines to treat this as the primary decision even though it is the most obvious one.**

---

### THE PRIMARY DECISION

> **Over the next three years, should Studio Sixty Eight be built as a destination proposition that customers travel to West Cork for, or consolidated as the production base for Sharon O'Rourke Photography, with third-party hire treated as incidental income from an asset that would exist anyway?**

Those two produce genuinely different businesses. The first requires marketing reach far beyond West Cork, packaged pricing, accommodation partnerships and an answer to the winter. The second requires almost no additional investment and accepts a ceiling in exchange for low risk.

## PHASE 3 — ANALYSIS

**No financial figures are constructed here.** Inventing revenue for a business whose pricing is not public would be the exact failure this exercise is designed to detect.

### The economics that can be reasoned about without numbers

**A studio is a fixed-cost asset.** Rent or mortgage, rates, insurance, heat, light and the capital in the sets run whether or not anyone books. Every unbooked day is a cost with no offset. **So the only question that matters commercially is utilisation, and it is unknown.**

**The addressable market for bare studio hire is very small.** Product and brand photography buyers cluster where brands are. Asking a Cork or Dublin client to drive two hours each way needs a reason the studio has and a city studio does not. **The site's own answer is the right one: the place itself, and the option of a full creative team.** That is a destination argument, not a hire argument.

**The seasonality is the sharpest fact on the record.** Garnish Island closes for four months. The village is two hundred people. **A destination proposition must answer November to February, or it is a seven-month business carrying twelve months of cost.** That single question decides whether the destination route is viable.

**Dependence on Sharon is total, on the evidence.** One named operator across both propositions, with her name on one of them. A destination business built on her personally does not scale and cannot be sold. **A production base that carries her name is entirely coherent.** This is a further argument that decision one has to be settled before anything else.

**Opportunity cost is the real cost.** Every hour spent marketing studio hire is an hour not spent on photography sessions, which are proven, priced and thirteen years established. **The studio must clear that bar, not a bar of zero.**

## PHASE 5 — THE CALL

> ### PROCEED SUBJECT TO CONDITIONS

**The decision is real, it is material, and it is worth deliberate work.** Meridian is confident of that on public evidence alone.

**Meridian does not recommend a direction, and will not on this evidence.** Anyone who tells an owner which strategy to pursue without knowing her utilisation, her occupancy cost or what she wants is guessing in a confident voice. **The condition is the five owner facts below.** With those, this becomes answerable in days.

**One thing Meridian will say without them.** *Not choosing* is itself a choice, and it is the expensive one. A studio marketed as a content-creation destination, used mainly as a personal studio, priced for neither, will underperform both routes while carrying the cost of both.

### WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER

- **If the premises cost is negligible or shared**, the utilisation argument largely dissolves and the studio becomes a low-risk option worth holding open indefinitely.
- **If hire enquiries are already arriving unprompted**, the destination thesis is validated by the market rather than by argument, and the recommendation moves sharply toward it.
- **If the photography business is at capacity**, the studio is a distraction from a proven business and should be consolidated.
- **If Sharon's objective is a business that runs without her**, the destination route becomes necessary rather than optional, because a personally-branded photography practice cannot deliver it.

## PHASE 6 — ATTACK ON MERIDIAN'S OWN ANALYSIS

Put at full strength, without softening.

**One. The seasonality concern may be exactly backwards.** Newborn photography has no season. Babies arrive in February. Christmas mini-sessions, communions and christenings fall precisely in the months Garnish is shut. **The photography business may naturally fill the exact window the destination thesis cannot**, which would make the two activities complementary rather than competing, and would argue for holding both rather than choosing between them. **This is the strongest argument against the framing of the primary decision and Meridian regards it as genuinely capable of reversing it.**

**Two. Meridian may be projecting growth ambition onto an owner who has not asked for it.** A studio in a beautiful place, used mostly by its owner, hired out when convenient, may be exactly what Sharon designed. **In that case the "decision" is Meridian's, not hers**, and the correct advice is to leave it alone. Public evidence cannot distinguish a constrained business from a deliberately chosen one, and it is arrogant to assume the first.

**Three. The utilisation argument assumes a commercial rent that may not exist.** If the space is owned outright, part of a family holding, or on a nominal arrangement at Bamboo Park, the fixed-cost reasoning weakens dramatically and much of the analysis above goes with it.

**Four. Two hours from Cork is not far to a destination.** People drive further for a wedding venue or a retreat. Meridian has treated distance as friction when for the right proposition it is scarcity. **If the offer is a day in West Cork with a full creative team and sea views, the location is the product**, and the comparison with a city studio is simply the wrong comparison.

**Five. The evidence base is genuinely weak.** No pricing, no utilisation, no accounts, no competitor set, and the tourism figures are undated. **A reasonable person could conclude that Meridian has identified a plausible tension and dressed it as a finding**, and that on this evidence the honest answer was INSUFFICIENT BASIS rather than PROCEED SUBJECT TO CONDITIONS.

**Meridian's response to point five**, offered rather than assumed: the structural tension between a B2B content-creation proposition and a two-hundred-person seasonal village is established from the businesses' own public descriptions and from census and opening-season facts. That is enough to say a decision exists. It is not enough to say which way it should go, which is why no direction is recommended.

## PHASE 7 — TEN QUESTIONS FOR SHARON

None of these can be answered from public sources.

1. What proportion of studio days in the last twelve months were your own work, third-party hire, events, or unused?
2. What does the space cost you each month, all in, and do you own it, lease it or hold it on some other arrangement?
3. How many genuine hire enquiries have you had, where did they come from, and how many converted?
4. What split of your photography clients are local families versus visitors to West Cork?
5. Which is more profitable per day of your time: a photography session with product sales, or a studio hire day?
6. In November, December, January and February, what actually happens in that building?
7. Which of the two names do people use when they contact you, and does anyone ever come to Studio Sixty Eight without knowing it is you?
8. Is there work you turn away, and what is it?
9. In three years, do you want a business that runs without you, or one that is yours to run?
10. If you could change one thing about how the studio currently works, what would it be?

**Question nine is the one that decides this.** Everything else adjusts the answer. That one determines which answer is right.
