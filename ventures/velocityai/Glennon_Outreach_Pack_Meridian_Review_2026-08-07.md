## FINDING ONE, AND IT IS THE ONE TO ACT ON BEFORE SENDING

### Portfolio conflict: Glennon Brothers is already in the Ambrion pipeline

The Ambrion AI briefing of 5 August names **Glennon Brothers** in its live outreach for the EU AI Act compliance campaign.

This pack goes out under **Velocity**, and one of its two documents is titled **The Compliance Window**.

**The client will not distinguish between the two.** Mike Glennon receives a document about regulatory compliance from Shane McCarthy at Velocity AI. If an Ambrion approach then arrives about regulatory compliance from Shane McCarthy at Ambrion, the second one does not land as a different proposition. It lands as the same person selling compliance twice under two names, and it makes both look less substantial.

The regulations are genuinely different, EUDR and whole-life carbon on one side and the EU AI Act on the other. **That distinction is obvious inside the house and invisible outside it.**

> **Recommendation: sequence, do not stack.** Velocity goes first with this pack, because it is built and it is about the client's own industry rather than about AI regulation. Ambrion's EU AI Act approach follows only after a Velocity relationship exists, and ideally comes *through* it as a natural extension rather than as a fresh cold approach. One conversation with Shane settles it, and it is a five-minute conversation.

This is the four-brand boundary problem arriving in a real client, four days after it was flagged in the abstract. It is worth noting that it took a live outreach to make it concrete.

## FINDING TWO: THE COMPLIANCE WINDOW HAS NO BOUNDARY STATEMENT

Velocity is telling a company which regulatory obligations apply to it, on which dates, and what being ready looks like. A board-level operator may plan capital expenditure and systems work against that runway.

There is no line anywhere in the document saying it is not legal or compliance advice and that the recipient should verify against their own advisers.

**That is a genuine exposure, and it is cheap to close.** One line in the footer. It also costs nothing in credibility: Meridian's own experience is that the boundary statement *increases* trust rather than diminishing it, because it signals a house that knows where its own edges are. Velocity's site already positions on operators who build rather than consultants who advise, so the line is consistent with the brand rather than a retreat from it.

Suggested, in Velocity's voice rather than a lawyer's:

> *Prepared as industry intelligence, not as legal or compliance advice. Dates and scope should be confirmed with your own advisers before you plan against them.*

## FINDING THREE: THE SOURCE GRADING HAS ONE WEAK CALL

The cover note says *"every line in them is sourced"* and the Outlook says *"resolves to a primary or authoritative source, current"* for anything graded FIRM. Those are load-bearing claims and they are mostly earned. One is not.

**The recovery-gain figure.** *"CT and AI log reconstruction has shown recovery gains above 11 points in crosscutting and above 4 points overall"*, graded **FIRM**, sourced to **MICROTEC · JOURNAL OF WOOD SCIENCE**.

Microtec is a CT scanning vendor. That is a supplier's performance claim, and grading a supplier claim as FIRM against a stated criterion of "primary or authoritative source" is the one place the pack's own discipline slips. The Journal of Wood Science citation carries it, but the vendor is named first.

**Glennon's engineers will know Microtec.** If a technical reader spots a vendor number graded FIRM, it puts every other grade in question, and the confidence table is the pack's best feature.

> **Two fixes, either works.** Lead the citation with the journal and place Microtec second. Or re-grade to FIRM / DEVELOPING, which is how the 98 per cent grading figure is already handled, correctly.

**And the wider point.** Source *names* are given throughout, but not resolvable citations: no paper titles, dates or page references. That is fine on a one-pager and normal in this format. **But Shane should have the underlying citations to hand before he sends**, because the natural response from a serious operator is *"where did the 11 points come from?"*, and the answer needs to be a document rather than a search.

## ARGUE-THE-OTHER-SIDE

**The strongest case against sending this.**

> Two designed documents plus a personal note is a large package for a cold approach, and effort can read as need. A three-generation family business that has just bought Pontrilas is not short of people wanting to talk to it. The pack also asks Mike to accept a premise, that AI is where the next increment of advantage sits, from someone with no timber track record. And the deepest risk is quiet: if Glennon's people already have a scanning and grading roadmap with Microtec or a competitor, the Outlook tells them things they know, from someone who does not know they know them, which is worse than sending nothing.

**Where that case is right.** The absence of a named timber reference is the pack's real weakness and no amount of sourcing fixes it. **Where it is wrong:** the compliance runway is genuinely useful to an operator whether or not they have an AI roadmap, EUDR is under five months out, and giving something with no ask is the correct answer to being an unknown quantity in a sector. The gift framing is not a tactic here, it is the honest position.

**One mitigation worth taking.** If Shane can find out, before sending, whether Glennon already runs CT scanning, it changes how the Outlook should be framed: for a mill that already scans, shifts 05 and 06 are the conversation and shifts 01 and 02 are noise.
