## ONE DOCUMENT OR TWO

**Keep two. But invert which one leads, and make the compliance piece the front door.**

### Why two, and not one

**They have different shelf lives.** The Outlook is a view, and views age slowly. The Compliance Window is a dated runway, and it must be refreshed every time a date, threshold or scope call moves, which for EUDR has already happened twice. Merge them and the whole pack dates the moment the compliance page does. Kept apart, one document can be reissued without touching the other.

**They are read by different people.** The Outlook is for Mike and the board: where value moves, what to invest behind. The Compliance Window is for whoever owns certification, procurement and chain of custody. **A recipient can forward the compliance piece to a direct report without forwarding an AI proposition to them.** That forwardability is worth more than the tidiness of a single file.

**The Compliance Window can travel with no pitch attached at all.** This is the strongest structural argument and it goes well beyond Glennon. That document contains no AI selling. It is a genuinely useful map of dated obligations for any softwood operator in Ireland or the UK. **That makes it a reusable sector asset, not a one-off attachment.** Shane can send it to every timber operator on his list as a pure gift, and the Outlook becomes the bespoke second step for whoever engages. Merging the two destroys that, because the merged document is unmistakably a pitch.

**Two short pieces read as generous. One eleven-page document reads as a report** and asks for a sitting the recipient has not agreed to give.

### The one real argument for merging, and how to answer it without merging

The commercial logic genuinely chains: fibre is tightening, so value has to come from data; the data compliance demands is the same data; therefore compliance becomes a selling position rather than a cost. Split across two files, that chain breaks, and the single best line in the pack ends up orphaned on page four of the second document.

**The fix is not a merge. It is the cover note.** The note is what carries the chain and directs the reading order. Which brings us to the second problem.

### The problem nobody has named: you do not control which one he opens first

Three attachments arrive together. If Mike opens the Outlook first, his first impression is an AI vendor explaining his own industry to him. If he opens the Compliance Window first, his first impression is that somebody has just saved him an afternoon.

**Those are very different openings, and right now it is a coin toss.** The strengthened note fixes it by naming the order explicitly.

## AMENDMENTS TO THE TWO DOCUMENTS

Six changes. All small, none structural, and the first two are the ones that matter.

### 1. The boundary line — The Compliance Window, footer

Closes a real exposure. Velocity is telling a company which obligations apply and by when, and an operator may plan capital spend against it.

> *Prepared as industry intelligence, not as legal or compliance advice. Dates and scope should be confirmed with your own advisers before you plan against them.*

### 2. The Microtec grading — The Next 36 Months, proof points table

*"Scanning lifts yield on a fixed log"* is graded **FIRM** against a stated criterion of "primary or authoritative source", and cites **MICROTEC · JOURNAL OF WOOD SCIENCE**. Microtec is a CT scanning vendor. Glennon's engineers will know that.

**Either** reverse the citation order to **JOURNAL OF WOOD SCIENCE · MICROTEC**, so the authority leads and the vendor corroborates. **Or** re-grade to **FIRM / DEVELOPING**, exactly as the 98 per cent figure already is. Reversing the order is the better fix, because it keeps the FIRM grade honestly.

### 3. The runway framing — The Compliance Window, page 2 heading

Currently *"The new rules coming for timber."* Two of the four dates are already live: September 2025 and June 2026.

Stronger and more accurate: **"Two already biting, two ahead."** It also sharpens the urgency rather than softening it, because a rule already in force is harder to defer than one on the horizon.

### 4. Dates

Both documents are marked **JULY 2026** and **SOURCED THROUGHOUT · JULY 2026**. Refresh to August, or the pack reads as shelf stock the moment it arrives.

### 5. The EUDR simplification package

The Commission published a simplification package on **4 May 2026**, following the December 2025 revision. If it is not reflected, the *what ready looks like* page is the one that dates fastest, and it is the page an operator will actually work from. One check.

### 6. Citations to hand, not to hunt

Source names are given, resolvable citations are not. That is right for the format. But the natural question from a serious operator is *"where did the 11 points come from?"*, and the answer should be a document Shane already has open, not a search he does afterwards.
