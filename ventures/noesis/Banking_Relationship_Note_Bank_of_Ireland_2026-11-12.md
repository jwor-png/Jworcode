## Short answer
1. **Will the bank want the business?** Probably yes, as a relationship-managed corporate client, but **it will decide after enhanced due diligence, and it can say no, delay, or impose conditions.** A new group with no trading history, AI and IP assets, international receipts and a Saudi-linked investor through a UK vehicle is a high-scrutiny profile. 2. **Should everything sit there?** No. Keep Bank of Ireland as the **primary operating bank**, but do not let €25m to €250m of reserves sit in one bank by default. The Irish deposit guarantee covers **€100,000 per depositor per bank** (CONFIRMED (search)), so almost all of the money would be unprotected exposure to one institution.
3. **Who manages it?** A board-approved treasury policy, run by the finance function with dual approval, overseen by an independent director and an audit and risk committee. Not by one person.

## Where the accounts should sit
- **Asterial receives the investment and holds it.** Do not route investor money through Noesis. Each company has its own accounts. Moves between companies are documented (subscription, loan or services charge), not pooled informally.
- **Bank of Ireland route:** ask for **Corporate and Commercial Banking** relationship banking, not the small-business start-up route, plus **Global Markets** for foreign exchange, large-value receipts and placing surplus cash (both CONFIRMED (search) as Bank of Ireland businesses). I could not verify a named technology team. Ask for the sector coverage through the corporate new-business contact.
- **A standby.** Start onboarding with a second bank in parallel. Onboarding can slip, and the Christmas target should not rest on one bank's timetable.
- **Completion mechanics.** Large sums are often routed through a solicitor's client account on completion. Agree the funds flow with Asterial's solicitors and the investor's.
- **Investor conditions.** The term sheet may restrict use of proceeds, require a ring-fenced account, or approve the bank. Ask before choosing.

Ownership and beneficial owners of each company, directors and signatories, the business and its risks, expected account activity including the investment amount, source of funds for the investment and source of wealth of the founders, sanctions and political-exposure screening of the investor and its owners, and the funds timetable. ## The size of the sums
- **Counterparty risk.** Above €100,000 the group is an unsecured creditor of the bank. At €25m to €250m the board needs a written limit per bank and a view on credit quality. Check the bank's current published credit ratings (not verified here).
- **Spread.** Options a treasury adviser should price: several banks, short-dated government or bank paper, and money-market funds, each with its own risk. Which are allowed is for the board and for the investor's consent.
- **The range matters.** €25m and €250m are different problems. At the top end the bank's scrutiny, the investor's requirements and the Irish foreign-investment screening position (the share acquired, not the amount, drives the threshold) all change. Confirm the real figure and the tranche timetable first.
- **Currency.** The investor may pay in dollars, sterling, riyals or euros. Decide whether to hold or convert, who decides and when, through Global Markets. Hold currency accounts if the receipts and spending match.
