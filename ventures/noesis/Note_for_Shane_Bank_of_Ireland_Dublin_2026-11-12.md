# NOTE FOR SHANE: WHERE THE GROUP'S BANKING SHOULD BE HANDLED

12 November 2026. Private and confidential.

## Recommendation
**Handle the group's banking relationship through Bank of Ireland's Dublin corporate banking team, not through Limerick.** Limerick can remain a convenience for local servicing. It should not be where the relationship sits or where decisions are made.

## Why Dublin
1. **The right team is in Dublin.** The bank's Corporate and Commercial Banking business and its treasury services are run from its Dublin corporate office. Its public pages list corporate relationship teams in Dublin, Cork, Belfast and Galway. I found no corporate banking office in Limerick, only a branch and a startup space aimed at small startups.
2. **The size and profile call for corporate banking.** The group expects to receive a very large investment from an international investor through a UK vehicle, with Saudi owners behind it. That is a relationship-managed corporate client, not a branch account.
3. **The decisions are made centrally.** For a profile like this, the checks on ownership, source of funds, sanctions and political exposure, and the credit and risk sign-off, would normally sit with specialist teams at the centre. A Limerick branch could not approve them and would refer upward, which adds time we do not have.
4. **Treasury and currency sit in Dublin.** A large receipt in dollars, sterling or another currency would be handled by the bank's Global Markets team, which deals from Dublin and London. Ongoing international payments run through the bank's payments platform.
5. **The people we deal with will be in Dublin and London.** The group's solicitors, the investor's London lawyers, the insurer and the intermediary all work from there. Meetings, signings and completion will be centred on Dublin.

## What Limerick is still good for
Very little day-to-day. We will not be lodging cash or making withdrawals at a counter. Everything will be done online, 24 hours a day, through the bank's online banking and treasury connections. A local branch or the accountants' Limerick office can help with local servicing, but not with the relationship.

## How the accounts should work
- **One central operating account for each company's everyday costs:** rent, staff, light and heat, rates and similar payments, paid online.
- **Separate accounts for each company.** The company that receives the investment holds it in its own accounts. Money moves between companies only under written agreements, not by informal pooling.
- **Currency accounts** if the investor pays in a currency we will also spend in. Decide in advance whether to hold or convert, and who decides.
- **A treasury policy approved by the board before the money arrives.** It should cover why the money is held, how much sits with one bank, what it may be placed in, how long, and who reports on it. The Irish deposit guarantee covers only €100,000 per depositor per bank, so most of a large balance is unprotected exposure to the bank. Consider spreading it, or placing part in short-dated instruments, once the investor's requirements are known.
- **Dual approval on every payment.** One person prepares, a second approves, and a third releases or holds the token. No one person sets up a payee and releases the payment. Shane should not be the sole approver of payments to himself or to connected parties.
- **Fraud controls.** An approved list of payees, a call back to a number on file before any change of bank details is accepted, daily limits, and crime and cyber insurance in place before the money arrives.
- **Oversight.** A monthly report to the board, with an independent director and an audit and risk committee reviewing balances and compliance.

## One question to put to the bank before we commit
"Which team will own this relationship, and where do the credit and compliance decisions sit?" Ask the corporate New Business Team in Dublin, and ask for a relationship manager with technology sector experience, plus Global Markets for currency.

## Decision requested
Agree that the group's banking is set up with Bank of Ireland's Dublin corporate team, with a second bank approached in parallel as a standby, and that the treasury policy and payment controls above are drafted now, so the accounts are ready before Christmas.
