## 7.3 Risks to design out
1. **Deposit protection.** The Irish Deposit Guarantee Scheme covers up to **€100,000 per depositor per bank**, aggregated across accounts, and business accounts are generally within scope (CONFIRMED (search)). A balance of many millions is largely unprotected. A board-approved **treasury policy** should set how much sits with Bank of Ireland and for how long, with Global Markets advising on short-dated placement. Sources mention higher temporary protection for specific events. I did not confirm it applies to investment receipts, so do not rely on it.
2. **Payment fraud on a large receipt.** Set dual authorisation, payment limits, a named list of approved payees, and a rule that no change of payment details is accepted without a call back to a number held on file. Supply bank details to the investor only through a verified channel and confirm them by call back. This ties to the crime and cyber cover in section 6.
3. **Receiving early.** Do not accept funds before the subscription documents are signed and the allotment is authorised. If funds arrive early, hold them under a written agreement in a segregated account. The solicitors should confirm the funds flow before completion.
4. **Mandates.** Signatories and limits must match the constitution and the delegation matrix. The bank will check the signing authority.
5. **Multiple accounts.** One group-wide treasury policy and one controller (Pat Carroll as CFO) with an independent reviewer, as set out in the earlier schedule.

# 8. Staff and contractors outside Ireland

**Short advice:** do not add overseas hires before completion unless each is handled through an employer of record or a contractor agreement with a signed IP assignment and advice for that country. The risks, from law-firm and accountancy commentary returned in search (mostly 2020 to 2023, so confirm current rules for each country):

| Risk | What it means | Tag |
|:---------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------|:----|
| **Permanent establishment** | Work abroad by staff can create a taxable presence for the company in that country, depending on the person's role, authority to conclude contracts, and the duration. | CONFIRMED (search) |
| **Social security** | Social security is generally payable where the work is done. Remote working from home for a significant share of working time (25% is cited) can shift liability to the country of residence, and employer rates abroad can be much higher. Certificates of coverage can keep a posted worker on Irish PRSI in some cases. | CONFIRMED (search); the figures vary by country |
| **Company tax residence** | If key decisions move abroad, the company's Irish tax residence can be questioned. Keep the board and senior management in Ireland. | CONFIRMED (search) |
| **Payroll and reporting** | The Irish employer still has reporting duties even where the employee pays tax abroad. | CONFIRMED (search) |
| **Employer of record** | Can take on local payroll and registration, but does not remove the company's own permanent establishment and residence questions (Meridian's inference, not stated in the sources). | UNVERIFIED |
| **Right to work and employment law** | Local law applies to the person's rights, whatever the contract says. | CONFIRMED (search) |
| **IP assignment under local law** | Some countries have formal or scope rules for assignments by employees and contractors, and moral rights that cannot be waived. | UNVERIFIED |
| **Sanctions and export controls** | Do not hire or pay people in sanctioned jurisdictions. AI technology can have export-control limits. | UNVERIFIED |
| **Security** | Remote staff handling the core IP need managed devices, access controls and logging. | Practice |

**Policy to adopt now (one page):**
1. An approved-countries list, agreed with the tax adviser.
2. Employees abroad only through an employer of record, and contractors elsewhere.
3. No authority for overseas staff to conclude contracts for the company.
4. Every individual signs the IP assignment, with local counsel checking it where needed.
5. Managed devices and access rules; no personal accounts for company work.
6. Sanctions screening of every person and payee.
7. Board and management meetings held in Ireland.
