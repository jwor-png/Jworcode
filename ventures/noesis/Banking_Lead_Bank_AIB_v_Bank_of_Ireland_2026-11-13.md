# 1. The question

The group will receive an investment of between €25m and €250m from an international investor, through a probable UK vehicle owned by a Saudi family. It needs a lead bank in Ireland. Shane is sceptical of Bank of Ireland after a conversation with the owner of a large business that is moving about €25m a month from Bank of Ireland to AIB because it found AIB more efficient, more responsive and keener to win the business, and after Shane's own difficulties in Portlaoise. The proposal is to make AIB the lead bank.

# 2. How AIB would handle this, and from where

**From Dublin, for the same reasons as Bank of Ireland.** AIB's corporate banking is organised by sector, with dedicated teams (Technology, Media and Telecoms among them) and a relationship manager who owns the client across the bank. Its corporate banking and customer treasury services sit at Molesworth Street in Dublin, with offices in Cork and Galway. A bank taking €25m to €250m from a new group with an overseas investor will not treat it as a branch account. The ownership, source-of-funds, sanctions and credit decisions would be taken by specialist central teams, not at a regional branch (UNVERIFIED reasoning). AIB also runs a foreign-direct-investment business unit for international companies, which may or may not be the right door for an Irish group with a foreign investor. Ask.

**Likely shape of the relationship (UNVERIFIED):**
- A relationship manager in Corporate Banking, supported by a technology specialist.
- Customer Treasury Services for FX, forwards and international payments, including a large currency receipt.
- Central compliance for onboarding, with enhanced checks on the UK vehicle, its Saudi owners, the investment manager and the source of funds.
- Online and file-based payment channels. No counter business.

# 3. What the evidence does and does not say

1. **The anecdote is one company.** A business moving about €25m a month for service reasons tells us that one corporate customer was unhappy with one relationship. It does not tell us how either bank would treat this group. I could not verify it, and it is REPORTED only.
2. **"Hungrier" is not a service commitment.** Eagerness shows in speed, pricing and flexibility. Test those in writing.
3. **No independent corporate survey exists in what I found.** Surveys that turned up cover Northern Ireland and UK business banking. Irish small-business sentiment surveys cover lending refusals, which are not the question here.
4. **Credit strength does not separate them.** S&P raised both operating banks to A+ in November 2025. Moody's and Fitch actions differ by entity and date. Both are strong banks. The deposit guarantee gives €100,000 per depositor per bank in either (CONFIRMED (search)), so concentration risk applies equally.
5. **Regulatory history does not separate them.** AIB's 2017 AML fine is confirmed. A Bank of Ireland fine is reported by a single blog, which I could not confirm. Both banks will treat an overseas-linked AI group with caution.
6. **Ownership is no longer a point of difference.** AIB is fully private after the State's last sale in June 2025. The State's remaining major bank holding is in PTSB (CONFIRMED (search)). PTSB is much smaller and is not suited to this scale on what I found (UNVERIFIED).
7. **Both are structured for this sector.** Both have technology sector coverage, foreign-investment teams and relationship models.

# 4. Recommendation: choose on proposals, with both banks in parallel

**Make AIB the lead if it wins on the written test below, and keep Bank of Ireland as the standby.** If Bank of Ireland's written proposal is better on the same questions, reverse them. Either way:
1. **Approach both Dublin corporate teams this week** with the same information pack and the same questions. This is not a delay. Onboarding is the long pole, and two applications protect the Christmas target.
2. **Require written responses by Friday 20 November** and decide the lead by Friday 27 November.
3. **Do not move all balances to one bank by default.** Operating accounts at the lead bank, reserves under the treasury policy, with limits per bank.
4. **Use competition for terms.** Two banks asked for the same business is the best way to test who is "hungrier", and to obtain a named team, a timetable and better pricing.

## The written test (ask both banks the same ten questions)
1. Which team and named relationship manager will own the group, and where do credit and compliance decisions sit?
2. How long from complete documents to open accounts for each company, and what do you commit to in writing?
3. What do you need for the UK vehicle, its owners and the investment manager, and can that start now?
4. Can you receive a large foreign-currency payment, and what are the FX margins and cut-off times?
5. What are the interest and fee terms on large operating and reserve balances, and what short-dated placement can you offer?
6. What online payment and approval controls are available (dual approval, limits, payee lists, call-back, token holders)?
7. How do you handle payments to and from Saudi Arabia, the UK and the US, and through which correspondent banks?
8. What is your appetite for AI and IP businesses with an overseas-linked investor, and what conditions would you apply?
9. Who is the escalation contact if onboarding stalls, and what is the response time?
10. Can you give a reference from a comparable client, and tell us what you would need to see on use of proceeds?

## A simple scorecard
Score each bank 1 to 5 on: named team and escalation, committed onboarding timeline, treasury and FX terms, payments and Saudi corridor capability, sector appetite, controls and online tools, and pricing. Weight timeline and sector appetite highest, because of the Christmas objective.

# 5. Running the accounts, whichever bank leads

The 12 November note applies in full: a central operating account per company for day-to-day costs such as rent, staff, light and heat and rates; separate accounts per company; currency accounts if receipts and spending match; a board-approved treasury policy before funds arrive; dual approval on every payment; no one person sets up a payee and releases the payment; fraud controls and crime and cyber insurance bound first; and monthly oversight by an independent director and an audit and risk committee. Everything is online. A branch has no role except local servicing.

# 6. Dependencies that do not depend on the bank

- Fabrica AI cannot apply until it is incorporated.
- Each application needs a consistent ownership and beneficial-ownership picture.
- Under the Companies Act 2014 an Irish company generally needs at least one director resident in the EEA (as reported in search, UNVERIFIED).
- Most delays in bank onboarding are caused by incomplete AML documents. Bank of Ireland has said this. Prepare the full pack once and send it to both banks.
- Vendor guides put onboarding of foreign-linked structures at roughly three to eight weeks, which is a rough guide from commercial sources, not a bank commitment. Starting both applications now is the safeguard.
