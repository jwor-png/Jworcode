# 4. The design decisions that come before the documents

## 4.1 Officer, director, employee: four different things

- **Officer.** In the Companies Act 2014 an "officer" is essentially a director or the secretary (UNVERIFIED on the exact definition). A Chief Financial Officer or Head of Investments is an employee with a job title unless the board also appoints them a director.
- **Director.** Owes statutory duties to the company (s.228, UNVERIFIED on the limbs) whatever their job title. An executive director has two relationships: the office (appointment, removal, duties) and the employment (contract, pay, notice). **Use two documents and say what happens to each if the other ends.**
- **Employee.** Has the statutory employee protections in 5.2. A director can be an employee, but an unpaid or non-executive chair usually is not. Employment status turns on the facts, not the label.
- **Contractor or consultant.** Different tax and protections, and by default owns what they create (see 5.3).

**Decision for each of Shane, John, Pat Carroll, Tom and Diarmuid:** which company, which status, which documents. Section 6 gives the matrix.

## 4.2 Who employs whom

## 5.3 IP, confidentiality and restrictive covenants

- **Employee work.** The employer is the first owner of copyright in a work made by an employee in the course of employment, unless otherwise agreed (Copyright and Related Rights Act 2000 s.23). **CONFIRMED (search).** Patents and designs follow a similar employer-favouring approach. **UNVERIFIED on the Patents Act text.** But "in the course of employment" is a fact question. Work done in spare hours or outside the role can be disputed. The founders' spare-time issue discussed on 21 October is exactly this.
- **Assignments must be in writing and signed** (s.120). **CONFIRMED (search).**
- **Contractors.** I found no source in this search, but the general rule is that the author owns the work unless there is a written assignment. **UNVERIFIED.** Every contractor and freelance builder therefore needs a written assignment, and any individual who built ODIN components before an employment contract existed needs a confirmatory assignment now.
- **Trade secrets** are protected only where reasonable steps are taken (the 2016 EU directive as transposed, UNVERIFIED on the instrument). The compounding "wiring" is the core of the value, so confidentiality terms, access controls and a secrets register are part of the compliance set.
- **Moral rights.** Employment IP clauses should include a waiver or consent to the extent the law allows (UNVERIFIED).
- **Non-competes and non-solicits.** Irish courts apply a reasonableness test and do not rewrite an over-wide covenant (**CONFIRMED (search)**: Ryanair v Bellew and Creganna v Cullen are cited as authority). Draft narrow, tied to a real interest, with a defined time and area. A worldwide or year-long ban on all AI work is likely to fail and to weaken the rest.
- **Employees' own AI tools.** Staff will use AI tools. The contract and policy must say which tools are approved, what data may go in, who owns outputs and what accounts are used (see section 9).

## 5.4 Anti-corruption and sanctions

- Section 18 of the Criminal Justice (Corruption Offences) Act 2018 makes a company guilty where a director, manager, secretary, officer, employee, agent or subsidiary commits a corruption offence, with a defence that the company took reasonable steps and exercised all due diligence. Officers can be personally liable. **CONFIRMED (search)** on the structure. Penalty figures are inconsistent between sources and unchecked.
- This matters because a Saudi investor, intermediaries paid to originate, and international sales all raise the bribery risk. A written anti-bribery and gifts policy, with training and records, is the "reasonable steps" evidence.
- EU and Irish sanctions apply to dealings with listed persons. Screening of the investor and its owners is a pre-signing task. **UNVERIFIED** on the instruments.

# 7. Governance spine for every company

1. **Registers and records:** members, directors and secretary, beneficial owners, charges, interests; board and member minutes; share certificates; CRO filings. Held by the secretary and checked by someone independent.
2. **Board composition:** at least two directors where possible, one resident in the EEA, an independent director before the raise, a secretary who is not the sole director.
3. **Board charter and reserved matters:** a list for Noesis (sale or pledge of shares, guarantees, intragroup value transfers, IP licences, new companies, equity issues, key hires, borrowing above a limit) and a matching list for each subsidiary.
4. **Delegation of authority matrix and bank mandates** that match the constitution, because constitutional limits do not bind outsiders (CONFIRMED in the 20 October review, ss.38 to 40 UNVERIFIED).
5. **Conflicts register and declarations** updated at each board meeting. Written s.231 disclosure for every interested contract.
6. **Related-party policy:** all transactions with directors, shareholders and their companies at arm's length, minuted, with the interested person out of the vote.
7. **Decision speed:** written resolutions as the default, time-boxed consent for non-core items, urgent power with ratification (21 October design).
8. **Key-person continuity:** second director or alternate, power of attorney arrangements, deputy for defined decisions, and key-person insurance.
9. **Annual cycle:** board calendar, annual return, accounts, audit decision, director and policy reviews.
10. **Audit:** small companies may be exempt (UNVERIFIED), but an institutional investor will require audited accounts. Appoint an independent auditor early. Do not use the practice that keeps the books.

# 9. Data, AI, security and people

- **Data protection (GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018).** Employee notices, a record of processing, retention schedules, processor agreements with every supplier, and a data protection impact assessment for any AI system that profiles people or handles health data (SanCelvio). Employee monitoring needs a lawful basis and a policy. **UNVERIFIED on specifics.**
- **International transfers.** If data moves to or from Saudi Arabia or the US, transfer mechanisms and the recipient country's data law matter. Saudi Arabia has its own personal data law (UNVERIFIED).

# 10. International trading and the Saudi investment

## 10.1 International trading
- **Employees or contractors outside Ireland** raise local employment law, social insurance, tax residence and, in some cases, a taxable presence for the company. Use a local employer of record or advice before hiring anyone abroad. **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Customers abroad.** Contracts need governing law, limits on liability, data terms, export-control and sanctions clauses, and for AI services the AI Act position.
- **Intragroup charges** must be arm's length. VAT on cross-border services needs advice.
- **Anti-bribery and sanctions** controls (5.4) apply to every market. US and UK anti-bribery laws can reach non-US and non-UK companies (UNVERIFIED).

## 10.2 The Saudi investor
1. **Verify first, before advisers are instructed.** Entity and registration, owners and controllers, a named individual with a verifiable role, source of funds, and the instructing law firm. Sovereign and state-linked investors, and persons connected to them, may be politically exposed, which triggers enhanced checks (UNVERIFIED on the Irish rules for a company that is not itself a regulated firm, but the investor's lawyers will ask for the same).
2. **Irish foreign investment screening.** The Screening of Third Country Transactions Act 2023 has applied since **6 January 2025**. It is a mandatory and suspensory regime. Notification is needed only where all of the criteria in section 9(1) are met, including a third-country investor (outside the EU, EEA and Switzerland), consideration of at least **€2 million** (counting related transactions in the last 12 months), a change in holding across the **25% or 50%** lines or acquisition of control, and a sensitive sector. **CONFIRMED (search)** on this structure from law-firm briefings. **Not confirmed:** whether artificial intelligence or related technology is within the sensitive sectors, and the call-in powers over transactions that are not notified. **A 20% stake would be below 25%, but later rounds, side rights that confer control or effective participation, and indirect holdings can change the answer.** Get Irish FDI advice before the term sheet, not at completion.
3. **Governing law and disputes.** Use English or Irish law and arbitration in a neutral seat, not Saudi courts. Saudi Arabia is a New York Convention state, has accepted Convention membership as satisfying reciprocity, and has enforced foreign arbitral awards, though practitioners still warn of documentation and public-policy hurdles (**CONFIRMED (search)** from practitioner sources). A Saudi law firm should confirm. Check sovereign immunity and waiver clauses if the investor is a state entity (UNVERIFIED).
4. **What a Saudi investor is likely to ask for (market practice, UNVERIFIED):** board or observer seat, reserved matters, information rights, anti-dilution and pre-emption, audited accounts, sanctions and anti-bribery warranties, compliance with its own investment policy, possibly Sharia-compatible terms. Each affects the constitution, the officer agreements and the policies above.
5. **Hospitality, gifts and travel.** Follow the anti-bribery policy and record everything. Irish and other anti-corruption rules attach to benefits given to or by officials of a state-linked investor (UNVERIFIED).
6. **Confidentiality and public statements.** No announcements, no investor name in marketing, until signed and cleared.
