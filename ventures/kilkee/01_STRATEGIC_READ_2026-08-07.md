## THE CENTRAL FINDING: THREE RECORDS THAT DO NOT AGREE

This is the strongest thing in the matter and it is not an environmental-impact argument. It is a **wrong-plant** argument.

| Source | What it says | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning permission PL03.321258** | A plant based on **preliminary and primary** treatment | REPORTED, from John's assembly |
| **EPA licence record D0078-01, 2024 AER** | Construction and operation of a **secondary** wastewater treatment plant, listed as a specified improvement, **expected completion 2027** | REPORTED, needs the AER itself |
| **Directive (EU) 2024/3019, Article 7(1)** | Secondary treatment required. The 2037 carve-out reaches only coastal agglomerations of 2,000 to under 10,000 p.e. **which were applying appropriate treatment on 1 January 2025** | **VERIFIED** via EUR-Lex |

> **Uisce Éireann holds a ten-year permission for a plant that appears to fall short of the standard its own EPA record specifies, and of the standard EU law appears to require.**

**And the design population makes the Directive bite.** The 7,926 summer design figure sits squarely inside the 2,000 to 10,000 band. If p.e. tracks it, Kilkee is precisely the cohort Article 7(1) addresses. **And because Kilkee was applying no treatment on 1 January 2025, the condition for the derogation is unmet on the face of the text.** No 2037 runway. Secondary treatment on the ordinary timetable.

*Design population is not the same measure as p.e. and the licence figure must be retrieved. But it is close enough that the argument should be built now and confirmed rather than the reverse.*

### Why this is the argument that answers Humphreys J

The judgment turned on proportionality: eighteen years of raw sewage, urgent need, quashing disproportionate.

**That reasoning holds only if the permitted plant solves the problem.** If the permitted plant is a primary plant that must itself be superseded to meet a secondary obligation, then quashing is not disproportionate. It is the cheaper outcome. **The State would be building the wrong asset now and the right one later, at public expense, on a site the community says is the wrong one.**

And the delay argument reverses cleanly, as set out in the engagement note: **the State's own eighteen years of not treating is precisely the fact that removes its access to the longer runway.** It cannot be both the emergency that justifies haste and costless in law.

## THE SECOND FINDING: TWO EXPERT BODIES SAID NO

- **Clare County Council refused**, with four stated reasons.
- **The Commission's own inspector recommended refusal.**
- **The Commission granted anyway**, on 11 June 2025, for ten years.

**The Direction is the single most important document in the entire file**, because it must disclose why the decision-makers departed from their own inspector. Two independent expert assessments went one way and the decision went the other.

That is a strong process narrative in public, and it is a legal question in private. Whether the reasons given in the Direction are adequate and rational is exactly the kind of ground that survives to a Supreme Court leave application better than a merits disagreement does.

**Retrieve in this order: Direction, Amended Direction, Inspector's Report, Council refusal with its four reasons.** Everything else waits.

## THE THIRD FINDING, AND THE ROUTE NOBODY IS TALKING ABOUT

**No EIAR was submitted.** A Natura Impact Statement only.

Whether EIA screening was correctly carried out for a coastal wastewater plant adjacent to a cliff walk is a classic ground. It may already have been argued and lost, which the **Statement of Grounds** will show. **If it was not argued, that is significant.** If it was argued and lost, it is likely spent.

**And the route that does not depend on the courts at all: the EPA licence.**

If the permitted plant is primary treatment and the licence record specifies secondary by 2027, **the licence must be reviewed, amended or replaced before that plant can lawfully discharge.** A licence review is an EPA process with public participation rights attached.

> **That is a live, non-litigation route, it is open to Ger and Yuksel as objectors rather than affected owners, and it does not depend on the leapfrog application succeeding.** It is also the forum where the Directive argument is most naturally heard, because it is a discharge-standard question rather than a planning-merits question.

**This should be the priority route.** The courts have been tried once and the appeal is a long shot by design. The licence is where the wrong-plant argument actually lands.

## ARGUE-THE-OTHER-SIDE

> Primary treatment now is better than no treatment for another decade, and every month of argument is raw sewage into the sea. Uisce Éireann will say the scheme is phased and that secondary capability follows. The AER's 2027 line may describe an intention that the permission is a first stage of, not a contradiction of it. A court has already weighed the environmental case. And a campaign that succeeds in stopping a primary plant, and delivers nothing in its place for five years, will be judged harshly by the same town it claims to speak for.

**Where that is right:** phasing is the obvious answer and it must be tested against the actual application documents before the wrong-plant argument is asserted anywhere. **If the permission genuinely provides for a secondary phase, the central finding weakens sharply.**

**Where it is wrong:** if there is no secondary phase in the permission, then a ten-year permission for a plant that cannot meet the 2037 standard is not a first step, it is a dead end with planning consent. And the residents' position is strengthened, not weakened, by being visibly **for** treatment.

## THE SIX QUESTIONS, REORDERED BY WHAT ACTUALLY DECIDES THINGS

Jürgen's six are right. This is the order they should be worked in.

1. **Does the permission provide for secondary treatment at any stage?** Everything turns on this. If yes, the central finding weakens. If no, it is the case.
2. **Why did the Commission depart from its inspector?** The Direction. The strongest legal ground and the strongest public narrative.
3. **How does the primary-treatment permission reconcile with the EPA record specifying secondary by 2027, and with Article 7(1)?** The wrong-plant argument, assembled properly.
4. **Is the 7,926 summer design population defensible, and what is the licensed p.e.?** It determines whether the Directive cohort applies at all.
5. **Were inland alternatives genuinely and comparably assessed?** The siting argument, which is what the town actually cares about.
6. **What grounds did the High Court reject, and is there an EU-law route the domestic case did not run?** The Statement of Grounds answers both.
