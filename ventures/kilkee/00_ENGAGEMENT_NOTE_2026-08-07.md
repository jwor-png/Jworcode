## THE INSTRUCTION, AND ONE THING THAT MUST BE SETTLED ABOUT IT

The instruction is to work **in favour of the local residents**. That is accepted, and Meridian will do it.

But it needs one distinction drawn at the outset, because the residents' interest depends on it.

> **Meridian can be retained in the residents' interest. It cannot produce advocacy dressed as verified analysis.**

Those are not the same thing, and the difference decides whether the output survives a CPO hearing or is dismissed at it. A document that reads as a campaign position will be attacked as a campaign position, and the strongest material in it will be discounted along with the weakest. A document that reads as verified analysis which *happens* to support the residents is far harder to dismiss, because every assertion resolves to a primary source and the other side has to argue with the source rather than with the author.

**This is not a caveat. It is the residents' best tactical asset.** They are opposing a State utility with counsel, engineers and a judgment already in hand. Their only realistic advantage is being provably right about something specific. The floor is what delivers that.

So the working rule for this matter: **partisan in purpose, neutral in method.** Meridian finds what genuinely helps the residents, and states plainly where a resident argument does not hold, because an argument that does not hold will be found by the other side anyway and it costs more when they find it.

## VERIFIED SO FAR

**The Directive point, and it is the strongest thing on the table. VERIFIED.**

Article 7(1) of Directive (EU) 2024/3019 disapplies the secondary treatment obligation for coastal agglomerations of 2,000 p.e. and above but below 10,000 p.e. **only where they "apply appropriate treatment in accordance with Article 7 of Directive 91/271/EEC on 1 January 2025."**

If Kilkee was applying no treatment on that date, the condition is unmet on the face of the text and the derogation does not reach it. This is textual, not inferential.

**Refinement on the date, going further than Jürgen's note.** The instrument visibly carries both a 31 December and a 30 December 2037 formulation for the same cohort. **Where two provisions of one instrument give different dates for the same class, no paraphrase is safe.** Quote the operative words verbatim with the article reference attached. Lighten the caveat on *whether the derogation reaches Kilkee*. Do not lighten it on *the end date*.

**The proceedings. VERIFIED from contemporaneous reporting, not yet from the judgment itself.**

Humphreys J dismissed the judicial review of An Coimisiún Pleanála's grant to Uisce Éireann (ACP 321258) for a plant at Dunlicky Road, Victoria Park. Grounds argued included failure to properly assess environmental impacts including on the Kilkee Cliff Walk. He found that, given the urgent need to end discharge of untreated sewage and the public interest in the project, **quashing would be disproportionate**. Campaigners have applied to the High Court for leave to appeal directly to the Supreme Court. That is a **leapfrog application under Article 34.5.4° of the Constitution and it is a high bar**, and it should be described as what it is rather than as an appeal in train.

## THE STRATEGIC REFRAME, AND IT IS THE REASON TO TAKE THIS ON

Jürgen is right that the delay argument has already been used against the residents, and that a briefing leading with twenty years of inaction hands the other side a judgment that rejected that framing.

**But the two arguments connect, and connected they run the other way.**

Humphreys J's reasoning rests on the fact that Kilkee has been discharging untreated sewage for eighteen years. Article 7(1) of the new Directive withholds the 2037 transitional relief from precisely those agglomerations that were **not** applying appropriate treatment on 1 January 2025.

> **The State's own failure to treat is what disqualifies it from the transitional regime.** The delay cannot simultaneously be the reason the scheme must proceed urgently and be costless in law. It is the very fact that removes the longer runway.

**This is offered as an argument to test, not as a finding**, because it depends on facts Meridian does not yet hold: whether Uisce Éireann is in fact relying on the 2037 date anywhere in its case, its scheme design or its funding profile. If it is, the residents have a genuine inconsistency to run. If it is not, the reframe still neutralises the delay point at a CPO hearing, which on its own is worth having.

**What to retrieve to test it:** any Uisce Éireann or EPA document that assumes or asserts a 2037 compliance horizon for Kilkee.

## ARGUE-THE-OTHER-SIDE

Run now rather than at the end, because it shapes what is worth building.

> A court has already weighed the residents' environmental case and found against it, expressly on proportionality. A leapfrog application is a long shot by design. The plant is needed, the discharge is real, and every month of delay is raw sewage into the sea, which is a fact no amount of Directive analysis makes go away. Opposing the scheme reads as opposing treatment. And the Directive point, even if entirely correct, argues that Uisce Éireann must do **more** and **sooner**, which is not obviously an argument against building a plant.

**That last sentence is the one that matters, and it should shape the whole engagement.** The Directive argument is not naturally an anti-plant argument. It is a *this plant, wrongly scoped or wrongly sited, on the wrong assumption* argument. The Irish Times framing already reported is instructive: the town is described as fighting for a plant that does not irreparably damage it, not against treatment. **If the residents' case is positioned as anti-treatment it loses on the facts. If it is positioned as pro-treatment and anti-this-siting, the Directive point becomes ammunition rather than a contradiction.**
