## FUNDING BY STAGE

### Stage 0 — Bridge (now): £50k
Covers the MVP build only (per the Velocity AI proposal, £42k–£50k). Already covered in the funding structure brief. No change here.

### Stage 1 — Seed: ESTIMATE £350k–£600k, targeted month 4–5
**Funds:** Manchester and Ireland launched in parallel, London brought fully live, early wider-UK groundwork. Roughly 12–18 months of runway for a small team (a handful of operators, a support/trust function, light marketing spend per market, continued platform development).
**Why parallel markets push this above a typical single-market seed:** launching Manchester and Ireland together roughly doubles the local go-to-market cost (separate marketing, separate barber/shop recruitment, Ireland adding a second regulatory/currency environment) compared with proving one cluster first. This is the main reason the range is wider and higher than a single-city seed would need to be.
**Gate before this round opens fully:** Phase One's proof metrics from the MVP build — real bookings, repeat-booking rate, trust record — should exist before pitching this round. Investors price evidence; this is the round where London's own proof does the talking.

### Stage 2 — Series A: ESTIMATE £2m–£4m, targeted roughly month 14–20 (once UK-wide + Ireland is proven)
**Funds:** Full wider-UK consolidation, plus the US market entry, run in parallel with early Europe. US entry alone is the dominant cost here — a US legal entity, US payments/compliance work, a local operating team, and materially higher marketing CAC in a bigger, more competitive market than the UK. Parallel Europe entry (even one or two markets) adds meaningfully to this, since each new country typically means new payments rails, new language/localisation, and new local trust-building from zero.
**This is the round where institutional capital becomes close to unavoidable** — the scale of spend needed for a US entry is well beyond what founder-led or angel-style capital can comfortably fund, which is the point flagged in the earlier brief about "steering clear of VCs" becoming harder to sustain from here.

### Stage 3 — Series B: ESTIMATE £8m–£15m+, targeted roughly month 26–36 (once US + Europe show traction)
**Funds:** Japan entry (a distinct, higher-cost market — different language, different payments and business-registration norms, and typically requires a dedicated local team or partner rather than a remote-run operation) plus genuine global scale-out, and this is also the natural point to bring in the adjacent-trades cross-sell (hairdressing, hospitality, bar & floor, events) named in the original proposal, since the platform architecture is designed to extend rather than rebuild.
**Range is wide deliberately** — Series B sizing depends entirely on how much traction Stage 2 actually produces, and putting a tighter number on it now would be false precision.

## THE CAUTION WORTH RAISING TODAY

Three simultaneous market launches at Stage 1 (Manchester, Ireland, London effectively together) is a materially riskier and more expensive path than proving one market first and funding the next from evidence. If cash discipline matters more than speed right now — which it likely does, given the whole financing conversation started from a £50k bridge — worth at least naming the option of gating Manchester/Ireland to start a few weeks after London rather than fully in parallel, without abandoning the ambition. This is a sequencing choice, not a scope cut.

## WHAT ED PUTS IN GOING FORWARD

**He may not need to "put in more" at all — the note already gives him growing equity as the company grows, via the cap and discount.** The real question is whether he wants a **pro-rata right** — the option, not the obligation, to invest further at the seed round to maintain (not just passively hold) his converted percentage as new seed investors dilute the round.

**Indicative sizing if he wants to defend roughly an 8–10% stake at seed** (the midpoint range from the earlier valuation table): as a rough planning approximation, maintaining X% of a round raising £Y typically costs roughly X% × £Y as a follow-on check. On a £350k–£600k seed round, that's approximately:
- To defend ~8%: roughly £28k–£48k additional
- To defend ~10%: roughly £35k–£60k additional

**This is a genuinely rough approximation, not a cap-table calculation** — the real number depends on the exact seed round size, the note's actual conversion price, and how much of the round is new investor money versus his own conversion. It's useful only as a planning-level sense of scale for today's conversation, not a number to quote back to Ed as precise.

**Recommended framing to Ed today:** don't ask him for a specific bigger number now. Offer him a pro-rata right written into the note terms, so he has the option to keep pace with the company's growth at each future round on his own terms, rather than being asked to commit fresh capital before the seed round's actual size and pricing are known.
