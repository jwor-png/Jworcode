## EQUITY STRUCTURE — TWO SCENARIOS FOR ED'S £50K

**All equity currently modelled as held 100% by Johnny.** Ed's position is shown against that base; Shane and John's position is addressed separately below, deliberately excluded from this table for now.

**Scenario A — Equity, no further contribution from Ed.** Ed's £50k converts via the capped, discounted note at the seed round (Stage 2) into roughly **9–10% of Barber Republic**, fixed at that point, on the assumption he puts in nothing further. Johnny holds the remaining **~90–91%** at that moment — before Shane/John's sweat equity is carved out (below) and before any further raise. Every subsequent round (Series 1, 2, 3) then dilutes both Johnny and Ed proportionally, since Ed is not topping up: if Series 1 raises £3m against, illustratively, a £12m post-money valuation, that round alone dilutes existing holders by roughly 25%, taking Ed from ~9–10% to roughly **~7%**, and so on through Series 2 and 3. By Series 3, without any further contribution, Ed's stake could reasonably land somewhere in the **3–5% range** — still meaningful in absolute value if Barber Republic scales as planned, but a materially smaller percentage than his starting conversion. This should be shown to Ed plainly: equity without follow-on money shrinks in percentage terms at every future round, even though its cash value can still grow if the company's value grows faster than the dilution.

**Scenario B — Loan, not convertible, repaid on a timeline with a strong coupon.** Ed's £50k is a straight loan: fixed repayment timeline, fixed coupon (interest rate), no equity at all, no conversion right. Johnny retains 100% of the company throughout (before Shane/John's sweat equity carve-out). The trade-off is the reverse of Scenario A: Ed carries no upside if Barber Republic succeeds beyond expectations, but he also carries materially less risk — he is owed a fixed sum on a fixed date regardless of how the trial, launch or later rounds perform, and he sits ahead of equity holders (including Johnny) if anything goes wrong. This is the cleaner, simpler instrument if Ed's real preference turns out to be certainty over upside — worth asking him directly which he actually wants, since the earlier call suggested he wants equity and passive participation, but a coupon-based loan is worth having costed and ready as the alternative he may land on once his own lawyers weigh in on Monday.

If Ed's £50k funds only the MVP build (Stage 0), Barber Republic will need fresh money to fund the Trial/Pre-Seed phase above (Stage 1) — a second, separate funding conversation, potentially with Ed again, or with a new third party such as Johnny's contact Dave. The same two structures apply and should be compared on the same terms as above: **equity** (a fresh priced or capped-note round, diluting Johnny — and, once agreed, Shane/John's sweat equity — further, but bringing in a partner sharing the risk of the trial phase which is pure cost with no revenue) versus **loan** (fixed repayment and coupon, no dilution, but a real repayment obligation sitting on the business before any revenue exists, which is a genuine risk if the trial takes longer or costs more than planned). Whether this is the same investor as Stage 0 or a new one changes the negotiating dynamic but not the structural choice — recommend deciding the instrument type before deciding who provides it.
