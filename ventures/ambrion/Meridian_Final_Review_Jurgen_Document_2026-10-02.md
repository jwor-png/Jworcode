## 1. CRITICAL — SECTION 3.1 IS WRONG, AND SECTION 6 TELLS JOHN TO LEAD WITH IT

**The charge:** *"The deck repeatedly gives 9 December 2026 as the operative date for the recast Product Liability Directive... This is the most serious factual error in the deck."*

**The deck does not say that.** Every PLD reference in all 43 slides is to the **transposition deadline**, which is correct:

| Slide | Wording |
|---|---|
| 15 | "Member State transposition **due** 9 Dec 2026. Ireland – when?" |
| 18 | "Member States **must have transposed** the recast Product Liability Directive" |
| 21 | "Product Liability Directive **due** 9 Dec 2026 — around three months away at date of this conference" |
| 33 | "Ireland **must transpose** the new legal requirement by 9 Dec 2026" |

**The Directive genuinely carries two different December dates and that is why this is easy to get wrong.** Article 22 requires Member States to bring the transposing measures into force by **9 December 2026**. Article 2(1), as corrected by the May 2026 corrigendum, applies the regime to products placed on the market or put into service after **8 December 2026**. Transposition on the ninth, application from the eighth.

**Pat states only the transposition deadline. He never states an application date. He therefore cannot have got it wrong, and the corrigendum does not touch anything he says.**

**Why this is critical rather than pedantic.** Section 6 instructs John to open the conversation with this as one of three verification failures. Pat would check it inside five minutes, find he is right, and read every one of the remaining twenty findings through that failure. **It would discredit a document that is otherwise good.**

**What to do.** Delete 2.3 entirely. Rewrite 3.1 keeping only its second paragraph, which is a fair and useful refinement rather than a correction: slide 32's line "From Dec 2026, the Product Liability Directive treats software, including AI systems, as products" could leave an audience with large installed bases believing legacy equipment is caught. **Reframe it as "one sentence worth adding to V6" rather than "a material misstatement".**

## 5. SECTION 3.2 — ONE SENTENCE TO SOFTEN

*"Irish law does not distinguish between executive, non-executive, nominee, shadow or de facto directors when applying these duties."*

Jürgen has correctly flagged this as secondary-sourced only, which is good discipline. **It is also slightly overstated.** Section 228(1)(g) measures conduct against a reasonable person having the knowledge and experience *"that may reasonably be expected of a person in the same position as the director"*. Position sits inside the test, so the duty is identical while what it requires can reflect the role.

**Suggested wording:** the duties apply identically to every category of director, and while the objective limb takes account of the position held, it never reduces to nothing because of a non-executive title.

**Stated that way it is unanswerable. As drafted, a company lawyer will find the gap and use it to discount the section.**
