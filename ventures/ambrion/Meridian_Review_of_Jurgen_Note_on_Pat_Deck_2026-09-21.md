## 1. THE TWO DATES CHECKED INDEPENDENTLY, AND BOTH HOLD

This was the first thing to do, because Jürgen's lead argument is a verification failure charge. **If his own dates were wrong, the note would collapse on contact.**

**The Product Liability Directive corrigendum: CONFIRMED.** Directive (EU) 2024/2853 as originally published applied to products placed on the market or put into service after 9 December 2026. A corrigendum issued in May 2026 corrected Article 2 to **8 December 2026**, and the consolidated EUR-Lex text now reads 8 December. Independent practitioner commentary describes the change in those terms. Jürgen's point stands and is safe to put in writing.

**The December 2027 date: CONFIRMED, and it can be stated more precisely.** The deferral was not a proposal at the time of Pat's talk. **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI, was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026.** It defers high-risk obligations for standalone Annex III systems to **2 December 2027**, and for AI embedded in Annex I regulated products to **2 August 2028**. Jürgen's "from December 2027" is right. **Say 2 December 2027 and cite the Regulation, because a named instrument in the Official Journal is unanswerable and "December 2027" invites a quibble.**

**Meridian's addition, and it sharpens point 9 considerably.** The Omnibus did **not** move everything. **Article 50 transparency obligations remain on their original 2 August 2026 timeline and are in force now.** A deck that points a construction audience at 2027 and 2028 while the one obligation already biting goes unmentioned is not merely incomplete. **It tells directors they have time, when in one respect they do not.** That is the strongest available version of the "no anchor in present liability" charge and it does not depend on the unverified Building Control material at all.

## 2. ONE OF JÜRGEN'S POINTS IS OVERSTATED AND SHOULD BE SOFTENED

**The claim:** Irish company law does not distinguish executive, non-executive, nominee or shadow directors when applying duties, which closes off the "I'm only a NED" escape.

**The correction.** The *duties* in section 228 apply to every director without distinction, and that much is right and is worth saying. But section 228(1)(g) measures conduct against a reasonable person having the knowledge and experience **"that may reasonably be expected of a person in the same position as the director."** Position is inside the test. **So the standard is the same for everyone, while what it requires of a particular director can legitimately vary with the role that director holds.**

**Why this matters practically.** If the note asserts that the law draws no distinction at all, any competent company lawyer in Pat's circle will point at the words "in the same position", and the correction will be used to discount the rest of the note. **Stated accurately it is still devastating and it cannot be answered:** the duty is identical, the escape does not exist, and the second limb means the bar rises for anyone who has just been briefed.

**Recommended wording:** the duties apply identically to executive, non-executive, nominee and shadow directors, and while what the objective limb requires can reflect the position held, it never reduces to nothing because of a non-executive title.

## 3. WHERE MERIDIAN AGREES WITHOUT QUALIFICATION

**The section 228(1)(g) second limb is the best point in the note and it should lead the substantive half.** A director is measured against the knowledge and experience they actually have. Everyone who sat through that talk now knows more about AI risk than they did. **The standard they are judged by rose in the room.** No Irish governance speaker says this, it is retrievable from the Revised Acts, and it converts a compliance talk into a personal exposure talk.

**Caremark has no force in Ireland.** Citing a Delaware doctrine to an Irish room while the Irish provision sits unused is a real miss, not a stylistic one.

**The deployer to provider trap under Article 25 is the right commercial point for this audience.** White labelling a vendor's tool is ordinary practice among contractors, and a deployer that puts its own name on a high-risk system, substantially modifies it, or changes its intended purpose can assume provider obligations. Raising substantial modification only for machinery, and never for the AI Act, is the kind of omission that costs somebody money.

**And the structural charge is the one that lands.** Opening on Cadbury, governance as how companies are directed and controlled, and then delivering instruments, dates and authorities. **It says who regulates and never who decides, on what evidence, with what record.** Using a speaker's own opening slide against the rest of the deck is difficult to dismiss and it is fair.
