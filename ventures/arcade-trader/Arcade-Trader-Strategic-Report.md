## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Arcade Trader has a real base, industry knowledge, engineering capability, machine-sales and used-machine trading, and European trading experience, but a thin digital footprint (arcadetrader.ie is currently a holding page) and a business that depends on lumpy, one-off equipment sales.

Two moves define the opportunity:

1. **Pivot from reseller to a recurring-revenue services company** (equipment + installation + contracted maintenance + refurbishment + trade-in + cashless retrofit + design consultancy). This fixes the top risk (over-reliance on one-off sales), builds a resilient income base, and is genuinely defensible for an Irish specialist on response time and local coverage.
2. **Turn the regulatory upheaval into the sales pitch.** Ireland's gambling regime is being rebuilt under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and the new regulator. The supplier who arrives "regulation-aware", keeping operators on the right side of the amusement-versus-gaming line, has a stronger proposition than one who simply sells machines.

There is also an immediate, verified lead: a **newly permitted arcade in Midleton** (Coalquay Leisure), at fit-out stage now.

## THE STRATEGIC MOVE: RESELLER → RECURRING-REVENUE SERVICES

This is the heart of it, and the pivot is low-conceptual-risk because it monetises an installed base and engineering capability Arcade Trader already has.

- **It fixes the binding risk:** one-off sales are lumpy and cyclical; contracted maintenance and spares are sticky and repeatable.
- **It changes the value of the business:** recurring contracted income is worth a materially higher multiple than the same revenue from project sales, so the pivot builds enterprise value, not just cash flow.
- **It is defensible for a small Irish specialist** on local response time and single-supplier convenience, which UK and European suppliers cannot match, rather than on proprietary technology.

The revenue lines, in priority sequence:
1. **Maintenance/service contracts + spare parts** (recurring) — the first and most important.
2. **Refurbishment cycles + trade-in** (fast-follow) — a natural on-ramp from the existing used-machine trading; capital-light.
3. **Cashless retrofit/installation** — rides the strongest structural trend in the industry; a natural attach to installation and service.
4. **Design/consultancy for new arcades and FECs** — the wedge that wins the whole fit-out (see the pipeline).
5. **Managed/revenue-share placement** — highest recurring value but capital-intensive and entangled with gaming rules; hold for later, selectively.

**Illustrative economics (Meridian model, ESTIMATE — needs John Lynch's real numbers):** a per-machine maintenance contract around EUR 900 to 1,600 a year at roughly 55 to 65% margin; a per-venue relationship around EUR 15,000 to 45,000 a year. These are shapes to show the compounding logic of recurring revenue versus lumpy project sales, not quotes.

## THE REGULATORY LANDSCAPE (the differentiator)

This is where the report earns its keep. The whole strategy should be framed around one legal fault line and one reform.

**The fault line: amusement machine versus gaming machine.**
- An **amusement machine** offers only a replay or a **non-monetary prize** (Revenue treats the prize threshold as roughly EUR 7). It sits under a lighter excise permit regime and largely outside the heaviest gambling controls.
- A **gaming machine** offers a **money prize**. It is gambling: it needs local-authority adoption of Part III of the 1956 Act, a District Court certificate, and a Revenue gaming-machine licence, with a maximum stake of EUR 5 and maximum prize of EUR 500 (2019 Act), and minimum age 18.
- **The more a machine pays out real money, the deeper into the regime it and its operator fall.** This single line should drive Arcade Trader's product positioning.

**The reform: the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and the GRAI.**
- The Act (No. 35 of 2024) creates the **Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland**, established 5 March 2025, as the single licensing body, replacing the fragmented Revenue/District Court/local-authority system. Roll-out is phased: betting first (2026), **gaming and in-person licensing later (2027 to 2028)**. Penalties reach EUR 20 million or 10% of turnover.
- New obligations reaching operators: licensing, advertising restrictions, safer-gambling measures, a National Gambling Exclusion Register, minimum age 18, and a Social Impact Fund levy. The Act appears to **preserve a lighter carve-out for genuine amusement machines.**

**What it means for Arcade Trader (the supplier):**
- The **licence sits with the operator, not the equipment supplier** — nothing reviewed requires a supplier to hold a licence to sell or lease machines today.
- **The single most strategically material open question:** whether the future GRAI B2B phase (2027 to 2028) will introduce any supplier-side licence or registration for those who supply gambling equipment. This must be monitored with the GRAI. Flag it to John Lynch.
- **The opportunity:** be the compliance-aware partner. Supply gaming machines configured to the EUR 5/EUR 500 limits out of the box; offer cashless, traceable, age-gated machines aligned with safer-gambling; provide compliance advice on Part III status, court certification, Revenue duty now and GRAI later; and lean into the **amusement/redemption/skill category** that the new Act keeps outside the heaviest rules. The pitch becomes: "this range keeps you out of the gambling licence regime, and this range is GRAI-ready if you do want gaming." That turns regulatory change from a threat into a sales conversation.

## RISKS AND THE HONEST CHALLENGE

- **The binding constraint is the single owner.** The business is one person deep. Scaling recurring service (which needs reliable technician coverage and honoured SLAs) requires the business to stop being just John Lynch. This is the thing that must be solved.
- **Cash flow through the transition** from lumpy sales to a recurring base.
- **Regulatory-cycle risk:** if tighter GRAI rules push operators out of cash-payout gaming, demand for classic gaming machines could soften, another reason to lean into amusement/redemption and services.
- **Two or three things must go right:** the installed base actually converts to contracts; the business gains depth beyond one person; and the service promise is honoured.
