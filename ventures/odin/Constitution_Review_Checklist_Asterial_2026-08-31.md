## WHAT YOU WILL ALMOST CERTAINLY FIND

**A model constitution for a private company limited by shares (LTD) under the Companies Act 2014**, adopted at incorporation by a formation agent, with a single class of ordinary shares and nothing bespoke in it.

**That is not a criticism and it is not unusual.** It is what almost every Irish company is incorporated with. It is entirely adequate for a company with one shareholder and no external capital. **It is not adequate for a company taking €20m from an institutional investor**, and it will have to be replaced or amended as a condition of completion.

**So the instinct is right.** The question is not whether the constitution needs work. It is how much, and whether that work is done in advance or discovered during diligence.

## THE EIGHTEEN POINTS TO CHECK

### The basics

1. **Company type.** LTD under Part 2 of the Companies Act 2014, or DAC. An LTD has a single-document constitution and no objects clause. Confirm which.
2. **Incorporation date and company number.** Cross-check against what has been represented.
3. **Authorised share capital.** An LTD may have none stated, which is normal and flexible. If a limit is stated, it may need increasing to issue the new shares.
4. **Issued share capital.** How many shares, of what nominal value, and who holds them. This is the cap table before the round.
5. **Share classes.** Expect one class of ordinary shares. **An investor will require a new class of preference shares**, and creating it requires a constitutional amendment and a shareholder resolution.

### What an investor will require to be added or changed

6. **Pre-emption rights on new issues.** Whether existing holders have the right of first refusal on new shares, and whether it is disapplied for this round.
7. **Pre-emption on transfer.** Restrictions on transferring shares, and the process for offering them round first.
8. **Directors' power to refuse a transfer.** Usually absolute in a model constitution. An investor will want their own shares carved out of it.
9. **Drag-along and tag-along.** Almost certainly absent. An investor will require both, and a founder should look very carefully at the drag threshold.
10. **Liquidation preference.** The order in which proceeds are paid on a sale or winding-up. **This is the single most valuable term in the investor's document and it does not exist in a model constitution.**
11. **Anti-dilution protection.** What happens to the investor's percentage if a later round is priced lower.
12. **Consent matters and reserved matters.** The list of decisions that cannot be taken without investor approval. Expect it to be long.
13. **Board composition and the investor director.** How many directors, who appoints them, and quorum requirements that give the investor a veto by absence.

### Governance mechanics that matter to the company secretary

14. **Quorum for board and general meetings**, and whether written resolutions are permitted.
15. **Notice periods** for meetings, and whether short notice can be given with consent.
16. **Casting vote of the chair.** Present in some model forms and frequently removed by investors.
17. **Indemnity of officers**, and whether the company is permitted to fund directors and officers insurance. Relevant to the D&O point already raised.
18. **Anything bespoke that was added at incorporation.** Unusual in a formation-agent constitution, but if something is there it was put there for a reason and it needs explaining.

## THE THREE FINDINGS TO EXPECT

**One. The constitution will be replaced, not amended.** On a round of this size the practice is to adopt a new constitution in full at completion, alongside a shareholders agreement that carries most of the commercial terms. Expect the investor's counsel to produce both. **They will be drafted from the investor's precedent and they will favour the investor**, which is normal and is why the company needs its own solicitor rather than relying on theirs.

**Two. The shareholders agreement matters more than the constitution.** The constitution is public and structural. The shareholders agreement is private and is where the reserved matters, the founder lock-in, the vesting, the non-compete and the information rights live. **The provisions most likely to reach into Meridian, Ambrion, Velocity or EOLAS will be in the shareholders agreement, not the constitution.** That is the document to read hardest.

**Three. Whatever the constitution says about share transfers, it does not create rights that are not there.** A constitution governs how shares move. It does not record understandings between people about shares that have never been issued. That point stands separately and is dealt with elsewhere in this file.

## WHAT TO DO WITH IT

1. **Obtain the constitution and the CRO printout this week.** Cheap, fast, and it is company secretary housekeeping regardless of the round.
2. **Read it against the eighteen points above** and mark which are absent. Most will be.
3. **Do not amend it in advance.** The investor will bring their own form. Amending now wastes fees and creates a document that gets replaced anyway.
4. **Do use it to check the cap table** against what has been represented. Issued shares, classes, and registered holders. **This is the one thing in the constitution and printout that is a fact rather than a form, and it should be verified.**
5. **Confirm the statutory registers are complete and up to date** — members, directors, secretaries, beneficial ownership. The RBO filing in particular. Gaps here are found in diligence and fixed at solicitor rates under time pressure.
