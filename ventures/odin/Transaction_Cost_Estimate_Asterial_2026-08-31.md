# ASTERIAL — TRANSACTION COST ESTIMATE

**Confirmed by John, 31 August 2026:** the agreed terms are **€20m for 20%**. That fixes a **€100m post-money** and an **€80m pre-money**. The €19m / 24% figure recorded in the earlier documents is superseded and should not be cited again.

**Question:** what would the legal and accounting fees and costs be to complete a €20m for 20% raise.

**Status:** IN REVIEW. **These figures are UNVERIFIED market estimate, not quotations.** They are Meridian's professional judgement from Irish market convention for growth-equity rounds of this size. No adviser has been approached and no fee has been quoted. Every number below moves on the actual scope, the actual firms, and how hard the negotiation gets.

---

## HEADLINE

**Expect €400,000 to €900,000 all-in without a corporate finance adviser, and €700,000 to €1.6m with one.**

The working rule for an Irish round of this size is **2 to 4 per cent of the raise**, and a broker or corporate finance mandate is what pushes it to the top of that band or through it. On €20m that is **€400,000 to €800,000 as the normal case**.

**Almost all of it is payable by Asterial, not by the investor**, and a material part is payable **whether or not the deal completes**.

## THE BUILD-UP

All figures are estimated ranges, excluding VAT at 23% unless stated.

### Legal

| Item | Who instructs | Estimated range |
|---|---|---|
| **Company counsel** — term sheet negotiation, subscription and shareholders agreement, constitution amendment, disclosure letter, warranties, completion mechanics | Asterial | **€125,000 – €275,000** |
| **Investor counsel** — their own drafting and review, **normally paid by the company under a negotiated cap** | Investor | **€75,000 – €175,000** |
| **Legal due diligence** — corporate, contracts, employment, data protection, litigation | Investor's firm, company bears | **included above or €30,000 – €70,000 if separately scoped** |
| **IP diligence, assignment and licence work** | Both | **€40,000 – €120,000** |
| **Company secretarial, CRO filings, share issue mechanics** | Asterial | **€3,000 – €12,000** |

**Legal sub-total: roughly €250,000 to €600,000.**

**The IP line is the one that is unusual in this deal and it is why the range is wide.** In a standard round the company already owns its IP and the lawyers confirm it. Here the ODIN IP sits with Shane personally, and Tairseach's boundary is unresolved. That converts a confirmation exercise into a construction exercise: assignment or exclusive licence documents have to be drafted, the Tairseach carve-out has to be defined, and the investor's counsel has to be satisfied that what they are funding is inside the company. **If that work is done in advance of diligence it is at the bottom of the range. If it is discovered during diligence it is at the top, and it delays completion.**

### Corporate finance adviser or broker, if engaged

**1 to 3 per cent of funds raised, so €200,000 to €600,000**, sometimes with a retainer of €10,000 to €25,000 per month against the success fee.

**This is the single largest swing item.** If Shane has sourced the investor directly and no mandate exists, this cost is zero and the all-in lands in the €400k to €900k band. If a mandate exists or is signed, it can exceed the entire legal bill on its own. **The first thing to establish is whether anybody is entitled to a success fee on this round**, including on an introduction already made.

## THE FOUR THINGS THAT ACTUALLY CONTROL THE NUMBER

**1. The investor cost cap, and it is a term sheet item.** The investor's legal fees being paid by the company is standard practice. The cap is negotiable and it should be a hard number written into the term sheet, not "reasonable costs". Uncapped, this line has no ceiling and Asterial has no control over how many hours the other side's firm spends. **A cap of €100,000 to €125,000 on investor legal would be a reasonable ask on a €20m round.**

**2. Broken deal costs.** If the transaction does not complete, a large part of the legal, accounting and diligence spend has already been incurred and is still payable. On this deal, where the counterparty has not yet been verified to Meridian's satisfaction, **that exposure is live from the moment advisers are instructed.** The term sheet should say who bears costs if the investor walks. Establishing the counterparty properly is therefore not just a governance point, it is a cost-control point.

**3. Whether the IP work is done before or during diligence.** Doing the ODIN assignment and the Tairseach boundary in advance is cheaper, faster and stronger than having the investor's lawyers find it open. Same work, materially different price, and a different negotiating position.

**4. Whether Asterial's records are diligence-ready.** Statutory registers, board minutes, contracts, employment documentation, data protection records. Every gap is billed at senior solicitor rates to fix under time pressure. **This is company secretary territory and it is John's own role.** It is the one line on this page John can reduce personally, starting now.

## FOUR COSTS NOT IN THE TABLE

**John's own advice is a separate cost and Asterial should not pay it.** A solicitor acting for John personally on any participation is €5,000 to €20,000 depending on complexity, plus tax advice. **If Asterial pays it, the adviser's independence is compromised and the conflict is on the file.** John pays it himself.

**Directors and officers insurance.** Likely a completion requirement in any event. Order of €5,000 to €25,000 annually depending on cover and limits.

**Ongoing cost of the investor relationship.** After completion there is board reporting, an investor director, monthly or quarterly management accounts to an agreed standard, and probably an audit requirement. That is a permanent increase in Asterial's finance function cost and it is frequently underestimated. **Budget €50,000 to €100,000 a year of additional finance and governance cost from completion.**

**Management time.** Not a fee, but the largest real cost. A round of this size will absorb a substantial share of the founder's attention for three to six months, at exactly the point the €80m pre-money is being justified by growth.

## WHAT MERIDIAN RECOMMENDS ON COST

1. **Get the investor legal cost cap into the term sheet as a hard figure.** Not "reasonable". A number.
2. **Establish now whether any success fee or introduction fee is claimable by anyone.** Before it is asserted at completion.
3. **Do the IP assignment and the Tairseach boundary before diligence starts**, not during. Cheapest single saving available.
4. **Bring the statutory records to diligence standard now.** John's own role, and it takes days rather than money.
5. **Agree who bears broken deal costs**, given the counterparty is not yet verified.
6. **Ask two Irish firms with genuine venture and growth-round experience for scoped fee estimates** rather than hourly rates. Fixed or capped fees per workstream where possible. The estimates above are Meridian's, not the market's, and should be replaced by real quotations as soon as the term sheet lands.
