## WHAT IT IS, IN ONE LINE

Nine stages that take a half-formed idea to a rough thing that runs, with two gates in the middle that ask **can it exist** and **what would it cost**, plus a third question on **what it answers to legally**. Commands: `/studio` for the full run, `/reality-check` for ten minutes, `/build-review` free and unlimited.

**The design principle that matters commercially:** the gates sit at stages 3 and 4, deliberately *after* the widening and never during it. And nothing blocks. It flags, scores, explains, and lets you continue.

---

## THE FINDING: IT FILLS THE ONE GAP IN MERIDIAN'S PRODUCT LADDER

Meridian has five products. Decision Brief, Executive Intelligence Review, Transaction Intelligence, Intelligence Partner, AI Equity Structuring.

**Every single one of them ends in a written position. Not one of them ends in a thing that exists.**

That is fine, and it is deliberate: Meridian sells the decision, not the build. But it leaves a specific and recurring hole. **When a Decision Brief concludes "yes, do this", the client's next question is always the same: can it actually be built, what would it cost, and can I see it.** Today the honest answer is "go and find somebody to tell you." That is the moment the engagement ends and somebody else's begins.

The Design Studio answers precisely those three questions, and it does it in an evening.

**Read as a chain, it is very clean:**

> **Meridian** answers *should we do this*. **The Design Studio** answers *can it exist, what would it cost, here is a rough version running*. **Velocity** answers *now build it properly*.

Three steps, three answers, and each one earns the next.

## FIVE COMMERCIAL USES, IN ORDER OF WHAT I WOULD DO FIRST

### 1. It upgrades the Decision Brief at no extra cost, and that is the immediate win

Any Decision Brief touching *should we build, buy or commission this* can now carry a **feasibility and cost section grounded in an actual tiering exercise** rather than in reasoning alone. Capability tiered against what works in production today, effort estimated in senior-people months, regulatory territory spotted.

**Same product, same price, materially better.** And it directly strengthens the two weakest parts of any advisory output: whether the recommended thing is technically real, and what it would genuinely cost.

**Do this first because it costs nothing and improves what is already for sale.**

### 2. A new product between the Brief and the Review: the Feasibility Brief

There is a genuine product sitting in stages 3, 4, 6 and 8.

> **"You have an idea. Here is whether it can exist, what it would cost in senior-people months, what it would have to answer to, and a rough version running that you can put in front of your board."**

That is not a decision brief and it is not a build. **It is the thing every owner wants before committing capital and cannot currently buy anywhere.** A consultancy will write them a deck. A developer will quote them a build. Nobody offers the honest middle.

Price it between the Decision Brief and the Executive Intelligence Review. The demo is what justifies the fee, because a board that can *see* the thing decides in one meeting rather than three.

### 3. Run it on Meridian itself, and this is the one I would push hardest

The build-to-sell question raised earlier this month has never been answered: **can Meridian become an asset somebody could buy, rather than a practice that is John?**

The corpus, the audit record, the decision precedent, the release gate. **Those are software.** They are currently a discipline held in files and in one person's head.

**Run Meridian through the nine stages.** Stage 3 asks whether the thing can exist. Stage 4 asks what it would cost. Stage 7 stress-tests the economics adversarially. That is exactly the question that has been sitting open, and the tool was built to answer it.

**It also costs nothing and needs nobody's permission.** It is the single highest-value use available this week.

### 4. It unblocks the ventures that have been stuck at "is this real"

Barber Republic's MVP. Property Properly. Childen. The build-to-sell tier that has never been formally structured. **Every one of those has been parked at the same point: nobody could say cheaply whether the thing was buildable and what it would cost.**

`/reality-check` answers that in ten minutes, for free, as many times as wanted. **Several of those should be run through it before the next conversation about any of them**, because a parked venture with an honest cost number attached is a decision, and a parked venture without one is just a worry.

### 5. It is a door-opener that costs nothing to give away

*"Bring me the idea you have been carrying around. I will tell you by Friday whether it can exist and roughly what it would cost."*

**That is a genuinely valuable free offer, it takes an evening, and no accountant, solicitor or consultant in Ireland can make it.** For a LEO mentoring conversation, a first meeting with an owner, or the opening of a succession discussion where the next generation has ideas, it is an unusually strong opening.

It is also, notably, the exact shape of the What-else scan skill: what does this client need next.

## THE BOUNDARY, BECAUSE THIS IS THE FOURTH-BRAND PROBLEM AGAIN

**The Design Studio does not make Meridian a builder.** Building is Velocity's ground and that boundary was settled on 5 August.

The line that holds, and it is a clean one:

> **Meridian uses the Studio to establish feasibility and cost so that its recommendation is sound. It does not use it to deliver the build.** Stage 8 produces a brief. **The brief goes to Velocity.**

Stage 6 produces a rough demo, and a demo is a decision aid, not a product. **If Meridian ever starts shipping stage 6 output as the deliverable, it has crossed into Velocity's territory and the boundary has failed.** Worth saying out loud to Shane rather than discovering later.
