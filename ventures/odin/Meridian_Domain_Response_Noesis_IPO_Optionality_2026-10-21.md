## 1. Re-registration as a PLC
- A private company limited by shares cannot list as it is. It must be a public limited company. Part 17 of the Companies Act 2014 is the PLC part. The authorised minimum share capital of a PLC is €25,000 (s.1000, VERIFIED-SECONDARY: search result quoting the statute page, with the Minister able to raise the figure by order).
- Process: special resolution, a PLC constitution, a CRO filing (a secondary source gives Form D20), and at least 25% of nominal value paid up on the minimum capital. **UNVERIFIED** on the form number, the 25% figure and the exact sections for private-to-PLC conversion. Meridian will not cite section numbers it has not seen.
- Officers: a PLC needs at least two directors and a secretary with the qualifications the Act prescribes (UNVERIFIED). **Flag for John:** check now whether his qualifications meet the PLC secretary test, because he is the named secretary of Noesis, Asterial and Sancelvio.
- Practical timing: re-registration is the easy step. The long steps are audited financials to listing standard, a prospectus or admission document, a listing agent, and governance compliance (the Irish Corporate Governance Code on a main market). REPORTED.

## 2. Is "an IPO clause in the constitution now" the right idea?
Largely your understanding is right. There is no standard device that makes a private company's constitution IPO-ready in advance. Re-registration and a new PLC constitution happen when a float is actually pursued.
Refinement: some provisions do belong in the private constitution now, because class rights live there.
- **Conversion on a qualifying IPO:** a clause converting Class A and Class B into ordinary shares (or into a defined listed-class structure) on a listing event. This is a constitution item.
- **Authority to reorganise:** directors empowered to take the steps needed (still subject to the special resolution for re-registration).

## 3. What sits in the shareholders' agreement instead (REPORTED)
- Cooperation undertakings, lock-ups, consent mechanics, termination of the shareholders' agreement on listing.
- A drag-along is a sale device, not an IPO device. A float needs a special resolution and cooperation, not a drag. If you want a minority not to block a float, the tool is a shareholder undertaking plus the class conversion clause, not a drag. UNVERIFIED as drafting practice; confirm with the solicitor.
- Listed shares must be freely transferable. Euronext Dublin's rules require securities to be freely negotiable, restrictions only with approval, and a free float of 25% (or lower agreed, not below 5% and €5m) (VERIFIED-SECONDARY: Baker McKenzie listing guide summary). So founder transfer restrictions, pre-emption and no-dilution provisions must fall away at listing. The carve-out you describe is therefore necessary, and the cleanest way is automatic cessation on a qualifying listing. Dual-class control on a listing venue is a question for the listing agent, not settled here.

## 4. Structural points on the vehicle (flags only, not a recommendation)
1. **Noesis as vehicle carries the whole group.** Most Noesis companies are dormant, Grassroots is deliberately outside, and the Class A/B design plus Noesis reserved matters were built to protect founder control. Those are the features a public market discounts or refuses. Class B holders (including John at about 1%) would be affected by conversion, lock-ups and dilution; their terms should be written for that day.
2. **The €20m Asterial round is the nearest constraint.** The investor will buy Asterial shares. If Noesis later lists, the investor is a minority in a subsidiary of a listed company, which investors resist, and if Asterial lists they will want conversion and exit terms. **The term sheet should contain an exit and reorganisation clause now** (share-for-share swap into the listed entity, consent, lock-up). This is the most time-sensitive point in the note.
3. **Clean IP.** An IPO vehicle must own the IP it is valued on. ODIN sits with Shane personally and the Tairseach boundary is open (REPORTED, earlier records). A carved-out vehicle with a clean assignment is easier than a holding company with mixed assets.
4. **Track record.** Listing venues expect audited accounts for a period (UNVERIFIED, varies by venue). A group of dormant and new companies has little. A subsidiary with real trading and audited accounts is a stronger candidate than a holding company.
5. **Related-party and intercompany flows.** The Noesis group governance agreement and intercompany IP licences will be disclosed and scrutinised in a prospectus. Draft them now as if a stranger will read them.
