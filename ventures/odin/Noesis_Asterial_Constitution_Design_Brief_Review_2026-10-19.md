## 1. WHAT THE BRIEF PROPOSES (SUMMARY)

Two connected constitutions with different jobs. **Noesis** preserves ownership, control and continuity across the companies and will not raise investment. **Asterial** protects ODIN while enabling investment, development and commercialisation.

**Noesis**
- Class A founder control and Class B economic participation designed explicitly, so voting, board appointment, meeting rules and special consent reinforce each other.
- Family, friends and partners get clear economic rights (dividends, sale proceeds, winding-up) not dependent on the founder's goodwill, and no automatic operational role.
- No-dilution commitment built into the share-capital provisions: new shares, options, convertibles and changes to economic rights. Noesis keeps its percentage even if it is diluted inside Asterial.
- Reserved decisions: sale or pledge of major shareholdings, substantial guarantees, exceptional borrowing, material transfers between connected companies. Dividends only from distributable profits.
- Transfer restrictions, first purchase opportunity, valuation procedure, permitted family transfers, death and incapacity rules, lock-up length still undecided. Inheriting economic value distinguished from inheriting authority to run the group. Minority protections.

**Asterial**
- Protected asset defined precisely: architecture, code, orchestration systems, methods, documentation and rights. A maintained ownership register distinguishing assigned ODIN assets, separately held founder IP, subsidiary work and third-party material, backed by signed assignments and licences.
- Approval framework for disposal, fragmentation or security over core assets. Enhanced approval for licences that restrict development, grant broad exclusivity or give another party control of core technology.
- Investment rights designed for future rounds, employee participation and investor protections, with a control-impact assessment before any funding structure is approved.
- Constitutional requirement for accountable human governance of AI operations. Agents cannot expand their own authority, approve their own exceptions or rewrite the policy limiting them. Emergency isolation permitted without any power to dispose of IP.

**Four layers:** company constitution; shareholder and investment agreements; IP and intercompany agreements; board-approved operating policies. Controls must be mirrored in signing authority, bank mandates and technical permissions. Stress tests: a funding round, a subsidiary sale, a shareholder leaving, the founder unavailable, an agent attempting an unauthorised commitment.

## 2. MERIDIAN'S READ

**Sound as a framework.** The four-layer separation is right because the layers change at different rates, and the stress tests are good. The statutory points (allotment authority, class rights, distributions from distributable profits, no objects clause for an LTD, special resolution to amend) are consistent with Meridian's understanding of the Companies Act 2014. No section numbers are given.

**Five concerns**

1. **Sequencing against the live raise.** The €20m for 20 per cent is awaiting a term sheet. The investor's counsel will very likely replace Asterial's constitution at completion and draft the shareholders agreement from the investor's precedent (Meridian note of 31 August). Asterial's constitutional protections are therefore **negotiating asks**, and a constitution drafted now is unlikely to survive. Reorganising ownership into a new holding company mid-raise adds diligence.
2. **A double lock will meet resistance.** Noesis founder vetoes over investor reserved matters is where a €20m round becomes difficult. Control routed through a holding vehicle will be questioned. Decide the position before the term sheet.
3. **The IP carve-out is the diligence target.** "Separately held founder IP" is exactly what an investor will probe. It links to the unresolved ODIN, Asterial and Tairseach boundary and to the 31 August finding that the IP is Shane's personally. A precise IP schedule comes before any constitution.
4. **Tax and who sells.** If Asterial shares move into Noesis, the seller of the €2.2m secondary, and its tax treatment, differ from the position modelled in the sources and uses briefing. Take tax advice before the term sheet.
5. **Cost.** Two constitutions, agreements and IP documents sit outside the company counsel (€125,000 to €275,000) and IP (€40,000 to €120,000) ranges in the transaction cost schedule.

**John's position.** The brief mentions "family, friends and partners" holding Class B economic rights. If John's participation is meant to be among those, that is where it would be recorded for the first time. If not, the question recorded on 16 August, 31 August and 11 September remains open, and the Company Secretary role still carries duties without economics. Either way John needs his own solicitor, and Meridian informs but cannot advise Shane on this.
