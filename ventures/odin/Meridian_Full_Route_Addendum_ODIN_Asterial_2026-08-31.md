## MANAGER 3 — FINANCE AND RESTRUCTURING

*High-stakes tier. Owns fundraising, tax structuring, succession planning, investor materials.*

Three findings, and the first is the one the main response missed entirely.

### 3.1 There is a tax event in this, and nobody has costed it

If a participation is ever created for John — shares, options, a convertible, or a fee settled in equity — the Irish tax treatment is not neutral and it is not the same across those instruments. Share awards, share options and growth or hurdle shares have materially different timing and different charge points, and the worst case is a charge crystallising on issue at a valuation set by a €19m–€20m institutional round, on paper John cannot sell.

**That is the specific reason "sort it after the raise" is expensive rather than merely late.** Before the round, the shares are worth what a small private company's shares are worth. After it, they are marked to an €80m pre-money. The same 1% costs a different amount of tax depending on which side of the term sheet it is created.

**UNVERIFIED and outside Meridian's competence to compute.** This requires a tax adviser, and it should be the same conversation as the solicitor, not a later one.

### 3.2 The company secretary role has a personal financial exposure attached

Officers of an Irish company carry statutory obligations with personal consequences for failure, and Jürgen has covered the duties. Finance adds the question nobody has asked: **is there directors and officers insurance in place for Asterial, and does it name the secretary?** For a company about to take €19m and appoint an investor director, D&O is standard and will likely be a completion requirement anyway. John should be inside it before completion, not after.

### 3.3 Succession, which is John's own specialism and applies to him here

John advises on succession. This file is a succession problem in miniature and it points at him.

ODIN IP is Shane's personally. Asterial has one shareholder. John's participation exists in conversation only. **If anything happens to either of them tomorrow, there is no document that tells anyone what was intended.** Shane's estate would inherit a €19m-backed asset with an undocumented understanding attached to it, and John's estate would inherit nothing but a company secretary's file.

This is not a morbid point. It is the standard argument John himself makes to family businesses: the arrangement everyone remembers is worthless the moment one of the people who remembers it is not in the room.

## MANAGER 5 — AI STRATEGY AND ADOPTION

*Owns EU AI Act compliance, AI governance frameworks.*

### 5.1 ODIN is an AI system and the AI Act applies to it, whoever owns it

This has not been addressed anywhere in the file. An investor putting €19m–€20m into an AI company in the European Union in 2026 will run AI Act diligence, and Article 4 on AI literacy has been enforceable since 3 August 2026. The questions that come are predictable: what is ODIN classified as, who is provider and who is deployer, what is the technical documentation position, what is the data governance and provenance position on training material.

**The commercial point, not the compliance point:** this is diligence Asterial can either pass cleanly or stumble through, and stumbling is a valuation event. Ambrion exists to do exactly this work.

### 5.2 And that is a conflict, so it is flagged rather than proposed

John holds 30% of Ambrion. Recommending Ambrion into an Asterial raise in which John seeks a personal participation puts John on several sides at once. **Meridian raises it as an exposure to be disclosed to Shane, not as an opportunity to be pursued.** See the portfolio conflict check below.

### 5.3 Tairseach compounds the AI Act question

If Tairseach is descendant from the same IP and sits outside Asterial, then there are potentially two AI systems from one lineage under different ownership. Provider obligations follow the system, not the shareholder. That boundary needs resolving for AI Act purposes as well as for the cap table, and it is the same piece of work.

## MANAGER 6 — BUSINESS TRANSFORMATION AND GROWTH

### 6.1 The value question the term sheet does not answer

A €60.2m to €80.0m pre-money on a company at this stage is being paid for expectation, not for trading history. That means the negotiation is really about what evidence exists for the expectation: the pipeline, the contracted revenue, the deliverability of what has been demonstrated, and the concentration of all of it in one person.

**Key-person concentration is the finding.** ODIN's IP is one person's, Asterial's equity is one person's, and the capability is substantially one person's. Any competent investor will price that risk or paper against it with founder lock-in, vesting, and restrictive covenants. Those are terms that will appear in the term sheet and John should expect them.

### 6.2 The consequence for John, which is not obvious

Investor protections written to bind a sole founder are drafted broadly. **Broadly drafted covenants can catch adjacent activity**, and John and Shane have four brands between them — Meridian, Ambrion, Velocity, EOLAS — with overlapping language and, on EOLAS, verbatim shared verification vocabulary. If the term sheet contains non-compete or IP-assignment language covering Shane's work generally, it may reach into work that Meridian or Ambrion regard as theirs.

**This has not been considered anywhere and it should be, before the term sheet is signed rather than after.** The EOLAS overlap recorded on 7 August is directly relevant: a document already exists in this knowledge base saying EOLAS shares Meridian's verification floor verbatim. An investor's lawyer reading that would ask who owns it.

### 6.3 Support and grant scan — nil return

Run as standing procedure. **No Irish state support materially bears on this matter.** A company raising €19m institutionally is outside the useful range of LEO, Skillnet and Innovation Voucher instruments, and Enterprise Ireland co-investment would be a strategic choice with its own conditions rather than a support to be claimed. Recorded as nil rather than omitted.

## CROSS-CUTTING SKILLS NOW RUN

### Change-the-answer test

*What would have to be true for Meridian's recommendation to be wrong?*

The recommendation is: have the conversation now, before the term sheet. It would be wrong if **any one** of these is true:

1. **A document already exists.** If John and Shane have anything in writing — an email, a message, a note in a shared file — that records the participation, the urgency drops sharply and this becomes a matter of tidying rather than creating. *Meridian has not asked this question and does not know the answer. It is the first thing to check and it takes one minute.*
2. **John has decided he does not want a participation.** If the position is settled in John's own mind that ODIN is Shane's and John's involvement is as a friend and officer, then there is nothing to paper, and the recommendation reduces to writing that down once so the question stops recurring.
3. **The raise collapses.** If the counterparty proves unreal, the valuation question and the urgency both evaporate, and only the company secretary duties and the D&O question remain.

**Item 1 is the one that would most change the answer and it is the one Meridian failed to ask.**

### Evidence gap before advice

Stated plainly. Meridian is advising on:

- A term sheet it has not seen and which does not yet exist.
- A counterparty whose identity is unconfirmed.
- Two valuation figures that are second-hand and unconfirmed.
- A constitution, cap table and IP position none of which have been produced.
- A conversation between John and Shane whose contents Meridian does not know.

**Everything above is method and sequencing, not a view on the deal.** No view on the deal is available on this evidence and none should be inferred. The advice that survives the evidence gap is narrow and it is procedural: check whether anything is already written down, get John his own solicitor, verify the counterparty to four points, and do it before the IP moves.
