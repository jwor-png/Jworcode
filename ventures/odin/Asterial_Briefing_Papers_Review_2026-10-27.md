## What the papers are
Three product concepts for a fund or allocator, each "living intelligence" that runs continuously on the fund's own systems: (1) a view of every portfolio company through five lenses (founders and teams, technology, market, regulation, capital) plus portfolio-wide concentration; (2) continuous verification of every company, bank, country and investment, "with proof and date", before and after money moves; (3) a weighted score of countries on about fifty factors in five groups, read over 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 years, telling the fund where money is safe and when to move. Each has the same shape: problem, solution, "inversion: what kills an investment", how we think, what it takes (weeks to a few months), where it is built (inside the fund's systems, owned by the fund).

## What is strong
- Clear, short, and structured around a concrete first deliverable ("the first thing the fund sees is...").
- The principle "every answer carries its proof and its date, and when two sources disagree the fund hears it that day" is the right standard and matches Meridian's verified, reported and unverified labelling.
- Data and judgements stay with the fund. Weights are the fund's own view, written down and testable.

## Challenges (ranked)
1. **The capability claim is unevidenced.** "Intelligence of this kind runs in our work today, on other subjects" and "standard work for us" are the load-bearing sentences in all three papers. No named example, demonstration, accuracy measure or reference. A fund will test it. Ask Shane what runs today, on what subject, with what result, and decide what can be shown.
2. **"Owned outright" and "inside the fund's own systems" conflict with the IP architecture.** Paper 3 says "the fund owns it outright" and all three promise "a model of the fund's own" and deployment on the fund's systems. That implies licensing or transferring the engine to a client. The working position from 21 October is that the ODIN core is never licensed (tiered access, crown jewels stay in Asterial). An investor at €100m post will read these papers. Decide what the fund owns (weights, configuration, outputs) and what Asterial keeps (the engine), and say so.
3. **The inversion logic overreaches.** All three papers argue that removing the listed failure causes leaves "a sound investment", "the portfolio worth backing further" or "countries that keep money safe". Absence of the named dangers does not make an investment sound. Reword to "remove the common causes of loss".
4. **Country scoring and ten-year horizons imply precision that cannot be supported.** Scoring is standard (ratings agencies, multilateral and commercial providers already publish it). What is claimed as new is forecasting "which countries will have to cut in the next one, two, three, five or ten years" and "when to move". No back-test is offered. Ask for one, and cut the ten-year view or label it scenario analysis.
5. **Regulatory exposure, for a solicitor (UNVERIFIED):**
   - Telling a fund where to move money may be investment research or advice under financial services law if it recommends specific action. Add a clear boundary: a tool and not advice.
   - Verification claims (ownership behind holding companies, sanctions exposure through a shareholder) overlap regulated anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening. The fund's own legal duty cannot be outsourced to a tool. Public beneficial ownership registers were restricted by the EU Court of Justice in 2022 (from memory), so access to ownership data is limited to those with a legitimate interest.
   - "How they hold under pressure" (paper 1) means profiling named individuals. That raises GDPR questions (lawful basis, profiling, accuracy). It is also a reputational risk if wrong.
6. **Data licensing.** "Every item is measured from a live source" depends on licences. Many commercial data terms restrict use in AI systems or redistribution. Needs checking per source.
7. **Internal inconsistencies.** Paper 3 summary says "a build of months at most, and the fund owns it outright"; the body says "weeks to a few months". Papers 1 and 2 say "weeks to a few months". Align, and do not promise a timeline before the team and scope are known.
8. **No boundary or disclaimer.** Meridian's equivalent is "informs, never represents". The papers should state what the intelligence is not (not advice, not a guarantee, not a regulated screening service).
9. **Audience is unclear.** "The fund" is generic and "the fund's own vehicles" implies a specific one. Who is it for, and has that counterparty been verified to the four points?
