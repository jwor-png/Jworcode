## Short answer

The two-tier idea is sound. Nine bespoke constitutions are not needed, and doing them risks cost, delay and inconsistency. Recommended shape: **one Noesis constitution, one standard subsidiary constitution with a short company-specific schedule, and a full bespoke build only for Asterial** (and any company that takes outside money). Control of the wholly owned companies is then exercised from Noesis through a group governance agreement and board policy, not through nine sets of articles.

## 1. What is right in the brief

- A constitution binds only its own company. Noesis rules do not reach Asterial. Correct.
- Constitutional limits do not bind outsiders. Under the Companies Act 2014 a company's capacity is not limited by its constitution, and third parties dealing in good faith are protected as to directors' authority (ss.38 to 40, UNVERIFIED). So bank mandates, signing authority and contract approval limits must enforce the same boundaries. Correct, and the most useful point in the brief.
- Four layers (constitution, shareholder and investment agreements, IP and intercompany agreements, board operating policies) is the standard architecture.
- Amendment by special resolution (75%, s.32 UNVERIFIED) with separate class consent for class-right changes (s.89 UNVERIFIED). Correct as a baseline.

## 2. Where the structure needs challenge

**2.1 Differentiation is only worth paying for where an outsider is on the register.**
Where Noesis is the sole member, a subsidiary constitution protects nobody from Noesis. What matters is who controls Noesis. For the dormant and wholly owned companies (Zubelsala, Equitas, Manly Velocity, VRH, Valhalla, and the two "to be created") a standard constitution plus a one-page schedule naming the company's core asset and its reserved matters is enough. Asterial is the exception, and Sancelvio becomes one if a child-safety partner or funder joins.

**2.2 Asterial's constitution cannot protect ODIN while the IP sits with Shane personally.**
A clause protecting ODIN's code, architecture and licensing is empty unless Asterial owns the IP or holds an exclusive licence. The assignment or licence is the foundation, and the constitution follows it. Sequence: IP assignment (or licence) first, constitution second.

**2.3 Is Asterial under Noesis at all?**
John reports Shane as sole shareholder of Asterial. The brief assumes Asterial sits under Noesis. If the shares are to move to Noesis, that is a transfer with tax and duty consequences for Shane (capital gains, possible share-for-share relief, stamp duty on the transfer, all UNVERIFIED). This belongs with Pat Carroll and a tax adviser, and it must be settled before the €20m round, because an investor buys into whatever holds the IP at that date.

**2.4 The live raise will rewrite the Asterial constitution.**
An institutional investor will require its own reserved matters, anti-dilution, board seat and consent rights. Asterial's constitution written now is a base text to be re-papered at completion. Draft it with that in mind: do not build a founder double lock that conflicts with investor reserved matters.

**2.5 "Structurally enforced no-dilution" needs the right mechanism.**
Irish constitutions are amendable by special resolution, so a promise inside the constitution is only as strong as the vote that can change it. The reliable levers are: (a) director authority to allot shares requiring Class A consent, (b) pre-emption rights, (c) a class right that cannot be varied without that class's own consent, and (d) a shareholders agreement binding the parties by contract. Weighted voting for Class A needs solicitor sign-off on enforceability. Use all four layers, not the constitution alone.

**2.6 The human-authority-over-AI clause.**
Sensible as a statement of principle and as a limit on directors, but weak as a constitutional remedy. Put the operational detail in the board-approved policy and the licence terms, and keep a short reserved-matter in the constitution (no deployment of autonomous functions beyond a defined threshold without Class A consent).

## 3. Lower-duplication alternative

1. **Noesis constitution:** Class A (control), Class B (economic), allotment and transfer controls, reserved matters over subsidiaries.
2. **Group governance agreement** among Noesis and its subsidiaries: intercompany value transfers, guarantees, IP flows, signing authority matrix. One document instead of nine sets of provisions.
3. **Standard subsidiary constitution** (common core) plus a two-page company schedule.
4. **Bespoke build:** Asterial only, then Sancelvio if outside capital arrives.
5. **Bank mandates and signing matrix** aligned to all of the above.

Estimated effect: drafting effort falls from nine full constitutions to roughly two. Cost saving is likely material but UNVERIFIED until a solicitor scopes it.
