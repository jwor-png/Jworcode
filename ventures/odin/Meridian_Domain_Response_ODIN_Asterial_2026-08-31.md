## 2. COMMERCIAL AND DEAL — THE TWO FIGURES ARE NOT A RANGE

The briefing reports **€19m for 24%** and an ask of **€20m for 20%**, both unconfirmed. Read as a negotiating range, that looks like a small gap. It is not.

| | Investment | Equity | Post-money | **Pre-money** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offer as reported | €19m | 24% | €79.2m | **€60.2m** |
| Ask as reported | €20m | 20% | €100.0m | **€80.0m** |

**Pre-money gap: €19.8m. The ask is 33% above the offer.**

That is not a haggle over a point of dilution. It is two different views of what the company is worth, and the €1m difference in the headline cheque disguises it. Anyone reading these as "roughly €19–20m" is reading the wrong number. The number that is being negotiated is the pre-money, and it is the one that should be written down and tracked.

Both figures are **UNVERIFIED**. Neither should be repeated to a third party in any form until a term sheet is in hand.

## 3. COUNTERPARTY — THE NAME RISK IS THE RIGHT CALL AND IT SHOULD BE ESCALATED

Jürgen flags "BNP Capital" against BNP Paribas as a near-miss name risk. Meridian agrees and would put it higher.

A near-miss institutional name is one of the most common features of investment approaches that do not complete, and it is cheap to resolve: an entity number, a regulator registration, a named individual with a verifiable role, and a law firm on the other side that will confirm its instruction. If those four cannot be produced, that fact is itself the finding.

**Meridian's addition — this is not only a deal risk for John, it is a reputational one.** John is a serving PLC chair and an audit and risk committee chair. If the counterparty proves not to be who it presents itself as, the question asked afterwards is not whether John lost money. It is what diligence a sitting ARC chair performed before associating his name and his signature with it. He has already signed Asterial's first B1. His name is on the public file.

**The verification of the counterparty is not Shane's job to do for John. It is John's own governance exposure.**

## 4. LEGAL — COMPANY SECRETARY IS DUTIES WITHOUT ECONOMICS

Confirmed 31 August: John is **Company Secretary only, not a director**. Jürgen has covered the statutory duties. Meridian adds the structural read.

The position as it stands is:

- John **carries** statutory obligations as officer of the company.
- John **signs** the filings, including the B1 already lodged.
- John **is named** on the public record and associated with the raise.
- John **holds** no shares, no options, no convertible, no consultancy agreement, no written participation of any kind.
- John **stands to benefit** only by conversation.

That is an asymmetry, and it should be named as one rather than lived with. It is not an accusation about Shane, whose good faith is not in question anywhere in this file. It is an observation about paper. Paper is what survives a disagreement, an illness, a divorce, a death, or a new investor's lawyer reorganising the cap table as a condition of funding.

**The investor's counsel will not preserve an undocumented participation. They will clean it off the table, and they will be right to, because from where they sit it does not exist.**

Note also William Fry: if they act for the investor they are conflicted out of acting for John or Asterial. That removes one obvious source of Irish corporate counsel and should be planned around now, not discovered later.

## 5. AI EQUITY AND INVESTMENT (MANAGER 7) — THE IP POSITION IS THE DEAL

Routed because this is precisely the domain: **IP contributed for equity, structured to survive a funding round.**

The briefing records ODIN IP as Shane's personally, Asterial as the vehicle, and Tairseach as a separate descendant organism, with the ODIN / Asterial / Tairseach structure **unresolved**.

An investor putting €19m–€20m into Asterial will require that the IP the valuation rests on is **owned by, or irrevocably licensed to, the company they are buying into**. That is standard and it is non-negotiable. So one of two things happens before completion:

- **Assignment or exclusive licence from Shane into Asterial.** This is the moment the IP moves, and it is the last moment at which anything can be attached to it, including any recognition of contribution.
- **Tairseach is carved out or left behind.** If Tairseach is descendant from the same IP and sits outside Asterial, the investor will want to know why, and where the boundary is. An unresolved boundary at term sheet becomes a completion condition at diligence.

**Meridian's read: the IP restructuring step is the only remaining window. After the IP is inside Asterial and the investor is on the register, the cap table is settled and adding anyone is a negotiation with a third party rather than a conversation with a friend.**

## 6. ARGUE THE OTHER SIDE — THE CASE FOR NOT PAPERING IT

Run at full strength, because it is a real case and John holds it.

**Trust.** John and Shane have built four brands together on a handshake basis that has worked. Asking for paper can read as a statement that the handshake was insufficient, and that is a cost to a relationship that is producing.

**Timing.** Shane is mid-raise. It is the worst fortnight of the year to arrive with a personal ask. Raising it now risks being heard as leverage applied at the point of maximum pressure, which is the opposite of how John does business.

**Optics.** A participation documented in the weeks before a €19m term sheet looks, to an incoming investor's lawyer, like a last-minute insider allocation. Documented in the calm afterwards, it looks like housekeeping.

**Contribution.** John may privately judge that his contribution to ODIN does not yet warrant equity, and would rather have the conversation from a position of demonstrated value than of asserted claim.

**All four are serious. None of them survives contact with the counter-argument, which is this:** every one of those reasons is a reason to *delay*, and delay is the mechanism by which the position has already deteriorated once since 16 August. "After the raise" is not a calmer moment. It is a moment with an institutional shareholder, a shareholders agreement, pre-emption rights, an anti-dilution ratchet and a board seat sitting between John and the answer.

**The reconciliation Meridian would offer:** the ask is not "give me equity". The ask is "before the IP moves, let us write down in two paragraphs what we have both been saying, whatever it says, including that it says nothing yet." A note recording that no participation currently exists is still worth having, because it dates the conversation and it forces the question of what would create one. That version costs Shane nothing and costs the raise nothing.
