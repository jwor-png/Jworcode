# Local Update: Kilmallock and Environs

**Tangible Ambassador Summer School 2026**
**Thursday 20 August 2026, 10.15am, Deebert House Hotel**
**Speaker: John Webb O'Rourke**
**Running time: approximately 10 minutes**

---

## THE TALK

**(0:00) Opening**

Good morning, and welcome to Kilmallock.

For those of you joining on Zoom from Melbourne, from New York, from the Bay Area, let me set the scene. You are looking at a walled medieval town of a little under two thousand people, in the Golden Vale, in south County Limerick. It has a King John's Castle, a Dominican Priory, and the best surviving medieval merchant house in the country.

That is the postcard. Here is the update.

Twelve months ago, when this group last gathered for Summer School, Kilmallock was a town with potential. Today it is a town with things actually in the ground, and with a very particular piece of timing coming down the tracks. I want to give you ten minutes on what has moved, what is moving, and why I think the next twelve months matter more than the last twelve.

**(1:00) Housing, which is where every conversation in Ireland starts**

Three residential schemes are live around this town.

The first is at Ash Hill, with its access off the Charleville Road, beside the SuperValu. Forty two units. Eight two beds, eighteen three beds, eight four beds, and eight apartments. That is not an estate, that is a neighbourhood.

The second is at Glenfield, out near the National School. Twenty eight homes, granted conditional permission this year to a developer called Loquatlane. And here is the detail I want you to hold onto, because it tells you something about how the ground has shifted. There is a planning condition on that site that those homes can only be sold to individual purchasers. Not to corporate entities. Not to funds. To people who will live in them. Five years ago that condition would have been unthinkable. Today it is policy in practice, in a small town in south Limerick.

The third scheme is a further thirty one dwellings, validated with the council in the spring of this year, twelve semi detached, three detached, sixteen terraced.

Add those together and you are at a hundred and one units in the planning system. On the ground here, the working assumption among people who watch this closely is that Kilmallock is looking at something in the order of a hundred and fifty new homes over the next year or two once the council's own social housing pipeline is counted alongside it. In a town of this size, that is not incremental. That is a fifteen to twenty per cent change in the size of the place.

That brings schools, it brings custom to the main street, it brings people who need somewhere to eat, to train, to worship, and to belong. And it brings pressure. I am not going to stand here and tell you it is all upside. A town that grows that fast without thinking about it grows badly.

**(3:30) The heritage play, which is the interesting one**

The Merchant's House on Sarsfield Street is a stone mansion built in the fifteen seventies. One of about thirty that once lined this street. It has been sitting there, magnificent and empty, for most of my lifetime.

In the last twelve months that changed. The Government allocated over one point two million euro under the Rural Regeneration and Development Fund to two Limerick projects, and eight hundred and eighty six thousand of that is coming here, to the Merchant's House. Contracts have now been formally signed with an integrated design team. Louise Cotter of Cotter and Naessens leading, with a Grade One conservation architect, structural engineers, and quantity surveyors appointed.

That money takes the project to shovel ready. The intention is a multi functional space for enterprise and community use.

Sitting alongside it, the SuperValu in the town is going through a two and a half million euro redevelopment this year, and the works package includes repairs to the medieval town wall itself. A supermarket investment that also restores a fifteenth century wall. That is the kind of thing that only happens in a town that has decided what it is.

**(5:30) Infrastructure and enterprise**

Kilmallock Business Park at Ardyoul has had a steady run of applications this year, an engineering warehouse with workshop and offices, and a retail warehouse. Separately, the council has been running a Part Eight process for a Limerick Fire Service Training Centre on that same business park. A regional training facility, in Kilmallock.

The Charleville Road footpath and public lighting upgrade has been formally identified as a scheme for the council's active travel programme. I will be honest with you, it is identified, not funded. Delivery depends on the annual allocation and the district's availability is tight. That is one to keep pressure on.

The railway bridge works are ongoing and members have been seeking updates through the summer.

And in the wider county in June, five infrastructure projects were announced that between them unlock land for more than ten thousand seven hundred homes across Limerick. Most of that is city side, Mungret and the Colbert Quarter. But the point stands. Capital is moving into this county at a scale we have not seen in a generation.

**(7:00) The thing on the horizon, and it is enormous**

Now. Thirty minutes up the road from this hotel is Adare Manor.

In September of next year, from the thirteenth to the nineteenth, Adare Manor hosts the Ryder Cup. Fifty eight million euro of State investment has gone into bringing it here. The Government's own projection is a return of up to a hundred and sixty million, and the stated ambition is to beat the two hundred and sixty two million that Rome generated in 2023. Around one thousand four hundred and fifty jobs are expected around the event.

Adare Manor is owned by JP McManus. And JP McManus lives at Martinstown, which is four miles from where we are sitting. This year he was crowned champion owner in National Hunt racing for the sixth year running, the twenty third time in his career. There is an application in at Martinstown at the moment for a solar installation on the estate, around three hundred and thirty six panels, with a decision due at the end of this month.

I raise that not for the celebrity of it. I raise it because it is the single most important commercial fact about this region, and most people here have not yet done the arithmetic on it.

The Ryder Cup will put a quarter of a million visitors into south Limerick inside one week. Every bed, every restaurant, every taxi, every guide, every craft maker, every venue within an hour of Adare is in play. Kilmallock is thirty minutes away. Deebert House, where we are sitting, is thirty minutes away.

**(9:00) Which brings me to why this matters to this room**

Here is the twelve month view.

Between now and the next Summer School, this town gets its housing built, gets its Merchant's House to shovel ready, and sits twelve months out from the biggest sporting event this island will host in our lifetimes.

When Tangible comes back to Kilmallock in August of 2027, we will be four weeks out from the Ryder Cup. Four weeks. That is not a coincidence we should waste.

So my ask of this group, and it is a genuine ask, is this. The businesses and the towns that will do well out of 2027 are not the ones with the most capital. They are the ones that started talking to each other in 2026. The ones that worked out who has beds, who has capacity, who has a product worth putting in front of a visitor who has flown in from Georgia or Wisconsin and has three days spare either side of the golf.

That conversation is not a council function. It is not a developer function. It is exactly what this room is for. Raymond has spent years building a network that reaches from Kilmallock to Dunolly to Crossmaglen to the Bay Area. Next year that network has a reason to point itself at this corner of Limerick.

We have the housing coming. We have the heritage asset being restored. We have the enterprise space filling. And we have the world arriving thirty minutes up the road.

My own rule of thumb, and I will leave you with it, is that you curate opportunities, you do not collect them. Kilmallock does not need to chase everything that comes with 2027. It needs to pick the two or three things it can genuinely be excellent at, and be ready.

That is the local update. Thank you.

## FLAGGED, CHECK BEFORE YOU SPEAK

Three things I could not verify. Handle each as noted.

1. **The 150 homes figure.** I could not source a single document stating 150 units for Kilmallock. The verified planning numbers add to 101 across the three schemes. The talk presents 150 as the local working assumption, attributed to people on the ground, not as a council figure. That is honest and it is defensible. Do not attribute it to the council.

2. **The new petrol station and forecourt.** I searched Limerick planning records and local media and could not confirm a new service station or forecourt development in Kilmallock. It is not in the talk. If you know the operator or the site, tell me and I will pin it down and add it.

3. **The location of the 31 dwelling scheme.** Confirmed as a Kilmallock application validated 30 April 2026, but I could not confirm which road. The talk deliberately does not name the site. If you know it, say it on the day.

4. **Quarter of a million visitors.** I have used this as a widely quoted Ryder Cup attendance figure but did not source it to a Limerick specific document. Safer alternative if challenged: "a quarter of a million people are expected through the gates across the week." If you want it airtight, drop the number and say "tens of thousands of visitors a day for a week."
